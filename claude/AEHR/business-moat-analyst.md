# AEHR Test Systems (AEHR) -- Business Quality & Moat Analysis

**Date**: 2026-02-27 | **Price**: ~$41 | **Market Cap**: ~$1.2B

## Business Model Summary

AEHR sells semiconductor test and burn-in equipment -- systems that stress-test chips under thermal/electrical load before they ship. Revenue comes from (1) system sales (capital equipment, lumpy) and (2) consumables/contactors (recurring, tied to installed base). The company operates in a niche within the $16B semiconductor test equipment market, focused specifically on wafer-level and package-level burn-in rather than broader ATE.

- FY2025 revenue: $59M (down from $66M in FY2024)
- FY2025 GAAP net loss: $(3.9M)
- Cash: $26.5M (down from $49.3M after Incal acquisition)
- Fiscal Q2 2026 revenue: $9.9M (down 27% YoY), net loss $(3.2M)

## Source(s) of Advantage

**Technical specificity in wafer-level burn-in**: AEHR's FOX-XP platform tests entire wafers simultaneously rather than individual dies, enabling higher throughput for reliability screening of SiC, GaN, and now AI processors. This is a narrow technical capability -- not a broad moat.

**Mechanism**: Burn-in requires custom thermal/electrical interfaces matched to specific device types. Once a customer qualifies AEHR's system for production, switching mid-production is costly (requalification, yield risk, downtime). This creates moderate switching costs at the device-generation level, but NOT across generations. Each new chip generation is a new qualification event where competitors can enter.

**Installed base consumable stream**: Each FOX-XP system requires device-specific contactors/WaferPaks that generate recurring revenue. This is the strongest structural element -- but the installed base is still small.

**What this is NOT**: This is not a network effect business. There are no demand-side economies of scale. Brand is irrelevant -- these are engineering-driven purchase decisions.

## Competitive Landscape

| Player | Focus | Revenue Scale |
|--------|-------|--------------|
| Advantest | Broad ATE (70%+ market share with Teradyne) | ~$4B+ |
| Teradyne | Broad ATE | ~$2.5B+ |
| Cohu | Handler/contactor/burn-in | ~$500M |
| AEHR | Wafer-level burn-in (niche) | ~$59M |

AEHR holds roughly 7.8% of the burn-in test system market. The company is a small specialist competing against firms 10-50x its size. Advantest and Teradyne dominate broader ATE but have historically not focused on wafer-level burn-in. The risk is that AI chip burn-in TAM growth attracts these incumbents.

**Why competitors haven't attacked yet**: The burn-in niche was too small to matter to Advantest/Teradyne when it was SiC/EV-focused. The AI pivot changes this calculus. If AI burn-in becomes a multi-billion-dollar market, larger players will enter.

## Customer Behavior & Revenue Quality

**Extreme customer concentration**: In FY2024, two customers represented ~84% of revenue (67% + 17%). Top five customers = 93% of revenue.

**Revenue quality is poor by durability standards**:
- Capital equipment sales are lumpy and project-based
- No long-term take-or-pay contracts disclosed
- Bookings swing wildly: Q2 FY2026 bookings were $6.2M; management guides $60-80M in H2 bookings
- Backlog of $11.8M as of Nov 2025 provides minimal visibility

**The hyperscaler dependency**: The recent Sonoma order and $14M FOX-XP order both come from the same unnamed "lead hyperscale customer." This is a single-customer story dressed up as a market expansion.

**Structural retention vs. inertia**: Switching costs are real but generation-limited. When a hyperscaler designs its next-gen ASIC, the burn-in vendor selection reopens. AEHR must re-win each generation.

## Evidence of Moat in the Financials

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| Revenue ($M) | $50.8 | $65.0 | $66.2 | $59.0 |
| Gross Margin | 46.6% | 50.4% | 49.1% | 40.6% |
| Net Income | Profitable | Profitable | Profitable | $(3.9M) loss |

Gross margins declined nearly 1,000bps in one year. This is NOT what a moated business looks like. The margin compression reflects: lower system volumes, mix shift, Incal acquisition intangible amortization, and inventory charges. A business with genuine pricing power does not see margins collapse when volumes dip modestly.

## Fragility Analysis

1. **Single-customer risk is existential**: If the lead hyperscaler delays, cancels, or insources burn-in capability, AEHR's revenue collapses. There is no diversified base to absorb the shock.

2. **Generational re-compete risk**: Each new chip generation is an opportunity for competitors (or the customer itself) to switch burn-in providers. AEHR must win repeatedly.

3. **Incumbent entry**: If AI chip burn-in becomes a $3B+ market (per industry projections), Advantest and Teradyne have the resources, customer relationships, and engineering talent to enter. AEHR's niche protection evaporates at scale.

4. **Cash runway concerns**: $26.5M cash with ongoing losses. The company is dependent on the H2 order ramp materializing.

## Moat & Durability Classification

**Moat Strength**: Weak

AEHR has technical specialization, not a moat. Switching costs are real but generation-limited. There are no structural barriers preventing larger competitors from entering as the market scales. Customer concentration means the "moat" is really one customer relationship.

**Earnings Durability**: Low

Current earnings are negative. Projected earnings depend on a single hyperscaler order ramp that hasn't fully materialized. Historical earnings were driven by a SiC/EV cycle that has since faded. The business has demonstrated an inability to sustain profitability through even modest demand fluctuations.

## The One Thing That Matters for Durability

**Whether AEHR can convert its first-mover position in AI chip burn-in into a sticky, multi-customer installed base before larger competitors enter.**

Right now, AEHR has a technical lead in high-power burn-in for AI ASICs. But this lead is fragile: it exists because the market was too small for Advantest/Teradyne to care about. The window to build an installed base large enough to create real consumable lock-in is narrow -- probably 2-3 years. If AEHR remains a one-customer story by FY2028, the moat thesis fails regardless of revenue growth.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**Bearish insight**: The market is pricing AEHR as an AI beneficiary ($1.2B market cap on $59M trailing revenue, currently unprofitable), but the competitive dynamics of AI burn-in are structurally different from SiC burn-in. SiC burn-in was a niche that large players ignored. AI chip burn-in is adjacent to the core business of Advantest and Teradyne, who already have deep relationships with every hyperscaler. AEHR's first-mover advantage in AI burn-in may be shorter-lived than its advantage in SiC, precisely because the market opportunity is larger and more attractive to incumbents.

The stock's 94% YTD gain prices in the hyperscaler order ramp. It does not price in the competitive response that a multi-billion-dollar burn-in TAM will inevitably attract.
