# AEHR Test Systems -- Geopolitical & Sovereign Risk Assessment

**Date:** 2026-02-27

## Applicability: Material

AEHR operates in semiconductor equipment, a sector directly targeted by U.S.-China export controls. The company has sales operations in China, South Korea, Taiwan, and the Philippines, with historically heavy Asian revenue concentration. Geopolitical risk is material and warrants full analysis.

## 2. Sovereign & Regime Risk Assessment

AEHR's key geographies -- South Korea, Taiwan, Japan, Germany, U.S. -- are stable democracies with strong rule of law. China is the exception.

- **China:** Dedicated sales resources but not a manufacturing base. IP litigation filed against a Chinese supplier for infringement (disclosed FY2025). Enforceability of IP rights in Chinese courts remains structurally unreliable for foreign small-caps without political leverage.
- **Taiwan:** Potential conflict zone but AEHR's exposure is sales/service, not manufacturing. Disruption would be demand-side, not operational.
- **South Korea:** Stable. Key SiC and semiconductor customers located here (likely ON Semiconductor's Korean operations or similar).

## 3. Regulatory & State Intervention Risk -- Export Controls

This is the central geopolitical question for AEHR.

**Current BIS Framework (December 2024 rules):** The expanded semiconductor equipment export controls target advanced-node manufacturing equipment -- lithography, etch, deposition, ion implantation, metrology. The controls are designed to restrict China's ability to produce advanced logic and memory chips.

**Critical distinction the market may misunderstand:** AEHR's FOX-P and FOX-XP systems are test and burn-in equipment, not manufacturing equipment. Test/burn-in occurs after fabrication. As of the December 2024 BIS rules, semiconductor test equipment is NOT in the same restricted category as manufacturing tools like those from ASML, Lam Research, or Applied Materials.

However, two escalation paths exist:

1. **BIS scope expansion:** If BIS broadens controls to include test/qualification equipment for silicon carbide power devices or AI accelerators, AEHR could be directly restricted from selling to Chinese customers. This is plausible but not imminent -- SiC is a power semiconductor technology, not an advanced logic node, and is primarily used in EVs and industrial applications rather than military AI.

2. **End-use controls:** Even without equipment-specific restrictions, AEHR could face compliance risk if Chinese end-users are placed on the Entity List or if end-use is deemed to support military applications. The Entity List expanded by 140 additions in December 2024.

**Tariff exposure:** Management has flagged tariff uncertainty affecting customer purchasing decisions. Some systems ship to customers outside the U.S. and face tariff risk. The company is exploring tariff drawbacks and drop-shipping to mitigate.

## 4. Conflict & Sanctions Exposure

- **Taiwan Strait:** A conflict would disrupt AEHR's Asian customer base broadly but would not destroy the company's operational capacity (U.S.-based manufacturing). Demand destruction, not supply destruction.
- **China sanctions escalation:** Secondary sanctions risk is low given AEHR's small scale and non-strategic product category. Primary sanctions risk exists only if test equipment is added to controlled items lists.
- **No exposure** to Russia, Middle East, or other active conflict zones in any material way.

## 5. Currency & Capital Controls

Low risk. AEHR is U.S.-domiciled, manufactures in the U.S., and transacts primarily in USD. FX exposure exists on Asian sales but is not a thesis-level risk. No capital repatriation issues identified.

## 6. Scenario & Tail Risk Framing

| Scenario | Description | Revenue Impact |
|----------|-------------|----------------|
| **Base Case** | Current export controls unchanged; SiC test equipment remains unrestricted; AI pivot continues | Neutral to positive |
| **Adverse Case** | BIS expands controls to include SiC or power semiconductor test equipment; Chinese customers cannot purchase | Loss of remaining Asian SiC revenue (magnitude unclear but declining share) |
| **Severe Tail** | Broad semiconductor equipment embargo including test/burn-in; Taiwan conflict disrupts Asian demand | Could impair 30-50% of revenue depending on geographic mix at time of event |

## 7. Mitigants & Offsets

- **Diversification underway:** AI processor test/burn-in now >35% of revenue, primarily U.S.-based customers. This structurally reduces China/Asia exposure.
- **U.S. manufacturing:** All systems built in Fremont, CA. No operational dependency on restricted geographies.
- **Product category:** Test equipment is downstream of fabrication and less strategically sensitive than lithography or deposition tools. Lower probability of export restriction.
- **Small scale:** AEHR is too small to be a geopolitical target. Unlike ASML or Applied Materials, it does not appear on political radar screens.

---

## The One Geopolitical Risk That Matters

**IP theft and technology transfer to Chinese competitors.** AEHR has already filed an IP lawsuit against a Chinese supplier. For a company with $59M in revenue and a niche technology position, the risk is not that the U.S. government restricts its sales -- it is that Chinese competitors clone the technology and undercut AEHR in Asian markets. This is a slow-burn risk that erodes competitive position rather than a binary event, and it is poorly captured by traditional geopolitical risk frameworks. Enforcing IP in Chinese courts as a small-cap U.S. company is structurally difficult.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**The market likely over-applies "semiconductor equipment export control risk" to AEHR.** The December 2024 BIS rules target manufacturing equipment for advanced logic/memory nodes. AEHR's test and burn-in systems -- particularly for SiC power semiconductors used in EVs -- sit outside this regulatory perimeter. SiC is not an advanced-node technology; it is a mature power semiconductor used in non-military applications. The "China risk discount" applied to semiconductor equipment names broadly may be excessive for AEHR specifically, given (a) its product category is not currently restricted, (b) its China revenue is already declining organically, and (c) its growth vector (AI test/burn-in) is U.S.-centric. The real risk -- IP leakage -- is more mundane than sanctions but potentially more damaging to long-term economics.
