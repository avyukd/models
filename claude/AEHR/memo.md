## Key Drivers (The 2 Things That Matter)

1. **H2 FY26 bookings of $60-80M from the lead hyperscaler.** This is the entire stock. If it converts, FY27 revenue could reach $80M+ and the narrative shifts to growth. If it slips, the stock returns toward its 52-week low of $6.27. There is no middle ground.

2. **Competitive durability in AI burn-in.** The SiC burn-in niche was too small for Advantest/Teradyne to contest. AI burn-in is not. If this becomes a multi-billion TAM, AEHR's niche protection evaporates precisely as the opportunity scales.

## Thesis Summary

- AEHR is a $1.2B market cap company generating $42M annualized revenue, losing money, burning $12M/year in FCF, with $21M net cash -- valued entirely on forward bookings guidance from a single unnamed hyperscaler
- The stock has rallied ~560% from its 52-week low of $6.27 on two February 2026 order announcements (Sonoma systems + $14M FOX-XP order), both from the same customer
- Consensus analyst price target is $21 (vs. $40 stock price) -- the stock has overshot even the bulls
- The company must roughly triple revenue within 2-3 years and restore 45%+ gross margins to justify today's valuation; current gross margins are ~33% and declining
- SiC (the legacy business) is in a severe downturn with trough not expected until 2027-2028; the AI pivot must fully replace this revenue stream

**Assessment of variant edge: We do not have a differentiated variant view.** The bear case (single customer, no moat at scale, cash burn) is intellectually sound but the stock is momentum-driven on order announcements. The bull case (AI burn-in TAM explosion, first-mover lock-in) requires assumptions we cannot verify. Both sides of this trade are thesis-dependent on events that have not yet occurred. This is a coin flip dressed up as analysis.

## Forward Path and Catalysts

- **Near-term (0-3 months):** H2 FY26 bookings announcements -- the single most important data point. Each order announcement moves the stock 10-25%.
- **Medium-term (6-12 months):** FY27 revenue ramp beginning June 2026. First quarter where booked orders convert to shipped revenue will prove or destroy the thesis.
- **Structural question (12-24 months):** Does AEHR win a second major hyperscaler customer? Without customer diversification, this remains a single-point-of-failure business regardless of revenue.
- **SiC recovery (2027-2028):** Yole projects SiC utilization trough in 2027-2028. Any recovery here is additive but distant.

## Risks and Kill Switches

- **Kill switch 1:** Lead hyperscaler delays, reduces, or cancels orders. With 84% customer concentration historically, this is existential.
- **Kill switch 2:** Advantest or Teradyne enters AI burn-in market with competitive product. AEHR's niche evaporates.
- **Kill switch 3:** Cash drops below $15M without bookings materializing -- forces dilutive raise at worst possible time.
- **Permanent loss risk:** The stock was $6.27 less than 12 months ago. A reversion to that level represents 85% downside.
- **IP theft:** Active litigation against Chinese supplier. A small-cap cannot effectively enforce IP in Chinese courts.

## Why This Is Too Hard

This fails three of our underwriting criteria:

1. **Binary outcome dependency.** The entire thesis rests on one unnamed customer's purchasing decisions over the next 6-12 months. We cannot independently verify demand, competitive position, or order durability.

2. **Assumption density.** To get comfortable, we need to believe: (a) $60-80M bookings materialize, (b) they convert to revenue on schedule, (c) gross margins recover from 33% to 45%+, (d) Advantest/Teradyne do not enter, (e) the customer does not insource, (f) a second customer emerges, (g) SiC recovers. Each assumption is plausible individually; the joint probability is low.

3. **No position sizing framework.** With a 52-week range of $6.27 to $43.13 (7x), the stock is not analyzable through traditional valuation. It trades on newsflow. We cannot size a position where a single order announcement moves the stock 25% in either direction.

**What would move this out of the too hard pile:**
- Visible bookings conversion: first FY27 quarter showing $25M+ revenue with 40%+ gross margins
- Second hyperscaler customer win, proving this is not a single-customer story
- Advantest/Teradyne explicitly passing on AI burn-in (unlikely to be observable)

**Geopolitical risk is a secondary concern.** The geopolitical analyst correctly notes that test equipment sits outside current BIS export control scope and that AEHR's growth vector is U.S.-centric. This is not a China risk story. The real risks are commercial, not sovereign.

## Portfolio Logic

No position. This is a momentum/event-driven stock masquerading as a fundamental semiconductor equipment story. The right way to play this, if one must, is through options around bookings announcements -- but that is a trading strategy, not an investment thesis. We do not have an informational edge on the hyperscaler's purchasing timeline, and without that edge, we are speculating.

The stock is trading 2x the consensus analyst price target. Either the sellside is wrong (possible -- they were caught flat-footed by the February order announcements) or the market is front-running a narrative that has not been validated by financials. We will not take a position in either direction until we see revenue, not bookings guidance.
