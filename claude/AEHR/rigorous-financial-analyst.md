## SBC & Normalized Earnings Reconciliation

The GAAP-to-non-GAAP bridge reveals the SBC magnitude:
- **FY2025**: GAAP net loss $(3.9M), non-GAAP net income $4.6M. The $8.5M gap includes SBC, acquisition-related costs, restructuring, and severance. SBC likely ~$5-6M (assumption; exact breakout unavailable from press releases).
- **FY2024**: GAAP income implied ~$25M+ (given non-GAAP $35.8M minus ~$10M adjustments). SBC was likely $8-10M at peak.

**Estimated SBC as % of revenue**: ~9-10% in FY25 on $59M revenue. This is material for a company currently losing money on a GAAP basis.

**Normalized mid-cycle earnings**: This is the critical question. At peak (FY24), AEHR earned $35.8M non-GAAP on $66.2M revenue (54% non-GAAP margin, clearly unsustainable). At trough (current), it is losing money. A mid-cycle revenue of ~$60-70M with 40% gross margins, 15% SG&A, 10% R&D yields ~15% operating margins or ~$9-10M pre-tax. After tax and SBC deduction, normalized earnings power is roughly **$5-8M**, or **$0.17-$0.27/share**.

## Key Financial Drivers

1. **Bookings trajectory** -- H2 FY26 bookings guidance of $60-80M is THE variable. This stock trades entirely on forward order visibility, not current financials. If bookings materialize, FY27 revenue could triple. If they slip, the stock reprices violently.

2. **AI/data center mix shift** -- The pivot from EV/SiC (declining) to AI processor burn-in (growing) determines margin sustainability. AI-related systems may carry different margin profiles than legacy SiC products.

3. **Gross margin recovery** -- From ~49% (FY24) to ~33% (LTM). Incal acquisition mix and lower utilization drove compression. Margin recovery to 40%+ is necessary for the business model to generate real earnings at scale.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**The cash burn math is worse than it appears.** Consensus is focused on the H2 FY26 bookings guidance ($60-80M) and the AI narrative. What gets overlooked: AEHR spent $11.1M acquiring Incal, is running negative FCF of ~$12M/year, and has only $20.7M in net cash. If the bookings ramp delays even 2-3 quarters, the company may need to raise capital -- dilutive at any price. The 52-week low was $6.27, meaning this stock was at ~$175M market cap less than a year ago. The current $1.2B valuation prices in a revenue trajectory that has not yet appeared in reported numbers.

**The GAAP-to-non-GAAP gap is widening.** In FY24, non-GAAP adjustments were ~$10M. In FY25, the gap between $(3.9M) GAAP loss and $4.6M non-GAAP income is $8.5M on much lower revenue. Acquisition-related amortization from Incal will persist for years. The "real" earnings power is closer to the GAAP number than the non-GAAP number.

**Assessment: Speculative. The stock at $41.50 is priced for a revenue tripling that exists only in bookings guidance, not in reported results. Current financials show a money-losing, cash-burning business trading at 22x LTM revenue. The risk/reward depends entirely on whether $60-80M in H2 bookings convert to shipped revenue in FY27.**
