# Semiconductor Equipment / SiC Test Industry: Cycle & Structure Analysis

*Date: 2026-02-27*

---

## Current Cycle Position: SiC Equipment Is in a Pronounced Downturn Within a Broader Semi Equipment Upcycle

The overall semiconductor equipment market is in a sustained upcycle -- SEMI forecasts global equipment sales reaching $133B in 2025 (+13.7% YoY), $145B in 2026, and $156B in 2027, driven by AI/HBM buildout. Test equipment specifically surged 48% to $11.2B in 2025 per SEMI data.

But the SiC power semiconductor subsegment -- the market that matters for wafer-level burn-in -- is in a severe counter-cyclical downturn. This divergence is the central fact of the analysis.

## SiC Overcapacity: The Numbers Are Ugly

| Metric | Current State | Source |
|--------|--------------|--------|
| SiC upstream utilization | ~50% | Yole Group, Dec 2025 |
| SiC device line utilization | ~70% | Yole Group, Dec 2025 |
| SiC substrate revenue (2024) | $1.04B, down 9% YoY | TrendForce |
| Government subsidies for SiC capacity (2023-24) | $4.6B globally | McKinsey |
| Expected trough timing | 2027-2028 | Yole Group |
| SiC equipment CapEx CAGR 2024-2030 | -7% | Yole Group |
| Top 5 SiC device makers market share | >90% | Yole Group |

The 2019-2024 SiC investment boom, fueled by $4.6B+ in global government subsidies, built capacity far ahead of demand. China alone received $1.7B for localized SiC fabs, adding an estimated 620K units/year in additional capacity by 2026. The result: utilization rates at 50% upstream, with the bottom not expected until 2027-2028.

## EV Demand: The Underlying Driver Is Decelerating

Global EV sales grew ~25% in 2025 to 20M+ units, but growth is decelerating toward ~15% in 2026 as subsidies phase out. The US $7,500 federal EV tax credit expired September 2025. SiC content per EV is rising, but volume growth moderation combined with massive capacity overbuild means SiC device makers (STMicro, onsemi, Wolfspeed, Infineon, ROHM) are cutting CapEx, not expanding test capacity.

Wolfspeed's situation is particularly telling -- enormous idle capacity and startup costs at new fabs (MVF, JP) while SiC demand falls short. STMicro and onsemi have reported revenue declines and projected further weakness in automotive/industrial chips.

## Wafer-Level Burn-In Market: Niche Within a Niche

Market size estimates for wafer-level test and burn-in range wildly ($395M to $2.5B for 2025 depending on scope definition), reflecting how fragmented and poorly defined this niche is. The addressable market for SiC-specific wafer-level burn-in is a fraction of these totals. This is not a large market.

## Historical Semi Equipment Cycle Patterns

| Period | Equipment Market Move | Trigger |
|--------|----------------------|---------|
| 2018 | Peak, $62B record | Memory overbuild |
| 2019 | -4% decline | DRAM oversupply, ASPs down 47% |
| 2020 | +21% recovery | COVID electronics demand |
| 2021-2022 | Sustained upcycle | Shortage-driven CapEx |
| 2023-2024 | Record $104B WFE | AI/HBM buildout begins |
| 2025-2027 | Continued growth to $135B WFE | AI sustains; SiC diverges |

Key pattern: during downturns, 35% of manufacturers postpone test equipment purchases, and equipment suppliers see margin compression. Test equipment is late-cycle and lumpy.

## Mid-Cycle Definition for SiC Test Equipment

"Normal" for this sub-industry means:
- SiC device fab utilization at 75-85%
- EV unit growth at 15-20% annually
- SiC device makers in capacity expansion mode, ordering test equipment
- No major government subsidy-driven capacity surges distorting supply

We are well below mid-cycle on every metric. Utilization at 50-70%, CapEx declining at -7% CAGR, and a 2027-2028 trough suggests we are in early-to-mid downturn, not recovery.

## The One Industry Factor That Matters

**SiC device fab utilization rates.** Nothing else matters for wafer-level burn-in demand. When utilization is below 70%, SiC device makers are not buying test equipment -- they have excess capacity on existing lines. Burn-in equipment orders will not recover until utilization crosses back above 80%, which Yole does not expect before 2028.

Monitor: Quarterly utilization disclosures from STMicro, Infineon, onsemi, and Wolfspeed's operational updates. Leading indicator: SiC device ASP stabilization (currently declining).

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**Bearish insight the market underweights:** The SiC test equipment "cycle" may not be cyclical at all -- it may be a one-time capacity buildout wave that is largely complete. The 2021-2023 SiC burn-in equipment boom was driven by greenfield fab construction as five major players simultaneously built SiC capacity from near-zero. That buildout is done. Future test equipment demand will be replacement/incremental, not greenfield. The installed base is largely built.

The bull case for near-term recovery depends on (a) AI/data center burn-in diversification replacing lost SiC revenue, and (b) SiC recovery in FY2027+. But the AI test opportunity is contested by larger, better-resourced competitors (Teradyne, Advantest), and the SiC recovery timeline keeps getting pushed out.

AEHR's own numbers confirm the severity: FY2025 revenue of $59M (down from $66M), FY2026 H1 revenue of ~$21M implying a run-rate well below FY2025, and expected H2 FY2026 revenue of $25-30M. The company is projecting $60-80M in H2 bookings to set up FY2027 -- but those bookings have not yet materialized and depend on SiC customer forecasts that have repeatedly disappointed.
