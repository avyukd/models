# TWR.V — Asset Quality & Geological Optionality

## Drill Results in Context

| Metric | TWR.V (Rabbit North) | Typical Open-Pit Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Grade | 0.64-0.65 g/t Au (bulk), 2.0-2.4 g/t (high-grade) | 0.5 g/t cutoff at current gold prices |
| Width | 80-173m intercepts | >50m is excellent for bulk tonnage |
| Depth | <40-50m from surface | Shallow = low strip ratio = good economics |
| Footprint | ~120m x 140m (Blue Sky alone) | Early stage but growing |

**Verdict**: These grades are marginal-to-good for open pit at historical gold prices, but **comfortably economic at current ~$2,900+ USD gold**. The width and shallow depth are the real story -- these are bulk-minable intercepts, not narrow veins.

## What Makes This Interesting

1. **Two zones converging**: Thunder North (high-grade, 2+ g/t) and Blue Sky (bulk-grade, 0.65 g/t) are only 400m apart. Structural model suggests they connect across a 2km trend. If proven, this transforms from "interesting intercepts" to "potential deposit."
2. **Porphyry Cu-Au kicker**: Till sampling identified a hidden alkalic porphyry Cu-Au target beneath cover. This is free geological optionality on top of the gold system.
3. **BC jurisdiction**: Tier-1 mining jurisdiction. Infrastructure, rule of law, permitting pathway (albeit slow).

## Acquirer Attractiveness

A mid-tier (Equinox, Alamos, Eldorado) would look at this if:
- Resource reaches 1M+ oz at 0.6+ g/t
- Open-pit geometry confirmed with more infill drilling
- Permitting pathway clarified

At $31M market cap, this is still 2-3 drill campaigns away from that conversation. The Sandstorm 3% NSR is a minor negative for acquirers.

## Key Risk

Without true widths confirmed and only 6 of 11 holes reported, the geological model is preliminary. The 120x140m footprint at Blue Sky is encouraging but small -- needs to grow significantly.
