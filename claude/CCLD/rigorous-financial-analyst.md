# CCLD (CareCloud) -- Financial Analysis

**Price: $2.52 | Shares: ~42.4M (diluted, post-Series A conversion) | Market Cap: ~$107M**

---

## The Capital Structure Is The Story

CCLD's share count nearly doubled in March 2025. The mandatory conversion of 3.5M Series A preferred shares into ~26M common shares inflated the diluted count from ~16M to ~42M. This is the single most important fact about this stock.

**Capital Structure Summary:**

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Common shares (post-conversion) | ~42.4M |
| Series B Preferred (CCLDO) | 1.0M shares, $25 par, 8.75% coupon = ~$2.2M/yr dividend |
| Series B liquidation preference | $25M senior to common equity |
| Series A conversion ratio | 7.3358 common shares per preferred share |
| Pre-conversion common shares | ~16.2M (Q3 2024) |
| Dividend resumption | Jan 2025 (had been suspended) |

**Implication:** The $25M Series B liquidation preference sits ahead of common equity. At $107M market cap, the enterprise is really valued at $107M common + $25M preferred = $132M total equity value, but common holders are subordinate.

## Income Statement -- Turnaround Real But Revenue Shrinking

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | 9M 2025 | 2025E (Guidance) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $117.1M | $110.8M | $86.1M | $117-119M |
| Revenue growth | -- | -5.4% | +4% YoY | +6-7% |
| GAAP Net Income | -$48.7M | $7.9M | $7.9M | -- |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $15.4M | $24.1M | $19.9M | $26-28M |
| GAAP EPS (diluted) | -- | -- | -- | $0.10-0.13 |

FY2023 net loss was dominated by goodwill impairment (non-cash). The "turnaround" to $7.9M net income in FY2024 is largely the absence of that write-off, not operational transformation. Revenue declined 5% in FY2024 as two large accounts wound down.

2025 guidance of $117-119M implies Medsphere acquisition ($16.5M purchase price) is backfilling the revenue hole. Organic growth appears minimal at best.

## Cash Flow & Normalized Earnings

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 |
|---|---|---|
| Cash from Operations | $15.5M | $20.6M |
| Free Cash Flow | $3.8M | $13.2M |
| Implied Capex | ~$11.7M | ~$7.4M |

**Normalized Earnings Reconciliation (FY2024 base, approximate):**

| Line Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| GAAP Net Income | $7.9M |
| Add back: goodwill impairment (FY2023, not FY2024) | $0 |
| Less: Series B preferred dividends | -$2.2M |
| Net income to common | ~$5.7M |
| Diluted shares (post-conversion) | 42.4M |
| **EPS to common** | **~$0.13** |

At $2.52/share, that is ~19x trailing earnings to common -- not cheap for a business with flat-to-declining organic revenue.

**FCF to common equity:** $13.2M FCF - $2.2M Series B dividends = ~$11.0M. At $107M market cap, that is a ~10% FCF yield. This is the bull case number. But FY2024 capex of ~$7.4M looks abnormally low vs FY2023's ~$11.7M. If capex normalizes to $10M, normalized FCF drops to ~$8.6M (8% yield).

**Critical unknown:** SBC expense is not disclosed in search results. Given 42M shares and healthcare IT peer norms, SBC could be $3-6M/yr, which would further reduce economic earnings. Management's "adjusted" metrics almost certainly exclude SBC.

## Red Flags Checklist

- [ ] Persistent negative FCF -- **No.** FCF turned positive in 2024.
- [x] Rising SBC as % of revenue -- **Unknown.** Not disclosed in available data. Share count explosion via conversion suggests dilution is a feature of this business.
- [x] Margin expansion driven by accounting choices -- **Partially.** FY2024 "record" net income is vs. a goodwill-impairment-depressed FY2023. Adjusting for that, improvement is real but modest.
- [ ] Weak cash conversion -- **No.** CFO of $20.6M vs $7.9M net income shows strong cash conversion.
- [x] Aggressive revenue recognition -- **Monitor.** Medsphere acquisition in 2025 masks organic revenue trajectory.
- [ ] Balance sheet fragility -- **Moderate.** Low cash, but manageable debt.

---

## Key Financial Drivers

1. **Organic revenue growth (or lack thereof).** Revenue fell 5% in FY2024. Medsphere acquisition masks this in 2025. If organic revenue continues declining, the cost-cutting turnaround has a ceiling. This is the load-bearing variable.

2. **FCF sustainability at current capex levels.** FY2024 FCF jumped because capex dropped ~37% YoY. If this reflects deferred investment rather than efficiency, FCF will mean-revert lower.

3. **Preferred stock overhang.** Series B holders have a $25M liquidation preference and $2.2M/yr dividend claim. This is real cash that does not flow to common.

---

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**The denominator changed, and nobody adjusted.** CCLD went from 16M to 42M diluted shares in March 2025. The stock "looks" cheap at $2.52 but the economic reality is a $107M common equity market cap for a business generating ~$11M of FCF to common (optimistic) on declining organic revenue.

The FY2024 capex drop from $11.7M to $7.4M is suspicious. This coincides with the "record" FCF year. A typical analyst celebrating the FCF inflection may not notice that capex was cut by 37%, potentially at the expense of product investment in a competitive healthcare IT market. If capex normalizes, the FCF story weakens materially.

Additionally, the Series A conversion eliminated ~$7M in annual preferred dividends, which is being touted as a positive. But it created 26M new common shares. At $2.52/share, those 26M shares represent $65M of market cap -- the market is paying far more in dilution than the $7M dividend savings. The conversion was value-destructive for existing common holders on any reasonable valuation framework.

## Valuation Framework

| Scenario | Normalized FCF to Common | Multiple | Implied Price |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bull (FCF sustains, growth returns) | $11M | 12x | $3.11 |
| Base (capex normalizes, flat revenue) | $7.5M | 10x | $1.77 |
| Bear (revenue declines resume) | $5M | 8x | $0.94 |

**At $2.52, the stock is priced between the bull and base case.** You need to believe organic revenue stabilizes AND capex stays low AND SBC is minimal. That is a lot of "ands."

**Verdict: Fairly to fully priced on normalized economics. The FCF yield looks attractive only if you ignore the capex question and preferred claims.**
