---

## 1. Recommendation

- **Decision:** Neutral (Too Expensive)
- **Conviction:** High

---

## 2. Key Drivers (The 3 Things That Matter)

**1. Score Price Increases.** This is the entire equity story. FICO raised mortgage B2B score prices from under $1 to $4.95 over three years -- a 400%+ increase with zero volume loss. Scores segment revenue grew 27% in FY2025, overwhelmingly driven by price, not volume. If pricing power stalls, the thesis breaks.

**2. Regulatory/Competitive Moat Integrity.** FHFA approved VantageScore 4.0 for conforming mortgages in July 2025 -- the first structural crack in FICO's decades-long monopoly. Experian is offering VantageScore for free. The moat is intact today but under real pressure for the first time.

**3. Buyback-Fueled EPS Growth vs. Organic Growth.** FICO spent $1.62B on buybacks in FY2025 at average prices of $1,600-2,000, partially debt-funded. If buybacks stop (balance sheet constraint, regulatory shock), EPS growth drops from ~25% to ~15%. The stock is priced for the former.

---

## 3. Thesis in 4 Bullets

- FICO is a near-monopoly toll booth on consumer credit with 80%+ margins in Scores, generating $740M+ in FCF on $2B revenue. Business quality is exceptional.
- At $1,200, the stock trades at 40-43x normalized mid-cycle earnings, implying 15%+ revenue growth for 3-5 years and sustained pricing power -- pricing in perfection.
- Management's aggressive pricing (400% mortgage score increase) is rationally harvesting the moat but simultaneously creating the economic incentive for ecosystem coordination around VantageScore. FICO may be trading durability for near-term extraction.
- The balance sheet has been hollowed out ($3.2B debt, negative $1.8B equity) to fund buybacks at increasingly expensive prices. FY2025 repurchases at $1,600-2,000 are likely value-destructive. The financial engineering that juiced EPS growth is reaching its limits.

## 5. The Setup

FICO sits at the nexus of consumer credit infrastructure. Every mortgage, credit card, auto loan, and personal loan in the US runs through a FICO Score. The moat is not product quality -- it is an ecosystem coordination problem. Switching requires every lender, regulator, and automated system to simultaneously recalibrate. This is extraordinarily persistent.

But the structural landscape shifted in 2025. FHFA's VantageScore approval removed the regulatory mandate that was FICO's deepest moat in mortgages. The bureaus (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion) are FICO's only Scores customers and simultaneously its most motivated competitors -- every dollar FICO collects is a dollar they could capture by promoting VantageScore. FICO's own pricing aggression has transformed a theoretical threat into an economic imperative.

## 6. Numbers That Matter

- **Normalized mid-cycle EPS:** $28-30 (FY2025 basis, adjusting for above-trend pricing)
- **FY2026 guided EPS:** $33.47 GAAP, ~$40.70 adjusted
- **P/E on normalized:** 40-43x
- **EV/FCF:** ~43x
- **FCF yield:** ~2.5%
- **Net debt/FCF:** 4.0x
- **Interest coverage:** 6.8x EBIT -- adequate but tightening

**What $1,200 implies:** 15%+ revenue CAGR for 3-5 years, operating margins sustained above 47%, no regulatory disruption to pricing, and continued buyback-driven EPS accretion. Miss on any of these and the multiple compresses.

**Sensitivity:** A 5x multiple compression (from 42x to 37x normalized) implies $1,050 -- a 12% drawdown. A return to 30x normalized (still premium) implies $870 -- a 28% drawdown. The downside skew is meaningful at this valuation.

## 7. Forward Path and Catalysts

**Bull catalysts:** Mortgage volume normalization (currently below mid-cycle) could add 10-15% to Scores revenue without further pricing. Software platform inflection -- if margins expand and ARR growth sustains, the market may assign a higher multiple to the software business separately. Credit cycle upturn.

**Bear catalysts:** A top-10 lender publicly adopting VantageScore for credit decisioning. CFPB or DOJ action on pricing practices. Credit cycle downturn compressing volumes while $3.2B debt remains fixed. Buyback halt due to balance sheet constraints. Refinancing $400M near-term maturities at higher rates.

**What happens next 6-12 months:** Most likely, FICO continues to execute and deliver strong EPS growth. The VantageScore threat remains theoretical in the near term due to ecosystem inertia. The stock probably does not blow up -- but the risk/reward is skewed negatively at 40x+ earnings for a business whose growth is substantially pricing-driven.

---

## 8. Risks and Kill Switches

- **Thesis invalidator (bear):** VantageScore gains meaningful share in credit decisioning (not marketing). If any bureau reports >10% decisioning share for VantageScore, FICO's pricing power narrative breaks.
- **Thesis invalidator (bull):** If FICO can sustain 15%+ organic growth for 3+ years while VantageScore fails to gain traction, the stock deserves a premium multiple.
- **Permanent capital loss risk:** Moderate. The balance sheet is aggressive ($3.2B debt, negative equity) but serviced by stable FCF. A simultaneous regulatory shock + credit downturn could create real stress, but outright bankruptcy risk is low.
- **Re-evaluation trigger:** Stock below $900 (30x normalized) merits a serious look as a Buy. Stock above $1,500 (50x+ normalized) merits consideration as a short.

---

## 9. Positioning and Portfolio Logic

**No position at $1,200.** This is a phenomenal business priced for perfection. The variant perception exists (moat harvesting, buyback math deteriorating) but the timing of moat erosion is inherently uncertain, and shorting high-quality monopolies at reasonable growth rates is a graveyard of hedge fund capital.

**What would change this to a Buy:** Price below $900 (30x normalized earnings), which would provide margin of safety against regulatory disruption and pricing power erosion. Alternatively, clear evidence that VantageScore has failed to gain traction post-FHFA approval (12-18 months of data showing no adoption), which would confirm the moat's durability.

**What would change this to a Short:** Price above $1,800 (60x+ normalized), or concrete evidence of VantageScore adoption in credit decisioning, or a credit cycle downturn that stress-tests the leveraged balance sheet.

**Watchlist priority:** High. This is a business worth owning at the right price. The right price is not $1,200.
