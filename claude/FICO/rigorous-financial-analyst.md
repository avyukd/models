# FICO (Fair Isaac Corporation) -- Financial Analysis at $1,200/Share

## Business Financial Profile

FICO operates two segments: **Scores** (59% of FY2025 revenue) and **Software** (41%). Scores is an ultra-high-margin toll booth on consumer credit -- near-zero marginal cost, deeply embedded in lender workflows. Software is a cloud/SaaS analytics platform transitioning from on-premise. Capital intensity is minimal. Revenue quality is high: Scores is transaction-based but effectively recurring given regulatory entrenchment; Software is shifting toward ARR. Operating leverage is extreme in Scores.

## Income Statement Analysis

**FY2025 (ending Sep 2025):** Revenue $1.991B (+16% YoY). GAAP net income ~$640M. GAAP EPS $25.65 (diluted, ~24.9M shares). Operating margin ~46.5%.

**FY2024:** Revenue $1.718B (+13% YoY). GAAP net income $513M. GAAP EPS $20.45.

**Segment breakdown (FY2025):** Scores revenue $1.169B (+27% YoY); Software revenue $822M (+3%). The divergence is stark -- Scores is the profit engine. Scores operating margins likely exceed 85% based on segment disclosures; Software margins are ~31% and improving as cloud transition progresses.

**SBC:** ~$124M in FY2023, with quarterly run-rate of ~$40-42M through early FY2025, implying ~$160M annualized. SBC as % of revenue: ~8% and rising modestly. This is a real cost that must be deducted.

**Revenue growth drivers:** Scores growth is overwhelmingly price-driven. FICO has raised B2B score prices aggressively -- unit volumes in mortgage originations are cyclical and were depressed through FY2023-2024. The 110% mortgage origination revenue increase in Q1 FY2025 reflects price hikes layered onto a modest volume recovery. Software growth is slower, driven by Platform ARR (+31% YoY in FY2024) offset by declining legacy/non-platform revenue.

## Cash Flow and Normalized Earnings

**FY2025 FCF: $739M** (up 22% YoY). FY2024 FCF: $607M.

**Normalized Earnings Bridge (FY2025 basis):**

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| GAAP Net Income | ~$640M |
| Add back: SBC (non-cash) | +$160M (est.) |
| Less: SBC as real economic cost | -$160M |
| Net SBC adjustment | $0 |
| Adjusted for non-recurring items | ~negligible |
| **Normalized After-Tax Earnings** | **~$640M** |
| Maintenance capex (~$30M) | already in FCF |
| **Normalized FCF** | **~$739M** |

Cash conversion is excellent: FCF/Net Income ~115%. The gap is explained by SBC add-back in operating cash flow plus minimal capex (~$30M). Working capital is a modest tailwind from deferred revenue. There are no major distortions here -- this is genuinely a cash-generative business.

## Balance Sheet and Financial Risk

This is where FICO demands scrutiny. **Shareholder equity is negative $1.7-1.8B.** Total debt is ~$3.1B against $134M cash, yielding **net debt of ~$2.95B**, or roughly **4.0x net debt/normalized FCF**.

FICO has been borrowing aggressively to fund share buybacks: **$1.4B in buybacks in FY2025 alone** (up 72% YoY). The company spent nearly 2x its FCF on buybacks, funding the excess with debt. Shares outstanding have declined from ~29M to ~24M over a few years -- effective financial engineering, but the balance sheet is now leveraged.

**Debt maturity risk:** Current maturities jumped from $15M in FY2024 to ~$400M in FY2025, indicating near-term refinancing needs. At current rates, interest expense is meaningful -- likely $150-180M annually on $3.1B of debt, consuming ~20-25% of operating income.

**Assessment:** The balance sheet is not fragile given the cash flow stability, but it is aggressive. Any disruption to the Scores pricing power or a regulatory intervention would stress this structure. The company has essentially traded balance sheet safety for EPS growth via buybacks.

## Capital Allocation

Buybacks dominate. FICO is buying back stock at $1,200+/share -- at 40x+ earnings. The implied return on buyback capital is ~2.5% (inverse of P/E), well below the cost of debt. **This is value-destructive capital allocation at current prices unless earnings growth sustains 15%+ annually.** Management is optimizing for EPS optics rather than economic value creation. Dividend: none.

## Red Flags Checklist

- [x] Persistent negative free cash flow: **No** -- FCF is strong
- [ ] Rising SBC as % of revenue: **Modest concern** -- ~8%, stable to slightly rising
- [ ] Margin expansion driven by accounting choices: **No** -- margins are real
- [x] Weak cash conversion: **No** -- cash conversion exceeds 100%
- [x] Balance sheet fragility: **Yes** -- negative equity, $3.1B debt, $400M near-term maturities

- [ ] Related party transactions: **No evidence**

## Normalized Earnings and Valuation

**Shares outstanding:** ~24M. At $1,200/share, **market cap = ~$28.8B. Enterprise value = ~$31.7B.**

**FY2026 guidance:** Revenue $2.35B (+18%), GAAP net income $795M, GAAP EPS $33.47. Consensus is slightly higher at ~$40.70 adjusted EPS.

**Normalized mid-cycle earnings:** I anchor to FY2025 as roughly mid-cycle for Scores volumes (mortgage still below normal) but acknowledge price increases may be above-trend. Normalized revenue: ~$2.1-2.2B. Normalized operating margin: 45-48%. Normalized after-tax earnings (after SBC): ~$680-720M. **Normalized EPS: ~$28-30.**

**Valuation at $1,200/share:**
- P/E on FY2025 GAAP: ~47x
- P/E on FY2026 guidance (GAAP): ~36x
- P/E on normalized mid-cycle: ~40-43x
- EV/Normalized FCF: ~43x

**What must be true for $1,200 to be fair:** Revenue growth of 15%+ for 3-5 years, sustained operating margins of 47%+, and continued score price increases without regulatory pushback. The market is pricing in a long runway of above-GDP growth from a business whose primary growth lever (score price increases) faces increasing political scrutiny.

## Key Financial Drivers

1. **Score price increases** -- This is the single most important variable. FICO's ability to raise B2B score prices 10-15% annually with zero incremental cost is the engine of the entire equity story. If pricing power stalls (regulation, competition from VantageScore, FHFA intervention), the thesis breaks.

2. **Mortgage origination volumes** -- Cyclical tailwind/headwind. Currently below mid-cycle. A normalization in housing activity provides upside to Scores revenue without requiring further price hikes.

3. **Software Platform ARR growth** -- The secondary driver. Platform ARR growing 30%+ is encouraging but the segment is still only marginally profitable. Successful cloud transition could add a second growth engine; failure means Software remains a drag on blended margins.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**The debt-funded buyback math is turning negative.** FICO is buying back stock at 40x+ earnings while issuing debt at 5-6% -- the spread is value-destructive. Sell-side models celebrate the EPS accretion from share count reduction without stress-testing the balance sheet. With $400M in near-term maturities and $3.1B total debt against negative equity, FICO is one regulatory shock away from needing to halt buybacks entirely. If buybacks stop, the EPS growth narrative collapses from ~25%+ to ~15% organic growth. The stock is priced for the former, not the latter.

Additionally, the Scores segment's 27% revenue growth is substantially price-driven, not volume-driven. The Street models this as sustainable growth; it is more accurately described as a one-time repricing of an embedded monopoly position. Each incremental price increase brings FICO closer to the political threshold where CFPB or FHFA intervention becomes likely.

**Bottom line:** FICO is a phenomenal business -- near-monopoly position, minimal capital needs, exceptional cash generation. But at $1,200/share (40x+ normalized earnings), the market is pricing perfection in a business whose primary growth lever (pricing power) faces rising regulatory risk, and whose balance sheet has been hollowed out to manufacture EPS growth. The risk/reward is unfavorable at this price.
