# SOLS (Solstice Advanced Materials) -- Financial Analysis

**Date:** February 17, 2026
**Stock Price:** ~$78 (NASDAQ: SOLS)
**Market Cap:** ~$12.4B | **Enterprise Value:** ~$13.9B (market cap + $2.0B debt - $0.534B cash)
**Shares Outstanding:** ~159M (implied from market cap / price)

---

## FINDING: Margin Compression Is Real, Not Transitory -- Market Is Pricing a Full Recovery That May Not Come Clean

The stock trades at ~52x trailing GAAP earnings ($237M in FY2025), reflecting a year distorted by ~$170M in spin-off frictional taxes and separation costs. On normalized earnings, the multiple compresses to ~26-30x, which is still rich for a specialty chemicals business, even one with regulatory tailwinds. The key question is whether the HFO transition restores 2024-era margins (~29% adj. EBITDA) or whether the current ~25% margin represents a new normal with higher standalone costs and capex intensity.

---

## 1. Business Financial Profile

| Attribute | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Capital intensity | **High and rising** -- capex $408M in FY2025 (10.5% of revenue), up 38% YoY; guided $400-425M in 2026 |
| Cost structure | Mixed fixed/variable; large manufacturing base with meaningful under-absorption risk |
| Operating leverage | Moderate -- revenue up 3%, EBITDA down 4% in FY2025; cost structure not yet optimized post-spin |
| Cyclicality | Moderate -- refrigerants have regulatory demand floor (AIM Act), but electronic materials are cyclical |
| Revenue quality | Mix of recurring replacement demand (refrigerant aftermarket) and project-based (semiconductor, nuclear) |

**Segment Mix (FY2024 pro forma):**
- Refrigerants & Applied Solutions (RAS): ~$2.7B (~71% of revenue)
- Electronic & Specialty Materials (ESM): ~$1.0B (~29% of revenue)

## 2. Income Statement Analysis

### Revenue Trend

| Period | Net Sales | YoY Growth | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY2024 (pro forma) | $3,770M | +3.3% | Includes $108M opportunistic nuclear sales |
| FY2025 | $3,890M | +3.2% | Organic ~6% ex-nuclear comps; 3% RAS, 5% ESM |
| Q3 2025 | $969M | +7% | RAS +9%, ESM +2% |
| Q4 2025 | $987M | +8% | RAS +10%, ESM +4%; organic +6% (2.5% vol, 4% price) |
| **FY2026E (guidance)** | **$3,900-4,100M** | **~0-5%** | |

Revenue growth is real but modest. Excluding the $108M in one-time nuclear transactions from 2024, underlying growth in FY2025 was closer to 6%. Q4 momentum was strong, driven by pricing power in refrigerants.

### Margin Trajectory -- The Core Issue

| Period | Adj. EBITDA | Margin | YoY Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY2024 (pro forma) | $1,098M | ~29.1% | -- |
| FY2025 | $957M | 24.6% | -450 bps |
| Q3 2025 | $235M | 24.3% | -290 bps |
| Q4 2025 | $189M | 19.1% | -670 bps |
| **FY2026E (guidance)** | **$975-1,025M** | **~25%** | **Flat to slight recovery** |

**This is a 450 bps margin compression year-over-year, with Q4 representing an acute trough at 19.1%.** Management attributes this to: (1) Transitory separation costs, (2) HFO/HFC product mix shift (HFOs now ~60% of refrigerant sales), (3) Planned plant downtime and under-absorption, (4) $30M in TSA costs expected in 2026H1 before roll-off.

The 2024 EBITDA of $1.1B was earned while still under Honeywell's cost umbrella. The FY2025 margin of 24.6% already reflects some standalone cost reality, and 2026 guidance of ~25% implies only modest recovery.

### GAAP vs. Economic Earnings Gap

| Metric | FY2024 | FY2025 | Delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net Income (GAAP) | $594M | $237M | -60% |
| Adj. EBITDA | $1,098M | $957M | -13% |
| Income Tax Expense | $192M | $362M | +$170M (frictional spin taxes) |

The $357M gap between net income decline and EBITDA decline is largely explained by: (a) ~$170M in frictional/spin-off related taxes, (b) new debt service ($2.0B in debt did not exist in 2024 -- interest was only $13M in FY2024 on allocated basis), and (c) standalone corporate costs.

### What Must Be True

1. EBITDA margins recover to 25%+ and sustain
2. Revenue grows 4-6% annually
3. Capex moderates from $400M+ toward $300M by FY2028

### What Breaks

- If margins stay at 24-25%, FCF is ~$200-250M = ~1.5-2.0% yield. Unattractive.
- If HFO pricing erodes from competitor entry or natural refrigerant alternatives, margin recovery thesis collapses.
- If SBC is $80M+, normalized EPS drops to ~$2.50, putting stock at 30x+ for a chemicals business.

## Key Financial Drivers

**1. Refrigerant Margin Trajectory (HFO/HFC mix and aftermarket)** -- The single most important variable. HFO installed base must age enough to generate aftermarket replacement demand. Watch RAS segment EBITDA margins quarterly.

**2. Standalone Cost Structure** -- $170M frictional taxes + ~$100M new interest + TSA costs. How much of FY2024's $1.1B EBITDA was Honeywell-subsidized overhead?

**3. Capex Normalization** -- At $400M+/year, true FCF is thin. Must moderate to $300M by FY2028 for the investment case to work.

---

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**The 2024 EBITDA of $1.1B is the wrong anchor.** Multiple sell-side models appear to use FY2024's $1.1B adjusted EBITDA as "normal" earnings power, treating FY2025's $957M as temporary. But $1.1B was earned under Honeywell's cost umbrella with only $13M in allocated interest (vs. ~$115M now), shared services, and higher-margin HFC sales being phased out. True normalized standalone EBITDA is likely $950M-$1,050M, not $1.1B+. The 2026 guide of $975-1,025M may be closer to steady-state than the Street assumes.

---

## Summary Assessment

**Solid but fully priced.** At ~$78, the market prices in full margin recovery, sustained growth, and eventual capex normalization -- all plausible but not certain. 26x normalized earnings and ~14x EV/EBITDA leave minimal margin of safety. The right entry is on a disappointment at $60-65 (~20-22x normalized earnings).
