# Memory Semiconductor Industry Analysis (DRAM/NAND/HBM)

## The One Industry Factor That Matters

**HBM capacity allocation is the single variable that dominates memory industry economics through 2027.**

Every HBM wafer consumes 3-4x the capacity of conventional DRAM. SK Hynix, Samsung, and Micron are all reallocating wafers from DDR4/DDR5/LPDDR to HBM. This creates a zero-sum constraint: HBM expansion mechanically starves conventional memory supply, regardless of end-demand.

**Inflection points to monitor:**
- HBM wafer share of total DRAM capacity (currently ~20%, heading to 25-30% by 2027)
- Hyperscaler capex trajectory (Meta, Microsoft, Amazon, Google GPU orders)
- HBM4 yield ramp (Q1-Q2 2026) - determines if 70% HBM share projections for SK Hynix hold

**What breaks the thesis:** AI capex pullback (hyperscaler budget cuts) or HBM4 yield problems that force capacity back to conventional DRAM.

---

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**Consensus view:** We are in a "memory supercycle" driven by AI that will extend pricing strength through 2027-2028.

**Where consensus may be wrong:** The market is underestimating how violently the HBM-conventional DRAM arbitrage will reverse when AI capex slows.

Here is the logic:
1. Today's 55-60% QoQ DRAM price increases are driven by HBM crowding out conventional supply
2. Memory makers are earning 50-60% operating margins on HBM vs. 20-30% on conventional DRAM
3. All three producers are adding HBM capacity aggressively (Samsung +50% in 2026, SK Hynix M15X by mid-2027)
4. When HBM demand growth slows (inevitable), that capacity floods back into conventional DRAM
5. The flip from shortage to glut will be faster than 2018-2019 because the capacity overhang is larger

**The variant insight:** Current margins are not "new normal" oligopoly returns. They are peak-cycle economics amplified by a temporary zero-sum capacity arbitrage. When HBM saturation occurs (likely 2027-2028), expect 30-50% margin compression in 12-18 months.

This is **bearish for industry participants** on a 2-3 year view, even if near-term (2026) remains tight.

## Cycle Position Assessment

| Metric | Current State | Historical Context |
|--------|---------------|-------------------|
| DRAM Operating Margins | 44% industry avg, SK Hynix 59% (Q3 2025) | Mid-cycle: 20-25%; Trough: Negative |
| Fab Utilization | >95% | Mid-cycle: 80-85% |
| Contract Price Trajectory | +55-60% QoQ (Q1 2026) | Unprecedented since 2017-2018 |
| Lead Times | 26-39 weeks (LPDDR5X) | Normal: 8-12 weeks |
| Supply Growth | 16-17% YoY (below historical norm) | Demand growth 30-35% |

**Current position: Late Cycle / Peak**

We are definitively past mid-cycle. Current margins (44-59% operating) are 2-3x historical mid-cycle levels. Utilization is maxed. Pricing is parabolic. These are textbook peak-cycle indicators.

The industry hit trough in Q4 2022/Q1 2023 (negative gross margins across all three producers). The recovery took ~18 months to reach current peak conditions. Typical DRAM cycles run 2-3 years trough-to-trough.

**Timing estimate:** Peak pricing likely mid-to-late 2026. Downturn risk rises materially in H2 2026 through 2027 as new capacity ramps.

## Industry Structure

### Oligopoly Dynamics

Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron control 95% of global DRAM capacity. This consolidation followed the 2011-2013 shakeout (Elpida bankruptcy, Hynix restructuring).

**Structural implications:**
- Explicit price-fixing era ended (2010 EU cartel fines)
- Tacit supply discipline has improved
- All three publicly discuss "rationality" on capacity adds
- But: Coordination breaks down at cycle extremes

The oligopoly provides floor support during downturns (producers cut utilization rather than destroy pricing) but does not eliminate cycles. 2022-2023 still saw negative margins despite consolidation.

### HBM Market Share (Q2-Q3 2025)

| Company | HBM Share | Total DRAM Share | HBM Qualification Status |
|---------|-----------|------------------|-------------------------|
| SK Hynix | 53-62% | 36% | Fully qualified with NVIDIA |
| Samsung | 17-35% | 40% | HBM3E issues, HBM4 TBD |
| Micron | 11-21% | 23% | HBM4 samples delivered |

SK Hynix's HBM dominance (particularly with NVIDIA) is the key differentiator. NVIDIA drives 27% of SK Hynix revenue directly. UBS projects SK Hynix at ~70% HBM4 share for NVIDIA's Rubin platform.

### China Risk

CXMT (DRAM) and YMTC (NAND) are 2-3 generations behind on leading-edge nodes but expanding capacity aggressively.

- CXMT: ~15% equivalent of global DRAM capacity by end-2025
- YMTC: <5% NAND share (falling due to US equipment restrictions)

**Assessment:** Chinese producers are a long-term structural risk to legacy DRAM/NAND pricing (DDR4, mature NAND), but 3+ years from threatening HBM or leading-edge DDR5. Not a near-term cycle factor.

## Historical Cycle Patterns

### Past Downturns

| Cycle | Trigger | Peak-to-Trough Pricing | Margin Impact | Duration |
|-------|---------|----------------------|---------------|----------|
| 2018-2019 | Smartphone/PC demand slowdown, inventory build | -50 to -70% | Margins to low single digits | 6 quarters |
| 2022-2023 | Post-COVID demand collapse, inventory glut | -50%+ | Negative gross margins (Q1 2023: -17%) | 5-6 quarters |
| 2011-2012 | Capacity glut, Elpida bankruptcy | Severe | Industry restructuring | Structural |

**Pattern:** Memory downturns are violent (-50% or worse pricing), compress margins to zero or negative, and typically last 5-6 quarters. The oligopoly structure prevents permanent value destruction but does not prevent cycles.

### Boom Characteristics

Current boom shares features with 2017-2018:
- Demand shock from new application (AI/data centers then, AI/HBM now)
- Supply constrained by capacity misallocation
- Parabolic pricing increases
- "Supercycle" narrative

2017-2018 was followed by the 2018-2019 crash. The market assumed that cycle was different too.

## Supply/Demand Dynamics

### Demand Drivers

| Segment | 2026 Growth | % of DRAM Bits | Commentary |
|---------|-------------|----------------|------------|
| AI/HBM | +50-60% | 20%+ | Hyperscaler GPU builds |
| Server DDR5 | +25-30% | 25% | Data center refresh |
| Mobile | +10-15% | 30% | Mixed, OEMs cutting specs |
| PC | +5-10% | 15% | AI PC launch cycle |

AI/HBM is the marginal driver. Traditional demand (mobile, PC) is growing below historical rates. The shortage is manufactured by capacity reallocation, not organic demand surge in aggregate bits.

### Supply Response

- 2026 DRAM supply growth: 16-17% (below historical 20%+)
- HBM consumes 3-4x wafers per GB vs. conventional DRAM
- New fabs: SK Hynix M15X (mid-2027), Samsung P5 (2028), Micron US ($20B capex commitment)

**Key observation:** Supply discipline is holding because HBM margins are so attractive. When HBM margins normalize, the discipline breaks. Each producer has announced massive capex plans that will come online in 2027-2028.

## Primary Industry Risks

1. **HBM demand inflection:** If hyperscaler AI capex slows or HBM per-GPU requirements decline, HBM capacity floods back to conventional DRAM
2. **Classic overbuilding:** $500B+ combined capex commitments across the three majors will create structural oversupply by 2028
3. **Margin mean reversion:** Operating margins at 44-59% must normalize toward 20-25% mid-cycle levels
4. **Geopolitical:** Export controls on equipment to China could tighten, but also risk Chinese retaliation on memory imports

**Bottom line:** The memory industry is at or near peak-cycle conditions. Current margins (44-59% operating) are 2x mid-cycle levels and will normalize. The "supercycle" narrative is overstated - this is a classic shortage driven by capacity reallocation to HBM, not a structural change in industry economics. SK Hynix's HBM dominance provides relative positioning strength, but all participants face margin normalization risk when AI capex growth slows. Industry economics alone warrant caution on capital deployment at current valuations.
