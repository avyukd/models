## 5. The Setup

**What is NextNav?**
NextNav holds lower 900 MHz spectrum licenses it seeks to repurpose for:
1. Terrestrial backup to GPS (Positioning, Navigation, Timing)
2. 5G broadband

**Why does this matter?**
GPS is a single point of failure for critical infrastructure. A terrestrial backup has national security value. Low-band spectrum is scarce and valuable for 5G coverage.

**The Regulatory Path:**
1. FCC issues Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (NPRM)
2. Comment period
3. FCC issues Final Rule approving spectrum use
4. NextNav commercializes via carrier partnerships

**Current Status:**
The NPRM has been repeatedly delayed. As of early 2026, it has not been issued. FCC Chair Brendan Carr is cited as supportive, but FCC is a 5-member body and formal approval requires majority vote.

**Carrier Relationship:**
AT&T agreement extended to 2028. This is a soft signal but not a commercial commitment.

## 9. Positioning & Portfolio Logic

**Why Not Own This:**

1. **No Edge:** We cannot independently assess FCC approval probability. The available information is either promotional (Frischer) or opaque (FCC proceedings). Without an edge on the binary outcome, this is gambling, not investing.

2. **Adverse Insider Behavior:** Insiders have halved their ownership while asking shareholders to hold through the binary. Management extracts value via SBC regardless of outcome. This is not alignment.

3. **Asymmetric Downside in Failure Case:** If FCC denies or delays, the company has no fallback. The equity goes to zero or near-zero. The upside is capped by spectrum value; the downside is total loss.

4. **Timeline Uncertainty Compounds Dilution Risk:** An 18-month runway against an unknowable regulatory timeline is uncomfortably tight. A financing event pre-approval would be highly dilutive.

5. **Single-Source Information Environment:** The bullish case is dominated by one vocal 5% shareholder on social media. The bear case is not articulated in detail. This is an information-poor environment.

**What Would Move This Out of the "Too Hard" Pile:**

- Verifiable FCC signals (published NPRM, public commissioner statements)
- Commercial commitment from AT&T or another carrier
- Insider buying (not selling) ahead of regulatory decision
- Independent expert analysis of approval probability
- Resolution of opposition arguments with supporting evidence

**If Forced to Size:**
If someone had a genuine edge on FCC approval probability, this could be sized as a small optionality position (0.5-1% of portfolio) with clear stop-loss at thesis invalidation. Without that edge, the correct size is zero.
