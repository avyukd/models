## Key Drivers (The 1-3 Things That Actually Matter)

**1. EBITDA margin trajectory from 15.9% back toward 17-18%.** This is THE variable. The EUR 500M productivity program, TSA exit (~50bps drag until end-2027), and cocoa normalization are three independent tailwinds that compound. If margins reach 17% on EUR 8.3B revenue, EBITDA is EUR 1,410M vs. EUR 1,255M today -- a 12% uplift that the market is not paying for at 9.4x trailing.

**2. Developed market volume trends in H1 2026.** Q4 2025's -3% volume decline is the data point driving the GLP-1 fear discount. The next two quarters either confirm or kill this narrative. Weather, comps, and seasonality all argue for recovery. This is a binary catalyst with asymmetric payoff -- confirmation of stable volumes re-rates the stock; continued weakness validates the discount.

**3. Resolution of the separation-era cash flow depression.** The market sees EUR 38M reported FCF and panics. This is a temporary artifact of EUR 564M in one-time separation costs. Comparable FCF was EUR 602M. As separation costs roll off through 2028, the gap between reported and normalized FCF closes, removing the optical overhang.

## Thesis in 3-5 Bullets

- **MICC trades at a 30-40% EV/EBITDA discount to consumer staples peers (9.4x vs. 12-14x) due to three transient factors: separation-distorted FCF, Q4 volume weakness reigniting GLP-1 fears, and Unilever overhang.** None of these are structural. All resolve within 18-24 months.

- **The GLP-1 narrative is overstated for ice cream specifically.** Penetration math implies 1.5-4% cumulative volume headwind in developed markets -- roughly one year of normal growth, not a category killer. Premiumization (fewer occasions, higher willingness to pay per occasion) can offset revenue impact. The market is pricing this as existential when it is manageable.

- **The freezer cabinet network (3M units, ~30% of global ice cream freezers) is a genuinely defensible asset.** It creates point-of-sale exclusion in the out-of-home channel (40% of revenue) and is capital-intensive to replicate. AI-driven inventory optimization is extracting 8-30% incremental growth from this installed base. This is an under-monetized infrastructure asset inside a consumer staple.

- **Normalized mid-cycle earnings power of EUR 600-650M in FCF (FY2027-2028) supports EUR 17-20 per share on 11-12x EV/EBITDA -- 20-40% upside from current levels.** The market is pricing in permanent impairment; we see a transition trough.

## The Setup

MICC is a textbook spinoff orphan. Unilever extracted EUR 3B in cash, loaded the debt onto MICC, retained a 19.9% overhang, and walked away. The stock listed in December 2025, immediately faced forced selling from Unilever holders who couldn't own MICC, reported its first earnings with EUR 38M headline FCF (distorted by separation costs), and saw Q4 volumes decline 3%, reigniting GLP-1 fears. The stock is down ~20% from its first-day highs and sits near 52-week lows.

This is what spinoff dislocation looks like. The structural characteristics -- forced selling, index orphan, distorted first-year financials, narrative fear (GLP-1) unrelated to near-term fundamentals -- are classic setup conditions.

The underlying business is the #1 global ice cream company with 21% market share, a defensible freezer network, four of the five largest global ice cream brands, and an industry structure (consolidated oligopoly, physical distribution barriers, low cyclicality) that supports stable economics.

## Risks and Kill Switches

**Kill switch 1: Two consecutive quarters of negative developed market volumes.** If H1 2026 shows continued volume declines in Europe and North America, the GLP-1 thesis graduates from narrative to structural risk. Exit the position.

**Kill switch 2: Leverage breaching 2.5x net debt/EBITDA.** The balance sheet is the binding constraint. If separation costs overrun, Kwality Wall's acquisition drains more cash than expected, or EBITDA disappoints, leverage creeps toward investment-grade downgrade territory. Monitor quarterly.

**Kill switch 3: Private label share exceeding 22% in US or 25% in Europe.** This signals the branded price premium is unsustainable and MICC's brand moat is eroding faster than expected.

**Permanent loss risks:**
- Sustained GLP-1-driven volume decline of 3%+ annually in developed markets (75% of revenue) would structurally impair the business.
- Cocoa remaining at 3x norms for 3+ years (climate-driven structural shift) compresses margins permanently.
- Ben & Jerry's dispute escalating into a brand-damaging public battle or forced sale at a discount.

**What I am most worried about:** The cash flow tightrope through 2027. MICC must simultaneously service EUR 3.4B debt, absorb EUR 1.06B in separation costs, fund the Kwality Wall's acquisition (~EUR 575M), complete the productivity program, exit TSAs, and begin paying dividends -- all on ~EUR 600M comparable FCF. There is no margin for error. One thing going wrong (a bad cocoa year, slower TSA exits, volume weakness) forces painful trade-offs.

## Positioning and Portfolio Logic

**Why this deserves capital now:** The spinoff orphan setup is time-limited. Forced selling exhausts. The optical FCF distortion resolves. Each quarter of clean standalone data reduces uncertainty. The best risk/reward in spinoffs exists in the first 6-12 months when the dislocation is widest. Waiting for "confirmation" means paying a higher price for less upside.

**Size: 1.5-2.0% initial position.** Not full-size because: (a) no independent capital allocation track record to underwrite, (b) the cash flow tightrope through 2027 is real, (c) conviction on volume trends requires H1 2026 data. Scale to 3% if Q1/Q2 volumes confirm recovery and margin trajectory is intact.
