# MICC (The Magnum Ice Cream Company N.V.) -- Financial Analysis

**Stock Price:** ~$15.65 (Feb 17, 2026) | **Market Cap:** ~$9.6B | **Shares Outstanding:** 612.3M
**52-Week Range:** $14.45 - $19.93

---

## 1. Business Financial Profile

- **Capital intensity:** Moderate-to-high for food/beverage. Ice cream requires cold chain logistics, freezer cabinets at retail, and seasonal production capacity. Management has acknowledged aging plants and underinvestment historically within Unilever.
- **Cost structure:** Significant fixed cost base (manufacturing plants, cold chain, cabinet fleet). Variable costs include dairy/sugar commodities, packaging, and distribution.
- **Operating leverage:** Present but muted -- this is a low-growth staple category. Volume leverage exists but is partially offset by commodity pass-through dynamics.
- **Cyclicality:** Low cyclicality in demand (consumer staple / indulgence), but meaningful **seasonality** (summer-weighted). Weather is a genuine revenue variable.
- **Revenue quality:** Largely transactional/seasonal. No subscription or contractual revenue. Brand-driven repeat purchase, but no contractual lock-in. ~75% developed markets (Europe, US, ANZ), ~25% emerging markets.
- **Market position:** #1 global ice cream company with ~21% market share. Key brands: Magnum, Ben & Jerry's, Cornetto, Wall's, Breyers.

## 5. Capital Allocation Assessment

- **Reinvestment:** High near-term via EUR 500M supply chain transformation. Necessary -- management admitted to chronic underinvestment under Unilever.
- **M&A:** None yet. Discipline unproven.
- **Buybacks:** No capacity near-term at 2.4x leverage.
- **Dividend:** No dividend disclosed or guided for 2026. 40-60% payout ratio policy starting 2027.
- **Insider buying:** Multiple insiders purchased shares in Dec 2025 - Feb 2026. Modestly positive signal.

## 6. Red Flags Checklist

- [X] **Persistent negative free cash flow** -- FY2025 reported FCF EUR 38M. Separation-driven, not operational, but real.
- [X] **Margin expansion driven by accounting choices** -- EUR 318M gap between adjusted EBIT and GAAP operating profit (53% gap). Heavy reliance on "adjusted" presentation.
- [ ] **Weak cash conversion** -- 66% on comparable basis. Acceptable but not stellar for a consumer staple.
- [ ] **Balance sheet fragility** -- No. Well-structured debt.
- [ ] **Aggressive revenue recognition** -- No evidence.
- **GLP-1 risk:** Q4 -3% volume decline. Management attributes to weather/comps, not GLP-1. Market is worried.
- **FX exposure:** ~470bps headwind on reported results. ~25% EM revenue with weaker currencies.

### What Must Be True

The stock at ~EUR 14.4 implies ~8.4x normalized EBITDA, requiring you to believe either: (a) margins never recover, (b) volumes structurally decline from GLP-1, or (c) the category deserves a permanent discount. If none are true and margins recover to 12.5%+, the stock is meaningfully cheap.

### What Breaks

If GLP-1 drives persistent 2-3% annual volume declines across developed markets (75% of revenue), organic growth flips negative. At 0% organic growth with no margin improvement, EBITDA stays flat at EUR 1,255M, and at 9x EV/EBITDA the stock is worth roughly current levels.

## Key Financial Drivers

**1. EBITDA margin trajectory (the load-bearing variable).** Management guides 40-60bps annual improvement. If margins recover from 15.9% to 17-18% EBITDA, this is a EUR 1,350-1,425M EBITDA business and the valuation gap to peers closes quickly.

**2. Volume trends in developed markets.** Q4 -3% volume is the single most important near-term data point. If Q1/Q2 2026 show recovery, the GLP-1 narrative fades. If volumes remain negative, the market re-rates downward.

**3. TSA exit execution.** Unilever TSAs run through end-2027. ~50bps EBITDA margin drag. TSA exits in spinoffs frequently cost more and take longer than planned.

---

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**The adjusted-to-GAAP gap is wider than it appears, and it cuts both ways.** If you reverse-engineer comparable FCF (EUR 602M) against adjusted EBIT (EUR 917M), you get 66% cash conversion. The comparable FCF -- management's best-foot-forward metric excluding separation costs -- already reflects elevated capex. This suggests the "comparable" FCF is itself a generous presentation, because it includes capital spending classified as "productivity" that is partly catch-up maintenance.

Street models anchoring to EUR 600M+ near-term FCF may be too optimistic. True steady-state FCF may be closer to EUR 500-550M for 2-3 years, rising to EUR 600M+ only once the supply chain program completes and TSAs fully exit. The discount to peers is partly justified, but if margins expand to 12.5%+, the eventual FCF power supports a price materially above current levels.
