# Hoyne Bancorp, Inc. (HYNE) - Financial Analysis

## Executive Summary

Hoyne Bancorp is a newly public Illinois thrift holding company that converted from mutual holding company form in December 2025. The stock trades at approximately 1.2x price-to-book value following a 38% pop from its $10 IPO price. This is a classic first-step thrift conversion play: overcapitalized, subscale, currently unprofitable, with significant potential for capital return through buybacks over the next 3-5 years. The investment case hinges entirely on (1) whether management deploys the excess capital intelligently and (2) whether the bank can return to profitability as interest rate pressures normalize.

**Key Metrics at a Glance:**
- Stock Price: ~$13.85 (as of late December 2025)
- Market Cap: ~$112M
- Shares Outstanding: 8.1M
- IPO Price: $10.00 (December 4, 2025)
- Total Assets (June 30, 2025): $453M
- Pre-Conversion Equity: $88.9M
- Gross IPO Proceeds: ~$79M
- Pro Forma Equity (est.): ~$150-160M
- P/TBV (est.): ~0.70-0.75x (based on pro forma equity)
- FY 2024 Net Income: -$2.0M (loss)
- H1 2025 Net Income: -$48K (near breakeven)

## 1. Business Financial Profile

**Classification:**
- **Capital Intensity:** Low (service business, but requires regulatory capital)
- **Cost Structure:** Predominantly fixed (branches, compliance, technology)
- **Operating Leverage:** High - small revenue changes drive large earnings swings
- **Cyclicality:** Moderate - tied to interest rate cycle and local economy
- **Revenue Quality:** Mixed - spread income is recurring but rate-sensitive; fee income minimal

**Geographic Concentration:** Cook County, Illinois and adjacent communities. Six full-service branches plus one loan production office in Oak Park.

**Franchise Age:** 138 years (founded 1887) - one of the oldest savings institutions in Chicagoland.

**Strategic Shift:** Beginning in 2023, management pivoted from a traditional residential mortgage thrift toward commercial lending. As of June 30, 2025, commercial real estate and C&I loans comprised 48.8% of the total loan portfolio ($124.7M). This represents a meaningful strategic repositioning.

## 2. Income Statement Analysis

### Revenue Trends

| Metric | FY 2023 | FY 2024 | H1 2025 (annualized est.) |
|--------|---------|---------|---------------------------|
| Total Revenue | $11.6M | $11.4M | ~$11M (declining) |
| Net Income | $1.5M | -$2.0M | -$0.1M |

**Key Observations:**
- Revenue is essentially flat/declining despite balance sheet growth
- Net Interest Margin (NIM) is under severe pressure - this is the core issue
- The bank swung from $1.5M profit to $2.0M loss in one year, a $3.5M swing on ~$11M of revenue
- The primary driver was higher interest expense (deposit repricing) and increased credit loss provisions

**Profitability Context:**
The FY 2024 loss of $2.0M represents negative ROA of approximately -0.44% and negative ROE of approximately -2.3%. These are clearly unacceptable returns, but the trajectory in H1 2025 shows improvement to near-breakeven.

### Stock-Based Compensation (SBC)

As a newly converted thrift, HYNE will establish an ESOP (Employee Stock Ownership Plan) and likely an MRP (Management Recognition Plan) in the coming years. The ESOP received 647,755 shares (~8% of outstanding) at the IPO.

**Critical Note:** SBC expense will become a material factor as these plans vest. Based on typical thrift conversion structures, I would expect annual SBC expense of $0.5-1.0M once plans are fully established. This is not yet reflected in historical financials but must be considered in normalized earnings.

## 5. Capital Allocation Assessment

### Expected Capital Deployment (Typical Thrift Conversion Playbook)

1. **Stock Buybacks:** Under OCC/FDIC rules, converted thrifts typically cannot repurchase stock for one year post-conversion. Starting December 2026, HYNE could potentially repurchase up to 5% annually initially. At current prices, a 5% buyback would retire ~400K shares.

2. **ESOP/MRP Plans:** Already established ESOP (648K shares). Expect MRP approval at first shareholder meeting, likely allocating 4% of shares to management/directors.

3. **Dividend Policy:** No dividend currently. Given the losses and capital deployment priorities, dividends are unlikely near-term.

4. **Organic Growth:** Management could deploy capital into loan growth, but the Chicago market is highly competitive.

### Capital Return Math

Pro forma TBV of ~$155M on $112M market cap = ~$43M of "value gap" if the stock traded at 1.0x TBV. The typical thrift conversion bull case is:
- Stock trades to TBV over 3-5 years via buybacks
- Buybacks at discount to TBV are accretive
- Eventually, the thrift sells to a larger bank at 1.2-1.5x TBV

**Back-of-Envelope Buyback Scenario:**

| Year | Buyback (% of shares) | Shares Repurchased (at $14) | TBV Accretion |
|------|-----------------------|-----------------------------|---------------|
| 2026 | 0% | 0 | 0 |
| 2027 | 5% | 405K | +$0.50/share |
| 2028 | 5% | 385K | +$0.55/share |
| 2029 | 10% | 690K | +$1.20/share |
| 2030 | 10% | 620K | +$1.40/share |

Cumulative TBV accretion: ~$3.50-4.00/share over 4 years, taking TBV from ~$19 to ~$23. If the stock eventually trades at TBV, that is a 65% return over 4-5 years, or ~11-13% annualized. **This is the bull case.**

## Key Financial Drivers

### The 1-3 Metrics That Actually Matter

1. **Return on Assets (ROA) / Net Interest Margin (NIM):** This is the single most important metric. At current NIM, the bank cannot earn its cost of capital. Every quarter, watch whether NIM is expanding or compressing. A return to 3.0%+ NIM would change the investment thesis entirely.

2. **Capital Deployment (Buybacks):** Starting December 2026, watch for buyback announcements. The pace and price discipline of buybacks will determine long-term value creation. A bank buying back stock at 0.7x TBV is creating significant value; one that does not is wasting its overcapitalized position.

3. **Credit Quality (NPAs, NCOs):** The commercial lending pivot introduces new risk. Non-performing assets at 0.4% is excellent currently. Any deterioration (especially in CRE) would be a significant red flag.

### What Would Cause Each to Inflect

- **NIM Improvement:** Fed rate cuts flowing through to lower deposit costs faster than loan repricing
- **NIM Deterioration:** Competition for deposits, prolonged high rates, or aggressive loan pricing to grow portfolio
- **Buyback Acceleration:** Management proving shareholder-friendly, stock remaining below TBV
- **No Buybacks:** Management hoarding capital, pursuing M&A, or regulatory restrictions
- **Credit Deterioration:** Chicago CRE market weakness, recession, specific borrower problems

## What Consensus May Be Missing

### Non-Consensus Insight: The "Hidden" Dilution of ESOP/MRP

Most thrift conversion analysis focuses on buybacks creating TBV accretion. What is often underappreciated is the offsetting dilution from ESOP and MRP plans.

**The Math:**
- ESOP: 648K shares already allocated (8% of shares)
- MRP (expected): Typically 4% of shares = ~324K shares
- Total management/employee allocation: ~972K shares = **12% of company**

These shares are issued at essentially zero cost to recipients but represent real dilution to outside shareholders. The ESOP shares were "purchased" at $10 with a loan from the holding company that the bank will effectively repay through earnings. The MRP shares are pure grants.

**Net Impact:** If you model 12% dilution over 5 years versus 25-30% buybacks over the same period, the net share reduction is only ~15-18%, not the headline 25-30% buyback number.

**Bottom Line:** The capital return story is real but more modest than pure buyback math suggests. Investors should model this on a net basis.

### Second Non-Consensus Point: Geographic Concentration Risk

Hoyne entire franchise is in Cook County, Illinois. Chicago population has been declining. The commercial real estate market, particularly office, has faced significant stress. While Hoyne focuses on smaller CRE loans (not downtown office towers), the regional economic exposure is concentrated.

Most thrift conversion analysis treats these as pure capital structure plays. But a regional economic downturn in Chicago could simultaneously:
1. Compress earnings (reduce loan demand, increase credit costs)
2. Destroy the M&A exit (no acquirer wants a troubled Chicago thrift)

This geographic concentration is underweighted in most analyses.

## Investment Conclusion

**Summary Assessment:** HYNE is a classic subscale thrift conversion trading at ~0.72x pro forma TBV with currently negative earnings. The investment case is purely a capital structure arbitrage: buy at discount to TBV, wait for buybacks, eventually exit via sale to acquirer.

**Financial Quality:** Weak. The bank cannot currently earn its cost of capital. Even normalized earnings are approximately breakeven. This is not a franchise you would want to own in perpetuity.

**Valuation:** Optically cheap at 0.72x TBV, but justified given unprofitability. Relative to other recent thrift conversions, this is a middle-of-the-pack valuation.

**Risk/Reward:** Asymmetric but with meaningful downside. Upside case is ~65% over 4-5 years if the playbook works. Downside case is capital destruction if the commercial lending pivot produces losses or management fails to return capital.

**Catalyst Timeline:**
- 2026: First post-conversion earnings reports (will they return to profitability?)
- December 2026: First eligible buyback date
- 2027-2029: Buyback execution phase
- 2028-2030: Potential M&A window (3+ years post-conversion)

**What I Would Need to Get Comfortable:**
1. Evidence of sustainable profitability (even $0.50M annual earnings)
2. Management signaling commitment to buybacks
3. Stable credit quality in the commercial portfolio
4. No aggressive M&A activity

**Final Word:** This is a "trust the process" thrift conversion investment. The process works mechanically (overcapitalized bank buys back stock at discount to TBV, eventually sells). But it requires patience, and the current operating performance gives no margin for error. Investors buying here are betting on capital structure mechanics, not franchise quality.
