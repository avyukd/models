## 3. Stock-Based Compensation Discipline

### Current State

**ESOP Allocation:**
- 647,755 shares sold to Hoyne Savings Bank Employee Stock Ownership Plan
- 8.0% of outstanding shares post-conversion
- Standard for thrift conversions

**Charitable Foundation:**
- 161,938 shares contributed to Hoyne Charitable Foundation
- 2.0% of outstanding shares

**Future Dilution Risk (Projected):**

Per federal regulations governing thrift conversions (12 CFR 192.500), within 12 months post-conversion:
- **Stock Option Plan:** Up to 10% of conversion shares = 793,500 shares maximum
- **Management Recognition Plan (MRP):** Up to 3-4% of conversion shares = ~238,000-317,000 shares maximum

If Hoyne implements maximum permitted plans:
- Total potential dilution: 13-14% of conversion shares
- Combined with ESOP: ~21-22% of pro forma shares

**Assessment:** This is *standard* for thrift conversions, not excessive. However, shareholders should expect a proxy vote within 12 months requesting approval for MRP and option plans.

**Assessment:** The bank has a 50-year history of small, digestible acquisitions of neighboring thrifts. No evidence of empire-building or overleveraged deals.

**However:** All historical M&A occurred under mutual ownership structure (no public shareholders). Capital allocation discipline under public ownership is untested.

### Recent Strategic Shift

- Prior to 2022: Traditional residential mortgage lender
- 2022-present: Pivot to commercial real estate and C&I lending
- As of June 2025: 48.8% of loan portfolio in CRE/C&I (up from ~0% in 2022)

**Risk:** Unproven competency in commercial lending. Track record will take 5+ years to evaluate.

### Buybacks & Issuance

- No buyback announced or authorized (expected given just-completed IPO)

**Regulatory Constraint:**

## 5. Forward-Looking Capital Allocation Risk

### What Management is Likely to Do Next (12-24 months)

1. **Stock benefit plans:** Propose MRP and option plans at first annual meeting (standard, expected)
2. **Continued commercial lending growth:** Deploy capital into CRE/C&I loans in Chicago market (stated strategy)
3. **Possible M&A:** Given excess capital and history of small acquisitions, management may pursue additional tuck-in deals

### Where Capital is Most Likely to Be Misallocated

**Risk #1: Overpaying for M&A**
- Overcapitalized balance sheet creates temptation to "put capital to work"
- Community bank M&A is competitive; sellers know conversion banks have capital
- No track record of M&A discipline under public ownership

**Risk #2: Commercial Lending Deterioration**
- Rapid pivot to CRE/C&I (0% to 49% in ~3 years) represents concentration risk
- Chicago CRE market faces stress
- Credit losses could consume capital intended for shareholder returns

**Risk #3: Excessive Stock-Based Compensation**
- Standard thrift conversion playbook includes 10% option pool + 4% MRP
- If management takes maximum permitted dilution, shareholders absorb 14%+ dilution

## The One Thing That Determines Value Creation

**Whether management treats excess capital as shareholder capital or a corporate war chest.**

Hoyne emerged from conversion with ~$79M in gross proceeds and an 18%+ equity-to-assets ratio. This overcapitalization creates two paths:

**Path A (Alignment):** Management recognizes that excess capital earning sub-2% ROE is value destruction. They pursue high-return deployment opportunities only and return remainder to shareholders via buybacks/dividends.

**Path B (Misalignment):** Management views excess capital as "capacity" to grow the franchise. They pursue M&A to increase assets under management. They resist buybacks because "we need capital for opportunities."

## What Consensus May Be Missing

### Non-Consensus Insight: Insider Buying Above IPO Price is Unusual and Meaningful

**Consensus view:** Thrift conversion insiders buy at IPO price because it's discounted to book value. This is mechanical participation, not conviction.

**What consensus misses:** CFO Manfre's family trusts bought additional shares at $13.75-$13.94 on December 5, 2025 - nearly 40% above the $10 IPO price. This is *not* the standard thrift conversion playbook.

**Implication:** This is modestly positive for alignment. The CFO is betting his own money alongside shareholders at prices above IPO.

### The CEO Ownership Gap

**What I couldn't find:** CEO Walter Healy's ownership position. Form 3/4 filings for the CEO were not returned in searches.

**If CEO ownership is low, this is a red flag.** The CEO should be the most aligned insider. Monitor for CEO Form 3/4 filings in coming weeks.
