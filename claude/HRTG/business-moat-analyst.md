# Heritage Insurance Holdings (HRTG) -- Business Quality & Moat Analysis

**Date:** 2026-02-24

## Business Model Summary

Heritage is a Florida-origin property & casualty insurer writing ~$1.44B in gross premiums-in-force across personal and commercial residential lines in 16 states plus Hawaii. Revenue comes from net premiums earned after ceding roughly 45-50% of gross premiums to reinsurers via a $2.48B catastrophe excess-of-loss tower. The company retains $40-50M per Southeast event. Fixed costs are moderate (technology, compliance, staffing); variable costs are dominated by reinsurance and claims. Profits concentrate in underwriting margin -- the spread between earned premium and losses plus expenses -- with a Q3 2025 combined ratio of 72.9% and pre-announced Q4 2025 net income exceeding $60M.

Heritage grew historically through Citizens Property Insurance depopulation -- assuming policies from Florida's state-run insurer of last resort. The company has since diversified: 71.8% of total insured value now sits outside Florida (per Q1 2025 disclosure), a meaningful shift from its Florida-centric origins.

## Source(s) of Advantage

**Regulatory access and licensing.** Florida's insurance market is heavily regulated. Participating in Citizens depopulation requires OIR approval after review of financials, reinsurance programs, and management. This creates a modest barrier -- but 17 new carriers have entered Florida since 2023 reforms, proving it is not a durable moat. Licensing across 16+ states adds operational complexity that deters the smallest entrants but not well-capitalized ones.

**Reinsurance program as infrastructure.** Heritage's $2.48B cat XOL tower, placed annually, represents a structural cost that smaller entrants cannot easily replicate. Reinsurance relationships take years to build, and Heritage's track record of program placement (even post-Hurricane Milton) demonstrates access. However, reinsurance is purchased, not proprietary -- any well-capitalized insurer can buy similar coverage at similar rates.

**Rate adequacy from tort reform tailwind.** Florida's SB 2A (2022) and SB 7052 (2023) eliminated one-way attorney fees, banned assignment of benefits abuse, and shortened claims filing windows. Personal insurance litigation in Florida fell 36% since 2021. This is the primary driver of Heritage's margin expansion -- loss ratios collapsed from the high-50s to 38.3% in Q3 2025. This is a market-wide benefit, not a Heritage-specific advantage.

**No meaningful switching costs.** Homeowners insurance is renewed annually. Policyholders can and do switch carriers, particularly during Citizens depopulation rounds. There is no lock-in beyond annual policy terms and inertia.

## Competitive Landscape

Florida homeowners insurance is structurally fragmented. Seventeen new carriers entered post-reform, and Citizens' policy count dropped to ~385,000 (lowest ever) as private carriers absorbed over 546,000 policies in 2025 alone. The OIR received 73 rate-decrease filings and 94 zero-increase filings in late 2025 -- early evidence of competitive pricing pressure as the market normalizes.

Heritage reduced policies-in-force by 26.5% while increasing premiums 12% through rate actions (per 2024 disclosures), suggesting disciplined underwriting. But this discipline is a management choice, not a structural barrier. Competitors with adequate capital and reinsurance can write the same risks.

The real competitive dynamic is that Florida P&C has been a graveyard for carriers historically -- hurricanes and litigation drove $1.8B industry underwriting losses in 2022. The 2023-2024 reforms changed the litigation environment, and the absence of major hurricane landfalls in 2025 provided a benign loss year. Both conditions are temporary or reversible.

## Customer Behavior & Revenue Quality

Homeowners insurance is mandatory for mortgaged properties, providing a structural demand floor. But customers are not loyal -- they follow price. Citizens depopulation policies are assigned, not chosen, and retention depends on Heritage maintaining competitive pricing. No meaningful contract duration beyond one-year terms. No disclosed churn metrics, but the industry standard for homeowners insurance retention is 85-90%.

Revenue quality is moderate: premiums are predictable on an annual cycle, but repricing happens annually, and catastrophe losses introduce significant earnings volatility. Heritage's ceding of ~45-50% of premiums to reinsurers means the retained book is smaller and more volatile than gross figures suggest.

## Evidence of Moat in the Financials

Heritage's financial trajectory tells the story of an industry cycle, not a moat:

- **2024 full year:** Net income $61.5M, book value $9.50/share, combined ratio ~89.7% (including $57M Hurricane Milton impact)
- **Q3 2025:** Net income $50.4M, combined ratio 72.9%, book value $14.15/share (up 49% YTD)
- **Q4 2025 (pre-announced):** Net income exceeding $60M, full-year ROE above 45%

The 45%+ ROE is extraordinary -- and almost certainly unsustainable. It reflects: (1) a low equity base after years of cumulative hurricane losses depleted book value, (2) tort reform benefits still flowing through as legacy litigation runs off, and (3) a benign 2025 hurricane season. High ROE on a low book value base flatters the metric. Book value per share went from ~$9.50 to ~$14.15 in three quarters -- this is recovery, not steady-state earning power.

## Fragility Analysis

**Hurricane risk is existential.** Despite geographic diversification, Heritage retains $40-50M per Southeast catastrophe event. A major Florida hurricane in the $50B+ insured loss range would blow through retentions, trigger reinstatement premiums, and potentially impair capital -- as Hurricane Milton demonstrated in Q4 2024. The business model requires periodic capital destruction from catastrophes.

**Tort reform reversal.** The entire margin improvement story rests on Florida's 2022-2023 legislative reforms. These are statutory, not constitutional. A future legislature could restore one-way attorney fees or expand bad-faith litigation standards. Political winds shift.

**Reinsurance repricing.** Heritage spends ~$430M annually on reinsurance (~30% of premiums-in-force). A hard reinsurance market following a major loss year could compress margins sharply, as occurred in 2022-2023.

**Competitive entry is accelerating.** Seventeen new carriers in Florida since reforms. As profitability improves industry-wide, more capital enters, rates decline, and excess returns compress. Heritage is already seeing rate-decrease filings approved.

## Moat & Durability Classification

**Moat Strength: Weak.** Heritage has no proprietary advantage. Its underwriting results are driven by market-wide regulatory tailwinds and weather luck. Any well-capitalized insurer can replicate its business model. Reinsurance access and multi-state licensing provide modest operational barriers but not economic moats.

**Earnings Durability: Low.** Current earnings reflect a cyclical peak driven by tort reform benefits, benign weather, and a recovering equity base. The 45% ROE will normalize materially as (1) book value grows, reducing the ROE denominator, (2) competitive entry pressures rates downward, and (3) catastrophe losses inevitably resume.

## The One Thing That Matters for Durability

**Florida tort reform durability.** Heritage's margin expansion from loss ratios in the high-50s to 38% is almost entirely attributable to the 2022-2023 legislative reforms reducing litigation abuse. If these reforms hold and are not reversed, Heritage and its peers can sustain reasonable (not extraordinary) underwriting margins. If reversed, the business reverts to its pre-2023 profile of persistent underwriting losses punctuated by capital raises. The tort reform question dominates all other factors -- more than hurricane risk, more than reinsurance costs, more than competitive dynamics.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**Bearish insight the bulls are ignoring:** The 45% ROE and >$2.00 quarterly EPS are creating a narrative of transformation, but the financial profile is structurally identical to every Florida P&C carrier right now. The entire industry posted its first collective profit since 2016 in 2024, and 2025 was even better across the board. Heritage is not outperforming peers through proprietary underwriting skill -- it is riding a market-wide regulatory and weather cycle. The 17 new entrants flooding into Florida are the leading indicator that excess returns will be competed away. The correct mental model is not "Heritage has transformed" but rather "Florida P&C is at a cyclical peak, and Heritage happens to be a participant." The speed at which rate-decrease filings are accumulating (73 decrease filings in late 2025) confirms competitive pressure is already building.
