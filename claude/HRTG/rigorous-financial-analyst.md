# Heritage Insurance Holdings (HRTG) -- Financial Analysis

**Price: $26.22 | Market Cap: ~$748M | Shares: ~28.5M diluted | Date: 2026-02-24**

## Business Financial Profile

Florida-focused personal and commercial residential property insurer, now diversified with 74% of total insured value outside Florida. Capital-light but catastrophe-exposed. Revenue is recurring (annual policy renewals) but earnings are inherently volatile due to hurricane/catastrophe risk. High operating leverage: a benign loss year produces outsized earnings; a bad year can wipe out multiple quarters of profit. In-force premium has grown from ~$1.1B to $1.44B (per Q3 2025 release).

## Normalized Earnings Reconciliation

The central question: what does HRTG earn in a "normal" catastrophe year?

**GAAP to Normalized Bridge (annual basis, estimated):**

| Item | Amount |
|------|--------|
| FY 2025 net income (est.) | >$189M |
| Add back: this was a light cat year; normalize cat losses upward | (~$55M) after-tax |
| Subtract: FY 2024 was heavy; actual cat losses ~$105M pretax (~$83M after-tax) vs. normalized ~$55M | -- |
| SBC adjustment (est. ~$8-10M/yr based on restricted stock grants to ~30.6K shares outstanding) | (~$8M) |
| **Normalized mid-cycle after-tax earnings** | **~$125-135M** |
| **Normalized EPS** | **~$4.10-$4.40** |

Key assumptions: (1) Normalized annual pretax catastrophe losses of ~$70M, roughly the average of the heavy 2024 ($105M) and light 2025 (~$32M). This produces ~$55M after-tax cat load. (2) SBC estimated at ~$8M annually. (3) Net premiums earned ~$790M at current scale. (4) Non-cat combined ratio of ~72-75% (consistent with Q2-Q3 2025 benign quarters).

The Street's 2026 EPS consensus of ~$4.15 (range $4.00-$4.30 per StockAnalysis.com) aligns closely with this normalized estimate, suggesting consensus has largely priced in normalization.

## Balance Sheet and Financial Risk

As of Q3 2025 (per 10-Q filed 11/07/2025):
- **Stockholders' equity:** $437.3M; book value per share: $14.15
- **Cash and equivalents:** $560.4M
- **Debt:** ~$102M (debt/equity: 0.23x)
- **Net cash position:** ~$459M

The balance sheet is strong. Net cash of $459M against a $748M market cap means roughly 61% of the enterprise value is backed by cash. Debt is modest and manageable at 0.23x equity.

Reinsurance tower provides coverage up to $2.48B (2025-2026 program) with first-event retentions of ~$50M for Southeast and Hawaii, ~$39M for Northeast. This is the key risk management tool.

## Capital Allocation

- $25M buyback authorized through 2026 (modest at ~3.3% of market cap)
- 12.2M treasury shares already held, indicating historical buyback activity
- At current earnings power, the company generates substantial excess capital; capital return is below potential

## Red Flags Checklist

- [x] Persistent negative free cash flow: **No** -- cash generation is strong
- [ ] Rising SBC as % of revenue: SBC appears modest relative to revenue (~1%); not a concern
- [x] Margin expansion driven by accounting choices: **No** -- driven by absence of catastrophes
- [x] Weak cash conversion: **No** -- insurance cash flows track earnings well
- [ ] Balance sheet fragility: **No** -- net cash, low leverage
- [ ] Aggressive revenue recognition: Not evident in standard insurance accounting
- [ ] Related party transactions: Not flagged in available disclosures

**Primary risk flag:** Earnings are catastrophe-dependent. A single major Florida hurricane could produce $50-100M+ in pretax losses in a single quarter, as demonstrated by Q3-Q4 2024. The stock's apparent cheapness must be weighed against this binary risk.

## Valuation Framework

At $26.22 per share:
- **P/E on FY 2025 (peak/light cat year):** ~4.2x -- artificially cheap
- **P/E on normalized earnings ($4.25 mid-point):** ~6.2x
- **Price/Book (Q3 2025 BV $14.15):** 1.85x
- **Price/Book (est. YE 2025 BV ~$16+):** ~1.6x

Comparable Florida-focused P&C insurers (HCI Group, Federated National, Universal Insurance) typically trade at 5-8x normalized earnings. HRTG at 6.2x normalized is mid-range for the peer group. The 1.6-1.85x book value is reasonable for a company generating 25-30% normalized ROE.

**What must be true for $26 to be fair value:** Normalized earnings of ~$4.25/share at a 6x multiple = $25.50. The stock is roughly fairly valued if you believe mid-cycle earnings power is ~$4.15-$4.40 and a 6x multiple is appropriate for a catastrophe-exposed insurer.

**What breaks:** A return to 2024-style hurricane activity compresses earnings to ~$2/share. At 6x that is $12. Conversely, continued benign weather plus premium growth could push normalized power toward $5+/share, implying $30+.

## Key Financial Drivers

1. **Catastrophe losses** -- This is the single dominant variable. The difference between FY 2024 ($2.01 EPS) and FY 2025 (>$6.17 EPS) is almost entirely explained by cat losses. Everything else is secondary.

2. **In-force premium growth** -- Growth from $1.1B to $1.44B expands the denominator against which losses are measured. Continued growth at current reinsurance terms improves normalized earnings power.

3. **Reinsurance cost and structure** -- The ceded premium ratio determines how much top-line translates to net premiums. Changes in reinsurance pricing directly impact margins.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

The market appears to understand the cat-year normalization (consensus 2026 EPS of ~$4.15 aligns with my normalized estimate). However, one potentially overlooked factor: **the balance sheet has transformed.** Book value has nearly doubled from ~$9.50 at YE 2024 to an estimated ~$16+ at YE 2025. This $437M+ equity base, combined with a $459M net cash position and only $102M in debt, means HRTG now has substantial capacity to either (a) retain more risk and reduce reinsurance costs, improving margins, or (b) accelerate premium growth without requiring external capital. The company's capital return policy ($25M buyback) is conspicuously undersized relative to this capital generation. If management shifts toward more aggressive buybacks or special dividends, the equity story changes materially.

## Conclusion

HRTG is roughly fairly valued at $26.22 on normalized mid-cycle earnings of ~$4.15-$4.40/share at a 6x multiple appropriate for catastrophe-exposed property insurance. The stock is not cheap on normalized numbers, despite appearing optically cheap on trailing 2025 earnings. The balance sheet is strong and improving. The key variant perception is whether the growing capital base enables structural earnings improvement beyond what consensus models, or whether it simply represents buffer capital for the next inevitable catastrophe season.
