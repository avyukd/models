# Florida Residential Property Insurance: Industry Structure & Cycle Analysis

**Date:** 2026-02-24 | **Context:** HRTG at $26.22 ($748M market cap), pre-announcing Q4 2025 net income >$60M, FY2025 ROE >45%

---

## Industry Economics: What Drives Profitability

Florida residential property insurance profitability is governed by three variables in order of importance: (1) catastrophe losses, (2) litigation/claims inflation, and (3) reinsurance costs. This is NOT a normal insurance market -- Florida domestic carriers posted **collective underwriting losses for seven consecutive years** (2017-2023), with cumulative losses in the billions. Eleven carriers were declared insolvent between 2017-2022, six in 2022 alone. Between 2017-2021, 71% of insurer payouts went to attorney fees and public adjusters; only 8% reached claimants (per III data).

The industry's "normal" state for most of the past decade was **structural unprofitability**. What we are witnessing now is not a return to normal -- it is the first period of genuine profitability in nearly a decade, driven by a regulatory regime change.

## The Regulatory Regime Change: SB 2-A (December 2022)

SB 2-A fundamentally altered Florida property insurance economics by:

- **Eliminating one-way attorney fees** in property insurance litigation
- **Banning assignment of benefits (AOB)** for new residential policies
- **Reducing claims litigation frequency** materially

This is not a cyclical tailwind. It is a structural shift in the loss ratio. The litigation machine that consumed 71 cents of every dollar paid out has been dismantled. Claims lawsuits have declined significantly since enactment. This reform is the single most important variable in the industry's current profitability.

## Current Cycle Position: Late-Stage Hard Market, Approaching Inflection

**The hard market is real but the competitive response is already underway.** Evidence:

- **17 new insurers** have entered Florida since reforms passed
- Citizens' policy count collapsed from 936,000 to ~385,000 in 2025 via depopulation -- the lowest level ever
- **73 rate decrease filings** and 94 zero-increase filings received by FL OIR as of late November 2025
- State Farm filed for 10% rate reduction; Citizens approved 8.7% average cut for 2026
- Heritage itself has been approved for rate decreases
- Reinsurance costs are softening heading into 2026 renewals

Heritage's own numbers tell the story of cycle peak: Q3 2025 net combined ratio of **72.9%** (loss ratio 38.3%, expense ratio 34.6%), net income of $50.4M in a single quarter, and book value per share up 49% year-to-date. A 72.9% combined ratio in cat-exposed property insurance is extraordinary. The ceded premium ratio of 46.1% means Heritage is paying nearly half its gross premiums for reinsurance and still posting these results -- rates are far above actuarial need.

## Is 45% ROE Sustainable? No.

A 45% ROE in property insurance is unambiguously peak-cycle. The mechanism for mean reversion is already in motion:

1. **Rate competition:** 17 new entrants plus incumbents filing rate decreases. Citizens cutting rates removes the price umbrella that allowed private carriers to charge elevated premiums.
2. **Reinsurance softening:** Lower cat reinsurance costs benefit carriers but also reduce barriers that kept weaker competitors out.
3. **Citizens depopulation exhaustion:** The 546,000-policy transfer in 2025 was a one-time bolus of premium growth. Citizens is down to ~385,000 policies -- the depopulation tailwind is largely spent.
4. **Hurricane reversion:** 2024 had Milton but 2025 was quiet. The benign loss environment is not structural. Florida's catastrophe exposure is permanent and increasing with coastal development and climate trends.

**Mid-cycle economics for Florida property insurance post-SB 2-A** likely look like: combined ratios of 90-98%, ROEs of 10-15%, modest premium growth, and periodic hurricane-driven loss years. This is far better than the pre-reform environment but nowhere near current results.

## What Makes This Cycle Different (And What Doesn't)

**Structurally different:** SB 2-A genuinely changed the litigation cost structure. The pre-reform loss ratios included 20-30 points of litigation friction that are not coming back absent legislative reversal. This means the *floor* on profitability is higher than the 2017-2022 period.

**Not different:** Catastrophe risk, competitive dynamics, and capital cycle behavior. When carriers post 45% ROEs, capital floods in. It already is -- 17 new entrants. Rate competition intensifies. Combined ratios normalize. This cycle is playing out on a compressed timeline because the regulatory catalyst was discrete and well-understood.

## The One Industry Factor That Matters

**Rate adequacy versus competitive erosion speed.** The question is not whether margins compress -- they will. The question is how fast. If rate decreases remain orderly (5-10% annually) while loss trends stay benign, the glide path to mid-cycle could take 3-4 years and carriers earn very attractive returns during the transition. If a price war develops among 17 new entrants hungry for premium, or if a major hurricane resets loss expectations, the compression accelerates dramatically.

Monitor: Florida OIR rate filing data (frequency and magnitude of decreases), new company formation/licensing, and the 6/1 reinsurance renewal pricing.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**Consensus view:** Florida property insurance is fixed, reforms are working, and profitability is sustainable at elevated levels.

**What consensus gets wrong:** The litigation reform created a *one-time* step-function improvement that is being confused with a permanently higher earning power. The market is pricing carriers as though 45% ROE is the new mid-cycle. It is not. The reform eliminated the abnormal drag but did not eliminate the competitive dynamics that govern all insurance markets. Capital is already responding -- 17 new entrants and widespread rate decreases signal the hard market is cresting. The structural improvement is real (mid-cycle ROEs of 10-15% versus pre-reform losses), but current profitability is 3-4x a sustainable level.

**This is bearish for current valuations** if the market is capitalizing peak earnings. Heritage at $26.22 on ~$14.15 book (1.85x P/B) looks reasonable only if you believe current earnings power persists. If mid-cycle EPS is $3-4 (versus the $7+ run-rate implied by Q4), the stock is pricing in continued peak performance.
