# Oxford Bank Corporation (OXBC) - Financial Analysis

## Executive Summary

Oxford Bank Corporation is a 140-year-old community bank headquartered in Oxford, Michigan, serving Oakland County and surrounding areas through seven full-service branches. The bank trades on the OTC market with a market cap of approximately $89 million at recent prices of ~$39/share. OXBC has a differentiated business model combining traditional community banking with a national commercial finance subsidiary (Oxford Commercial Finance, or OCF) that provides factoring and asset-based lending.

**Variant Perception**: The market appears to treat OXBC as a generic small community bank trading at a discount to book value. What the market may underappreciate is: (1) the exceptional deposit franchise quality evidenced by a 5.23% NIM vs. industry average of ~3.0-3.5%, (2) high insider ownership (25%+ concentrated among two aligned shareholders), and (3) the potential value of OCF as a scalable higher-margin business, albeit with elevated credit risk.

---

## Business Financial Profile

**Business Model Classification:**
- **Capital Intensity**: Moderate (banking is inherently asset-heavy)
- **Cost Structure**: Primarily fixed (branches, personnel) with some variable components in OCF
- **Operating Leverage**: High - incremental deposits/loans generate strong incremental margin
- **Cyclicality**: Moderate - bank earnings correlate with interest rates and regional economy
- **Revenue Quality**: Largely recurring (interest income) with some transactional (OCF factoring fees)

**Geographic Footprint**: Seven branches in Oakland County, Michigan (Clarkston, Davison, Dryden, Lake Orion, Oakland Township, Ortonville, Oxford). Additional customer experience centers with ITM-only service in Ann Arbor, Macomb, and Rochester Hills. OCF operates nationally from offices in Phoenix and Michigan.

**Business Segments**:
1. **Traditional Community Banking**: Commercial and consumer deposits, commercial real estate lending, business loans, residential mortgages
2. **Oxford Commercial Finance (OCF)**: National factoring, asset-based lending, equipment leasing (launched 2022, acquired FSW Funding assets in April 2022)

**Critical Note**: The large jump in Q4 2025 deposits ($170M increase) is explicitly temporary. Per management: "A large temporary deposit relationship materially increased cash and deposits and may reverse in H1 2026." This is related to a client M&A transaction.

**Normalized deposit base** (excluding temporary): ~$780-800M

### Deposit Quality Assessment

**Strengths:**
- CEO Lamb: "We continue to be pleased with the reliable performance of our loyal deposit base, and the strength of our client relationships. This is the foundation of our low-cost funding mix."
- Q1 2024: "No current exposure to borrowings or wholesale funding" - demonstrating minimal reliance on hot money
- Core deposits described as "sticky" and "resilient"
- Management has strategically moved some large commercial client deposits to Oxford Wealth Management to reduce deposit concentration risk

**Wholesale Funding Usage:**
- Limited wholesale funding utilized in Q2 2024 due to large depositor fluctuations
- Bank subsequently reduced wholesale funding as deposit activity improved
- By year-end 2024, management noted "facilitating further decrease in the Bank's amount of wholesale funding"
- Q2 2025 saw modest increase in wholesale funding ($12M) due to loan growth outpacing deposit growth

**Verdict**: The deposit franchise is genuinely valuable. A 1.31% cost of funds in this rate environment indicates high-quality, sticky retail and commercial relationships. The 140-year operating history in Oakland County has created a durable franchise.

## Key Financial Drivers

**The 1-3 metrics that actually drive this stock:**

1. **Net Interest Margin (NIM)**: At 5.23%, OXBC's NIM is the crown jewel. If NIM compresses materially (say, below 4.5%), the earnings power of this franchise declines significantly. Conversely, if NIM holds above 5% through a rate cycle, the stock deserves a premium valuation.

2. **Credit Quality (NPAs, Charge-offs)**: The 2025 uptick in credit issues, particularly in OCF and commercial real estate, is the key risk. If charge-offs accelerate or reserves need to be materially increased, earnings power declines and the stock trades to a deeper discount.

3. **OCF Scalability**: Oxford Commercial Finance is the growth engine and the wild card. If OCF successfully scales nationally while maintaining acceptable credit quality, it could drive above-average earnings growth. If credit losses in OCF mount, it becomes a drag.

**What causes each to inflect:**
- NIM: Fed rate moves, deposit pricing competition, loan repricing
- Credit: Michigan economy, CRE market, performance of OCF borrowers
- OCF: Sales pipeline, credit discipline, operating leverage

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**Non-Consensus Insight**: The market likely underappreciates the durability and value of OXBC's deposit franchise.

A 5.23% NIM in today's competitive environment is extraordinary. Most banks with similar profiles run NIMs of 3.0-3.5%. The 150-200 bps advantage translates to roughly $10-15M of incremental annual pre-tax income on the current asset base vs. an average bank.

This advantage stems from:
1. 140-year operating history creating entrenched customer relationships
2. Rural/suburban Michigan geography with less competitive deposit pricing
3. Commercial relationships that value service over rate
4. Minimal reliance on wholesale funding

**Why this matters**: If OXBC's deposit franchise is genuinely durable (and the evidence suggests it is), then the bank should trade at least at book value, not a discount. The ~0.89x P/TBV implies the market either doesn't believe the deposit franchise is sustainable or is penalizing the stock for illiquidity/size.

**Additional non-obvious observation**: The large temporary deposit in Q4 2025 (~$170M from a client M&A transaction) will reverse in H1 2026. This will cause reported assets and deposits to decline significantly, which could create negative headlines ("deposits down 18%!") even though it's expected and normal. This could create a buying opportunity if the stock reacts negatively to optical deposit outflows.

## Rate Environment Sensitivity

**Asset Sensitivity**: Bank described as "asset-sensitive" (management noted NIM headwinds in declining rate environment).

**Implications:**
- **Rising rates**: Positive for earnings - loans reprice faster than deposits
- **Flat rates**: Neutral - current NIM should hold
- **Falling rates**: Modest negative - some NIM compression, but low-cost deposit base provides cushion

The deposit franchise provides a floor on NIM even in declining rate scenarios. Cost of funds rose only from 0.69% (2023) to 1.31% (2025) despite significant Fed hikes, demonstrating deposit stability.

## Conclusion

Oxford Bank Corporation is a high-quality community bank with an exceptional deposit franchise, evidenced by industry-leading net interest margins. The stock trades at ~0.89x tangible book value and ~8.8x earnings, a modest discount to intrinsic value for a bank of this quality.

**Bull Case (P/TBV 1.2x+, ~$52/share):**
- NIM holds above 5%
- OCF scales profitably
- Credit losses normalize
- Market recognizes deposit franchise value

**Base Case (P/TBV 1.0x, ~$44/share):**
- NIM compresses modestly to ~4.5-5.0%
- OCF breaks even or modestly profitable
- Credit losses remain elevated but manageable
- Stock trades to book value

**Bear Case (P/TBV 0.75x, ~$33/share):**
- Significant credit deterioration in OCF or CRE
- NIM compresses below 4.5%
- Larger-than-expected deposit outflows
- Michigan economic weakness

**Final Assessment**: At current prices, OXBC offers reasonable value with meaningful upside if the deposit franchise and OCF execution are validated. The primary risks are credit quality (particularly OCF) and the H1 2026 optical impact of temporary deposit reversal. High insider ownership provides alignment. This is a "solid but modestly undervalued" situation, not a screaming bargain but not expensive either.
