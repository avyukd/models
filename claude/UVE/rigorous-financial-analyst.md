# Universal Insurance Holdings (UVE) -- Financial Analysis

## Business Financial Profile

Florida-concentrated residential property insurer. ~90% Florida exposure. High operating leverage to catastrophe losses -- a single hurricane season determines the year. Revenue is recurring (policy renewals) but earnings are deeply cyclical. Capital-light in good years, capital-destructive in bad ones. Direct premiums written of $2.14B (FY2025) with ~33% ceded to reinsurers, leaving ~$1.44B net premiums earned.

## Income Statement: The 2025 Breakout in Context

| Metric | FY2024 | FY2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direct Premiums Written | $2.07B | $2.14B | +3.4% |
| Net Premiums Earned | ~$1.37B | $1.44B | ~+5% |
| Total Revenue | ~$1.52B | $1.60B | +5.5% |
| Net Income | $58.9M | $182.9M | +210% |
| Diluted EPS | ~$2.08 | ~$6.30 (est.) | +203% |
| Net Combined Ratio | 104.1% | 94.1% | -10.0 pts |
| Net Investment Income | ~$60M | $70.6M | +18% |

Q4 2025 was extraordinary: 87.5% combined ratio, $66.6M net income, 50.9% annualized ROCE. But Q4 is outside hurricane season. The critical question is whether the full-year 94.1% combined ratio represents the new normal or a benign-cat anomaly.

FY2024 included three landfalling hurricanes (Debby, Helene, Milton). Milton alone was a $45M net retention event with $66M excess covered by reinsurers. FY2025 had no comparable events. The 10-point combined ratio improvement is substantially explained by weather, not structural underwriting gains.

## Reinsurance Structure

The ceded premium ratio has been running 31-33% of direct premiums, meaning UVE pays roughly $680-700M annually to reinsurers. The 2025-2026 program provides a $2.526B catastrophe tower with $45M first-event retention. 90% of the 2026 tower is already placed, including $352M of multi-year coverage.

This is the load-bearing structure of the business. UVE retains only $45M of first-event cat losses, which is manageable relative to $551M equity. But multi-event seasons (like 2024) erode results fast through retention stacking and reinstatement premiums. Reinsurance cost as a percentage of direct earned premium has been roughly stable despite three 2024 storms -- a positive signal on reinsurance market conditions.

## Reserving: The Key Risk

Management claims "more than adequate aggregate reserves." Q3 2025 showed $3.9M of adverse prior-year development from prior catastrophes; Q2 2025 had negligible development. This is modest relative to premium volume.

However, Florida property reserving has historically been treacherous. The 2019 combined ratio spiked to 103.9% on reserve strengthening and litigation costs. Assignment of Benefits (AOB) abuse inflated losses for years. The 2022-2023 Florida legislative reforms (SB 2-A, SB 7052) eliminated one-way attorney fees and restricted AOB, which should structurally reduce loss adjustment expenses. This is a real tailwind, but its magnitude is still being proven out. I would want 2-3 more accident years of data before concluding reserves are truly adequate at current loss picks.

## Balance Sheet and Capital

- Stockholders' equity: $551M
- Book value per share: $19.67 (+48.1% YoY from $13.28)
- Total assets: $2.84B
- Shares outstanding: 28.0M

The book value rebuild from $13.28 to $19.67 in one year is impressive but reflects the earnings windfall from a benign cat year. Stock trades at ~1.5x book ($30.23/$19.67).

## Capital Return

- Quarterly dividend: $0.16/share = $0.64 annualized = ~$18M/year
- New $20M buyback authorization; $6.9M repurchased in Q4 2025
- Total capital return capacity: ~$25-30M annually at current pace
- Payout ratio on FY2025 earnings: ~13% (dividends only), ~15% including buybacks

Capital return is modest relative to earnings. At $183M net income, UVE could return far more. The conservatism is appropriate given cat exposure -- one bad hurricane season can consume multiple years of earnings.

## Normalized Earnings and Valuation

**Historical combined ratio context:**
- FY2019: 103.9% (reserve strengthening + AOB litigation)
- FY2020: ~113% (Hurricane Isaias + Sally)
- FY2021: ~105% (improved but still elevated)
- FY2022: ~100-101%
- FY2023: ~104%
- FY2024: 104.1% (three hurricanes)
- FY2025: 94.1% (benign cat year + tort reform benefits)

The pre-reform average (2019-2023) was roughly 105%. FY2025 at 94.1% benefits from both no major cats AND early tort reform benefits. A reasonable mid-cycle estimate post-reform, incorporating a normal cat load, is 97-100%.

**Normalized earnings build:**
- Net premiums earned: $1.44B (current run rate)
- Mid-cycle combined ratio: 98% (assumption)
- Underwriting income: $1.44B x (1 - 0.98) = $28.8M
- Net investment income: $70M (current run rate, reasonable given rate environment)
- Other revenue: ~$20M (fee income, commission income)
- Pre-tax income: ~$119M
- Tax at 24%: ~$28M
- Normalized net income: ~$90M
- Per share (28M shares): ~$3.20

At 98% combined ratio, normalized EPS is roughly $3.20. At a more optimistic 96% (giving full credit to tort reform), normalized EPS is ~$4.20.

**Valuation:**
- Current price: ~$30.23
- On FY2025 reported EPS (~$6.30): ~4.8x -- optically cheap but peak-cycle
- On normalized $3.20 EPS (98% CR): ~9.4x
- On optimistic normalized $4.20 EPS (96% CR): ~7.2x
- Price/book: 1.54x

Florida property insurers typically trade at 5-8x normalized earnings given cat risk. HCI trades at ~3x current earnings with a ~70% normalized combined ratio (structurally superior). Heritage (HRTG) trades at ~5.5x.

At $30, UVE is priced for a permanently improved combined ratio in the 95-96% range. If you believe tort reform durably removes 5-7 points from the historical combined ratio, the stock is reasonably valued to modestly cheap at 7-9x normalized. If the mid-cycle CR reverts closer to 100%, the stock is fairly to slightly overvalued.

## Red Flags Checklist

- [ ] Persistent negative FCF: No -- FY2025 was strongly cash generative
- [ ] Rising SBC: Not a material issue for this business
- [ ] Margin expansion from accounting: No evidence; improvement driven by lower losses
- [ ] Weak cash conversion: Insurance accounting generally ties closely to cash; no red flags
- [ ] Balance sheet fragility: Manageable given reinsurance tower; $45M retention vs $551M equity
- [x] Aggressive revenue recognition: Not applicable, but reserve adequacy is the insurance equivalent -- still unproven post-reform
- [ ] Related party transactions: No flags identified

## Key Financial Drivers

1. **Hurricane season severity** -- This is a binary earnings driver. A single major Florida landfall can swing results by $50-100M+ net. No amount of underwriting skill eliminates this.

2. **Net combined ratio trajectory post-tort-reform** -- The sustainability of the 94-96% combined ratio vs the historical 104% is the entire investment debate. Every 1 point of combined ratio = ~$14M pre-tax.

3. **Reinsurance cost and availability** -- UVE cedes ~33% of premiums. Any hardening in reinsurance markets directly compresses net margins.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

The market may be underappreciating the **asymmetric downside in a multi-event cat year under the new reinsurance structure**. UVE's $45M first-event retention is manageable, but a repeat of 2024 (three storms) means $45M x 3 = $135M in retentions before considering reinstatement premiums and development. Against $551M of equity, that is a 25% hit before any reserve development. The 2025 results look transformational, but the cat risk has not changed -- only the litigation tail has improved. The stock at 1.5x book is pricing in durably improved profitability that remains one bad hurricane season from reversal.
