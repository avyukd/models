## 5. The Setup

UVE is the largest private residential property insurer in Florida (~10% share, ~567K policies). The business model is straightforward: collect premiums, cede ~33% to reinsurers, retain the first $45M of any cat event, and earn the spread between net premiums and losses.

The structural backdrop has genuinely improved. Florida's 2022-2023 tort reforms eliminated one-way attorney fees and restricted assignment of benefits, reducing litigation filings 36% and cutting defense costs by 5-8 points of combined ratio. This is real and durable.

But the current results also benefit from two consecutive years with no major Florida hurricane landfall -- an unusually favorable stretch. The industry ran a cumulative zero underwriting profit from 2016-2022. The historical pattern in Florida is clear: reform leads to new entrants, benign years produce strong earnings, competition erodes pricing, then the next major storm exposes the fragility. We are in the "strong earnings attract competition" phase of that cycle.

## 8. Risks and Kill Switches

**What invalidates the short thesis:**
- Multiple consecutive benign hurricane seasons (possible but not the base case over a multi-year horizon)
- UVE successfully diversifies out of Florida faster than expected (currently only 31% non-Florida, growing 18% but off a small base)
- Tort reform proves even more beneficial than the 5-8 points currently assumed, pushing sustainable CR below 93%

**What causes permanent capital loss (for longs):**
- Category 4+ hurricane in a major Florida metro. $45M retention is manageable for one event, but retention stacking, reinstatement premiums, and reserve development can consume 25-50% of equity
- Reinsurance market dislocation post-event makes the business temporarily uneconomic (as in 2022-2023)

**Kill switch for the short:** If the stock pulls back to $22-24 (7-8x mid-cycle EPS), the risk/reward reverses. Cover.
