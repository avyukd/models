# U.S. Community Banking Industry: Structure & Cycle Analysis

## Industry Definition and Scope

Community banks are generally defined as FDIC-insured institutions with less than $10 billion in assets. Security Midwest Bancorp falls within the sub-$500 million asset tier, which represents the smallest and most fragmented segment of the industry.

---

## What Fundamentally Drives Profitability in This Industry?

### Primary Profit Drivers

1. **Net Interest Margin (NIM)**: The spread between interest earned on loans and interest paid on deposits. This is THE dominant driver, typically accounting for 70-80% of community bank revenue.

2. **Credit Quality**: Loan loss provisions directly consume earnings. A single large credit deterioration can eliminate a full year's profits at a small bank.

3. **Operating Efficiency**: Measured by efficiency ratio (non-interest expense / revenue). Community banks typically run 60-75% efficiency ratios; the best operators achieve below 55%.

4. **Fee Income**: Non-interest income from deposit fees, loan origination fees, and specialty services. Historically 20-25% of revenue; increasingly important as NIM compresses.

### The Critical Economics

Community banking is fundamentally a **spread business with embedded operating leverage**. Fixed costs (branches, compliance, technology) create significant operating leverage. A 10 basis point improvement in NIM flows almost entirely to the bottom line for an efficiently run bank.

---

## What Determines Pricing Power at the Industry Level?

### Deposit Pricing Power: Limited and Declining

- **Local competition**: Deposits are sticky but increasingly mobile with digital banking
- **Rate environment dependency**: Community banks are price-takers on deposits in rising rate environments
- **Beta sensitivity**: Deposit betas (how much deposit rates rise relative to Fed funds) have increased structurally from ~30% historically to 50%+ in recent cycles

### Loan Pricing Power: Moderate but Relationship-Dependent

- **Relationship lending**: Community banks can command modest premiums for speed, flexibility, and relationship continuity
- **CRE concentration**: Most community banks are heavily concentrated in commercial real estate, where pricing is highly competitive
- **Credit union competition**: Tax-advantaged credit unions increasingly compete in consumer and small business segments

**Key Insight**: Pricing power at the industry level is WEAK. Community banks compete on service, speed, and relationships, not price. The industry is structurally unable to pass through cost increases to customers.

---

## Supply Dynamics: How Elastic is Supply?

### Supply is DECLINING Structurally

- **Bank consolidation**: The number of FDIC-insured institutions has declined from ~18,000 in 1985 to ~4,500 today (per FDIC data)
- **De novo activity**: New bank charters collapsed post-2008 crisis and never recovered. Only 5-15 new bank charters per year versus 100-200+ historically
- **Regulatory burden**: Basel III, stress testing, BSA/AML compliance create economies of scale that disadvantage smaller institutions

### Supply Response Time

- **Exit**: Slow. Banks fail or merge over years, not quarters. Regulatory interventions create extended zombie periods.
- **Entry**: Extremely slow. De novo banking requires 2-3 years for charter approval and regulatory approval.

**Implication**: Supply is structurally declining but the adjustment is glacial. Excess capacity can persist for extended periods.

## Capacity Utilization and Expansion Incentives

### What Triggers Expansion?

1. **Loan-to-deposit ratios**: When L/D exceeds 90-95%, banks become deposit-constrained and must raise capital or slow growth
2. **Capital ratios**: Banks with excess capital face ROE pressure and incentives to deploy into loans
3. **M&A opportunity**: Acquirers expand by purchasing smaller banks at book value or modest premiums

### Current Capacity State (2025-2026)

- **Excess liquidity**: Post-COVID deposit surge created excess liquidity industry-wide
- **Loan demand softening**: Higher rates have cooled loan demand, particularly CRE
- **Capital positions strong**: Most community banks are well-capitalized but underleveraged

**Assessment**: Industry is operating BELOW capacity with excess liquidity and moderate loan demand.

## Cyclicality Assessment

### Cyclicality: MODERATE to HIGH

Community banking is cyclical because:

1. **Credit losses are procyclical**: Loan losses spike in recessions, especially for CRE-concentrated portfolios
2. **NIM is rate-cycle sensitive**: Changes in the yield curve directly impact profitability
3. **Loan growth tracks GDP**: Commercial loan demand is highly correlated with economic activity

### Historical Cycle Characteristics

| Cycle Element | Typical Pattern |
|---------------|-----------------|
| Duration | 7-10 years (tied to credit cycle) |
| Peak-to-trough earnings decline | 40-60% |
| Recovery time | 3-5 years |
| Leading indicator | CRE vacancy rates, unemployment claims |

### What Causes Mean Reversion?

- **Credit normalization**: Provision expense rises from trough levels
- **Deposit competition**: Betas rise as rate cycle matures
- **Regulatory tightening**: Post-crisis regulatory response constrains activity
- **Competition**: Periods of strong profitability attract competition and compress spreads

## The One Industry Factor That Matters

**The yield curve shape and Fed funds rate trajectory will dominate community bank economics over the next 2-3 years.**

Community banks are essentially levered bets on the yield curve. A steep curve benefits asset-sensitive community banks; an inverted curve destroys NIM.

### What Would Cause Inflection:
- **Bullish**: Fed cuts faster than expected, curve steepens, deposit costs decline while loan yields remain elevated
- **Bearish**: Stagflation forces Fed to hold rates high while credit deteriorates; worst-case scenario

### How to Monitor:
- 2-10 year Treasury spread
- Fed funds futures curve
- Weekly H.8 deposit data from Federal Reserve

## What Consensus May Be Missing

### Non-Consensus Insight: The "CRE Crisis" in Community Banking is Overstated for Most Participants

**Consensus View**: Community banks face existential CRE risk, particularly from office exposure. Headlines focus on regional bank stress and CRE workouts.

**The Variant View**: The CRE stress is highly concentrated in:
1. Office properties in major metros (most community banks have minimal exposure)
2. Large regional banks with outsized CRE concentration
3. Banks that stretched for yield with low-cap-rate loans

**What's Being Missed**:
- Most community banks underwrite conservatively with 60-70% LTVs
- Community bank CRE is dominated by owner-occupied properties with different risk profiles
- Local market knowledge provides genuine information advantage in CRE
- The "crisis" narrative has already compressed community bank valuations, creating potential opportunity

**Implication**: MODERATELY BULLISH for well-underwritten community banks. The tarring of all community banks with the CRE brush may create mispricing for disciplined operators.

**Caveat**: This insight does NOT apply to banks with concentrated office exposure or those that stretched underwriting standards in 2020-2022.

## Primary Industry Risks

### What Breaks Community Banking Economics:

1. **Credit Cycle Turns**: A sharp recession drives NCOs to 1%+ and eliminates 2-3 years of earnings

2. **Prolonged Yield Curve Inversion**: Sustained negative spreads destroy the business model

3. **Regulatory Intensification**: Post-crisis regulatory waves always follow stress events

4. **Technology Disruption**: Digital banks and fintechs erode deposit franchises over time

5. **Concentration Risk**: Single-name or sector concentration can be existential for small banks
