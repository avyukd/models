# Security Midwest Bancorp, Inc. (SBMW) - Financial Analysis

**Date:** January 29, 2026
**Ticker:** SBMW (OTCQB)
**Analyst Framework:** Rigorous Buy-Side Financial Diligence

---

## Executive Summary

Security Midwest Bancorp, Inc. is the holding company for Security Bank, s.b., a 144-year-old community savings bank headquartered in Springfield, Illinois. The company completed a mutual-to-stock conversion IPO on July 31, 2025, raising approximately $8.9 million in gross proceeds at $10.00 per share. The stock trades on OTCQB under ticker SBMW.

**Key Finding:** This is a subscale community bank (~$220M assets) with a concentrated cannabis banking niche that generates meaningful fee income but faces structural profitability challenges. The company itself disclosed expectations of a net loss for FY2024 due to declining net interest income. The valuation appears expensive at 12.6x P/E versus 6.6x peer average, particularly given the profitability headwinds.

## Income Statement Analysis

### Historical Profitability (per S-1 filing, September 2024)

| Period | Net Income |
|--------|-----------|
| FY2023 (full year) | $684,000 |
| H1 2024 (6 months ended June 30) | $537,000 |
| FY2024 (expected) | **Net loss expected** |

**Source:** S-1 Registration Statement filed September 2024; management guidance from prospectus.

### Revenue Composition

**Net Interest Income:** Primary revenue driver, but declining due to:
- Rising deposit costs in the rate environment
- Limited loan growth capacity (only 1 loan officer as of June 2024)
- Fixed-rate loan portfolio facing duration mismatch

**Non-Interest Income:** CRB fee income represents over 50% of total non-interest income (per S-1). This is the bank's most differentiated and valuable revenue stream.

### Profitability Concerns

Management explicitly stated in the prospectus: *"Because of declines in our net interest income and reduced demand for our fixed-rate loans, we currently expect to incur a net loss for the year ending December 31, 2024."*

They further noted: *"We believe we will need to hire additional loan officers and grow our loan portfolio before we can return to sustained profitability, which will take time and increase our non-interest expense in the short term."*

**Assessment:** This is a turnaround story with execution risk. Profitability is not currently assured.

## Key Financial Drivers

**The 1-3 Metrics That Matter Most:**

1. **Net Interest Income Trajectory** - This is the core revenue driver. If NII continues declining, losses will persist regardless of CRB fee income. Management must grow the loan book to stabilize/grow NII.

2. **CRB Fee Income Sustainability** - Over 50% of non-interest income comes from cannabis banking fees. Any regulatory change (federal legalization creating bank competition, or conversely, state-level cannabis industry contraction) would materially impact this stream.

3. **Customer Concentration** - A single customer representing 13%+ of deposits is an existential risk. If that customer leaves or fails, it would trigger a liquidity crisis.

**What Would Cause Each to Inflect:**

- **NII Positive:** Successful loan officer hires, loan portfolio growth >10% annually, rate stability
- **NII Negative:** Continued rate pressure, inability to hire/retain talent, CRB loan losses
- **CRB Fees Positive:** Federal cannabis rescheduling without full banking access, continued state expansion
- **CRB Fees Negative:** Major bank entry into cannabis banking, customer concentration exodus
- **Concentration Risk:** Deposit diversification through geographic expansion or customer acquisition

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**Non-Consensus Insight:** The market may be underestimating the operational fragility of this institution. Having only **one loan officer** (as disclosed in the S-1) is not merely a growth constraint - it is an operational risk. Key-person dependency in the core lending function means:

1. Any departure creates immediate business continuity risk
2. Loan underwriting quality depends on a single individual
3. Growth requires hiring and training before any revenue benefit

This is atypical even for micro-cap thrift conversions. Most mutual-to-stock conversions have some baseline lending infrastructure. The candid disclosure that they "will need to hire additional loan officers...before we can return to sustained profitability" suggests this is not a minor issue.

**Additionally:** The CRB customer concentration (one customer = ~50% of CRB deposits = ~13% of total deposits) is a tail risk that typical screens would not catch. A liquidity event from this single customer relationship could create deposit flight exceeding the bank's liquid assets.

**Alternatively, if I found nothing non-consensus:** The above observations are present in the S-1 but may not be fully appreciated by investors focused on the cannabis banking theme or the superficial cheapness of P/TBV.

## Investment Conclusion

**Assessment:** Financially weak, with a differentiated but concentrated business model.

**Positives:**
- Well-capitalized post-IPO (~10%+ equity/assets)
- Differentiated CRB banking niche with fee income diversification
- Long operating history (144 years)
- Trading below tangible book value

**Negatives:**
- Expects FY2024 net loss
- Severe customer concentration risk
- Subscale with limited operational capacity (1 loan officer)
- No analyst coverage; illiquid OTCQB stock
- Execution-dependent turnaround

**Valuation Summary:** At ~0.39x tangible book, the stock prices in significant uncertainty. Whether this is cheap or a value trap depends entirely on:
1. Can they hire loan officers and grow profitably?
2. Will the concentrated CRB customer remain stable?
3. Can management execute a turnaround without burning through the IPO proceeds?

**Bottom Line:** This is a speculative micro-cap thrift conversion with a cannabis banking angle. The numbers do not support investment at current prices without conviction in operational execution. The P/E of 12.6x is misleading given expected losses; P/TBV of 0.39x is cheap but justified by earnings uncertainty.
