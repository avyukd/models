## 2. Key Drivers (The 1-3 Things That Matter)

**1. Federal Cannabis Policy** - This is THE variable. SBMW's competitive position exists solely because federal cannabis illegality deters large banks. If SAFER Banking passes or cannabis is rescheduled, the CRB advantage evaporates within 12-24 months. This is not a business quality bet; it is a policy bet.

**2. Customer Concentration** - One unidentified customer represents 50%+ of CRB deposits (~13% of total deposits). This is binary risk. Loss of this single relationship would trigger a liquidity event and materially impair fee income.

**3. Operational Capacity** - The bank has ONE loan officer. This is not merely a growth constraint; it is operational fragility. Any departure creates immediate business continuity risk. Management has stated they cannot return to sustained profitability until they hire additional loan officers.

---

## 3. Thesis Summary

This is a subscale (~$220M asset) community bank that has carved out a differentiated niche in cannabis banking. The CRB business generates meaningful fee income (>50% of non-interest income) and provides low-cost deposits. However:

- The bank expects a net loss for FY2024
- The CRB advantage is temporary and policy-dependent
- Extreme customer concentration creates binary risk
- The bank has no moat in its traditional community banking business
- Thrift conversion structure favors insider enrichment
- No analyst coverage, illiquid OTCQB stock, no post-IPO financials yet

**The valuation (0.39x P/TBV) looks cheap, but cheap for a reason.** This is a turnaround story with execution risk, regulatory risk, and concentration risk. The margin of safety is illusory without conviction on multiple unverifiable assumptions.

## 5. The Setup

**Business:** Security Bank is a 144-year-old community savings bank in Springfield, IL. In 2018, it entered cannabis banking - providing deposit accounts, cash management, and now lending to licensed cannabis operators in IL, MI, and OH. This differentiated strategy generates >50% of non-interest income but concentrates risk.

**Why IPO Now:** The mutual-to-stock conversion raised ~$8.9M at $10/share. Stated uses: hire loan officers, support growth, general corporate purposes. The timing - when cannabis banking premiums may be peaking and SAFER Banking has momentum - raises questions about insider motivations.

**Industry Position:** Community banking is late-cycle with NIM pressure and CRE concerns. Cannabis banking is a niche with ~815 institutions participating. Competition is intensifying (Dart Bank grew from $200M to $1.3B). SBMW is subscale in both its traditional and cannabis banking operations.

## 8. Risks & Kill Switches

**What Invalidates the Thesis:**

1. **SAFER Banking passage** - Immediately commoditizes CRB banking; large banks enter within 12-24 months
2. **Loss of concentrated customer** - One customer leaving = ~13% deposit flight + material fee income loss
3. **Strict federal enforcement** - Bank forced to exit CRB entirely
4. **Illinois cannabis industry stress** - SAR filings up 115% in IL in Q4 2024; operator failures could impair CRB loans and deposits

**What Causes Permanent Loss:**
- Customer concentration liquidity event exceeding liquid assets
- Regulatory action forcing CRB exit without replacement revenue
- Extended losses burning through IPO capital

**Kill Switch Triggers:**
- Any news suggesting concentrated customer is leaving or failing
- SAFER Banking passing either chamber
- Management adopting maximum dilutive stock plans + no insider buying

## 10. Final Assessment

This is a speculative micro-cap thrift conversion with cannabis banking optionality. The business has a differentiated niche, but the advantage is:
- Temporary (policy-dependent)
- Concentrated (one customer = binary risk)
- Fragile (one loan officer, expected losses)

The valuation discount reflects genuine uncertainty. I cannot identify a variant view that creates positive expected value. The bull case requires multiple assumptions I cannot verify. The bear case has timing uncertainty.

**Decision: Neutral (Too Hard)**

This belongs in the too hard pile. Not because I cannot understand it, but because:
1. The key variable (federal cannabis policy) is a political bet, not a business analysis
2. The customer concentration creates binary risk that cannot be sized or hedged
3. The execution requirements (hiring, growth, profitability) are assumption-dependent
4. The thrift conversion structure likely enriches insiders more than recent shareholders

A clean pass is the right decision. Revisit after:
- 2-3 quarters of post-conversion financials
- Evidence of insider alignment (buying, comp restraint)
- Clarity on SAFER Banking trajectory
- Deposit concentration reduction
