# Security Midwest Bancorp (SBMW) - Business Moat Analysis

**Note**: The user referenced "Singularity Bio Media Works Inc" but the ticker SBMW corresponds to **Security Midwest Bancorp, Inc.**, the holding company for Security Bank, s.b., a mutual savings bank based in Springfield, Illinois. This analysis covers Security Midwest Bancorp.

---

## 1. Business Model Summary (Economic, Not Narrative)

**How the company makes money:**
- Traditional community banking: mortgage lending (83.6% of loan portfolio secured by real estate), consumer loans, commercial loans, deposit-taking
- Cannabis-related business (CRB) banking: deposit accounts and cash management services for licensed cannabis operators in Illinois, Michigan, and Ohio
- CRB lending: initiated in December 2022, with $20.3 million in loans to cannabis customers as of filing date

**Who pays:**
- Traditional depositors and borrowers in Sangamon County, Illinois
- Licensed cannabis operators paying premium fees for banking access
- Mortgage borrowers (net interest margin)

**Cost structure:**
- High fixed costs: compliance infrastructure for cannabis banking (BSA/AML monitoring, enhanced KYC, ongoing due diligence)
- Branch operating costs (3 locations in Springfield)
- Regulatory examination costs (FDIC, Illinois Division of Banking)

**Where profits are generated:**
- CRB fee income: over 50% of total non-interest income during last 18 months (per S-1 filing)
- Net interest margin on traditional lending
- CRB deposits provide low-cost funding (operators have limited banking alternatives)

**Key financials (June 30, 2024, per S-1):**
- Total assets: $219.6 million
- Total deposits: $204.5 million
- Total equity: $13.9 million
- CRB deposits: $54.8 million (~27% of total deposits)
- CRB loans: $20.3 million (~19% of loan portfolio)
- Net income: $537,000 (H1 2024), $684,000 (FY 2023)

## 2. Source(s) of Advantage

### A. Regulatory Arbitrage (Primary Advantage)

**Mechanism**: Cannabis remains federally illegal (Schedule I), creating a legal gray zone that national banks avoid entirely. Community banks willing to navigate this regulatory complexity gain access to a customer segment desperate for banking services.

**How it shows up economically**: Security Bank charges premium fees ($1,000-$2,500+/month per account is industry standard) because CRB operators have severely limited alternatives. Fee income from CRB represents >50% of non-interest income.

**Why competitors struggle to replicate**: Large banks face reputational and regulatory risk disproportionate to the opportunity size. Their compliance infrastructure is not designed for this level of individual-account monitoring. Risk-averse management and boards at most community banks also self-select out.

**Durability assessment**: This is a **temporary, policy-dependent advantage**. It exists only because federal law creates friction. If SAFER Banking Act passes (currently bipartisan support, passed Senate Banking Committee 14-9), or if cannabis is rescheduled/descheduled, large banks will enter and commoditize the service immediately.

### B. Early Mover in CRB (Modest Advantage)

**Mechanism**: Security Bank started its CRB program in 2018, before Illinois legalized recreational cannabis (January 2020). This created compliance experience and operational know-how.

**Why this is weaker than it appears**: The "early mover" advantage in cannabis banking is eroding rapidly. Dart Bank in Michigan grew from $200M to $1.3B in assets since 2019, serving 1,100 cannabis accounts across 37 states. Competition is intensifying. Security Bank has self-limited to 35 CRB relationships with a deposit cap per customer, suggesting they recognize they cannot compete at scale.

### C. Switching Costs (Weak to Moderate)

**Mechanism**: CRB operators face friction switching banks due to:
- Limited alternatives (few banks accept cannabis money)
- Compliance documentation burden (re-underwriting with new bank)
- Operational disruption (cash-heavy businesses need reliable banking)

**Why this is fragile**: Switching costs exist only because alternatives are scarce. If more banks enter (via SAFER Banking or enforcement relaxation), switching costs collapse. This is not a structural moat; it is an artifact of scarcity.

### D. Local Market Position (Negligible Advantage)

**Mechanism**: Security Bank is the only mutual savings bank in Springfield, Illinois, operating for 144 years with three branches.

**Why this provides no durable advantage**: Community banking in a small market like Springfield is commoditized. No pricing power, no meaningful differentiation from competing banks and credit unions. The traditional banking business is subscale and structurally unattractive.

## 3. Competitive Landscape

### CRB Banking Competition

**Number of competitors**: ~815 financial institutions actively banking CRBs as of March 2024 (FinCEN data). 507 banks and 182 credit unions filed marijuana-related SARs in Q4 2024.

**Key named competitors:**
- **Dart Bank (Michigan)**: Dominant player, $1.3B in assets, 1,100 cannabis accounts in 37 states. Far larger scale and geographic reach.
- **Wright-Patt Credit Union (Ohio)**: Leading MRB market share in Ohio.
- **Herring Bank, TASI Bank, others**: Active in multi-state cannabis banking.

**Degree of differentiation**: Low. Cannabis banking services are commoditized (deposit accounts, cash management, compliance monitoring). The only differentiation is willingness to serve and geographic coverage.

**Evidence of price competition**: Fees are coming down industry-wide as more institutions enter. Early movers who charged premium fees face fee compression as competition intensifies.

**Ease of customer switching**: Currently moderate (scarcity creates friction). Will become easy if SAFER Banking passes.

**Historical entry/exit**: Net new banks entering cannabis banking. Credit unions reached record 182 in Q4 2024 (up from 172 in Q3). Dart Bank reports other banks exited in early 2024, allowing it to gain share.

### Traditional Banking Competition

**Springfield, IL market**: Multiple banks and credit unions compete for deposits and loans. Security Bank is subscale (~$220M assets) with no differentiated product or distribution advantage. Local community banking is a commodity business with no moat.

## 4. Customer Behavior & Revenue Quality

### Customer Concentration (Critical Risk)

**Fact**: One unidentified customer accounts for just over 50% of CRB deposits (per S-1). This is an extreme concentration.

**Implication**: Loss of this single customer would eliminate ~13.5% of total deposits and a disproportionate share of fee income. This creates binary risk.

### Revenue Visibility

**CRB fee income**: Recurring monthly fees provide some visibility, but contracts are not disclosed. CRB operators can leave if a competitor offers better terms or if their business fails (cannabis industry has high business failure rates due to price compression and regulatory burdens).

**Interest income**: Mortgage-heavy loan book tied to local real estate. Sensitive to local economic conditions and rate environment.

### Pricing Power vs. Discounting

**Current state**: Security Bank has pricing power over CRB customers due to scarcity of alternatives.

**Trajectory**: Pricing power is eroding. More banks are entering. Industry sources report fees are declining. Security Bank's pricing power exists only because of federal enforcement uncertainty, not because of any structural advantage.

### Locked-in vs. Lazy Customers

CRB customers are **scarcity-locked**, not structurally locked. They stay because alternatives are limited, not because switching is technically difficult or value-destroying. The moment more banks enter, these customers will comparison-shop.

## 5. Evidence of Moat in the Financials

### Margin Stability

**Limited data**: H1 2024 net income of $537,000 and FY 2023 net income of $684,000 suggest modest profitability. The bank is small and the CRB business is relatively new (lending started 2022).

### Ability to Raise Prices

**CRB segment**: Currently yes, but declining. Industry-wide fee compression is occurring.

**Traditional segment**: No pricing power. Community banking is commoditized.

### Resistance to Commoditization

**CRB segment**: Already commoditizing. The service is standardized (account, compliance, cash management). No proprietary technology or process.

**Traditional segment**: Already a commodity.

### Why Margins Are High (CRB)

Margins in CRB banking are high because:
1. Federal illegality deters most competitors
2. Compliance costs create a hurdle that filters out casual entrants
3. CRB operators have inelastic demand (they need banking to function)

**None of these are durable advantages**. All depend on continued federal illegality and enforcement uncertainty.

## 6. Fragility Analysis (How the Moat Breaks)

### Primary Fragility: Federal Policy Change

**What breaks it**: Passage of SAFER Banking Act or cannabis rescheduling/descheduling.

**Mechanism**: Large national banks (JPMorgan, Bank of America, regional banks) would immediately enter cannabis banking. They have superior scale, lower cost of capital, broader product suites, and national distribution. Security Bank's CRB advantage would evaporate within 12-24 months of policy change.

**Probability**: SAFER Banking Act passed Senate Banking Committee with bipartisan 14-9 vote in 2025. 32 state attorneys general support it. The trend is toward normalization.

### Secondary Fragility: Customer Concentration

**What breaks it**: Loss of the single customer representing 50%+ of CRB deposits.

**Mechanism**: Business failure of that customer, customer switching to competitor, or regulatory action against that customer would immediately cut deposits and fee income.

### Tertiary Fragility: Regulatory Enforcement

**What breaks it**: Federal government strictly enforces cannabis laws.

**Mechanism**: Security Bank would be forced to exit CRB banking entirely. Per S-1 risk factors: "strict enforcement of federal laws regarding cannabis would likely result in their inability to continue this line of business."

### Quaternary Fragility: Illinois Cannabis Market Stress

**What breaks it**: Illinois cannabis industry is experiencing stress. SAR filings in Illinois increased 115% in Q4 2024 vs prior year. Cannabis operators facing price compression, oversupply, and regulatory burden may fail at elevated rates, impairing Security Bank's CRB loan book and deposit base.

## 7. Moat & Durability Classification

**Moat Strength: Weak**

Security Bank's advantage in CRB banking is real but temporary. It exists because of a regulatory anomaly (federal illegality deterring competition), not because of any structural cost advantage, network effect, or customer lock-in. The moment policy changes, larger and better-capitalized competitors will enter and Security Bank becomes subscale with no differentiation.

The traditional community banking business has no moat.

**Earnings Durability: Low**

- Over 50% of non-interest income depends on CRB fees, which are policy-dependent
- Extreme customer concentration (1 customer = 50%+ of CRB deposits)
- CRB business is exposed to cannabis industry stress and potential federal policy change
- Traditional banking business is subscale and commoditized
- The bank is actively converting from mutual to stock ownership, suggesting insiders may be seeking liquidity ahead of potential changes

---

## The One Thing That Matters for Durability

**Federal cannabis policy is the single determinant of this company's competitive position.**

Everything else is secondary. The CRB business exists because federal illegality creates an artificial barrier that deters large banks. Security Bank has no technological advantage, no scale advantage, no network effect, and no structural switching costs. If SAFER Banking passes or cannabis is rescheduled, Security Bank's CRB advantage disappears entirely.

The traditional community banking business is subscale and provides no durability.

An investor in SBMW is making a bet that federal cannabis policy will remain in its current ambiguous state. This is a policy bet, not a business quality bet.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

### The Bearish Non-Consensus Insight

**What consensus believes**: Security Bank has carved out a valuable niche in cannabis banking and early-mover advantage provides durability.

**What consensus may miss**: The mutual-to-stock conversion timing is telling. Insiders are seeking liquidity (via stock offering) at a moment when SAFER Banking has unprecedented bipartisan support and the policy window for CRB banking premiums may be closing. The conversion values the bank at $9.5 million—a modest sum that may reflect insider awareness of fragility rather than confidence in durability.

Additionally, the 35-customer cap on CRB relationships suggests management recognizes they cannot compete at scale against banks like Dart Bank ($1.3B assets, 1,100 cannabis accounts). Security Bank is playing defense, not offense.

### The Bullish Counter-Consideration

If federal policy remains gridlocked (not impossible given congressional dysfunction), cannabis banking premiums could persist longer than expected. But this is a duration bet on policy paralysis, not a moat.
