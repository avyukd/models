## 2. Incentive Structure & Alignment

### What We Know

**Compensation Committee:** Board has established a Compensation Committee with a formal charter (adopted July 2025). CEO participates in meetings but does not serve as a member.

**ESOP:** The bank's Employee Stock Ownership Plan purchased 62,285 shares in the IPO (~7% of shares outstanding), representing $622,850 at $10/share. This is standard for thrift conversions and creates some alignment between employees and shareholders.

**Stock-Based Benefit Plans (Potential Dilution):** Per the S-1, if adopted within 12 months of conversion:
- Stock option plan: Up to 10% of shares sold (~88,978 shares at midpoint)
- Management Recognition Plan (MRP): Typically 4% of shares in similar conversions

**What Behavior This Compensation Structure Encourages:**
- ESOP creates employee alignment, though concentrated at management/director level in typical thrift conversions
- If stock option/MRP plans are adopted, management will have strong incentive to grow book value and pursue conversion appreciation (typical thrift conversion playbook)
- No disclosed performance metrics beyond standard community bank profitability

**Critical Gap:** No proxy statement (DEF 14A) is publicly available yet. Exact compensation figures for CEO and named executives are unknown. We cannot assess:
- Cash vs. equity mix
- Bonus triggers
- Severance/change-of-control provisions
- Historical compensation trends

### Who Benefits If Stock Underperforms

This is a thrift conversion. The standard playbook is:
1. Management/directors/employees buy stock in offering at $10
2. Stock options and MRP grants awarded post-conversion
3. Bank gets acquired 3-5 years later at premium to book

If stock underperforms, management still benefits from:
- Cash salary (level unknown)
- ESOP participation
- Potential MRP grants (free stock awards)
- Time-based vesting creates incentive to stay, not necessarily perform

**Incentive Alignment Assessment: INSUFFICIENT DATA / Likely Mixed**

The ESOP creates some alignment, but without proxy disclosure of actual insider purchases in the offering and cash/equity compensation mix, true alignment cannot be verified. Thrift conversion insiders typically accumulate significant stakes cheaply.

## 4. Capital Allocation Track Record

### Context: Fresh Conversion

Security Midwest Bancorp has no capital allocation track record as a public company. The relevant question is: **What does the pre-conversion bank's history tell us about management's capital allocation instincts?**

### a) Organic Reinvestment

**Cannabis Banking Strategy:** The most notable capital allocation decision is the 2018 launch of cannabis-related business (CRB) banking.

- CRB deposits: $54.8M (27% of total deposits at June 2024)
- CRB loans: 19% of outstanding loans
- Expanded from Illinois to Michigan and Ohio
- Began CRB lending in December 2022

**Assessment:** This is a differentiated strategy for a 144-year-old community bank. Cannabis banking is higher-risk, higher-fee business. The decision to commit 27% of deposits to a regulatory grey area suggests management is willing to take risk for growth. This is neither clearly good nor bad - it depends on execution and regulatory evolution.

**Loan Portfolio:** 83.6% real estate secured, concentrated in Sangamon County. Standard community bank lending - no evidence of aggressive expansion or reckless growth.

### b) M&A

**No M&A history disclosed.** The bank has operated as a single-market community bank for 144 years. This suggests conservatism rather than empire-building.

### c) Buybacks & Issuance

**Pre-conversion:** Not applicable (mutual ownership)

**Post-conversion outlook:** At current scale ($8.9M market cap, thin profitability), buybacks are unlikely. However, thrift conversion math often works as follows:
- Convert at ~70-80% of book value
- Grow book value through earnings + market appreciation
- Either repurchase shares or sell to acquirer at premium

Watch for: Management signaling buyback intent without capital to execute.

### d) Debt Usage

**Current leverage:** Total equity of $13.9M on $219.6M assets = 6.3% equity/assets ratio. This is adequate but not fortress-level for a bank.

**No disclosed debt at holding company level.** The conversion raised $8.9M in equity, not debt.

### Capital Allocation Track Record Assessment: INSUFFICIENT HISTORY

The cannabis banking expansion shows willingness to take strategic risk. No evidence of empire-building or M&A addiction. But with only 6 months of post-conversion existence, there is no real track record to judge.

## 6. Forward-Looking Capital Allocation Risk

### Most Likely Near-Term Capital Allocation

1. **Adopt stock-based benefit plans:** Near-certainty. Management will almost certainly adopt option/MRP plans, creating 10-14% dilution.

2. **Continue CRB expansion:** The cannabis banking niche is profitable and differentiating. Management will likely expand lending to CRB customers, concentrating risk.

3. **Maintain status quo:** The bank is profitable, well-capitalized, and faces no pressure to change. Expect incremental growth, not transformation.

### Primary Risks

**Dilution:** The combination of ESOP (7%) + potential option/MRP grants (14%) means new shareholders could face 20%+ dilution to insiders over the first few years. This is the primary value leak.

**Concentration risk:** 27% of deposits and 19% of loans tied to cannabis. Federal regulatory change (positive or negative) could materially impact the bank.

**Acquisition at premium to current but discount to potential:** Thrift conversions often sell out too early. If management is incentivized by option vesting rather than long-term value, they may accept an offer that enriches insiders but leaves money on the table.

## The One Thing That Determines Value Creation

**The critical factor is insider discipline on stock-based compensation.**

At a $8.9M market cap, the difference between adopting minimum vs. maximum stock-based benefit plans could represent 5-10% of company value transferred from shareholders to insiders.

If management:
- Adopts plans at regulatory maximum (10% options, 4% MRP): Expect significant dilution, suggests self-dealing instincts
- Adopts plans below maximum or delays adoption: Signals alignment, worth monitoring
- Pursues meaningful insider purchases in open market: Strong positive signal

The fundamental economics of thrift conversions favor insiders. The question is degree. Watch for insider Form 4 filings in the next 12 months.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**The non-consensus observation:** This is not a boring community bank; it's a cannabis banking play disguised as a thrift conversion.

Most thrift conversion analysis focuses on:
- Discount to tangible book value
- Time to acquisition
- Insider accumulation

What's different here:
- 27% of deposits from cannabis businesses
- 19% of loans to cannabis businesses
- This concentration is unusual for a 144-year-old mutual savings bank

**Implication:** The bank's value (and risk) is tied to cannabis banking regulation more than typical thrift conversion dynamics. Federal legalization/normalization is a significant upside catalyst. Aggressive federal enforcement is a material risk.

This makes SBMW more of a regulatory bet than a pure thrift conversion play. Most investors evaluating small thrift conversions won't focus on this.
