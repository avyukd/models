# Coursera Inc (COUR) - Financial Analysis

## Executive Summary

**Stock:** NYSE: COUR | **Price:** ~$7.43 | **Market Cap:** ~$1.2B | **EV:** ~$0.5B (net cash position)

**Verdict:** Coursera trades at a deceptively cheap headline valuation (1.6x EV/Revenue) but normalized earnings power is materially lower than non-GAAP figures suggest. The pending Udemy merger introduces execution risk but also potential synergy value. The market is pricing in continued losses on a GAAP basis while ignoring the fortress balance sheet and improving FCF trajectory.

---

## What Actually Matters: Key Financial Drivers

### 1. Stock-Based Compensation Is The Elephant in the Room

The single most important insight: Coursera's GAAP vs. non-GAAP spread is enormous, driven primarily by SBC.

**Deriving SBC from the GAAP/Non-GAAP Bridge (FY2024):**
- GAAP Net Loss: $(79.5)M
- Non-GAAP Net Income: $55.6M
- **Implied SBC + Related Adjustments: ~$135M**

This represents **~19% of revenue** being paid out in stock compensation. At $695M revenue, this is a massive drag on true economic earnings.

**Corroborating Evidence:**
- Shares outstanding increased 3.65% YoY (per companiesmarketcap.com)
- GAAP operating loss was $(102)M vs Adjusted EBITDA of $41.5M - a $144M swing
- The gap between GAAP gross profit ($371M at 53%) and Non-GAAP gross profit ($378M at 54%) implies ~$7M of SBC is allocated to COGS

**Critical Point:** Management touts "first year of positive Adjusted EBITDA" but this excludes ~$135M of real economic cost. True normalized earnings are negative.

### 2. Enterprise Net Retention Rate is Collapsing

This is the most alarming trend buried in the disclosures:

| Period | Enterprise NRR |
|--------|----------------|
| Q4 2022 | 108% |
| Q3 2024 | 89% |
| Q4 2024 | 87% |

**Enterprise NRR dropped from 108% to 87% in two years.** This indicates:
- Existing customers are churning or downsizing
- Land-and-expand strategy is failing
- Enterprise revenue growth (9% in 2024) is entirely from new logos, not expansion

At 87% NRR, Enterprise revenue will eventually shrink unless new customer acquisition accelerates significantly. This is a structural problem the market may be underweighting.

### 3. Consumer Segment Drives Profitability (But Has Margin Ceiling)

Segment breakdown (FY2024):
| Segment | Revenue | YoY Growth | Gross Margin |
|---------|---------|------------|--------------|
| Consumer | $398M | +9% | 54% |
| Enterprise | $239M | +9% | 69% |
| Degrees | $58M | +13% | 100% |

- **Consumer** is the largest segment but has the lowest gross margin due to content partner revenue share
- **Enterprise** has better unit economics but is struggling with retention
- **Degrees** is 100% margin (no content cost) but tiny and slow-growing

The blended gross margin of 53% is constrained by Consumer's ~54% margin. Without Enterprise expansion or Degrees scaling, margin improvement is capped.

## Normalized Earnings Power Analysis

### GAAP to Normalized FCF Reconciliation (FY2024)

| Line Item | Amount | Notes |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| GAAP Net Loss | $(79.5)M | Reported |
| Add: D&A | ~$8M | Estimated from EBITDA bridge |
| Add: SBC & Related | ~$135M | Derived from GAAP/non-GAAP spread |
| Add: Restructuring | ~$15M | 10% workforce reduction in 2024 |
| Less: Interest Income | ~$(30)M | On $726M cash balance |
| **Operating Cash Flow** | **$95M** | Reported |
| Less: CapEx | $(36)M | Reported |
| **Free Cash Flow** | **$59M** | Reported |

### True Economic Earnings (SBC-Adjusted)

| Metric | Amount | % of Revenue |
|--------|--------|--------------|
| Adjusted EBITDA (Mgmt Definition) | $41.5M | 6.0% |
| Less: SBC (Estimated) | $(135)M | (19.4%) |
| **SBC-Adjusted EBITDA** | **$(94)M** | **(13.5%)** |

**Conclusion:** On a true economic basis (treating SBC as a real expense), Coursera is burning ~$94M annually at the EBITDA line. The "positive Adjusted EBITDA" celebration is misleading.

### Path to GAAP Profitability

For GAAP breakeven, Coursera would need to:
1. Grow revenue to ~$900M+ (holding cost structure constant), OR
2. Cut SBC from ~$135M to ~$50M (unlikely without destroying retention), OR
3. Achieve dramatic operating leverage (requires 400+ bps margin expansion)

At current 8-9% revenue growth and stable SBC intensity, GAAP profitability is 3-4+ years away.

- Shares outstanding: 166.4M (up 3.65% YoY)
- At current SBC intensity, expect 3-4% annual dilution
- Over 5 years, this compounds to ~17-20% dilution
- Present value impact: Material but not catastrophic given cash balance

## Valuation Framework

### Current Multiples

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| EV/Revenue (LTM) | 0.7x |
| EV/Revenue (NTM ~$750M) | 0.7x |
| EV/Gross Profit | 1.4x |
| P/FCF | 20x (on $59M FCF) |
| P/S | 1.7x |

### What the Market Implies

At $1.2B market cap and $500M EV:
- If you apply a 2x EV/Revenue multiple (modest for software), fair value = ~$1.4B EV = ~$2.1B market cap = ~$13/share
- Current price of $7.43 implies the market expects either:
  - Revenue to decline, OR
  - Margins to never reach profitability, OR
  - Continued heavy dilution

### Scenario Analysis

**Bull Case ($12-15/share):**
- Udemy merger closes, synergies achieved ($115M cost savings)
- Combined entity reaches ~$1.6B revenue
- Enterprise NRR stabilizes at 90%+
- 2x EV/Revenue on $1.6B = $3.2B EV + $800M cash = $4B market cap
- Upside: 60-100%

**Base Case ($8-10/share):**
- Standalone revenue grows 8% annually to ~$810M by 2026
- SBC intensity remains elevated (17-19% of revenue)
- FCF improves to $80-100M
- 1.5x EV/Revenue = $1.2B EV + $750M cash = $2B market cap
- Upside: 10-35%

**Bear Case ($4-6/share):**
- Enterprise continues deteriorating (NRR drops to 80%)
- Consumer growth slows (AI disruption, competition)
- Merger fails or is dilutive
- 0.5x EV/Revenue = $400M EV + $700M cash = $1.1B market cap
- Downside: 20-45%

## Key Financial Drivers

1. **Enterprise Net Retention Rate** - The load-bearing metric. If NRR stabilizes above 90%, Enterprise can contribute to growth. If it falls below 85%, Enterprise becomes a drag. Watch quarterly disclosures closely.

2. **SBC as % of Revenue** - Must trend down toward 12-15% for true operating leverage. Currently at ~19%. Any reduction here flows directly to economic earnings.

3. **Consumer Segment Growth** - At 57% of revenue, Consumer is the engine. Coursera Plus subscription health (renewals, ARPU, churn) determines whether 8-9% topline growth is sustainable.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**The Udemy Merger Changes Everything - And Nobody is Pricing the Synergies Properly**

The announced all-stock merger with Udemy at 0.8x exchange ratio creates:
- Combined ~$1.6B revenue entity
- $115M run-rate cost synergies within 24 months (7% of combined revenue)
- At current valuations, Coursera is acquiring Udemy's $800M revenue base for ~$1B implied value

**Hidden Synergy Math:**
- $115M annual cost synergies capitalized at 10x = $1.15B value creation
- Transaction breakup fee is only $49M (trivial)
- If merger closes, the combined entity should trade at a premium to standalone Coursera

**Why the market may be wrong:**
1. Merger risk is overpriced - EdTech consolidation has regulatory tailwinds, not headwinds
2. Synergy targets appear conservative (back-office, overlapping content, sales teams)
3. Combined enterprise sales force could arrest NRR decline through broader product suite

**Contrarian View:** The market is pricing Coursera as a struggling standalone EdTech when it's about to become the dominant Western MOOC platform with significant cost synergy potential.
