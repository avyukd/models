## 2. Key Drivers (The 1-3 Things That Actually Matter)

1. **Enterprise NRR Trajectory:** Collapsed from 108% to 87% in two years. If this continues toward 80%, the core B2B business is in structural decline regardless of merger synergies. If it stabilizes above 95%, the thesis changes materially.

2. **AI Disintermediation:** ChatGPT/Claude/Khanmigo can now provide personalized tutoring at zero marginal cost. The MOOC value proposition ("access to expert instruction at scale") is being commoditized in real-time. This is not priced as an existential risk.

3. **Udemy Merger Execution:** An all-stock deal at cycle bottom that dilutes existing shareholders 41% while combining two struggling platforms. Success requires $115M cost synergies AND revenue stabilization. Failure means two broken businesses merged into one larger broken business.

---

## 3. Thesis: Why We Pass

- **No variant perception exists.** The bull case (merger synergies, fortress balance sheet, cheap on EV/Revenue) is consensus. The bear case (AI disruption, collapsing NRR, empire-building management) is also well-known. We cannot identify a non-consensus insight that gives us an edge.

- **The business is structurally impaired.** Enterprise NRR at 87% means existing customers are churning faster than they expand. Consumer retention is undisclosed (telling). The moat specialist correctly identifies that Coursera is a distribution platform without proprietary content - when Google demanded better terms, margins dropped 11 points.

- **Management is not aligned.** Former CEO extracted $30M while the stock fell 78%. New CEO has $38M equity package with no cost basis. The Udemy merger is empire-building, not value creation. SBC at 15-19% of revenue means "positive Adjusted EBITDA" is fiction.

- **The merger makes this too complex to underwrite.** Standalone Coursera has clear problems. Post-merger Coursera + Udemy introduces integration risk, cultural risk, and customer confusion - all while both platforms face the same AI headwinds.

- **The industry is structurally unfavorable.** 12+ years of MOOC unprofitability is not a cycle - it may be the steady state. AI is collapsing the marginal cost of tutoring to zero. "Mid-cycle" may never arrive.

## 5. The Setup

**What Coursera Is:** A MOOC aggregator that licenses content from 350+ universities and companies, takes a revenue share, and sells access to consumers, enterprises, and degree-seeking students. It owns almost no proprietary content.

**Why It's Challenged:**
- Content providers hold leverage (proven by Google renegotiation that crushed margins)
- AI tutors provide the same instruction, personalized, 24/7, for less money
- Credentials are only valuable if scarce; they are no longer scarce
- Enterprise customers are contracting, not expanding
- Consumer retention is weak (undisclosed is the tell)

**The Merger Context:** Coursera is acquiring Udemy in an all-stock deal. Combined entity will have ~$1.5B revenue, 270M registered learners, and the same structural problems at larger scale. Management claims $115M cost synergies. History suggests synergy targets are optimistic, especially when both parties are struggling.

## 6. Numbers That Matter

| FCF | $59M | Positive, but SBC is 183% of FCF |

The "first year of positive Adjusted EBITDA" celebration is misleading. On a true economic basis, Coursera loses money.

### Valuation Math

- At 0.7x EV/Revenue, the stock is statistically cheap for software
- But cheap assumes margins normalize. This business has been unprofitable for 12+ years
- The $723M cash balance provides downside protection (~60% of market cap)
- If you strip out cash, you're paying $500M for a $695M revenue business that loses money

### What the Market Implies

At $7.43/share, the market is saying:
- Revenue growth will remain low single digits
- Margins will not reach profitability
- Dilution will continue (SBC + merger)
- AI disruption is a real risk

**We do not disagree with this assessment.**

## 9. Positioning and Portfolio Logic

**Why This Does Not Deserve Capital:**

1. **No edge.** The bull and bear cases are both consensus. We cannot identify what we know that the market does not.

2. **Binary merger outcome.** The Udemy deal closes in H1 2026. Until then, we are betting on integration execution, which is inherently unknowable.

3. **Structural headwinds.** AI disruption is real and accelerating. We would rather be short the MOOC category than long a specific name, but shorting into a potential merger squeeze is unattractive risk/reward.

4. **Management is not aligned.** SBC at 15-19%, buybacks that don't offset dilution, and a former CEO who extracted $30M during an 78% decline. These are not the behaviors of stewards.

5. **Opportunity cost.** Capital deployed here is capital not deployed in situations with clearer edges and better risk/reward.

**Position:** 0% weight. Do not initiate.

**What Would Move This Out of "Too Hard":**
- Merger closes successfully with NRR stabilization visible in Q2 2026 data
- AI substitution threat proves overstated (MOOCs retain pricing power)
- New management demonstrates real SBC discipline (sub-12% of revenue)
- Stock falls to levels where cash alone provides sufficient margin of safety (~$4-5/share, where you're paying ~$0 for the business)

---

## 10. Summary

Coursera is a cheap stock in a structurally challenged industry with weak moat characteristics, misaligned management, and a pending merger that may or may not create value. The balance sheet is a fortress, but the cash is not yours - it will be consumed by SBC, integration costs, and continued operating losses.

The bull case (merger synergies, cheap valuation, optionality on enterprise recovery) is known. The bear case (AI disruption, collapsing NRR, empire-building management) is also known. There is no variant perception that gives us an edge.

When the best argument for a long is "it's cheap" and the best argument for a short is "it's structurally impaired," the right answer is to pass. This is a coin flip with merger execution risk layered on top.

**Decision: Neutral (Too Hard). Do not deploy capital.**
