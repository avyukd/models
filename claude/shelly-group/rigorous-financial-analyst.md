## Executive Summary

Shelly Group is a Bulgarian IoT company specializing in smart home devices (relays, dimmers, sensors) with strong financial performance. The company has demonstrated 31 consecutive quarters of revenue growth with industry-leading EBIT margins (24-30%). However, the stock trades at elevated multiples that embed substantial future growth expectations. This analysis identifies key drivers, quantifies normalized earnings, and surfaces what consensus may be missing.

**Bottom Line:** Financially strong and rapidly growing, but current valuation (~38x trailing P/E) requires continued 25-35%+ growth and margin expansion to justify. Solid balance sheet (81% equity ratio), but weak historical free cash flow conversion due to working capital absorption warrants monitoring.

**Source:** Stock Analysis (BUL:SLYG) Cash Flow Statement

### Cash Flow Quality Assessment

**Critical Finding:** There is a persistent gap between accounting earnings and free cash flow.

| Year | Net Income (BGN M) | FCF (BGN M) | FCF/Net Income |
|------|-------------------|-------------|----------------|
| 2023 | 33.6 | 10.3 | 31% |
| 2024 | 45.1 | (2.2) | Negative |
| TTM 2025 | ~46.3 | 11.7 | 25% |

**Root Cause: Working Capital Absorption**

The company has deliberately invested in working capital (primarily inventories and receivables) to support growth:

- 2024: Inventory build-up and advance payments to manufacturers absorbed operating cash flow
- 2025 guidance: Management expects working capital optimization from SAP implementation and faster inventory turnover

**Judgment:** This is characteristic of a high-growth, asset-light manufacturer scaling rapidly. The cash is not "lost" but tied up in inventory that should convert to sales. However, FCF conversion of 25-30% of net income is below what I would expect for a mature company. This is acceptable during hypergrowth but must improve as growth normalizes.

**Stock-Based Compensation (SBC) Analysis:**

## 9. Key Financial Drivers

### The 1-3 Metrics That Actually Matter

**1. Revenue Growth Rate (Most Critical)**

This stock trades primarily on growth. The deceleration from 57% (2023) to 42% (2024) to 34% (TTM) is the key variable. If growth drops below 25%, the multiple will compress significantly.

- **Positive Catalyst:** UK and Poland expansion success; professional installer channel gains traction
- **Negative Catalyst:** German economy remains weak; competition intensifies; consumer spending slowdown

**2. EBIT Margin Trajectory**

The Q3 2025 margin of 29.5% exceeded expectations. Sustainability of margins >25% is crucial for earnings power.

- **Positive Catalyst:** Operating leverage continues; premium app subscription grows; procurement efficiencies
- **Negative Catalyst:** Marketing spend remains elevated without proportional revenue lift; price competition

**3. Free Cash Flow Conversion**

Currently the weakest metric. FCF/Net Income of 25% is inadequate for a "quality" growth company. Must improve to 50%+ as growth normalizes.

- **Positive Catalyst:** Working capital optimization (SAP implementation delivers); inventory turnover improves
- **Negative Catalyst:** Continued WC absorption; capacity investments accelerate

## 10. What Consensus May Be Missing

### Non-Consensus Insight #1: The Q3 2025 Margin Jump May Not Be Sustainable

Q3 2025 EBIT margin of 29.5% was 600+ bps above Q3 2024. While management attributes this to operating leverage and scale, I note:

- Q4 is typically the highest revenue quarter (Black Friday) with fixed cost absorption
- Marketing expenses surged 283% in 2024 - these costs may have been weighted to H1
- If Q3's margin is partially timing-related, Q4/2026 may show normalization

**Implication:** Analysts extrapolating 29-30% margins into their models may be overly optimistic. A 25-26% normalized margin is more defensible.

### Non-Consensus Insight #2: Germany Concentration is Underappreciated Risk

47% of revenue comes from Germany/DACH, which experienced near-zero GDP growth in 2024-2025. The German housing market has been weak.

- Shelly's growth despite this headwind is impressive
- But if German consumer sentiment deteriorates further, or if the company relies on German strength for its guidance, there is downside risk

**Implication:** The bull case assumes continued 30%+ growth, but German macro could be a meaningful headwind not fully reflected in consensus estimates.

### Non-Consensus Insight #3: The Premium App is Immaterial and May Stay That Way

Bulls highlight the "100% gross margin" premium app as a recurring revenue opportunity. At EUR 0.6M in 2024, it is 0.6% of revenue.

- Smart home device users are notoriously reluctant to pay subscription fees
- Competitors (including open-source alternatives) offer similar functionality for free
- The Shelly brand is built on value and openness - subscription paywalls may conflict with brand identity

**Implication:** Do not model meaningful SaaS revenue contribution for at least 3-5 years. The company remains a hardware business.

## 11. Investment Conclusion

### Summary Assessment

| Dimension | Rating | Commentary |
|-----------|--------|------------|
| **Business Quality** | Strong | Market leader in niche, high margins, no debt |
| **Financial Strength** | Strong | 81% equity ratio, net cash, profitable |
| **Earnings Quality** | Moderate | GAAP clean, but FCF conversion weak |
| **Growth Sustainability** | Moderate | 30%+ growth impressive but decelerating |
| **Valuation** | Full/Stretched | 38x trailing requires continued execution |
| **Key Risks** | Moderate | Germany concentration, China manufacturing |

### What Must Be True for Current Valuation

1. Revenue growth sustains 25%+ for 3+ years
2. EBIT margins expand to and hold 27%+
3. No significant competitive disruption
4. China supply chain remains stable
5. German economy does not deteriorate materially

### What Breaks the Thesis

1. Revenue growth decelerates to <20%
2. EBIT margins compress to <22%
3. Large tech player (Google, Amazon) aggressively enters relay/switch market with integrated solutions
4. China tariffs or supply disruption forces manufacturing relocation
5. FCF conversion remains weak as growth slows (capital allocation becomes concerning)

### Final Assessment

**Financially solid, operationally impressive, but fully priced.**

Shelly Group is a high-quality business generating 25%+ EBIT margins with minimal debt and strong growth. However, at 38x trailing earnings, the stock offers limited margin of safety. Investors are paying today for 3-5 years of continued exceptional execution.

For new investors, a more attractive entry point would be:
- P/E of 25-28x on normalized earnings (EUR 38-42/share)
- Or evidence that FCF conversion is sustainably improving above 50%

For existing investors, the position can be held with monitoring of: (1) revenue growth trajectory, (2) EBIT margin sustainability, and (3) FCF conversion improvement.
