# Shelly Group SE (SLYG.F) - Capital Allocation & Management Analysis

**Date:** January 2025
**Analyst Framework:** Incentive-Driven, Behavior-Based Assessment
**Company:** Shelly Group SE (formerly Shelly Group AD, formerly Allterco AD)
**Listing:** Bulgarian Stock Exchange (Dec 2016), Frankfurt Stock Exchange Prime Standard (Nov 2021), XETRA (Apr 2024)

---

## Executive Summary

Shelly Group is a rare case in European small-cap tech: a founder-controlled company where management incentives are structurally aligned with shareholders through ownership, not contractual arrangements. The two co-founders collectively own approximately 59% of outstanding shares, making their economic interests nearly indistinguishable from outside shareholders. This structural alignment is the dominant factor in assessing management quality and future capital allocation behavior.

The company has demonstrated disciplined capital allocation during its growth phase: minimal dilution, conservative acquisition strategy (one distressed-asset purchase for EUR 0.15m), reinvestment into high-return R&D, and modest cash retention. However, the growing cash position and maturing business model present a future inflection point where capital allocation discipline will be tested.

## 2. Incentive Structure & Alignment

### Ownership as Primary Alignment Mechanism

**Founder Ownership (Q2 2025):**
- Dimitar Dimitrov: 30.16% (5,473,000+ shares)
- Svetlin Todorov: 29.11% (5,276,000+ shares)
- **Combined: 59.27%**

This ownership level creates structural alignment that compensation design cannot replicate or substitute. At current market cap (~EUR 550-600m), each founder's stake is worth EUR 165-180m. Every 1% move in share price represents EUR 1.65-1.8m of personal wealth change per founder.

**What this incentivizes:**
- Long-term value creation over short-term metrics
- Capital preservation (founders bear direct losses from value destruction)
- Resistance to empire-building at high valuations (dilution hurts founders proportionally)
- Likely conservative acquisition strategy (founders' wealth at risk)

### Board Compensation Structure

**Total Board Remuneration (2024):** BGN 790 thousand (~EUR 404 thousand)

Per 2024 consolidated financial statements:
- Board remuneration paid in compliance with approved remuneration policy
- Variable remuneration scheme approved for 2022-2025 period providing remuneration in shares

**Assessment:** Board compensation is modest relative to company size (EUR 404k for 5 directors against EUR 107m revenue). This is materially lower than Western European peers. The share-based variable component aligns directors with shareholders but the small absolute amounts suggest this is supplemental to the primary alignment mechanism (founder ownership).

### Professional Management Compensation (Kirsch)

Wolfgang Kirsch's compensation details are not fully disclosed, but behavioral evidence suggests alignment:
- Made 3 share purchases over past 5 years (all buys, zero sells)
- Most recent: December 2024 purchase
- Previous: 1,219 shares at ~EUR 20.50 (Dec 2023), 1,300 shares at EUR 20.00 (Aug 2023)

**Assessment:** Small purchases relative to likely total compensation, but direction is positive. Kirsch is buying alongside shareholders, not selling into strength.

### Personnel Cost Trend

| Year | Personnel Costs (BGN '000) | YoY Growth |
|------|---------------------------|------------|
| 2023 | 18,820 | - |
| 2024 | 23,808 | +26.5% |

Personnel costs growing at 26.5% vs revenue growth of 42.4% indicates operating leverage and cost discipline. Personnel costs represent 29.4% of total operating expenses.

### Who Benefits If Stock Underperforms?

In a scenario where the stock underperforms:
- Founders lose: EUR 3.3-3.6m per 1% decline (combined)
- Professional management: Modest impact via share-based variable comp
- Board members (ex-founders): Minimal exposure

**Conclusion:** The incentive structure is founder-dominated. Founders bear concentrated risk, creating strong alignment. Professional management has directionally correct incentives but less "skin in the game" proportionally.

- 54,614 new shares issued
- Eligible participants: Employees as of December 31, 2023 (excluding Board members)
- Dilution: 0.3%
- Board members explicitly excluded (reduces self-dealing risk)

### SBC as Percentage of Revenue

Data not explicitly disclosed in searched materials. However:
- Personnel costs (including any SBC expense) at BGN 23.8m represent ~11.4% of revenue (BGN 208.6m in 2024)
- Employee share issuance of 54,614 shares at ~EUR 30/share = ~EUR 1.6m value

### SBC as Percentage of Free Cash Flow

- 2024 FCF: Negative BGN 2.23m
- SBC cannot be expressed as FCF percentage when FCF is negative
- 2023 FCF: BGN 10.26m; if similar SBC, would represent ~16%

### Buybacks vs. Net Dilution Reality

**Buyback Activity:** Minimal. Small treasury share transactions (0.22% of capital, BGN 780k in 2022) executed for potential acquisition currency, subsequently sold back to market.

**Net Assessment:** There is no systematic buyback program. The company does not return capital via share repurchases. Given the growth phase and high-return reinvestment opportunities, this is rational. Management is not disguising dilution with offsetting buybacks.

**Flag Status:** GREEN - Low dilution (~1% annual), no buyback obfuscation, honest treatment of shareholder base.

**Assessment:** CapEx intensity increasing modestly as the company scales. Investment focus is on R&D (product development for new device categories, software/cloud platform) and geographic expansion (distribution channels).

**Return Evidence:**
- Revenue CAGR (2021-2024): ~51%
- EBIT margin expansion: 21.9% (2022) to 25.6% (2024 adjusted)
- Gross margin expansion: 50.3% (2022) to 57.1% (2023)

Reinvestment is generating high returns. Incremental revenue is being converted to EBIT at attractive margins. No evidence of growth-at-all-costs mentality.

### b) M&A

**Acquisition History:**

| Date | Target | Deal Value | Strategic Rationale |
|------|--------|------------|---------------------|
| 2021 | Allterco Asia Ltd (30% stake) | CNY 30,000 (~EUR 4k) | China presence, manufacturing proximity |
| July 2024 | LOQED B.V. (assets) | EUR 150,000 | Smart locks IP, inventory; distressed asset |

**LOQED Analysis:**
- Target was in insolvency proceedings
- Acquired IP, inventory, and China manufacturing access for EUR 150k
- Q1 2025: Initial 1,000 lock shipment sold out quickly
- Now branded "LOQED by Shelly"
- Smart lock market growing 15.4% annually to USD 5.8B by 2029

**Assessment:**
- Deal size is immaterial (<0.15% of market cap)
- Opportunistic distressed acquisition vs. competitive auction
- Timing suggests discipline: bought bankrupt assets, not paying premium
- Early results positive but too early to assess ROI

**M&A Posture:** Highly conservative. The company has not pursued transformative or large-scale M&A despite having cash and a premium-valued stock. This is notable restraint for a founder-led tech company with growth pressure.

**Flag Status:** GREEN - No empire-building evidence. One tiny, opportunistic acquisition.

Per corporate announcements:

### e) Debt Usage

**Cash Position (Dec 2024):** EUR 14.0m (BGN 27.3m)
**Debt Position:** Minimal/negligible (not separately disclosed as material)
**Net Cash:** ~EUR 14m

**Assessment:** The company operates with a net cash balance sheet. No financial engineering. No leveraged recapitalizations. Conservative capital structure appropriate for a growth-stage hardware company with inventory and R&D investment needs.

## 5. Learning vs. Repetition (Mandatory)

### Evidence of Learning

1. **Listing Strategy Evolution:**
   - 2016: Bulgarian Stock Exchange (small capital raise, local liquidity)
   - 2021: Frankfurt Prime Standard (EUR 14.50/share, international access)
   - 2024: XETRA trading (first Bulgarian company, improved liquidity)

   This progression shows deliberate, staged capital market strategy rather than one-time opportunism.

2. **Free Float Management:**
   - 2023: Todorov sold 2% to institutions (increased liquidity, attracted institutional capital)
   - 2024: Founders sold combined 2.7% to BIT Capital and others
   - Both transactions at prices above 3-month average

   Founders diluted themselves (not the company) to improve trading liquidity and shareholder base quality. This demonstrates learning about capital market dynamics.

3. **Employee Incentive Design:**
   - 2024 employee capital increase explicitly excluded Board members
   - Reduces conflicts of interest and self-dealing optics

4. **Acquisition Discipline:**
   - LOQED purchased from insolvency at EUR 150k
   - No competitive auction premiums paid
   - Demonstrates patience and discipline vs. FOMO-driven M&A

### Evidence of Repetition (Negative Patterns)

None identified from available data. Searched for:
- Repeated guidance misses: Not found (guidance generally met or exceeded)
- Pattern of dilutive acquisitions: Not found
- Insider selling at peaks: Not found (founder sales were liquidity-driven, at market prices)
- Capital allocation failures requiring correction: Not found

**Assessment:** The company shows evidence of learning and adaptation without repeated mistakes. This is a positive indicator for future capital allocation.

## 6. Forward-Looking Capital Allocation Risk

### Where Capital Is Most Likely to Go

Based on stated priorities (per 2024 Investor Report and earnings calls):
1. R&D investment (new product categories, software platform)
2. Geographic expansion (North America, Asia-Pacific)
3. Potential tuck-in acquisitions (adjacent smart home categories)

### Risks to Future Capital Allocation

**Risk 1: M&A Escalation**
- Current cash: EUR 14m and growing
- FCF (normalized): EUR 5-10m annually
- By 2026-2027: Could have EUR 40-50m cash
- Risk: Pressure to deploy cash into larger acquisitions
- Mitigant: 59% founder ownership creates discipline (their money at risk)

**Risk 2: Geographic Expansion Overspend**
- US and Asia expansion ongoing
- Sales infrastructure investment required
- Risk: Overinvestment ahead of revenue (classic growth-stage error)
- Mitigant: Co-CEO structure with commercial expert (Kirsch) provides operational discipline

**Risk 3: Stock-Based Compensation Creep**
- Current SBC: ~1.5% of revenue (estimated)
- As company scales and hires more professional managers, SBC may expand
- Risk: Silicon Valley-style SBC creep (5-10% of revenue)
- Mitigant: Bulgarian/European corporate culture less SBC-intensive; founder majority limits dilution tolerance

**Risk 4: Founder Liquidity Events**
- Combined founder stake: 59% (EUR 330-360m)
- Neither founder has materially exited
- Risk: Eventual large-scale secondary sale could pressure stock
- Mitigant: Both have stated intention to remain major shareholders; sales to date have been small, orderly, and liquidity-focused

### Behavioral Forecast

**Most Likely Scenario (70% probability):**
Continued disciplined reinvestment with opportunistic small acquisitions. Cash builds on balance sheet. Eventually initiates more material dividend or systematic buyback once growth decelerates. Founders maintain >50% ownership through 2027.

**Adverse Scenario (20% probability):**
Competitive pressure in smart home market drives larger, value-destructive acquisition (EUR 30-50m) to acquire product categories or geographic presence. Returns disappoint, margin compression follows.

**Downside Scenario (10% probability):**
Founders begin material exit (selling 10%+ combined stake) signaling reduced conviction. Professional management influence increases. Capital allocation discipline degrades.

## 7. The One Thing That Determines Value Creation

**The single most important factor for predicting management's future capital allocation is the continuation of majority founder ownership.**

As long as Dimitrov and Todorov collectively own >50% of shares:
- Empire-building risk is structurally contained (their money)
- Dilution is self-limiting (they bear the cost)
- Acquisition discipline is embedded (concentrated downside)
- Time horizon is naturally long (no activist pressure, no short-term comp metrics)

If founder ownership drops below 50% (through sales or dilutive issuance), the governance dynamic fundamentally changes. Professional management influence increases, compensation structures become more important, and historical track record becomes less predictive of future behavior.

**What would cause a change in behavior:**
- Founders selling >10% combined within 12 months
- Introduction of acquisition-linked management bonuses
- Board composition shifting to independent/investor-appointed majority
- CEO succession to non-founder

None of these are currently evident.

## 8. What Consensus May Be Missing

### Non-Consensus Insight: The Freenet Connection Is Underappreciated

Christoph Vilanek's appointment as Chairman (January 2024) has strategic implications beyond governance window-dressing:

1. **Freenet AG is Germany's largest independent telecom provider** with 9+ million subscribers
2. **Freenet has a platform distribution capability** for smart home products
3. **Vilanek led Freenet's diversification** into TV and consumer electronics
4. **He has M&A and restructuring experience** at scale

**Potential implications:**
- Distribution partnership opportunity (Freenet as Shelly distribution channel)
- Governance upgrade signal for institutional investors
- M&A advisory capability if larger deals considered
- Potential strategic interest from Freenet (speculation, no evidence)

Consensus views Vilanek as a corporate governance upgrade. The potential commercial/strategic dimension is underappreciated.

**Does this make me more or less trusting of management?**
More trusting. Vilanek's appointment shows founders are bringing in experienced German corporate leadership as the company scales, rather than maintaining an insular Bulgarian management structure. This suggests self-awareness about capability gaps.
