## Why Not Buy: The SBC Problem Voss Ignores

Voss Capital frames FLYW as "10x 2026 FCF." This is misleading.

**GAAP FCF vs. Real Owner Earnings:**

| Metric | GAAP | Reality (SBC-Adjusted) |
|--------|------|------------------------|
| Net Income | $2.9M | ($62M) after SBC |
| "FCF" | ~$80M | ~$8-12M real owner earnings |
| EV/FCF | 10x | **67-100x** |
| Dilution | "Offset by buybacks" | Net 3-4% annual dilution |

SBC is not free money. It's equity transferred from shareholders to employees. At $65M/year (13% of revenue), Flywire is paying employees with your ownership stake while reporting "profitability."

**The Voss thesis uses the same GAAP flattery that killed 2021 SaaS investors.**

---

## Why Not Buy: The Moat Doesn't Exist

My moat analyst was clear: **"Weak to Moderate"**

**What Voss claims:** "Technologically dominant in vertical niches"

**Reality:**
- Universities use 3-4 payment providers simultaneously
- Students choose based on FX rates (commodity)
- No workflow lock-in (contrast with Toast's POS = brain of restaurant)
- 130% NRR was driven by landing new freshman cohorts, not true stickiness
- Convera, PayMyTuition, TransferMate all compete directly

**The Toast comparison is wrong:**
| Factor | Toast | Flywire |
|--------|-------|---------|
| Core product | POS (mission critical) | Payment processing |
| Switching cost | High (retrain staff, lose data) | Low (students pick on price) |
| Lock-in mechanism | Workflow embedded | None |
| Moat type | Platform | Scale (weak) |

Toast deserves 15x GP. Flywire at 3x GP may still be expensive for a commodity payments business with 13% SBC drag.

---

## The UK SFS Story: Interesting But Unproven

Voss's best argument is UK SFS - expanding from international-only to all-campus payments (2-25x revenue per university).

**What's real:**
- Unit4 ERP integration complete at 50+ universities
- Manchester Metropolitan case study shows cost savings
- Finance director actively pitching

**What's assumption:**
- Scaling from ~5% to 20-30% penetration
- Universities actually adopting at pace
- Competitors not responding

**The problem:** Even if SFS works, it doesn't create a moat. It expands TAM but doesn't create switching costs. A competitor could integrate with Unit4 too.

## What Would Make This a Buy

1. **Price below $7** - Closer to cash + realistic business value
2. **SBC drops to 5-7% of revenue** - Real profitability emerges
3. **Evidence of actual switching costs** - Customer concentration data, churn rates, pricing power
4. **Insider buying** - CEO putting real money in (currently 1.4% ownership, 42 sells, 0 buys)

---

## The Bear Case for the Business (Above Cash)

1. **SBC is structural, not transitional.** Tech talent expects equity. 13% of revenue to employees continues.

2. **Moat is weak.** Universities use multiple providers. Students choose on FX rates. No lock-in.

3. **UK SFS expands TAM but not moat.** More revenue per university doesn't mean stickier revenue.

4. **Management alignment is poor.** CEO has sold 42 times, bought 0 times. Why should you be bullish when he isn't?

5. **In the new regime (higher rates, FCF matters), SBC-heavy businesses get re-rated down.** Terminal value assumptions that worked at 0% rates don't work at 4%+.

---

## Why Not Short?

The cash floor is real:
- $4.35/share in net cash = 40% of market cap
- Management could theoretically return it (but won't - they'll do M&A)
- Bear case floor ~$7-8 (cash + trough value)
- Max short gain ~30% with significant tail risk if UK SFS works

**Shorting a 40% cash balance is painful.**

---

## Final Verdict

**Neutral.**

The cash ($4.35/share) is real and provides a floor. The business above the cash ($6.65/share) is priced for a moat that doesn't exist, using GAAP metrics that ignore 13% of revenue going to employees as equity dilution.

Voss makes a compelling narrative case, but uses the same GAAP flattery that made 2021 SaaS look cheap before it collapsed 70-80%. In the new regime where FCF is king and terminal value isn't guaranteed, a business with 67-100x real earnings and weak switching costs doesn't deserve capital.

**Wait for:**
- Price below $7, or
- SBC below 7% of revenue, or
- Real evidence of moat (pricing power, customer lock-in data)
