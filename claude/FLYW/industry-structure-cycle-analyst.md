# Industry Structure & Cycle Analysis: Payments/Fintech (Flywire Context)

## Executive Summary

**The One Industry Factor That Matters:** International student flows to major English-speaking destinations. Flywire's education vertical (~75% of revenue) is directly tied to cross-border student mobility, which is now facing unprecedented policy headwinds in Canada, Australia, UK, and the US simultaneously. This is not a temporary disruption---it represents a structural reset in the operating environment for education payments specialists.

**What Consensus May Be Missing:** The market views the international student policy restrictions as a cyclical headwind that will normalize. This is likely wrong. Three of the four major English-speaking destinations have enacted structural caps or significant policy tightening, driven by domestic housing/political pressures that are unlikely to reverse. The US is now joining this restrictive trend under the Trump administration. The 2015-2023 era of steadily growing international student flows that enabled Flywire's growth may not return. Consensus is also underestimating how quickly competitors can replicate Flywire's "vertical specialization" in a fundamentally commoditizing payments value chain.

## Industry Structure Analysis

### Payments Industry Overview

The payments industry is undergoing a structural shift from horizontal platforms toward vertical-specific solutions. Per McKinsey, vertical-specific software captured >50% of SME spending in 2023 in the US. However, this "vertical moat" thesis requires scrutiny.

**Key structural dynamics:**
- **Commoditization at the processing layer:** Pure payment processing is a declining margin business. Acquirer margins have compressed, driving consolidation (Fiserv/First Data, FIS/Worldpay, Global Payments/TSYS).
- **Value migration to software:** Embedded payments in vertical software generates 3-4x revenue versus licensing alone, with margins 3x higher than pure subscriptions.
- **Take rates under pressure:** Horizontal PSPs face margin compression; differentiation requires value-added services (fraud, analytics, workflow integration).
- **Scale economics:** Global payments revenue reached $2.4T in 2023, projected to hit $3.1T by 2028, but revenue per transaction remains thin outside premium verticals.

### Competitive Structure

**Flywire's segment (vertical education/healthcare payments):**

| Competitor | Focus | Scale |
|------------|-------|-------|
| Convera (fka Western Union Business Solutions) | Cross-border education payments | 800+ institutions, 140+ currencies |
| PayMyTuition | International student payments | Multi-university partnerships |
| TransferMate | Education, SMB cross-border | Global coverage |
| CIBC International Student Pay | Canada-focused | Banking relationship leverage |

**Critical observation:** Major universities (Columbia, Duke, USC, Indiana, Emory) contract with multiple providers (typically 3-4) simultaneously. This is not winner-take-all. Universities offer Flywire, Convera, PayMyTuition, and others as parallel options to students. The student chooses based on FX rates and convenience. This limits pricing power and creates ongoing competitive intensity.

### Vertical Software Payments Moat Assessment

**Bull case for vertical moats:**
- Workflow embeddedness creates switching costs ($500K+ for mid-market per industry data)
- 3-5 years for competitors to replicate industry-specific features
- Net revenue retention 120%+ for leading vertical SaaS vs 110% for horizontal
- Valuation premium: ~12.3x revenue for vertical SaaS vs ~7.6x for broader SaaS (60%+ premium)

**Bear case (applies to Flywire's model):**
- Flywire is not a workflow-embedded software platform like Toast. It processes payments but doesn't control front-office operations.
- Universities can and do use multiple payment processors simultaneously
- The student, not the institution, often chooses which payment provider to use
- FX rates and fees are transparent and commoditized
- Switching costs for institutions are lower than true vertical SaaS

## Education Payments Market: The Core Business

### Market Size and Flywire's TAM Claims

- Flywire estimates education TAM at $660 billion (net household payments to educational institutions in OECD)
- Total claimed TAM across verticals: $11.7 trillion (education, healthcare, travel, B2B)
- Education remains ~75% of Flywire revenue

**TAM scrutiny:** The $660B figure includes all education payments, but Flywire's actual addressable market is international student tuition payments requiring cross-border/FX services---a small fraction of total education spend. This TAM framing is aggressive.

### International Student Flows: Structural Headwinds

**The fundamental driver of Flywire's education business is declining:**

| Country | 2024-2025 Policy Change | Impact |
|---------|------------------------|--------|
| **Canada** | Hard cap: 437,000 study permits for 2025 (10% below 2024) | Study permit approvals down 45% YoY; only ~80K new permits projected for 2025---lowest in a decade |
| **Australia** | Proposed 270K cap; visa fees doubled ($475 to $1,100) | Applications down to 427K in 2024/25 vs 600K prior year |
| **UK** | No family dependents for graduate visa holders; stricter documentation | Non-EU enrollments down 5% in 2024/25, second consecutive year of decline |
| **US** | F-1 visa issuance down 12-22% (Jan-May 2025); proposed 4-year visa limits; $100K H-1B supplemental fee | New enrollment down 17% fall 2025---largest non-pandemic decline in 11 years |

**This is not cyclical---it is structural:**
- Housing affordability and domestic political pressures drove Canada/Australia caps
- Post-study work concerns and immigration politics drove UK/US restrictions
- These pressures are unlikely to reverse in the near term
- 2026 forecasts: Another 10-15% US enrollment decline if policies stand

**Second-order effects:**
- Student source country mix is shifting (more from South Asia/Africa, fewer from China/South Korea)
- Students from emerging markets have lower ability to pay full tuition
- Pricing power for universities (and payment processors) may compress

### Historical Context: The Boom Is Over

The 2015-2023 period saw record international student flows (6.9 million globally in 2024, up from ~4.5 million in 2015). This was the tailwind that built Flywire's education business. Three of four major destinations simultaneously tightening is unprecedented.

## Healthcare Payments: The Diversification Story

### Industry Structure

- US RCM market: $141.6B in 2024, projected $272.8B by 2030 (11.5% CAGR)
- Highly fragmented, consolidating
- Major players: Optum, Change Healthcare (UHG), R1 RCM, Epic Systems, McKesson
- 40% of providers outsource RCM functions; 61% plan to increase outsourcing

**Key dynamics:**
- Massive private equity interest (R1 RCM acquired for $8.9B)
- AI/automation is structural trend (74% of orgs adopting by 2025)
- Cybersecurity risk elevated (Change Healthcare breach 2024 disrupted industry)

**Flywire's healthcare position:**
- Smaller than education vertical
- Patient payment solutions, not full RCM
- Competes against much larger players with integrated offerings
- Healthcare is higher margin than education, but scaling requires significant investment

## Cross-Border Payments: B2B Opportunity

### Market Size
- Cross-border payments market: ~$213B in 2024, projected $320B by 2030 (7.1% CAGR)
- B2B segment: 60-73% of market
- Transaction corridors growing rapidly (China-ASEAN up 14% in 2024)

**Competitive dynamics:**
- Fintech entrants pressuring incumbents on transparency and pricing
- Consolidation accelerating
- AI-driven compliance and faster settlement compressing incumbent advantages

Flywire's B2B/travel verticals are growing but remain small contributors. The travel acquisition (Sertifi) is a diversification play but doesn't change the education dependency.

## Cyclicality Assessment

### Industry-Level Cyclicality: MODERATE-HIGH

**Education payments cyclicality:**
- Tied to international student flows (now structurally challenged)
- FX volatility impacts take rates and volumes
- Enrollment decisions made 12-18 months ahead (lagged impact)
- Economic conditions in source countries affect ability to pay

**Healthcare payments cyclicality:**
- Lower cyclicality (healthcare spend is defensive)
- Regulatory changes drive volatility (reimbursement policy, ICD coding updates)
- Outsourcing trend is secular, not cyclical

**Payments broadly:**
- Transaction volumes tied to economic activity
- Take rates compress in competitive periods
- Scale advantages create winner-take-more dynamics

### Current Cycle Position: LATE CYCLE / EARLY STRUCTURAL DECLINE (Education Segment)

**Evidence for late cycle / structural headwind:**
- Peak international student flows hit 2023-2024
- Policy restrictions simultaneously in 4 major destinations (unprecedented)
- US F-1 issuance and new enrollment already declining
- Flywire 2025 guidance: 10-14% revenue growth (deceleration from 22% in 2024)
- 10% workforce reduction announced (cost discipline, not growth investment)

**Counter-evidence (bull case):**
- Global student mobility projected to grow to 10M by 2030 (ICEF)
- Emerging destinations (Germany, Ireland, South Korea) growing
- Healthcare/B2B diversification reducing education dependency

## Mid-Cycle Definition

For vertical payments specialists like Flywire, "mid-cycle" economics would feature:
- International student flows growing 3-5% annually
- Stable regulatory environment in major destinations
- Take rates stable (1.5-2.5% blended)
- Revenue growth 15-20% (mix of volume + new client wins)
- Adjusted EBITDA margins 20-25%
- Client churn <5%

**Current position vs mid-cycle:**
- Student flows: Declining (below mid-cycle)
- Regulatory environment: Hostile across major markets (below mid-cycle)
- Revenue growth: Decelerating to 10-14% (approaching mid-cycle or below)
- EBITDA margins: 25-28% (at or above mid-cycle---cost discipline)

## Primary Industry Risks

1. **Student flow policy risk:** Further restrictions in major destinations; current headwinds become permanent
2. **Competitive intensity:** Universities already use multiple providers; Convera, PayMyTuition, TransferMate all improving
3. **Take rate compression:** FX spreads are transparent and commoditizing
4. **Source country mix shift:** More students from lower-income countries with less ability to pay
5. **Concentration risk:** Education ~75% of revenue; diversification slower than needed
6. **US policy escalation:** H-1B fee increase, visa terminations, travel bans reducing US attractiveness

## What Consensus May Be Missing (Expanded)

**The consensus view:**
- International student restrictions are temporary policy headwinds
- Flywire's vertical specialization creates durable competitive moat
- Healthcare and travel diversification reduces education dependency
- 10-14% growth is a "trough" that will reaccelerate

**The variant view:**
1. **The policy restrictions are structural, not cyclical.** Housing affordability, immigration politics, and domestic voter pressure drove these caps. These forces don't reverse quickly. The 2015-2023 growth era was the anomaly.

2. **Flywire's "moat" is overstated.** Unlike Toast (which controls restaurant operations), Flywire processes payments at institutions that offer 3-4 competing options. Students choose based on FX rates. This is not workflow lock-in.

3. **Diversification math doesn't work fast enough.** Healthcare and travel are growing, but from small bases. Education will dominate for years, and education is structurally challenged.

4. **The US market is now in play.** Previous analysis assumed US was the stable market while Canada/Australia/UK faced restrictions. The Trump administration's aggressive immigration policy (17% new enrollment decline, proposed visa limits, $100K H-1B fee) changes this fundamentally.

5. **Second-order effects on pricing power.** As student flows shift from China/South Korea (high ability to pay) toward South Asia/Africa (lower ability to pay), universities may face pricing pressure. This flows through to payment processor economics.

**Bullish or bearish?** This variant view is bearish for the education payments segment. The consensus may be anchoring to a growth trajectory that assumed a policy environment that no longer exists.
