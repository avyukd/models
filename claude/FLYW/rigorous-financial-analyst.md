# Flywire Corporation (FLYW) - Financial Analysis

**Stock Price:** ~$14.15 | **Market Cap:** ~$1.7B | **Analysis Date:** February 2026

---

## Executive Summary

Flywire is a payments processor for complex, high-value transactions in education, healthcare, and travel/B2B verticals. The business achieved GAAP profitability in FY2024 ($2.9M net income) for the first time, but this masks significant structural concerns: ~$65M in annual stock-based compensation, slowing revenue growth (from 39% to 22%), and deteriorating net revenue retention (114% vs. historical 120%+) due to Canadian/Australian visa policy headwinds. At ~3.5x forward revenue, the stock prices in continued execution despite material macro risks to its core education vertical.

**Variant View:** Consensus focuses on "Rule of 40" metrics and adjusted EBITDA, but SBC-adjusted economics show minimal real earnings power. The market may be underestimating the duration and magnitude of international education headwinds.

## Key Financial Drivers

**The 1-3 metrics that actually matter:**

1. **Net Revenue Retention (NRR):** At 114% (down from 120%+), this is the load-bearing metric. If NRR falls below 110%, growth stalls and the multiple compresses materially. Watch Canada/Australia visa policy closely.

2. **Education Vertical Volume:** Education is 50%+ of the business and highly seasonal. International student enrollment trends drive Q3 peak results. Any further visa restrictions are directly material.

3. **SBC as % of Revenue:** Currently ~13%, targeting 12%. Every point of improvement adds ~$5M to real earnings. This determines whether GAAP profitability is sustainable or illusory.

---

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**Non-Consensus Insight:** The market is anchoring on "Rule of 40" adjusted metrics and net cash, but the duration of international education headwinds is likely underestimated.

- Canada study permit caps are multi-year policy, not a one-quarter blip
- Australia is tightening visa requirements as of 2024-2025
- UK remains strong but cannot fully offset North American weakness
- NRR of 114% with "10 points of pressure from Canada" implies underlying NRR of ~124% - but this assumes Canada stabilizes. If it continues deteriorating, normalized NRR could fall below 115%

The street models imply a quick return to 120%+ NRR; the reality may be 2-3 years of grinding recovery. At 40-50x normalized earnings, there is limited margin of safety for prolonged weakness.

**Verdict:** The balance sheet provides downside protection (~$4.35/share in net cash), but the equity is priced for recovery that may not materialize in the expected timeframe. A more conservative entry would be below $12, implying ~3x EV/forward revenue on FY2026 estimates.
