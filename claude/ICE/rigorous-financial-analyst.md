# ICE (Intercontinental Exchange) - Financial Analysis

**Stock Price:** $173.18 | **Market Cap:** ~$100B | **Date:** February 2026

---

## Key Financial Drivers (What Actually Moves This Stock)

1. **Exchanges segment margin leverage** - 75% adjusted operating margins on 54% of revenue. Incremental volume = high incremental profit. Energy/rates volatility directly translates to earnings.

2. **Mortgage Technology turnaround** - The segment lost money on GAAP basis for 9 consecutive quarters through Q1 2025, then inflected to $22M operating income in Q3 2025. Street is pricing in continued improvement; any reversion to losses would be a negative surprise.

3. **Leverage reduction pace** - Started at ~4.5x post-Black Knight, now ~3.0x. Buybacks resumed in 2025. The capital return trajectory matters more than current yield.

---

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**The $1.3B GAAP-to-adjusted gap is almost entirely real economic cost that will persist.**

FY24 GAAP EPS: $4.78 vs Adjusted EPS: $6.07 = **$1.29 gap (~$740M pretax)**

The delta is primarily:
- Acquisition intangible amortization (~$700M annually from Black Knight)
- Integration costs (~$150M, declining)

Management presents adjusted EPS to show "run-rate" profitability, but the amortization reflects **real premium paid for Black Knight** that will depress GAAP earnings for 10+ years. The $11.9B acquisition price above tangible book must be earned back through future cash flows.

**Implication:** ICE trades at 29x GAAP earnings but only 26x "adjusted" earnings. If you believe amortization of acquired intangibles is a real cost (it represents capital deployed), the stock is less cheap than it appears. This is not a red flag - the business generates real cash - but the adjusted multiple is misleading.

## Segment Analysis

### FY24 Segment Performance

| Segment | Revenue | % of Total | Adj Op Margin | Character |

**Exchanges (the engine):**
- 12th consecutive year of record futures revenue
- Energy revenues: $1.9B (+25% YoY)
- Interest rate revenues: +30% YoY (rate divergence thesis)
- This segment generates 75%+ of profits on 54% of revenue

**Fixed Income & Data (the compounder):**
- 6% recurring revenue growth
- Index business +30% (ETF AUM growth)
- ASV improved from 2% to 4% through 2024
- High-quality, but growth decelerating

**Mortgage Technology (the drag turning asset):**
- GAAP operating loss of $170M in FY24
- Adjusted operating income of $724M (36% margin)
- Turned GAAP profitable in Q2 2025 ($11M) and Q3 2025 ($22M)
- Low single-digit revenue growth expected 2025
- $175M of $230M expense synergies achieved; remainder by end 2025

## Valuation Framework

### What Must Be True at $173.18

At 28.5x adjusted EPS ($6.07) and ~26x forward ($6.73E for 2025):
- Exchanges must maintain 75%+ margins despite volume normalization
- Mortgage Technology must achieve GAAP profitability sustainably (appears on track)
- Leverage must continue declining toward 2.5x, enabling buyback acceleration
- 10-13% EPS growth must persist (consensus: +14% 2025, +11% 2026)

### Peer Comparison

| Metric | ICE | CME | NDAQ |
|--------|-----|-----|------|
| P/E (forward) | 26x | 25x | 29x |
| LT Growth Estimate | 13% | 8% | 11% |
| PEG | 2.0x | 3.1x | 2.6x |
| FCF Yield | 3.7% | 3.5% | 3.2% |

ICE has the highest expected growth rate among major exchange peers, justifying slight premium to CME. The PEG ratio suggests reasonable value relative to growth.

### What Breaks the Thesis

1. **Mortgage origination volumes collapse** - Segment is 21% of revenue, leveraged to housing. Sub-5% mortgage rates would be a tailwind; sustained 7%+ rates would pressure volumes further.

2. **Energy/rates volatility normalizes** - Exchanges segment benefited from rate divergence and commodity volatility in 2023-24. Return to low-vol regime would compress transaction revenues.

3. **Leverage reduction stalls** - If EBITDA disappoints, the 3.0x leverage could persist, delaying buyback acceleration the market expects.

## Conclusion

ICE is a high-quality compounder with 75%+ margin exchanges business, improving mortgage segment, and strong FCF generation. The stock is **fairly valued** at ~26-29x forward earnings. Upside requires:
1. Mortgage segment sustaining GAAP profitability (on track)
2. Continued deleverage enabling buyback acceleration
3. Exchanges volume remaining elevated

**Risk:** The $1.29/share GAAP-to-adjusted gap is real capital cost that consensus may be underweighting. The stock is not cheap on normalized economic earnings.
