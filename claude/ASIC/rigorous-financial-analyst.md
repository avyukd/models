# Ategrity Specialty Insurance Company Holdings (NYSE: ASIC)
## Rigorous Financial Analysis

**Date:** January 7, 2026
**Analyst Framework:** Buy-Side Financial Diligence Standard

---

## Executive Summary

Ategrity Specialty Insurance Company Holdings is a recent IPO (June 2025) focused on the excess and surplus (E&S) lines market, serving small-to-medium businesses across property and casualty lines. The company demonstrates strong premium growth, improving combined ratios, and early signs of operating leverage. However, several analytical limitations exist due to the company's brief public history, controlled ownership structure, and the inherent uncertainty in casualty reserve development.

**Bottom Line:** Financially solid specialty insurer with genuine underwriting improvement, but the stock trades at a premium to book value that requires continued execution. The key uncertainty is whether the 88-89% combined ratio is sustainable or represents favorable loss development timing.

## 5. Loss Reserves Analysis

### Critical Uncertainty

**Available Data:**
- Management describes a "conservative approach to property loss reserves"
- Casualty mix shifting to 60-70% of target range, extending reserve tail
- Paid-to-incurred ratio rising YoY as casualty reserves mature
- No specific prior year reserve development figures available

**Assessment:**

This is the most significant analytical gap. For a specialty insurer:
- Casualty lines have long-tail liabilities (3-10+ year development)
- Reserve adequacy can only be assessed with triangular development data
- The company's short operating history (founded 2018) means reserves are still immature

**Risk:** If the 60% loss ratio reflects favorable development on prior accident years, future development could reverse. Without access to Schedule P or equivalent statutory data, this cannot be verified.

### Peer Context

E&S specialty insurers typically trade at:
- 1.0-2.5x book value depending on ROE
- 10-15x earnings for quality operators
- Premium multiples for consistent combined ratios <95%

**Ategrity's Adjusted ROE:** 15.9% (Q3 2025)

At 15.9% ROE, a 1.5-1.6x book value is reasonable but not cheap. The implied cost of equity for this valuation is:

**Implied COE = ROE / (P/B) = 15.9% / 1.56 = 10.2%**

This is a reasonable required return for a small-cap, recently-IPO'd specialty insurer.

### What Must Be True for Current Valuation

1. Combined ratio must remain sub-93% (current ~89% unsustainably good)
2. Premium growth must continue at 20%+ to justify growth multiple
3. Reserves must prove adequate (no adverse development)
4. Cat losses must remain manageable despite FL/CA concentration

### Downside Scenario

If combined ratio reverts to 96-97% (2023 levels):
- Underwriting income approaches break-even
- ROE compresses to 8-10%
- Book value multiple should compress to 1.0-1.2x
- Implied downside: $12-15/share (25-35% below current)

### Upside Scenario

If combined ratio stabilizes at 90-91%:
- ROE sustains 14-16%
- Book value compounds at 12-15% annually
- Multiple maintained at 1.5-1.7x
- Implied upside: $22-25/share (15-30% above current)

## 9. Key Financial Drivers

**The 1-3 metrics that actually matter for this stock:**

### 1. Combined Ratio (Primary Driver)

The single most important metric. Every 1 point of combined ratio = ~$4M pre-tax income on current premium base. The swing from 95% (Q3 2024) to 88.7% (Q3 2025) represents a $25M+ annual pre-tax improvement.

**Watch for:** Q4 2025 and Q1 2026 combined ratios. Management guides to 90% for Q4. If they can sustain sub-92%, the stock works. If it reverts above 94%, the thesis breaks.

### 2. Casualty Reserve Development

As casualty grows to 60-70% of the book, reserve adequacy becomes critical. The company is still young (founded 2018), so loss triangles are immature.

**Watch for:** Any disclosure of prior year reserve development (favorable or adverse). This will be the "gotcha" risk for investors who don't read the 10-Q footnotes.

### 3. Expense Ratio Trajectory

The expense ratio improvement from 33% to 28.7% is substantial operating leverage. But this only continues if premium growth persists. If growth slows, expense leverage stalls.

**Watch for:** Operating expenses as % of net earned premiums. The 10.8% ratio (net of fee income) should be sustainable, but 17.9% acquisition cost ratio has less room to improve.

## 10. What Consensus May Be Missing

### Non-Consensus Insight: The Casualty Mix Shift Creates a Ticking Clock

Consensus focuses on the combined ratio improvement and premium growth. What may be under-appreciated:

**The shift from property to casualty fundamentally changes the risk profile.**

Per earnings call commentary:
- Casualty now targeting 60-70% of mix (up from historical property-heavy mix)
- Casualty grew 41% in Q3 2025 vs 11% for property
- Management acknowledges "liability tail implications"

**Why this matters:**

1. **Loss ratio volatility will decrease** (casualty is less cat-exposed) - POSITIVE
2. **Reserve uncertainty will increase** (casualty has 5-10 year development tails) - NEGATIVE
3. **Today's reported results reflect yesterday's property-dominated book** - The current 60% loss ratio may not be representative of the future casualty-heavy book

**The market is pricing current results, but the business being built is structurally different.**

If casualty loss experience proves different from property, the 2027-2028 reserve development will reveal it. By then, the stock will have re-rated one way or the other.

### Secondary Insight: Geographic Concentration Risk

37% of GWP comes from California and Florida - two of the most challenging property markets. While management is "reducing property cat exposure," the geographic concentration remains. A major California earthquake or Florida hurricane could stress the book.

## 12. Conclusion

Ategrity is a financially solid specialty insurer demonstrating genuine operational improvement. The combined ratio compression is real, driven by both loss ratio benefits (favorable cat experience) and expense ratio improvement (operating leverage). The AM Best A- rating and post-IPO capital position provide financial stability.

**However:**
- Current earnings are ~20% above normalized levels
- Reserve adequacy cannot be verified with available data
- The casualty mix shift creates medium-term uncertainty
- The stock trades at 1.56x book, requiring continued execution

**Valuation Assessment:** Fairly valued to slightly expensive on normalized earnings. The stock is priced for continued improvement, leaving limited margin of safety if execution falters.

**If I had to take a position:** I would wait for either (a) a pullback to 1.2-1.3x book value, or (b) 2-3 more quarters of reserve development data confirming adequacy before building a position. The business quality is good, but the price requires perfection.
