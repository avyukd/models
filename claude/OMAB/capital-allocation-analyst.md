# OMAB (Grupo Aeroportuario del Centro Norte) - Management & Capital Allocation Analysis

## Executive Summary

OMAB operates 13 airports in central and northern Mexico under 50-year concessions expiring in 2048. Since December 2022, VINCI Airports (world's largest private airport operator) has held a controlling 29.99% stake. The business generates exceptional returns (ROIC ~25%, EBITDA margins ~75%) but operates within a regulatory framework that constrains both upside and downside. Management's capital allocation is largely predetermined by government-mandated Master Development Plans (MDPs), leaving limited discretionary capital to misallocate. The key question is not whether management will destroy value, but whether the regulatory and concession framework allows for meaningful value creation beyond the base case.

## 2. Incentive Structure & Alignment

### What We Know (Limited Disclosure)

OMAB is a Mexican company filing Form 20-F (foreign private issuer). Executive compensation disclosure is less granular than a U.S. DEF 14A. Available information indicates:

- Management average tenure: 6.9 years
- No disclosed stock-based compensation programs for executives
- No disclosed meaningful insider ownership stakes
- No disclosed performance-based long-term incentive plans tied to ROIC or TSR

### What This Incentive Structure Encourages

**Positive:**
- VINCI's 29.99% stake creates structural alignment at the board level
- VINCI manages airports globally; reputational capital is at stake
- VINCI paid $1.17 billion for its stake; has strong incentive to extract returns

**Negative:**
- Management (CEO, CFO, COO) has no disclosed skin in the game
- Without equity ownership, management is incentivized to optimize salaries and perks, not shareholder returns
- The regulatory structure (government-mandated capex, rate caps) means management has limited upside from outperformance anyway

### Who Benefits If The Stock Underperforms?

- Management: Likely unaffected (salaries continue, no disclosed equity at risk)
- VINCI: Directly harmed (holds $1.17B position)
- Minority shareholders: Fully exposed

**Assessment:** VINCI's ownership provides board-level alignment, but operating management appears to be salaried agents without meaningful equity incentives. This is tolerable given the regulated nature of the business but is not optimal.

## 3. Stock-Based Compensation Discipline

### Dilution Analysis

**Shares Outstanding:**
- ~386-390 million shares (stable over time)
- No disclosed stock option or equity compensation programs
- Share count has been essentially flat for years

**SBC as % of Revenue:** Effectively zero or immaterial

**Buyback Activity:**
- Minimal buyback programs ($1.5 billion pesos allocated in 2020, ~$75M USD)
- Buybacks are not a core capital return mechanism
- Share count has remained stable, not declining

**Key Finding:** Unlike U.S. tech companies where SBC is a hidden cost, OMAB appears to have negligible SBC. This is a genuine positive. Shareholders are not being diluted through employee compensation. However, this also means management has no equity upside, which is a double-edged sword.

## 4. Capital Allocation Track Record

### a) Reinvestment

**Mandated Capex:**
- Capital expenditure is not discretionary. The Mexican government mandates investment levels through 5-year Master Development Plans (MDPs)
- MDP 2021-2025: Ps. 15,993 million (~$800M USD)
- MDP 2026-2030: Ps. 16,005 million (~$800M USD)
- ~53% of MDP allocated to Monterrey Airport

**Incremental Returns:**
- ROIC: ~25.6% (per recent data)
- ROE: ~54%
- EBITDA margins: 73-75%

**Assessment:** Returns on capital are exceptional, but this reflects the monopoly nature of airport concessions rather than management skill. The regulated structure means capex levels are predetermined; management cannot over-invest or under-invest. This limits both upside from brilliant capital allocation and downside from empire building.

### b) M&A

**Historical Activity:** Minimal

OMAB has not pursued material M&A. The company operates under concessions that define its airport portfolio. Key non-airport investments:

- 2009: NH Hoteles partnership (287-room hotel at AICM Terminal 2)
- 2015: Hilton Garden Inn at Monterrey Airport
- 2015: Industrial Park at Monterrey (JV with VYNMSA)

**Assessment:** M&A discipline is high by default. The concession structure prevents OMAB from acquiring other airport groups. The ancillary investments (hotels, industrial park) are small, related to core operations, and appear value-accretive (industrial park revenue up 53% YoY in recent quarter). Management has not destroyed value through empire-building acquisitions.

### c) Dividends & Capital Returns

**Dividend Policy:**
- Dividends paid every year since 2006 IPO
- Current yield: ~6-7%
- Payout ratio: ~83-84% of earnings
- FCF payout ratio: ~95% (nearly all FCF returned)
- Recent dividend: $5.62/share annually

**Dividend History:**
- Stable dividends of ~Ps. 1.6 billion annually (~$70-80M USD)
- 5 dividend increases in past 5 years
- 20% dividend growth over past 5 years

**Assessment:** Dividends are the primary capital return mechanism. The high FCF payout ratio (~95%) signals management prioritizes returns to shareholders over cash hoarding. However, this also means limited financial flexibility for opportunistic investments or crisis buffers.

### d) Debt Usage

**Current Position:**
- Net Debt/EBITDA: 0.91x (very low)
- Debt/Equity: 1.33
- Cash: $242M; Debt: $740M
- Investment grade balance sheet

**Assessment:** Conservative leverage. OMAB maintains low debt specifically because it knows upcoming MDP capex cycles will require funding. This is prudent capital structure management. The 2026-2030 MDP will require ~$800M in capex, and management has preserved balance sheet capacity to fund this without distressed equity issuance.

## 5. Learning vs. Repetition

**Pattern Recognition:**

| Event | Response | Evidence of Learning |
|-------|----------|---------------------|
| COVID-19 (2020-2021) | Maintained dividend, no equity raise | Discipline held under stress |
| Concession fee increase (5% to 9%, 2024) | Accepted regulatory change, passed through costs | No confrontational approach |
| VINCI acquisition (2022) | Integrated VINCI operational practices | Brought in VINCI executives for CTO, CCO roles |
| MDP capex cycles | Maintained low leverage ahead of capex | Anticipated capital needs |

**Assessment:** Management has demonstrated consistent, if unspectacular, behavior. No evidence of learning from mistakes because there is little evidence of mistakes. The regulated framework constrains variance in outcomes. This is a "don't mess it up" business, and management has not messed it up.

## 6. Forward-Looking Capital Allocation Risk

### Where Capital Is Most Likely To Go

1. **MDP Capex (High Certainty):** Ps. 16B over 2026-2030. Half to Monterrey expansion. This is mandated, not discretionary.

2. **Dividends (High Certainty):** ~95% FCF payout ratio likely to continue. VINCI needs cash flow from its investment.

3. **Debt Paydown/Modest Leverage Increase (Moderate):** May lever up modestly to fund MDP while maintaining dividend.

### Where Value Could Leak

1. **Concession Renewal Risk (2048):** The concession expires in 2048. Extension is possible (50-year extensions allowed under law) but not guaranteed. As 2048 approaches, terminal value uncertainty increases. Management has no control over this outcome.

2. **Regulatory Extraction:** The 2024 concession fee increase (5% to 9%) demonstrates the Mexican government's willingness to extract rents. Future fee increases or tariff caps could compress margins.

3. **Currency Risk:** Revenue in pesos, ADR in dollars. Peso depreciation directly impacts USD returns. Management cannot hedge this.

4. **Overbuilding Risk (Low):** Monterrey expansion is aggressive (terminal capacity from 5.9M to 12.5M passengers). If nearshoring tailwinds fade, capacity could exceed demand. However, MDP terms are government-approved, limiting management's discretion.

## 7. The One Thing That Determines Value Creation

**The Single Most Important Factor:**

Whether the Mexican government extends the concession beyond 2048 on favorable terms.

**Why This Dominates:**

- Management cannot materially outperform or underperform in operating the airports. Margins are 75%, ROIC is 25%, and the regulatory structure ensures stability.
- Capital allocation is largely mandated (MDP capex) or constrained (dividends to satisfy VINCI).
- The only source of transformational value creation or destruction is the terminal value question: Does OMAB operate these airports for another 50 years after 2048, or does the government reclaim them?

VINCI's involvement increases the probability of favorable extension (VINCI has negotiated long-term concessions globally), but the outcome is ultimately political.

**What Would Change Behavior:**

- A hostile regulatory environment (more fee increases, tariff caps) could push VINCI to under-invest
- Political regime change in Mexico could alter concession renewal prospects
- If nearshoring reverses, capacity expansion could become stranded

## 8. What Consensus May Be Missing

**Non-Consensus Insight:**

**Consensus View:** OMAB is a high-quality, wide-moat infrastructure asset with a disciplined operator. Buy for the dividend and nearshoring exposure.

**What Consensus Misses:**

1. **VINCI Controls More Than 30%:** Despite owning "only" 29.99%, VINCI effectively controls the company. The board chair is VINCI's CEO of Concessions. The CTO and CCO are VINCI veterans. Management succession is in VINCI's hands. This is de facto control without paying a control premium. Minority shareholders are along for the ride.

2. **The 95% FCF Payout Ratio Is VINCI Extracting Cash:** A 95% FCF payout ratio is not a sign of shareholder-friendly management. It is VINCI extracting cash from its investment as quickly as possible. VINCI paid $1.17B for 30% of a ~$4B market cap company. They need dividends to generate returns. Minority shareholders benefit from this alignment, but should understand the dividend is not discretionary generosity.

3. **Management Is Not Aligned; VINCI Is Aligned:** CEO Ricardo Duenas has no disclosed equity ownership. He is a caretaker manager implementing VINCI's strategy. This is fine because VINCI's interests are aligned with shareholders. But if VINCI ever sold its stake, minority shareholders would be left with an unaligned management team.

4. **The Concession Fee Increase Was A Warning Shot:** The 2024 increase from 5% to 9% of gross revenue was unilaterally imposed. The government signaled it views airport concessions as a source of fiscal revenue. Future fee increases are possible. The "stable regulatory environment" narrative may be overstated.

**Does This Make Me More or Less Trusting?**

Net neutral. VINCI's alignment substitutes for management alignment. The regulatory risk is underappreciated but not imminent. The business quality is real. I would not pay a premium for "great management" because management is not the driver of outcomes.

## 10. Can Management Be Trusted With Capital?

**Answer:** This is the wrong question for OMAB.

Management does not have discretion over capital. Capex is mandated by MDPs. Dividends are driven by VINCI's need for cash returns. M&A is precluded by the concession structure.

The right question is: **Can VINCI be trusted to protect minority shareholder interests?**

Answer: Probably yes, as long as VINCI's interests remain aligned (i.e., they continue to own 30%). VINCI is a sophisticated, long-term infrastructure operator. They overpaid for their stake ($1.17B for 30% of a ~$4B company) and need the investment to work.

**Where Value Is Most Likely To Leak:**

1. Regulatory fee increases (government extraction)
2. Peso depreciation (currency risk)
3. Concession non-renewal or unfavorable renewal terms (terminal value risk)
4. If VINCI sells, minority shareholders would be left with unaligned management
