## Key Drivers (The 1-3 Things That Actually Move This Stock)

1. **Monterrey Passenger Traffic Growth**: This is a Monterrey bet disguised as a 13-airport portfolio. Monterrey represents ~42% of revenue, ~53% of MDP capex, and nearly all incremental value. Nearshoring is real, but the market knows it.

2. **Regulatory Tolerance for Private Airport Profits**: The October 2023 concession fee increase (5% to 9%) demonstrated the Mexican government will extract rents when it perceives excess returns. This is not a one-time reset; it is a structural feature. Future margin compression is government-controlled, not market-controlled.

3. **P&W Engine Recall Resolution**: 2024's traffic "weakness" was supply-constrained (30-40% of Volaris/VivaAerobus fleet grounded), not demand weakness. Resolution by 2026-2027 should release pent-up traffic. This is a near-term catalyst but not a variant view.

## Thesis in 3-5 Bullets

- OMAB is a high-quality infrastructure asset with 75% EBITDA margins, 25% ROIC, and <1x Net Debt/EBITDA, operating under 50-year concessions (2048 expiry with renewal rights)
- The investment case hinges entirely on Monterrey: a nearshoring beneficiary with 14% traffic growth, industrial vacancy at 0.82%, and 22% of Mexico's 2021-2024 nearshoring investment
- Regulatory risk is asymmetric and under-appreciated: government controls tariffs, can raise concession fees unilaterally, and operates competing airports (AIFA) it needs to fill
- VINCI controls the company in practice (board chair, CTO, CCO are VINCI), and the 95% FCF payout is VINCI extracting cash, not shareholder-friendly generosity
- At 10x EV/EBITDA and 17x earnings, the stock is fairly priced for mid-single-digit traffic growth and current margin levels; no mispricing to exploit

## The Setup (Only What Matters)

OMAB operates 13 airports in central and northern Mexico under exclusive 50-year concessions expiring in 2048. The business is effectively a Monterrey airport bet with 12 smaller supporting assets. Monterrey is Mexico's third-largest metro, the heart of the nearshoring boom, and home to VivaAerobus's hub.

Three structural forces define the setup:

1. **Nearshoring tailwind is real but priced**: Monterrey industrial vacancy is 0.82%. The region captured 22% of nearshoring FDI from 2021-2024. OMAB's 14% Monterrey traffic growth in Q3 2025 reflects this. But every Mexican infrastructure investor knows this story.

2. **Regulatory asymmetry dominates**: The October 2023 tariff reset was not an anomaly; it was a precedent. The government can modify concession terms unilaterally, and it has political incentive to do so (AIFA needs traffic and subsidies). Upside is capped by regulated TUA rates; downside is government-controlled.

3. **Airline concentration creates countervailing power**: VivaAerobus (50% of traffic) and Volaris (23%) together control 73% of OMA's passengers. The proposed Volaris-VivaAerobus merger would create a 70%+ domestic market share carrier with significant negotiating leverage over airports. Even without merger approval, airline oligopoly offsets airport monopoly.

## Forward Path and Catalysts

### Near-Term (0-12 months)
- **P&W engine availability improvement**: As GTF recalls resolve, Volaris/VivaAerobus fleet utilization should recover to 85%+. Traffic growth could accelerate to 8-10%. This is expected, not a surprise.
- **Monthly traffic reports**: Sustained Monterrey growth above 10% would validate nearshoring thesis. But the market is watching this closely.
- **Volaris-VivaAerobus merger ruling (CNA)**: Expected 2026. Approval terms matter more than yes/no. Merger with conditions could be neutral to modestly negative for airports.

### Medium-Term (12-24 months)
- **MDP 2026-2030 execution**: MXN 16B capex commitment, with 49% directed to Monterrey. If traffic disappoints but capex is locked in, margins compress. This is an embedded put option on capital that OMA cannot escape.
- **AIFA traffic share trends**: If government successfully diverts traffic to AIFA, precedent worsens for private concessionaires.

### What the Market Will Notice Next
The market will focus on: (1) whether Monterrey traffic growth sustains at 10%+, (2) merger outcome, and (3) any signals of additional regulatory intervention. None of these are actionable variant views.

## Risks and Kill Switches

### What Invalidates the Thesis (If We Had a Position)

1. **Government raises concession fee above 12%**: This would signal political will to compress margins toward utility-like returns. Terminal value would reprice lower.

2. **Monterrey-specific shock**: Manufacturing exodus, tariff disruption from US-Mexico trade policy, or security deterioration. A 20% Monterrey traffic decline would drive 25-30% earnings decline.

3. **Airline distress**: If Volaris or VivaAerobus enters financial difficulty (post-P&W cash burn, merger integration failure), traffic and TUA collections would suffer immediately.

4. **VINCI sells stake**: If VINCI exits, minority shareholders would be left with unaligned, non-owner management.

### What Forces Re-evaluation

- Traffic growth consistently below 3% despite P&W resolution
- Additional regulatory extraction (tariff caps, fee increases)
- VINCI increasing related-party fees or signaling reduced commitment

## Positioning and Portfolio Logic

### Why This Does Not Deserve Capital Now

This is a fairly valued, high-quality infrastructure asset with no clear edge. The thesis requires believing:
- Nearshoring is bigger than consensus thinks (no evidence of this)
- Regulatory risk is lower than consensus fears (October 2023 says otherwise)
- The market has mispriced a widely followed, liquid ADR (unlikely)

The stock yields 5-6% and will likely compound at mid-to-high single digits if nothing goes wrong. That is fine for a passive infrastructure allocation, but it is not a hedge fund opportunity.

### If Forced to Size

If we owned this, it would be a 1% position at most: a dividend-paying infrastructure stub with EM exposure. No conviction to go larger. No conviction to short.

### Pair/Hedge Considerations

If constructing a nearshoring basket, OMAB would be a reasonable component alongside US industrials exposed to Mexico manufacturing. But as a standalone long or short, there is no edge.

### What I Would Watch Post-Entry

- Monthly Monterrey traffic reports
- Volaris/VivaAerobus merger ruling and terms
- Any AFAC communications on tariff structures
- Peso/USD rate movements

## Final Assessment

OMAB is a legitimate high-quality business: 75% EBITDA margins, 25% ROIC, <1x leverage, and a regulated monopoly on Monterrey. The nearshoring tailwind is real. VINCI ownership provides board-level alignment.

But there is no variant perception. The market knows the nearshoring story. The market absorbed the October 2023 regulatory shock. The valuation at 10x EV/EBITDA is neither cheap nor expensive for a regulated EM infrastructure asset with political risk.

**What Consensus Gets Right:**
- High-quality, cash-generative asset
- Nearshoring beneficiary
- Conservative balance sheet
- Defensible franchise

**What Consensus Underweights:**
- Regulatory risk is asymmetric (upside capped, downside government-controlled)
- VINCI extracts value through fees and dividend requirements
- MDP capex is a binding put option on capital
- Airline consolidation creates countervailing power

**The Honest Answer:**

This is a "fine company at a fair price." There is nothing broken. There is nothing mispriced. There is no catalyst that the market is missing. The nearshoring story that would make this interesting is already consensus.

**If the pitch is "nearshoring tailwind + quality business + reasonable valuation," that is not a hedge fund thesis. That is an index fund rationale.**

Passing is the correct decision.

Not because the business is bad. Not because we are bearish. Because there is no edge. The stock will likely do what the market expects it to do.

We allocate capital where we have a view the market is wrong. Here, the market appears to be approximately right. Move on.
