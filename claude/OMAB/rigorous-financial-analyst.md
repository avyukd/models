# OMAB (Grupo Aeroportuario del Centro Norte) - Financial Analysis

## Executive Summary

OMAB operates 13 airport concessions in central and northern Mexico under 50-year government contracts expiring in 2048. The business is a high-margin, regulated infrastructure asset generating 74-78% EBITDA margins with strong cash conversion. Since December 2022, VINCI Airports holds a controlling 29.99% stake.

**Bottom Line**: OMAB is a genuine cash flow machine with exceptional unit economics, but two structural issues dominate the investment case: (1) Monterrey concentration risk (approximately 50% of traffic and revenue) and (2) the new 9% concession tax that permanently reset the economics in January 2024. The market appears to have absorbed the concession tax change but may be underappreciating the MDP capex ramp from MXN ~4B/year to MXN 16B over 2026-2030.

## 1. Business Financial Profile

| Characteristic | Assessment |
|----------------|------------|
| Capital Intensity | Low-to-moderate; concession assets require periodic expansion per MDP commitments |
| Cost Structure | Heavily fixed; operating leverage is significant on passenger throughput |
| Operating Leverage | High; incremental passengers flow through at 70%+ margins |
| Cyclicality | Moderate; tied to Mexican GDP, tourism, and nearshoring trends |
| Revenue Quality | Mixed; aeronautical revenues (~62%) are regulated/capped; non-aeronautical (~38%) is unregulated and higher growth |
| Concession Duration | Until 2048 with potential 50-year extension subject to government terms |

**Key Structural Feature**: Aeronautical tariffs (TUA) are negotiated every 5 years as part of the Master Development Plan (MDP). This provides revenue stability but caps upside. Non-aeronautical revenue (parking, retail, VIP lounges, car rental, hotel, industrial park) is unregulated and represents the primary margin expansion lever.

## 2. Income Statement Analysis

### Revenue Trends (MXN Billions)

| Year | Revenue | YoY Growth | Notes |
|------|---------|------------|-------|
| 2021 | 8.72B | Recovery year | COVID rebound |
| 2022 | 11.93B | +36.8% | Strong post-COVID recovery |
| 2023 | 14.46B | +21.1% | Peak growth rate |
| 2024 | 15.07B | +4.3% | Growth deceleration; concession tax impact |

**Critical Observation**: The 2024 growth deceleration to 4.3% from 21%+ reflects the new structural reality. This is NOT a temporary slowdown - the 9% concession tax (up from 5%) took effect January 1, 2024, representing a permanent ~4% revenue headwind absorbed through higher costs.

### Margin Evolution

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | TTM Q3 2025 |
|--------|------|------|------|-------------|
| Adjusted EBITDA Margin | ~72% | 75.8%-77.5% | ~74-75% | 74.8% |
| Operating Margin | ~54% | ~56% | ~56% | 61.2% |
| Net Margin | ~31% | ~35% | ~33% | ~33% |

**EBITDA Margin Context**: Mexican airports historically earned 65-70% EBITDA margins for years. COVID relief measures temporarily boosted margins to 75%+. The October 2023 tariff change was explicitly designed to "roll back excess profitability." Current ~75% margins remain elevated vs. historical norms and may face pressure as MDP capex accelerates.

### Earnings Gap: GAAP vs. Economic

OMAB reports under IFRS. Key adjustments to monitor:

1. **Depreciation of Concession Rights**: These are amortized over the concession life. This is a real economic cost representing the consumption of a finite asset.

2. **Stock-Based Compensation**: OMAB operates as a Mexican company with VINCI as controlling shareholder. SBC appears minimal compared to US tech companies - management compensation flows primarily through cash. This is a positive for earnings quality.

3. **Construction Revenue/Costs**: IFRS requires airports to recognize construction under concession arrangements as both revenue and cost (pass-through). This inflates both lines but nets to zero margin impact. Always analyze "excluding construction costs" metrics.

| **Net Debt/EBITDA** | ~0.9x |

### Debt Quality Assessment

- **Credit Rating**: AAA (Mexico local scale) per Fitch and Moody's for recent 2025 bond issuance
- **Recent Refinancing**: MXN 2.75B local bonds in 2025 (MXN 820M 3-year floating, MXN 1.93B 7-year fixed at 9.34%)
- **Debt Mix**: Combination of local bonds and bank facilities; primarily peso-denominated (natural hedge)
- **Covenant Headroom**: Not explicitly disclosed but AAA ratings suggest comfortable coverage

**Assessment**: Balance sheet is conservatively leveraged at <1x Net Debt/EBITDA. This provides capacity for the MXN 16B MDP capex commitment through 2030. No immediate refinancing or liquidity concerns.

### Dilution Risk

- **Shares Outstanding**: 386.17M (stable)
- **No History of Dilution**: No stock splits, no significant equity issuance
- **SBC Impact**: Minimal; management compensation primarily cash-based
- **Dividend Payout**: 81-84% of earnings; high payout reduces retained capital for buybacks

**Assessment**: Dilution risk is negligible. OMAB is a return-of-capital story through dividends, not a growth-through-retention story.

## 8. Key Financial Drivers (What Actually Matters)

### The 1-3 Metrics That Drive This Stock

1. **Monterrey Passenger Traffic Growth**: Monterrey is ~50% of revenue and the company's crown jewel. Any inflection - positive (nearshoring boom, new routes) or negative (recession, competitive hub) - moves the needle on consolidated results. Watch monthly traffic reports.

2. **Non-Aeronautical Revenue per Passenger**: This is the margin expansion lever. Aeronautical tariffs are capped by regulation. Non-aero revenue (parking, retail, VIP lounges, industrial park) is unregulated. Growth here flows straight to EBITDA. Current non-aero growth of 8-13% YoY is the leading indicator of margin trajectory.

3. **MDP Capex Execution Risk**: The MXN 16B 2026-2030 commitment is binding. If traffic disappoints but capex is locked in, margins compress. If traffic exceeds, returns are exceptional. The spread between actual passenger growth and committed capex determines whether OMAB is a 10x or 15x EBITDA business.

**Why These Matter More Than Others**: Revenue growth, EBITDA margin, and FCF are all downstream of these three drivers. Analysts who model "revenue grows 6%" without understanding the Monterrey concentration and non-aero mix miss the structural story.

## 9. What Consensus May Be Missing

### Non-Obvious Insight: The VINCI Technical Assistance Fee Leakage

Post the 2022 acquisition, VINCI Airports extracts value through "technical assistance fees" that appear as an operating cost. While small (~2-3% of costs), this represents a permanent transfer of value from minority shareholders to the controlling party.

**Why This Matters**:
- These fees are effectively guaranteed and grow with revenue
- VINCI has no incentive to minimize them (agency conflict)
- The announced "best practices" and "capital deployment" synergies largely benefit VINCI's global network, with OMA paying for the privilege
- Over a 25-year horizon, these fees compound to hundreds of millions in value extraction

A typical analyst celebrates VINCI ownership as governance improvement. A skeptical analyst recognizes the cost.

### Secondary Insight: Concession Tax Floor, Not Ceiling

The October 2023 concession tax change (5% to 9%) was framed as a one-time adjustment. However, the stated government purpose was to fund military-operated airports competing with private concessionaires. This creates an adversarial dynamic where higher private airport profits incentivize further fee increases.

The 9% rate should be modeled as a floor, not a permanent state. Additional increases to 12-15% are plausible over the concession life.

### Third Insight: MDP Capex Is a Binding Put Option on Capital

The Master Development Plan commits OMAB to specific investments regardless of traffic outcomes. If Mexico enters recession and passenger traffic declines 20%, OMAB still owes the government MXN 16B in airport improvements. This is an embedded put option that the company cannot escape.

Currently, this risk is not priced because traffic is strong. But the asymmetry is real: upside is capped by regulated tariffs, downside is floored by committed capex.

## Conclusion

OMAB is a genuine high-quality infrastructure asset with exceptional cash generation characteristics. The 74-75% EBITDA margins, <1x leverage, and 25%+ ROIC are rare in global infrastructure.

However, the investment case hinges on understanding that:
1. This is a Monterrey bet first and a 13-airport portfolio second
2. The 9% concession tax reset is permanent and possibly a floor
3. VINCI ownership brings operational benefits but also value extraction via fees
4. MDP commitments create asymmetric downside in traffic decline scenarios

At 10x EV/EBITDA, the market is pricing a "fair but not cheap" valuation. The stock is appropriate for investors seeking infrastructure-like income with emerging market exposure, but it is not a mispriced opportunity. True variant perception would require a view on either Monterrey's nearshoring trajectory or the likelihood of further regulatory changes - both of which are fundamentally unpredictable.

**Final Assessment**: Financially solid, appropriately valued, with hidden frictions (VINCI fees, MDP commitments) that consensus glosses over.
