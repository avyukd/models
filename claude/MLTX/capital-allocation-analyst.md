## 2. Incentive Structure & Alignment

### Compensation Design

- **CEO total compensation:** $1.41M (per 2025 proxy), split ~50/50 between salary and bonuses/equity. This is below the $3.46M median for US companies of comparable market cap.
- **2024 base salary increases:** 5% YoY for Named Executive Officers.
- **Annual bonus payout:** 150% of target in 2024, based on goal achievement.
- **Equity plans:** MoonLake Immunotherapeutics 2022 Equity Incentive Plan (primary active plan). Legacy ESOP fully vested as of Jan 2024. Restricted Founder Shares fully vested as of Apr 2023.
- **Peer group:** Immunovant, Bicycle Therapeutics, Vera Therapeutics, Verona Pharma, Summit Therapeutics, and others -- selected by market cap and headcount.
- **Clawback policy:** In place. No hedging/pledging permitted. No tax gross-ups.

### What This Incentive Structure Encourages

The compensation is modest by US biotech standards. The critical alignment mechanism is founder-level equity ownership, not contractual incentives. With ~4% ownership, the CEO's wealth is heavily tied to share price.

**However, insider behavior tells a different story:**

- CEO Santos da Silva sold 184,740 shares in Feb 2024 at ~$55/share ($10.2M proceeds) and 200,000 shares in late 2025 at ~$15/share ($3.0M proceeds). Total insider sales across 2024-2025: ~$13M in proceeds.
- CSO Kristian Reich sold ~200K+ shares, reducing his position by 64%.
- Former 10% owner Bihua Chen (SPAC sponsor-linked) dumped ~6.5M shares at ~$7 in Sep-Oct 2025, a 76% reduction in her position -- timed right around the Phase 3 failure.

**Who benefits if the stock underperforms?** Management had already monetized significant equity at high prices ($55 range) before the Phase 3 crash. The Feb 2024 sales, while pre-dating the failure by 18 months, occurred while Phase 3 was ongoing and before results were known. The CSO's 64% position reduction is a notable signal of declining conviction or simple liquidation.

## 4. Capital Allocation Track Record

### a) Reinvestment

MoonLake is pre-revenue and clinical-stage. All capital deployment is R&D spending. The key question is whether clinical dollars were well-spent.

- **R&D spend trajectory:** $31.8M (2023) to $112.8M (2024), a 3.5x increase driven by Phase 3 VELA program and IZAR (PsA).
- **Outcome of primary R&D spend:** The Phase 3 VELA program -- representing the bulk of the R&D ramp -- produced a catastrophic result. VELA-2 failed its primary endpoint (p=0.053). Even VELA-1, which achieved statistical significance, showed efficacy substantially below the competitor (BIMZELX). The stock dropped 90% in a single day.
- **Cash runway:** $394M as of Dec 31, 2025, projected to fund operations through second half of 2027.

**Judgment:** The company's single largest capital allocation decision -- pouring $100M+ into Phase 3 for sonelokimab in HS -- produced an outcome that destroyed ~$3B of market capitalization. The drug's differentiation claim (tri-specific Nanobody superiority over monoclonal antibodies) was the thesis, and Phase 3 data failed to support it. Whether this reflects poor science selection, trial design errors, or simply the inherent risk of drug development is debatable. But the concentration risk -- one drug, one lead indication -- meant this capital allocation failure was existential for the equity.

### b) M&A

No M&A activity to evaluate. MoonLake is a single-asset company developing one drug (sonelokimab) across multiple indications. Not applicable.

### c) Buybacks & Issuance

No buybacks -- consistent with a pre-revenue, cash-burning biotech. The company is a serial equity issuer:

- SPAC close: ~$230M raised ($115M trust + $115M PIPE at $10/share)
- 2023: $250M public offering + $200M ATM facility
- Nov 2025: $75M offering at $10.50/share

**Total capital raised since going public: approximately $555M+ in equity.**

**Total cumulative net losses: ~$270M (FY2021-FY2024).**

The company has raised roughly 2x what it has spent to date, which explains the remaining ~$394M cash position. However, the latest $75M raise at $10.50 -- representing equity issuance after a 90% crash -- is a textbook example of financing from a position of weakness. Management had to issue shares at a fraction of the price they were worth months earlier because the clinical failure eliminated their ability to raise on favorable terms.

### d) Debt Usage

Debt-to-equity ratio of 0.25 as of latest reporting. MoonLake carries minimal debt, consistent with a clinical-stage biotech model. Current ratio of 8.5x suggests ample liquidity. No concerns here -- the company is almost entirely equity-financed.

## 5. Learning vs. Repetition

This is a young public company (listed Apr 2022), so the track record is limited. What can be assessed:

- **Trial design:** The VELA-2 failure has been partially attributed to unexpectedly high placebo response rates. Whether the company should have anticipated this and designed the trial differently (larger enrollment, different primary endpoint, different comparator) is a legitimate question. The Phase 2 data that supported the Phase 3 investment showed strong results, but Phase 2-to-Phase 3 transitions are the highest-risk moment in biotech.
- **Pivot speed:** Post-failure, management moved quickly to schedule a Type B FDA meeting (Dec 15, 2025) to discuss a BLA pathway based on the available evidence package. Preliminary data showing continued efficacy improvement through week 24 (~50%+ HiSCR75) suggest the drug may work, just not as impressively as hyped. The company is also advancing other indications (PsA, axSpA, PPP) as de-risking efforts.
- **Capital preservation:** The $75M raise in Nov 2025, while dilutive, extended runway through H2 2027. This was a necessary, if painful, decision.

**Judgment:** Too early to assess a pattern of learning vs. repetition. The critical question is whether management overmarketed sonelokimab's differentiation during the class period (Mar 2024 - Sep 2025), which is the core allegation in the pending securities fraud lawsuit.

## 6. Forward-Looking Capital Allocation Risk

### Where Capital Will Go

- **Remaining clinical programs:** IZAR (PsA Phase 3), S-OLARIS (axSpA Phase 2 -- 81% ASAS40 result is promising), LEDA (PPP), VELA-TEEN (adolescent HS). These represent meaningful optionality but also continued cash burn.
- **Potential BLA filing in HS:** If the FDA accepts the existing evidence package, commercial build-out costs will begin. If not, the company faces additional trials and years of delay.
- **Cash burn trajectory:** Operating cash burn of ~$186M TTM (through Sep 2025) against ~$394M in cash. At current burn rate, runway is ~2 years without additional capital raises.

### Primary Risk Scenarios

1. **Another dilutive equity raise at depressed prices.** If the FDA requires additional HS trials, or if other indications fail, the company will need to raise capital again -- potentially at prices well below $20. The 2.4x share count expansion since SPAC close could easily become 3-4x.
2. **Securities fraud litigation overhang.** Multiple class action suits pending. Even if ultimately dismissed or settled, the litigation creates uncertainty and may limit management's ability to raise capital on favorable terms.
3. **Single-asset concentration.** Despite multiple indications, all programs involve the same molecule (sonelokimab). A safety signal or mechanism-class concern would be catastrophic.

## The One Thing That Determines Value Creation

**Whether the FDA grants a BLA pathway for sonelokimab in HS based on existing data.**

This single regulatory decision dominates all other capital allocation considerations. If the FDA accepts the existing evidence (VELA-1 success + extended data), the company can pursue commercialization and the equity story rebuilds. If the FDA requires a third pivotal trial, the company faces 2-3 additional years of cash burn ($300M+), likely requiring one or more dilutive capital raises from a position of weakness, further compounding the share count expansion that has already eroded per-share value by ~60% since listing.

Management cannot control the FDA outcome, but their prior capital allocation choices -- concentrating all resources into one molecule, aggressively marketing differentiation claims that Phase 3 did not support -- created this binary situation. The capital allocation failure is not the R&D spend itself, but the failure to manage expectations and the resulting destruction of the equity currency needed to fund operations going forward.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**The insider selling pattern deserves more scrutiny than it receives.**

Three observations that typical analysts overlook:

1. **CEO and CSO both sold significant equity in Feb 2024 at ~$55/share**, while Phase 3 was ongoing. These were structured 10b5-1 plan sales, which provides legal cover, but the timing meant insiders monetized at peak valuation while outside investors bore the full 90% drawdown. The CEO realized ~$10M and the CSO realized ~$1.6M from sales before the crash.

2. **Former SPAC sponsor-linked shareholder Bihua Chen dumped 76% of her position at ~$7 on the day of the crash (Sep 29-30, 2025).** This $47M liquidation by the entity most closely linked to the original SPAC structure suggests the SPAC sponsors' alignment with long-term shareholders was always temporary. The SPAC channel (Cormorant/Helix) has moved on -- Cormorant has already launched Helix Acquisition Corp. III.

3. **The Nov 2025 equity raise at $10.50 transferred ~7.1M shares to new investors at 83% below the pre-crash price.** These new investors are better positioned than anyone who owned shares before September 2025. The equity raise was necessary for survival, but it effectively rewarded crash buyers at the expense of pre-existing shareholders -- a wealth transfer characteristic of biotech capital allocation failures.

**Net assessment:** The insider selling pattern makes me *less* trusting of management alignment at the margin. The CEO's absolute ownership (~$54M at peak) sounds meaningful, but ~$10M was already monetized before the crash, and the remaining stake at current prices (~$14.9M at $5.05 or ~$54M at $18.31) is a fraction of peak value. The CSO's 64% position reduction is harder to explain away.
