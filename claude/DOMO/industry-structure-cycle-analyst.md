# Industry Structure & Cycle Analysis: Business Intelligence / Data Analytics Software

## Prepared for: DOMO Inc. (NASDAQ: DOMO) Evaluation Context
## Date: February 20, 2026

---

## EXECUTIVE FINDING

**The BI/analytics software industry is undergoing a structural consolidation that is existentially threatening to sub-scale standalone vendors.** Hyperscaler bundling (Microsoft Power BI, Google Looker) has permanently altered industry economics by collapsing the price floor for "good enough" BI. The survivors among standalone vendors will be those with either (a) sufficient scale to invest in AI/agentic analytics, or (b) differentiated positioning that hyperscalers cannot replicate through bundling. DOMO, at ~$318M revenue with flat growth, 88% net retention, negative equity, and debt maturing April 2026, falls into neither category. The strategic alternatives process is not optional -- it is the rational outcome of industry structure.

## 3. THE HYPERSCALER BUNDLING PROBLEM

This is the single most important structural force in the BI industry.

### The Mechanism

1. Microsoft bundles Power BI Pro at $14/user/month with Microsoft 365 E5 licenses (effectively "free" for many enterprises)
2. Google bundles Looker into Google Cloud Platform commitments
3. Salesforce bundles Tableau (CRM Analytics) into its platform
4. AWS promotes QuickSight as native BI within its ecosystem

### The Economic Impact

| Factor | Pre-Bundling Era (2010-2018) | Post-Bundling Era (2019-Present) |
|--------|------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| Standalone BI ASP | $30-60/user/month | Compressed to $15-25/user/month |
| Enterprise deal cycles | 3-6 months | 6-12+ months (incumbent advantage) |
| Customer switching costs | Moderate | HIGH (ecosystem lock-in favors hyperscalers) |
| Standalone vendor growth | 15-30% typical | Decelerating; <10% for mature standalone |
| Net retention rates (standalone) | 110-120% | 85-100% (under consolidation pressure) |

### Who Survives Bundling

| Survival Strategy | Example | Viability |
|-------------------|---------|-----------|
| Get acquired by platform | Tableau (Salesforce), Looker (Google) | Proven path |
| Scale + differentiation | Qlik (data integration + analytics) | Viable at >$600M revenue |
| AI-native disruptor | ThoughtSpot, Sigma | Viable if growth sustains |
| Embedded analytics niche | Sisense | Narrow but defensible |
| Sub-scale, undifferentiated | **DOMO** | NOT viable long-term |

## 5. AI / LLM DISRUPTION ANALYSIS

### How AI Changes BI

| Dimension | Impact on Traditional BI | Impact on Standalone Vendors |
|-----------|--------------------------|------------------------------|
| Natural Language Query (NLQ) | Reduces need for pre-built dashboards | Commoditizes basic BI; favors vendors with deep AI investment |
| Agentic Analytics | Automates multi-step analysis | Favors hyperscalers with LLM infrastructure (Microsoft Copilot, Google Gemini) |
| Text-to-SQL | Democratizes data access | Lowers barriers for new entrants; threatens existing moats |
| Semantic Layer | Becomes critical infrastructure | Benefits vendors with strong data modeling (Looker, dbt) |
| Embedded AI Copilots | Table stakes by 2026 | Sub-scale vendors cannot match hyperscaler AI investment |

### The AI Arms Race Favors Scale

Microsoft is investing $80B+ annually in AI infrastructure. Google is spending comparably. These companies can embed AI into Power BI and Looker at effectively zero marginal cost. A standalone vendor like DOMO (~$318M revenue) cannot match this R&D intensity. Per McKinsey, GenAI could automate 60-70% of data processing and decision-making tasks -- this fundamentally shrinks the addressable market for manual-configuration BI tools.

### AI Creates Both Threat and (Limited) Opportunity

- **Threat:** "Good enough" AI-powered BI from hyperscalers eliminates the mid-market standalone BI value proposition
- **Opportunity:** Specialized AI analytics for specific verticals/use cases may remain defensible
- **Net assessment:** AI is a net NEGATIVE for sub-scale standalone BI vendors and a net POSITIVE for hyperscaler-bundled solutions

## 6. INDUSTRY CYCLICALITY & MID-CYCLE ECONOMICS

### Cyclicality: LOW-MODERATE

BI software has subscription-based recurring revenue, which dampens cyclicality versus transactional software. However:
- Enterprise IT budget cuts during downturns reduce new bookings
- "Shelf-ware" consolidation accelerates during cost-cutting cycles
- Hyperscaler bundling intensifies during budget pressure (CIOs choose "free" Power BI)

### Mid-Cycle Industry Economics (Reference Point)

| Metric | Mid-Cycle Benchmark | Current State |
|--------|---------------------|---------------|
| Industry growth | 8-12% | ~10% (at industry level) |
| Standalone vendor growth | 10-15% | <5% for mature standalone; 30%+ for AI-native |
| Gross margins (SaaS BI) | 72-78% | 74-80% (stable) |
| Operating margins (scaled vendors) | 15-25% | 10-20% (improving post-2022 efficiency focus) |
| Net retention rates | 105-115% | 85-100% (standalone); 110-120% (hyperscaler-bundled) |
| Rule of 40 score | 40%+ | 20-35% (most standalone); 50%+ (leaders) |

### Current Cycle Position: LATE CYCLE for standalone vendors; MID-CYCLE for platform-bundled BI

The overall BI market is mid-cycle and growing. But for standalone vendors specifically, we are in a late-cycle consolidation phase. The industry has bifurcated:
- **Platform-bundled BI** (Power BI, Tableau/Salesforce, Looker/Google): Healthy growth, expanding TAM, AI investment
- **Standalone BI** (DOMO, Sisense, smaller vendors): Margin pressure, churn acceleration, M&A or death

### Why DOMO Cannot Survive Independently (Industry-Level Argument)

1. **Scale economics are against it.** At $318M revenue, DOMO cannot invest adequately in AI/agentic analytics while also servicing debt and reaching profitability. The AI arms race requires hundreds of millions in annual R&D that hyperscalers spend as rounding errors.

2. **Net retention below 100% = structural revenue erosion.** At 88-94% net retention, DOMO's existing customer base is shrinking in dollar terms every year. New bookings must first backfill churn before contributing to growth. This is the hallmark of a product losing competitive positioning.

3. **The "consumption model" pivot is too late.** While consumption NRR of 108% is encouraging, 80% of ARR is now consumption-based only after years of transition pain. The cohort is still small and unproven at scale.

4. **Debt maturity creates forced-seller dynamics.** With $47.9M cash, negative equity, and debt maturing April 2026, DOMO has essentially no leverage in negotiations. Any acquirer knows this.

## 9. THE ONE INDUSTRY FACTOR THAT MATTERS

**Hyperscaler bundling economics -- specifically Microsoft Power BI's pricing and distribution -- is the single variable that determines the structural viability of every standalone BI vendor.**

When Microsoft bundles Power BI at $14/user/month (or effectively $0 within M365 E5), it sets a price ceiling for the entire industry. Every standalone vendor must justify a premium over "free," which means they must offer capabilities that Power BI cannot replicate. As Power BI adds AI copilot features (backed by Azure OpenAI's multi-billion-dollar investment), the list of differentiated capabilities shrinks.

**What would cause this to inflect:**
- Microsoft raising Power BI pricing significantly (unlikely; bundling is strategic)
- Regulatory intervention on bundling practices (possible in EU; not imminent)
- Enterprise backlash against hyperscaler lock-in (cyclical, not structural)

**How to monitor:**
- Microsoft 365 E5 attach rates and Power BI MAU growth (reported in earnings)
- Gartner Magic Quadrant movements (annual; tracks competitive positioning shifts)
- Standalone vendor net retention rates (leading indicator of competitive displacement)

## 10. WHAT CONSENSUS MAY BE MISSING

### The Consensus View
"The BI market is growing at 8-10%, DOMO just needs to execute better, and the strategic alternatives process will unlock hidden value. At 0.48x revenue, it is deeply undervalued."

### The Variant (Bearish) View
**Consensus underestimates how quickly AI-powered bundled BI is making standalone tools obsolete, and overestimates the strategic value of DOMO's customer base to potential acquirers.**

Specifically:
1. **The M&A comp multiples are stale.** Qlik at 4.9x (2016), Alteryx at 4.5x (2023), and Informatica at 4.8x (2025) all had something DOMO lacks: either scale ($600M+ revenue), a data integration/management moat, or a clear cloud transition runway. DOMO is already cloud-native (no transition upside), already flat (no growth acceleration thesis), and sub-scale.

2. **The $10B Qlik valuation is misleading as a comp.** Qlik's 16x multiple reflects 14 strategic acquisitions that transformed it from a BI vendor into a data integration platform. DOMO has not made this pivot and cannot afford to.

3. **PE firms are sophisticated enough to exploit DOMO's leverage deficit.** With debt maturing April 2026 and $47.9M cash, DOMO has approximately zero negotiating leverage. Any PE bidder will lowball, knowing the alternative is potential bankruptcy. The "strategic alternatives process" announcement the day before debt comes due is not a position of strength.

4. **The consumption model pivot may be value-destructive in a sale context.** Consumption-based revenue is inherently less predictable than subscription, making financial modeling harder for acquirers. While consumption NRR is higher, the transition creates near-term revenue volatility that depresses acquisition multiples.

**Net assessment: This variant view is BEARISH for DOMO's sale price.** The realistic acquisition range is likely 1.5-2.5x revenue ($477M-$795M EV), not the 3-5x that optimistic analyses suggest using peak-era comps.
