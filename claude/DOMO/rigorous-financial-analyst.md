# DOMO Inc. (NASDAQ: DOMO) -- Rigorous Financial Analysis

**Date:** February 20, 2026
**Stock Price:** ~$3.90 (close Feb 18; strategic alternatives announced after close Feb 19)
**Market Cap:** ~$163M (41.8M shares x $3.90)
**Enterprise Value:** ~$257M (market cap + ~$142M total debt - ~$48M cash)
**EV/Revenue (TTM):** 0.81x | **Price/Revenue:** 0.51x

---

## CRITICAL FINDING: This Is a Forced-Sale Situation Priced as a Going-Concern Failure

The market is pricing DOMO as if it will either (a) run out of cash or (b) never generate meaningful economics. The 8-K filing on Feb 19 announcing strategic alternatives changes the calculus entirely. At 0.81x EV/TTM revenue for a 75% gross margin SaaS company that just turned FCF-positive, the stock is either a deep value takeout candidate or an existential risk. The answer depends almost entirely on the debt structure and what a buyer would pay to own this revenue base.

**The three things that matter:**
1. Can the debt (~$142M with PIK accrual) be serviced long enough to complete a sale?
2. What would a rational acquirer pay for ~$318M of 75% gross margin SaaS revenue?
3. How much equity value survives after the debt is paid in a takeout?

### Normalized Earnings Reconciliation (Mid-Cycle)

This is the critical calculation. What does DOMO earn in a normalized, steady-state environment?

**Starting point: FY2026 estimated financials (annualized from Q3 run-rate)**

```
Revenue (TTM)                              $318.0M
(-) Cost of Revenue                        ($81.0M)
= Gross Profit                             $237.0M     (74.5% margin)

(-) SG&A (ex-SBC, normalized)             ($150.0M)    (see note 1)
(-) R&D (ex-SBC, normalized)               ($65.0M)    (see note 2)
= Non-GAAP Operating Income                $22.0M      (6.9% margin)

(-) SBC (real economic cost)               ($50.0M)    (see note 3)
= GAAP-Equivalent Operating Income        ($28.0M)     (-8.8% margin)

(-) Interest expense (cash)                ($11.0M)    (see note 4)
(-) PIK interest (non-cash but real)        ($7.0M)    (see note 5)
(-) Other/warrant remeasurement             ($3.0M)    (estimate)
= Pre-tax Loss                            ($49.0M)

(+) Tax benefit                              $0M       (NOL, no cash taxes)
= GAAP Net Loss                           ($49.0M)

Adjustments for normalized earnings:
(+) Add back warrant remeasurement           $3.0M     (non-recurring volatility)
(+) Add back PIK interest above market       $2.0M     (incremental cost of distressed debt)
= Normalized Net Loss                     ($44.0M)
```

**Notes:**
1. SG&A ex-SBC: FY26 Q3 run-rate total opex ~$66M/qtr GAAP, minus ~$12M SBC = ~$54M cash opex/qtr. S&M is roughly 55-60% of that. Normalized annual SG&A ex-SBC ~$150M assumes current cost discipline holds.
2. R&D ex-SBC: Normalized at ~$65M based on recent run-rate after SBC exclusion.
3. SBC: Using the declining run-rate of ~$12M/quarter ($48-50M annualized). This is a REAL cost to shareholders via dilution. Non-negotiable.
4. Cash interest: ~$125M principal x 8% = ~$10M, plus amendment fees. Per 10-Q disclosures.
5. PIK interest: ~$130M outstanding x 5% = ~$6.5M annually, compounding. Per credit facility amendment.

### The Harsh Reality of Normalized Economics

Even excluding SBC entirely (which management does), DOMO barely breaks even operationally. Including SBC as a real cost (as we must), the company loses ~$44M per year on a normalized basis. There is no scenario where this company generates positive normalized after-tax earnings at current revenue levels.

**What would it take to reach normalized profitability?**

At $318M revenue with 74.5% gross margins ($237M gross profit):
- Need total cash opex + SBC < $237M to break even at operating level
- Current: ~$215M cash opex + ~$50M SBC = $265M (exceeds gross profit by $28M)
- Need ~$28M+ in additional cost cuts OR revenue growth to reach operating breakeven
- At current gross margins, need ~$355-360M revenue (holding costs flat) to reach GAAP operating breakeven
- OR need to cut SBC to ~$20-25M (implying significant headcount reduction / lower comp)

### Critical Debt Observations

1. **PIK interest is growing the debt**: At 5% PIK on ~$130M, roughly $6.5M/year gets added to principal. By maturity (Aug 2028), total debt could be $155-160M+ if no prepayment.

2. **Cash interest burn is ~$10-11M/year**: This consumes essentially all the operating cash flow the company generates. Cash position has been remarkably stable at ~$47M for three consecutive quarters -- but only because SBC is creating "free" labor while real cash is going to debt service.

3. **Negative equity of ($192M)**: Accumulated deficit is massive. This company has destroyed significant capital since inception.

4. **Current liabilities exceed current assets by $127M**: This is primarily deferred revenue ($160M+), which is a SaaS company feature, not a solvency risk per se. But it means the company has very limited financial flexibility.

5. **Refinancing risk**: The Aug 2028 maturity gives ~2.5 years of runway. But at 13%+ effective interest, this is distressed-company pricing. A buyer would need to refinance this debt, likely at better terms.

### Liquidity Assessment

| Metric | Amount |

| Cash | $47.9M |

| Annual debt service (cash interest) | ~$10-11M |
| Cash runway without revenue | ~4.5 quarters |

The company is NOT in immediate liquidity distress. Cash has stabilized at ~$47M. But the debt is expensive and growing via PIK. The strategic alternatives process is likely driven by the recognition that (a) the stock is un-investable at these levels and (b) the debt burden makes organic deleveraging nearly impossible at flat revenue.

DOMO has a $150M ATM (at-the-market) offering agreement with Cantor Fitzgerald. At $4/share, this represents potential issuance of up to 37.5M additional shares -- nearly doubling the share count. This is a nuclear option that would massively dilute existing holders but could pay off the entire debt. The existence of this facility is both a risk (dilution) and a backstop (survival).

### No Buybacks, No Dividends

Capital allocation is entirely defensive: service debt, fund operations, minimize cash burn. There is no return of capital to shareholders.

- Median public SaaS takeout multiple (through May 2025): 8.4x revenue -- but these companies had 19% median growth and 6% EBITDA margins. DOMO has 0% growth.
- Everbridge (declining SaaS, ~flat revenue) was taken private by Thoma Bravo at ~4.5x revenue in 2024.
- For a flat-revenue SaaS company, 1.5-2.5x revenue is the realistic PE takeout range. A strategic buyer (who could cut significant overlapping costs) might pay 2.5-3.5x.

#### Framework 2: What Must Be True at Current Price

At $3.90/share ($163M market cap, $257M EV):

The market is implying:
- EV/Revenue of 0.81x on $318M revenue
- The business has essentially zero terminal equity value beyond its current cash
- Either revenue will decline significantly, debt will consume all value, or no buyer will emerge

**For the current price to be correct, you must believe:**
- No acquirer will pay more than ~0.8x revenue for a 75% gross margin SaaS platform
- The $142M debt cannot be refinanced at reasonable terms
- Revenue will decline, not stabilize
- The strategic alternatives process will fail

**For a $8/share outcome (~$335M equity, 1.5x EV/revenue):**
- A PE buyer sees this as a cost-cutting opportunity
- They can refinance the debt at 5-7% (vs. 13% current)
- They cut SBC/headcount by 20-30%, getting to $20M+ FCF
- 1.5x is a reasonable floor for 75% gross margin subscription revenue

**For a $12/share outcome (~$500M equity, 2.0x EV/revenue):**
- Multiple bidders create competitive tension
- A strategic buyer sees cross-sell/platform value
- AI/data analytics positioning attracts a premium
- 2.0x is still well below median SaaS acquisition multiples

## 7. Key Financial Drivers

### The 1-3 Metrics That Actually Drive This Stock

**1. Outcome of the Strategic Alternatives Process (Binary)**
This is now the dominant variable. If a deal happens, the stock is worth $8-15+ depending on the buyer and competitive dynamics. If no deal happens, the stock likely reverts to $2-3 as the market prices in ongoing dilution and debt burden. Everything else is secondary to this binary outcome.

**2. Revenue Trajectory (Stability vs. Decline)**
The difference between flat revenue ($318M) and declining revenue ($290M) is the difference between a company worth acquiring and a company spiraling. Current subscription RPO of $406M (up 15% YoY) and long-term RPO of $190M (up 44% YoY) suggest the revenue base is more durable than the stock price implies. Consumption model transition (80%+ of ARR) creates near-term billings noise but should improve long-term retention.

**3. Debt Service Capacity**
With $48M cash, ~$10-11M annual cash interest, and marginally positive FCF, the company can survive ~3-4 years without external capital. The Aug 2028 debt maturity is manageable IF revenue doesn't decline. But any revenue deterioration would quickly create a cash crisis, forcing a fire-sale or deeply dilutive equity raise via the ATM facility.

## 8. What Consensus May Be Missing

### The Non-Obvious Insight: SBC Decline Is Creating a Hidden Margin Improvement That a Buyer Can Accelerate

Most analysts focus on DOMO's flat revenue and negative GAAP earnings, concluding this is an uninvestable, declining business. The consensus narrative is "no growth = no value."

**What consensus likely misses:**

1. **SBC has fallen 40% in two years** (from $84M to ~$50M run-rate) and continues to decline. This is not just an accounting improvement -- it reflects genuine headcount rationalization and lower equity grant values. A private buyer who eliminates public-company SBC mechanics entirely (replacing with cash comp at lower levels) could immediately unlock $30-40M of incremental operating income.

2. **The PIK debt structure inflates the apparent distress.** The 13%+ effective interest rate makes the balance sheet look worse than it is. Any acquirer with investment-grade credit could refinance $142M at 5-7%, saving $8-10M annually in interest expense. This turns a company that barely breaks even into one generating $30-40M+ FCF.

3. **Subscription RPO is actually growing.** Total subscription RPO of $406M (15% YoY growth) and long-term RPO up 44% suggest future contracted revenue is IMPROVING even as recognized revenue is flat. The consumption model transition is masking underlying demand improvement. A buyer who understands this would value the business on forward bookings, not trailing revenue.

4. **The ATM facility is paradoxically the backstop that makes a lowball offer harder.** If a buyer offers too little, the Board can theoretically reject and use the $150M ATM to pay off debt entirely (at massive dilution). This creates a floor price -- the company cannot be forced into a deal below the liquidation value of its revenue base minus refinanced debt.

5. **888 employees generating $318M revenue = $358K revenue per employee.** This is below SaaS benchmarks (~$400-500K for mature companies), suggesting room for headcount optimization. A PE buyer taking this to 700 employees would generate ~$30M+ in annual savings.

## 9. Tactical Assessment: The Strategic Alternatives Process

### What Does "Strategic Alternatives" Actually Mean Here?

Given the context (stock at all-time lows, negative equity, expensive debt, Jefferies as advisor), this is almost certainly a sales process. The Board is not exploring alternatives because things are going well. Key signals:

1. **Timing is telling**: Announced right before FY2026 ends (Jan 31, 2026), which means the company will have a full year of improved financials (first FCF-positive year) to present to buyers.

2. **Jefferies as advisor**: Jefferies is a credible mid-cap M&A advisor. They also advised on Dayforce/Thoma Bravo ($12.3B). This signals a real process, not a defensive PR move.

3. **Reaffirming guidance simultaneously**: This is designed to stabilize the stock and show buyers that the business is performing as expected.

4. **No timetable**: Standard language, but typically these processes take 3-6 months.

### Likely Buyer Universe

| Buyer Type | Examples | Likely Multiple | Probability |
|------------|----------|-----------------|-------------|
| PE (Thoma Bravo, Vista, Clearlake) | Cost-cutting playbook | 1.5-2.5x rev | Highest |
| Strategic (Salesforce, SAP, Oracle) | Platform consolidation | 2.5-4.0x rev | Medium |
| Infrastructure (Snowflake, Databricks) | Data ecosystem play | 2.0-3.5x rev | Lower |
| No deal / status quo | -- | 0.5-0.8x rev | Non-trivial |

### Risk of No Deal

The "no deal" outcome is a real possibility. Reasons it could fail:
- Debt holders (BlackRock) may have change-of-control provisions that complicate a sale
- CEO Josh James owns significant Class A shares with super-voting rights -- he may not accept a low price
- Buyer universe may view flat revenue as a declining business
- AI disruption risk in BI/analytics may deter buyers

### What Happens If No Deal?

If the process fails:
- Stock likely falls to $2-3 range
- Company continues grinding toward marginal profitability
- Aug 2028 debt maturity becomes the next forcing function
- ATM equity raise becomes more likely, causing significant dilution
- Eventually would need to cut deeper or find a buyer at worse terms
