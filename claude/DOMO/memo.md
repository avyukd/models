## PART 1: LONG-TERM FUNDAMENTAL READ

### Verdict: This Business Has No Standalone Investment Case

I want to be unambiguous. On a standalone, going-concern basis, DOMO equity is worth zero or near-zero to a long-term fundamental investor. Here is why:

**Normalized Economics Are Disqualifying**

The financial analyst's reconciliation is thorough and damning:
- Revenue: $318M, flat for 3 consecutive years
- Gross Profit: $237M (74.5% margin -- decent)
- Cash OpEx (ex-SBC): ~$215M
- SBC (real cost): ~$50M
- Total real operating costs: ~$265M
- Operating Loss (including SBC as real cost): ~($28M)
- After interest ($11M cash + $7M PIK): Pre-tax loss of ~($49M)

There is no path to normalized profitability at $318M revenue. The company needs ~$355-360M in revenue (holding costs flat) just to reach GAAP operating breakeven. With 0% organic growth and 83-86% gross retention, revenue is more likely to erode than grow. The treadmill requires constant customer acquisition to replace 14-17% annual churn -- and the company is barely keeping pace.

**The Moat Does Not Exist in Any Meaningful Sense**

The moat analyst is correct and I weight this view heavily:
- 83-86% gross retention is disqualifying for any "durable competitive advantage" thesis
- 0% revenue growth in a market growing 10-15% annually means DOMO is losing share at an alarming rate
- Hyperscaler bundling (Power BI at $14/user or free with M365 E5) permanently compressed the price ceiling
- The consumption model transition improves optics (consumption cohort retention >90%) but suffers from selection bias -- the weakest customers already churned before conversion
- 0.66% market share in BI. This is not a platform. It is a niche tool being squeezed from every direction.

**The BI Industry Structure Is Hostile**

The industry analyst frames this correctly: the standalone BI category is in terminal consolidation. Microsoft Power BI captures 20% share and growing. Tableau (Salesforce), Looker (Google), and Qlik (Thoma Bravo) have the scale to invest in AI. DOMO does not. The AI arms race requires hundreds of millions in annual R&D. DOMO spends $65M. This gap widens every year.

**Capital Allocation Has Been Value-Destructive**

$1.49 billion in accumulated deficit. 58% share dilution since IPO. Stock down 82% from IPO price. Never generated sustained positive FCF. Failed to raise equity at $98/share in 2021 to strengthen the balance sheet. The capital allocation analyst's verdict is correct: this is a pattern of spend, dilute, repeat -- and the pattern has not broken.

**Is 0.48x Revenue a Value Trap?**

Yes. On a standalone basis, it is a value trap. The 0.48x price-to-revenue multiple reflects the market's accurate assessment that:
- Equity holders receive none of the cash flows (debt service consumes them)
- Dilution runs at 6-7% annually
- There is no organic growth engine
- The debt compounds against equity via PIK

The "cheap" multiple is a rational price for equity that has no claim on distributable cash flow and faces ongoing dilution. A stock can trade at 0.1x revenue and still be expensive if the equity never receives any value.

**Long-Term Standalone Decision: SELL / DO NOT OWN. No fundamental case exists for holding DOMO as a going-concern investment.**

### Verdict: Asymmetric Risk/Reward as a Special Situation, But Size It Like a Binary

This is where the analysis gets interesting. Strip away the fundamentals and evaluate this purely as a deal arbitrage.

### The Setup

On 2026-02-19, DOMO's Board announced a formal strategic alternatives process with Jefferies as financial advisor and Goodwin Procter as legal counsel. FY2026 revenue guidance was reaffirmed at $317.5-318.5M. The stock closed at ~$3.90 pre-announcement (price action post-announcement pending next trading session).

**Why This Process Is Credible (Not a PR Stunt)**

1. Jefferies is a legitimate mid-cap M&A advisor (advised on Dayforce/Thoma Bravo $12.3B). You do not hire Jefferies and Goodwin Procter to issue a press release.
2. The timing is deliberate: announced right after FY2026 ends (Jan 31, 2026), giving the company its best-ever financial year to present to buyers (first-ever positive GAAP FCF).
3. Josh James's personal incentives strongly favor a deal. His equity comp is deeply underwater. Change-of-control provisions trigger accelerated vesting. He bought stock in 2025.
4. The standalone path is clearly unviable. The Board and James appear to have accepted this reality.

### What Would an Acquirer Actually Pay?

**PE Buyer Math (Most Likely Scenario, 50% Probability)**

A PE firm (Thoma Bravo, Vista, Clearlake) would underwrite this as:
- Acquire at 1.5-2.5x revenue ($477M-$795M EV)
- Refinance $142M debt at 5-6% (saving $8-10M/year in interest)
- Cut SBC from $50M to $15M (replacing with lower cash comp)
- Reduce headcount 15-20% (from 888 to ~700-750, saving $25-35M)
- Result: $40-55M in Year 1 FCF on a $300-600M equity check
- 5-year exit at 3-5x revenue = 2-3x MOIC

This is textbook PE playbook for mature SaaS. Thoma Bravo did exactly this with Qlik. Clearlake did it with Alteryx.

The constraint on price is not "what is it worth to a buyer" but "what is the minimum James will accept." His $13.8M FY2024 comp package at higher prices, combined with change-of-control acceleration, suggests he needs at least $7-10/share to feel whole on his equity. That is the functional floor if he is rational.

**Strategic Buyer Math (Lower Probability, Higher Payoff)**

Snowflake or Databricks acquiring DOMO would be a data platform play -- buying 2,600 enterprise customer relationships and 1,000+ connectors to embed in their ecosystems. They would pay 2.5-3.5x revenue ($795M-$1.1B EV) because the customer acquisition cost of these relationships organically would be far higher. But both companies have their own BI strategies, making this less likely than PE.

### What Kills the Trade

**Kill Switch 1: No credible offers materialize.**
If Jefferies runs a full process and no bidder offers more than ~$5/share (1.0x revenue), the deal math does not work. This would signal the market is right that this revenue base is impaired beyond what private markets will pay. The stock falls to $2-3.

**Kill Switch 2: Josh James blocks a reasonable deal.**
If James rejects a $8-10 offer because he wants a continued CEO role or a higher management carve-out, minority shareholders are stuck. His 80% voting control means there is no forcing mechanism. This is the governance nightmare scenario.

**Kill Switch 3: Revenue deterioration during the process.**
A "strategic alternatives" announcement can be destabilizing to customers. Enterprise procurement teams will question DOMO's viability. If Q4 FY2026 or Q1 FY2027 shows revenue declining below $318M, buyers will reprice downward. Every quarter of revenue decline mechanically reduces the takeout value.

**Kill Switch 4: Debt holder complications.**
BlackRock holds $142M in secured debt. Change-of-control provisions may require consent or trigger accelerated repayment. If BlackRock plays hardball, the equity available for shareholders shrinks. This is manageable in most scenarios but could complicate the process.

**Kill Switch 5: Macro/credit market dislocation.**
A PE take-private requires financing. If credit markets seize (rates spike, recession fears), the buyer universe shrinks and bids fall. DOMO's deal is small enough (~$500M-1B) that this is a tail risk, not a base case.

**Capital Allocation Analyst (80% voting / 5% economic = governance risk):** This is the most important risk factor in the entire analysis. **My Synthesis:** The fundamentals are terrible but irrelevant. The only question is deal probability and deal price. This is a binary outcome with a controlled-company governance overhang. It deserves allocation but not conviction sizing. The reasons for keeping it small:

1. **Binary risk.** A no-deal outcome means 25-35% loss. In a portfolio context, a 1% position losing 30% is 30bps of drawdown -- acceptable.
