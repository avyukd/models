# TIC Solutions (NYSE: TIC) - Capital Allocation & Management Analysis

## The One Thing That Determines Value Creation

**Martin Franklin's Series A Preferred Share structure creates a performance fee on any stock appreciation above $10, extracting 20% of gains on a notional 121M share base.** This is the dominant factor shaping capital allocation incentives.

- Franklin captures ~20% of upside via preferred dividends if stock appreciates
- CEO Pizzey owns only 0.22% ($6.3M) with 81% of his $3.2M comp being bonus/equity-linked
- Structure incentivizes growth and stock price appreciation, not per-share value creation
- Risk: Aggressive M&A and leverage to drive top-line/EBITDA at expense of returns on capital

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**Consensus view:** "Martin Franklin is a proven capital allocator with a great track record at Jarden (5,000%+ returns). TIC is the next APi Group."

**Non-consensus insight:** Franklin's SPAC economics are structurally different from his Jarden ownership. At Jarden, Franklin was a major common equity holder with multi-decade alignment. At TIC, his primary economics flow through:
1. Series A Preferred shares with 20% of appreciation as annual dividend
2. 10% common ownership (significant but less than Jarden)
3. Preferred has 5-year lockup, but common shares appear to have no lockup

This structure rewards stock price appreciation, not free cash flow per share. The 4.4x post-NV5 leverage and $250M equity raise at $12 (below current market) suggest willingness to dilute for growth. Franklin's track record includes Platform Specialty Products -- less successful than Jarden, with aggressive acquisition pace and subsequent asset sales.

## 2. Incentive Structure & Alignment

### Franklin's Economics (Series A Preferred)
| Element | Terms |
|---------|-------|
| Preferred Shares Outstanding | 1,000,000 |
| Dividend Calculation | 20% x (Avg Price - $10) x 121.5M shares |
| First Dividend (Dec 2025) | 668,347 common shares issued (~$10.28 avg price) |
| Lockup | 5 years on preferred; common appears unlocked |
| Conversion | 1:1 to common after 10 years or change of control |

**What this encourages:** Stock price appreciation above $10, not per-share intrinsic value growth. Franklin benefits from EBITDA growth, multiple expansion, and M&A even if dilutive to common shareholders.

### CEO Pizzey Compensation
| Component | Amount | % of Total |
|-----------|--------|------------|
| Salary | ~$600K | 18.8% |
| Bonus/SBC | ~$2.6M | 81.2% |
| **Total** | $3.18M | 100% |

- Below industry average ($7.1M median for similar market cap)
- Ownership of 0.22% ($6.3M) is modest but meaningful for an operating CEO
- Pizzey is an operator, not capital allocator -- 40+ years in the business

**Who wins if stock underperforms?** Franklin's common shares would suffer, but his 10% stake still represents alignment. However, the preferred structure means he captures disproportionate upside, creating asymmetric incentives favoring growth over value.

## 5. Learning vs Repetition

**Evidence of discipline:**
- NV5 acquired at ~8.5x (reasonable, not heroic premium)
- $250M equity raise post-merger to strengthen balance sheet
- Stated target of 3.0x leverage
- Franklin has exited businesses when appropriate (Arysta sale at Platform for $4.2B)

**Evidence of concern:**
- Platform Specialty Products was less successful than Jarden (aggressive M&A, subsequent divestitures)
- Two transformational deals in 18 months suggests urgency to deploy capital
- General Counsel sold 100% of holdings ($503K) in Nov 2025 -- not a large sale but notable

**Pattern recognition:** Franklin's playbook is "buy fragmented industries, consolidate, improve margins, exit." Success varies -- Jarden was exceptional, Platform was mixed, APi Group appears on track.

## 6. Forward-Looking Capital Allocation Risk

### What management will likely do:
1. **Bolt-on M&A (4-6x EBITDA range)** -- They have stated this as priority post-deleveraging
2. **Continue deleveraging** toward 3.0x target
3. **No buybacks** until leverage normalized (likely 2027+)
4. **Integration of NV5** -- Three segments being reorganized

### Where capital is most likely to be misallocated:
1. **Pursuing growth over returns** -- Preferred structure rewards appreciation, not ROIC
2. **Overpaying for bolt-ons** -- TIC industry is competitive for deals (PE activity, SGS/Bureau Veritas consolidation)
3. **Dilutive equity raises** -- $12 private placement was below market; pattern could repeat

### Whether FCF is at risk of misuse:
- 2025E EBITDA: $240-250M
- Interest expense: ~$130M
- Capex: ~$60-70M (est.)
- FCF available: ~$40-60M (tight)
- FCF conversion constrained by leverage costs until deleveraging complete

## Industry Context (Scope Boundary)

TIC industry highly fragmented (top 10 players <38% share). Roll-up economics are attractive in theory:
- Recurring/sticky revenue
- Low capex
- Local relationships create moats
- Multiple arbitrage via consolidation

But competition for deals is intense (PE, strategics like SGS/Bureau Veritas). TIC is now subscale vs. global leaders ($2B revenue vs. $7B+ for SGS).
