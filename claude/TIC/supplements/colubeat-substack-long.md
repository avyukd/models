TIC SOLUTIONS (NYSE: $TIC)

1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY: Investment Case
Looking at $TIC at the start of 2026, the stock is basically stuck. Since the NV5 acquisition closed in August, the market has put the name on hold. There is no euphoria, only caution.

So, why pay attention to a stock that isn't moving? Right now, consensus sees an industrial firm with a heavy balance sheet after a big merger. My goal isn't to guess if the stock goes up tomorrow, but to look past the headline debt and see if the market is being too pessimistic about the management's ability to execute.

A. Current Situation: Tense Calm
The chart today is what I call “dead money.” Institutional interest is low. Algos see an industrial services company with too much leverage and simply move on. That’s actually good for us. It’s usually when a stock is ignored that you find the best entries. The market is just waiting to see if the integration works or fails.




To understand TIC today, you have to look at the assets they service: refineries, grids, and pipelines. These are multi-billion dollar infrastructures that need constant maintenance.




Before (Acuren alone): The business was mostly about inspection. They used sensors to find cracks or corrosion and wrote a report. It was recurring revenue, but it stopped at identifying the problem.

Now (TIC + NV5): Adding NV5 makes them a full-service provider. They don't just diagnose the issue. Their engineers now design the fix and manage the entire repair project.


For the client, it’s simpler: instead of hiring three firms, they hire one partner for the whole cycle. That is the strategy. Now we need to see if the numbers actually support it.

B. The Disconnect: Fear vs. The Franklin Playbook
The stock price is stagnant due to the $1.6B debt load. Investors see leverage and execution risk. I see the standard Martin Franklin strategy

He’s done this with Jarden, Element Solutions, and most recently APi Group ($APG). The process is consistent:

Acquire a stable business with essential services.

Use debt to fund the deal.

Use operational cash flow to pay down that debt.

Investors focus on the $1.6B figure. I'm focusing on the cash generation. The current price assumes TIC can't integrate NV5. In 18 months, the leverage should be under control and the capital structure will be more efficient.

2. BUSINESS AUTOPSY (How do they make money?)
To understand why this merger is so powerful, we must dissect the company. Forget the old structure. TIC Solutions operates with three distinct engines. If you were the owner of TIC, this is what your portfolio would look like:




Inspection & Mitigation (I&M)
Legacy Acuren | 55% of Revenue | Gross Margin: 28.5%

This is the operational base. It generates the cash flow needed to pay the bills and service the debt.

What exactly do they do? Technicians work via rope access on oil rigs or use ultrasound to detect cracks in pipelines (NDT).

The Financial Key: 90% of revenue is Recurring (OpEx). Clients pay this from operating budgets, not capital accounts. Maintenance is legally mandated. It isn't optional. If a recession hits, this segment continues billing.

The Revenue Drop Revenue fell 4% last quarter. Management is terminating low-margin contracts to retain only profitable clients. They prioritize margin over volume. It reduces short-term revenue but improves long-term unit economics.

Engineering & Consulting (CE)
Legacy NV5 (Infrastructure) | 30% of Revenue | Gross Margin: 51.4%

This segment drives the margin thesis. The margin is 51.4%, nearly double the inspection business. For every dollar of revenue, they retain half as gross profit.

What do they do? They design new infrastructure rather than inspecting existing assets. This covers civil engineering, electrical systems, and energy efficiency.

Data Centers: Work for "Hyperscalers" (Amazon, Google, Microsoft) grew over 100% last year. TIC designs cooling and electrical systems for AI Data Centers. It provides infrastructure exposure to AI.

Synergy: NV5 had strong US presence but no footprint in Canada. Acuren dominates the Canadian market. The strategy is to cross-sell these high-margin services to Acuren’s existing client base.

Geospatial (GEO)
Legacy NV5 (Tech) | 15% of Revenue | Gross Margin: 48.4%

This is the “Sci-Fi” segment. If the other two are people, this is Software and Data.

What do they do? They use lasers (LiDAR), satellites, and drones to create ultra-precise digital maps.

Client Base: 50% comes from Government and Defense, providing reliable funding. Utilities also leverage this for vegetation management to monitor power line risks.

The Edge: A significant portion acts as subscription software (SaaS). Following the initial scan, clients pay annual fees for data access. This creates high retention and revenue visibility.

SYNTHESIS
The market overlooks the efficiency of this mix, but the strategy is straightforward:

We use the stable, boring cash flow from Inspection (I&M) to provide security.

We use that cash to fund the explosive growth of Data Centers in Engineering (CE).

We use Geospatial technology (GEO) to ensure no competitor can keep up with us technologically.

The thesis in one sentence: We use steady cash flows from the legacy business to finance high-margin growth verticals, while paying down debt.







A. Barriers to Entry: What Money Can’t Buy
Tech giants and low-cost competitors cannot easily enter this space due to specific physical constraints.

1. The Logistic Moat
TIC has over 90 strategically located offices across North America. This is key. When a plant in the middle of nowhere in North Dakota has an emergency, TIC can have a team there in 2 hours. A new competitor would take 2 days. In this sector, immediate availability IS the product. Replicating that logistics network would take decades and hundreds of millions of dollars.

2. The Database (The Invisible Moat)
They have spent 40 years measuring the corrosion of the exact same pipes. They own the historical data. TIC can tell a client: “Based on our records from 1998, 2005, and 2015, this structure will fail in October of next year.” No new competitor has that history. That data turns TIC into an oracle for its clients. The switching cost is high. If a client leaves, they lose the long-term data on their assets.

3. Certifications and Licensing
Their technicians hold thousands of individual certifications that take years to obtain. You cannot simply hire 1,000 people tomorrow and send them into a nuclear power plant. The barrier to entry is certified technical talent, which is currently in short supply.

3. THE HUMAN FACTOR

A. The Architect: Martin Franklin
Let’s be honest: If Martin Franklin weren’t involved, I probably wouldn’t be looking at this company. His track record with Jarden, Element Solutions, and most recently APi Group ($APG) is the main reason. He doesn't handle daily operations; he strictly manages capital allocation. He decides where the free cash flow goes.

His Role: He isn’t there to decide what color hard hat the workers wear. He is there to decide exactly what to do with every single dollar of surplus at the end of the month.

The APG Precedent: His success at APi Group is the blueprint here. He executed a transformational acquisition (Chubb), managed a similar leverage spike (4.5x), and successfully paid down debt while expanding margins. This is the same playbook.

His son, Robert Franklin (Co-Chairman), has defined this merger as “genuinely additive.” To me, this suggests they see a way to increase equity value per share, rather than just increasing total revenue. Their control over capital allocation is a key factor in mitigating the leverage risk.

Even during the merger process, they acquired 9 small companies in 2025. They paid multiples of 4x to 6x EBITDA for these assets.

The logic is straightforward: if TIC trades at 10x–14x and they buy earnings at 4x, they create immediate value. This arbitrage strategy is typical of Franklin’s approach.

B. Skin in the Game
I checked the ownership structure for alignment:

“Mariposa” Vehicle: Franklin and his team don't have free stock options. They hold a significant investment through Mariposa Acquisition.

They own 1 million Preferred shares. Note this: these shares often participate in losses. If the stock drops, they lose real money out of their own pockets.

Their downside is tied to ours. If the share price decreases, they face real financial loss.

C. Talman Pizzey (CEO)
While Franklin handles capital allocation, Talman Pizzey runs operations. The 2025 transcripts show the integration is moving faster than expected.

Language Evolution: In Q1, Pizzey discussed Acuren and NV5 as separate entities. By Q3, he only used the name "TIC Solutions." It's a clear sign that management is already operating as a single company.

Synergies: My initial concern was that synergies would only come from cost-cutting. Pizzey is executing on revenue synergies as well.

Evidence: He cited specific cross-selling examples, such as using Acuren technicians for laser scanning at 1,000 retail locations, with the data then processed by NV5 engineers.

The Goal: They have set a target of $40 million in cross-revenue for the first year. This is a hard target backed by new contracts.

D. Culture and Incentives
Here is the most critical cultural shift I’ve detected in the filings. Previously at NV5, reps often earned bonuses for signing contracts, regardless of the eventual project profitability.

Under Pizzey, this structure has changed. Bonuses are no longer tied to bookings, but to recognized revenue and cash collection.

Alignment: Management has introduced a specific target: Free Cash Flow (FCF) conversion of 60% of EBITDA. Executive compensation is directly tied to hitting this number. This ensures the team focuses on actual cash generation to reduce leverage, rather than just growing top-line revenue.

E. Talent Retention and Stability
A major risk in a merger of this size is talent drain or cultural conflict. The filings indicate the board has taken specific measures to mitigate this:

Board Continuity: Adding Dickerson Wright (Founder of NV5) and Ben Heraud to the Board signals continuity. It assures the incoming workforce that their leadership remains involved in strategic decisions.

Non-Compete Agreements: The company actively enforces non-compete clauses. This is standard procedure to prevent departing employees from taking client relationships to competitors.

Retention Packages: Beyond enforcement, they focus on alignment. They issued over 63,000 restricted shares to retain key talent, effectively turning critical employees into shareholders.

4. FINANCIAL ANALYSIS AND RISKS
A. Debt Structure and Liquidity
Management made a specific choice on the capital structure: they canceled all interest rate swaps in 2024.

The Debt: The company has $1.6 billion in debt, and it's almost entirely variable rate. This makes them sensitive to interest rates. If rates drop, interest costs fall immediately. If they rise, the bill goes up. They aren't hedged.

Liquidity: To manage this risk, liquidity is strong. Following a $250 million capital injection in October 2025, combined with Q3 cash balances, the company holds over $414 million in cash (total liquidity exceeds $530 million including credit facilities). This provides sufficient capital to service debt while executing the deleveraging plan.




This image is as of Sept. 30. It does not include the $250M capital injection from October, which reduces the net debt.
B. Interest Coverage and Cash Taxes
The $1.64B debt is why the market is worried. But the interest coverage and tax structure make the obligations manageable.

1. The Interest Bill: High, but Controlled CFO Kristin Schultes projects annual cash interest payments of $105 million for 2026. Management actively addressed this on January 31, 2025, by repricing their loans and lowering the margin to SOFR + 2.75%.

Coverage: $350M in EBITDA against $105M in interest gives a coverage ratio of roughly 3.3x. The cash flow covers the debt service.

2. The Tax Shield: There's a big gap between the accounting tax rate and the cash taxes. In Q3 2025, TIC reported a tax benefit of $16.6M.

Why it happens: The NV5 acquisition created Deferred Tax Liabilities. This let TIC release an $11.1M valuation allowance. Also, new R&D expensing rules help lower the immediate tax bill.

Cash Impact: While the accounting tax rate may appear high, estimated Cash Taxes are only $20–$30 million annually.

The Math:

+ $350M (EBITDA)

- $105M (Cash Interest)

- $25M (Cash Taxes)

- $50M (Maintenance CapEx)

= $170M in Free Cash Flow for debt repayment.

The leverage is high, but the math works. The business generates enough cash to handle its debt and deleverage.

C. Understanding the Drop in Inspection Margins
The core Inspection business reported a 4% decline in revenue and a gross margin contraction from 25.1% to 22.8%. While a drop in margins is never ideal, the MD&A and CFO commentary explain this is driven by three specific factors rather than a structural loss of pricing power.

The Financial Metamorphosis (Predecessor vs. Successor)


Above: The "Old" Acuren. A single-engine business with 25% Gross Margins and zero exposure to Engineering.



Above: The "New" TIC Solutions. A triple-engine platform. Note how the addition of Engineering (NV5) has structurally lifted the consolidated Gross Margin to 32%.
The “Red Flag” (Gross Margin 25.1% → 22.8%)
If you look at the Inspection segment, the Gross Margin fell from 25.1% to 22.8%. On the surface, it looks like pricing power is dying. It isn't. The CFO's comments and the MD&A show this is a mix of three temporary issues and one deliberate choice.

High Margin vs. Low Margin Inspection revenue comes from two sources: “Call-out” work (emergencies/spot jobs, which carry high margins) and “Embedded” work (permanent staff, lower margin but stable).

In Q3, call-out volume dropped significantly because several major LNG construction projects were completed. As the mix shifted away from high-margin emergency work toward lower-margin maintenance work, the average margin naturally compressed. Management expects this to normalize as new projects ramp up in 2026.

The Timing Lags (Turnarounds & Wages)

Clients moved major maintenance shutdowns from Q3 2025 into 2026. This pushed revenue out while fixed costs stayed, causing temporary inefficiency. Also, technician wages went up earlier in the year. Contracts allow for price increases, but there's a 3–6 month lag before they can bill the higher rates. They're currently in that gap.

The Strategic Drop CFO Kristin Schultes confirmed they are actively exiting relationships with low-margin clients. They are trading volume for profitability. This shrinks the top line in the short term but improves the margin profile long-term.

The Consolidated View: Despite the headwinds in the legacy Inspection business, the total company Gross Margin expanded from 25% to 32%.

The Big Picture: Despite the pressure on the legacy Inspection business, the total company Gross Margin expanded from 25% to 32%.

The inclusion of NV5 (which runs at 50% margins) absorbed the volatility in the inspection unit. The diversification strategy is working.

D. Transaction Costs and EBITDA Adjustments
The report shows $18.4 million in transaction costs alongside significant EBITDA adjustments. I checked to see if these are real one-time hits or just recurring costs hidden as adjustments.

What they are: These expenses are for the integration, specifically merging IT systems and closing duplicate offices. Management says these costs will stick around until mid-2027, then drop off.

The Check: Executive bonuses are linked to Adjusted EBITDA. However, as noted in the governance section, they also have a Free Cash Flow conversion target. This acts as a safeguard: they cannot simply inflate EBITDA with accounting add-backs because they still need to generate the actual cash to hit the conversion metrics.




Many investors see the bottom line ("Net Loss") and run. But look few lines up. The company recorded $18.7 million in Transaction Costs (legal and banking fees for the NV5 merger). If we strip away this one-time, non-recurring expense, the company is already profitable on an operating basis. The "loss" is a strategic M&A decision, not an operational failure.
5. GAAP Earnings vs. Cash Flow
Looking at the projected Net Income for the next 24 months, the headline numbers appear negative. If investors rely solely on EPS (Earnings Per Share), the company looks unprofitable.

This is almost entirely due to non-cash accounting from the merger. The acquisition created intangible assets, like backlog and customer relationships, that have to be amortized.

The Divergence: This amortization reduces reported Net Income, but it is a non-cash expense. It does not affect the bank account. While GAAP earnings are down, the operational cash flow is positive. It’s a temporary gap between accounting losses and real cash.

A. Purchase Price Allocation (PPA) Impact
The divergence between Net Income and Cash Flow is primarily due to Purchase Price Allocation. TIC recorded an acquired backlog valued at $111.7 million from NV5. Accounting standards require this asset to be amortized over a short period of roughly two years.

This creates a significant expense on the Income Statement that reduces reported Net Income. But again, it’s a non-cash charge. As seen in the Cash Flow Statement, this expense is added back to the operating cash flow. The reduction in EPS is strictly accounting-driven and does not represent cash leaving the business.




The Resurrection of Cash. While Net Income shows a loss of $(39.9M), the massive Amortization add-back (+$56M) proves the cash engine is intact.
B. Backlog vs Customer Retention
The rapid 2-year amortization schedule raises a valid question about customer churn: does the business lose clients every 24 months? The notes in the financial statements provide a crucial distinction between specific orders and client relationships.


Here is the difference that the market misses:

Acquired Backlog ($112M): This represents specific, signed Purchase Orders (e.g., a single pipeline inspection). By definition, these tasks are completed quickly.

Customer Relationships ($1.3 Billion): This asset represents the long-term Master Service Agreements (MSAs). The auditors assigned this asset a useful life of 14.3 years.

Assessment: The fast amortization just shows they are finishing specific tasks, not the end of the client relationship. The MSAs ensure that when one specific task ends, the relationship continues, generating new orders. The "churn" happens at the project level, but the customer stays.

C. Pricing Power and Portfolio Quality
A key risk in this environment is wage inflation. I wanted to see if they'd kill margins to keep volume. They didn't.

Margin Expansion: Gross Margin hit 32%. High margins from the Engineering unit are absorbing the rising costs elsewhere. It shows they can pass inflation through to the client.

Cleaning the Client List: Management is walking away from low-margin contracts. This makes the "Book-to-Bill" ratio look flat (around 1.0x) for now, but it's a deliberate trade: less volume for more profit.

Backlog Growth: Even after firing bad clients, the Total Backlog grew 10.6% to over $1B. Management already has 88% of next year’s revenue locked in. That’s solid visibility for 2026 cash flow.

D. Risk Monitoring
I'm staying objective; there are red flags to monitor.

The Valuation Risk: The intangible asset values aren't final until August 2026. Remember, NV5 struggled with valuing the Axim backlog before.

The Metric: I'm watching the Q4 "Book-to-Bill." If it drops hard and margins don't move up, the thesis weakens.

CONCLUSION: Is value being destroyed? No. The negative Net Income is a temporary accounting hurdle, not a liquidity crisis. Under the adjustments, the core is healthy:

Retention: Relationships average over 14 years.

Pricing: Margins are up to 32%.

Visibility: 88% of next year’s revenue is already on the books.

6. THE ECOSYSTEM AND COMPETITION
The market valuation looks low because investors are categorizing TIC Solutions incorrectly.

A. The Thesis “Alpha”
Currently, investors seem to place the company in the wrong sector bucket, putting it in the wrong box.

The Cheap Group (Pure Inspectors): Companies like Mistras Group (MG) or Team Inc. typically trade at 6x – 8x EBITDA. The market views them as “commodity” businesses with low value-add.

The Expensive Group (Engineering Consultants): Giants like Tetra Tech (TTEK), Parsons, or Stantec trade at 15x – 20x EBITDA. The market pays a premium for their intellectual capital and sustainable designs.

TIC Opportunity: Right now, due to the debt load and the legacy name (Acuren), algorithms are valuing TIC as if it belonged to the “Cheap” group.

But here is the catch: Following the NV5 acquisition, half of the EBITDA already comes from the “Expensive” group (Engineering and Geospatial).

The Re-rating Thesis: We don’t need the business to grow 20% to make a lot of money. We only need the market to realize that TIC is no longer a boring inspection firm, but an engineering consultancy in disguise. If the multiple expands from 8x to 12x (still well below Tetra Tech), the stock revalues by 50% without needing to sell a single extra dollar.

B. Market Fragmentation and Barriers to Entry
A recent industry report suggests that barriers to entry in NDT (Non-Destructive Testing) are low. This is technically true: small operators with basic certification and a vehicle can compete on price.

But TIC doesn’t play in that segment. Their clients, refineries, aerospace, and power grids, care more about risk than the lowest bid.

Small Firm Risk: If a small shop messes up and there’s an accident, they don’t have the capital to cover the fallout.

The TIC Advantage: Clients pay for TIC’s balance sheet and insurance. It’s a hedge against liability.

Conclusion: Market fragmentation actually benefits TIC. It positions the company as the primary consolidator. As compliance costs and insurance requirements rise, smaller players often choose to sell their business to TIC rather than struggle to compete.

C. Competition: The Global Giants
A common question is why larger global competitors like SGS or Bureau Veritas do not take market share from TIC given their scale.

The Geospatial Advantage: The NV5 acquisition provides a specific technical advantage. They own proprietary geospatial mapping software that is difficult to replicate. This service is not just about data collection; it involves processing large amounts of data using specific algorithms. Generalist firms like SGS typically lack this specialized software capability.

Specialist vs. Generalist: While companies like SGS inspect a wide range of products (from consumer goods to commodities), TIC focuses strictly on North American critical infrastructure. It’s hard for a generalist to match the technical depth needed for high-risk industrial assets.

D. Talent Retention Strategy
The number one risk in this sector is that technicians learn the trade and leave to start their own firms. That is the nature of the guild.

The Strategy: To prevent this, TIC has implemented Equity Retention Packages for key staff.

The Effect: Lead technicians and engineers have a financial reason to stay. If they leave, they lose their unvested stock. This turns potential competitors into shareholders aligned with the company's growth.

C. Infrastructure: AI and the Power Grid
While everyone is watching NVIDIA chips, few are looking at the physical infrastructure. AI Data Centers consume a brutal amount of energy, and the U.S. power grid is obsolete.

TIC’s Position: The company provides a dual solution for this sector:

NV5: Designs the electrical substations and cooling systems for the data centers.

Acuren: Inspects and certifies the transmission lines that deliver the power.

Conclusion: TIC is a direct play on AI infrastructure spending. They provide the necessary services without the high volatility of the tech sector.

7. MACRO TRENDS AND TAILWINDS
Aside from the management, the market numbers are solid. The "Asset Integrity" sector is around $25B, projected to hit $28B this year, growing at 5% to 10% depending on the segment. Two specific trends are pushing the business:

A. Aging Infrastructure and OPEX Spending
The trend is to keep old assets running longer to avoid the cost of new builds.

The Logic: With high rates and expensive construction, building new plants (CAPEX) doesn't make sense. Companies are prioritizing maintenance on what they already have (OPEX).

The Upside: As infrastructure ages, the law requires more frequent and complex testing. Clients delaying new projects directly results in more recurring revenue for TIC’s maintenance and inspection teams.

B. Digital Transformation
The sector is shifting from paper reports (”I found a crack”) to digital predictive models (”This crack will appear in 6 months”).

How it fits: This validates the rationale behind the NV5 acquisition:

Acuren: Possesses decades of historical data on asset corrosion and wear.

NV5: Possesses the software and engineering capabilities to digitize that data.

The Result: TIC now offers "Digital Twins." Clients use TIC’s data to predict failures before they happen. This turns TIC into a software-integrated partner rather than just an outside contractor, which makes it much harder for a client to switch to a competitor.

C. Political and Regulatory Winds
The company benefits from demand drivers in both traditional and renewable energy sectors.

Oil & Gas: If US energy production stays high, pipelines and refineries run at full capacity. More usage means more wear and tear, which drives the need for Acuren’s inspections.

The “Mega Rule”: A 2019 safety regulation expansion added thousands of miles of pipeline to the list of mandatory inspections.

Energy Transition: Newer tech like wind turbines and aerospace parts use advanced materials. Since we don't have 50 years of data on how these materials age, the law requires more frequent testing than it does for steel.

D. The Outsourcing Trend
Large industrial firms are shutting down their internal inspection departments because the tech and regulations have become too complex to handle in-house.

Why it Matters: Maintaining an internal team is becoming inefficient and expensive for a client.

The Impact: Clients are moving to outside firms like TIC. It turns a fixed cost (employees) into a variable cost (contracts) and shifts the compliance headache to TIC.

8. VALUATION AND VERDICT
To understand the opportunity, it isn’t enough to look at a static snapshot of 2028. You have to watch the entire movie unfold year by year. I have modeled the detailed Free Cash Flow (FCF) for the year 2028 under three probability scenarios, integrating the impact of the recent capital injection.

Starting Point: Price $11.25 | Shares: 241.4M (Diluted post-injection) | Initial Net Debt: $1,226M.




A. SCENARIO ANALYSIS
For these price targets to materialize, we don’t need miracles; we just need math to work. Below is the operational and market rationale behind each scenario, grounded in the financial evidence presented in this thesis:

SCENARIO 1: BEAR CASE (The “Floor”)

Operating Assumption: The integration stalls. We assume a “Stagflation” environment where revenue growth is flat and EBITDA margins compress to 15.5%. This assumes labor costs rise faster than the company can raise prices, reducing profitability in the legacy business.

Valuation (7x): Wall Street ignores the engineering assets and values TIC strictly as a commoditized industrial service firm with high debt. A 7x multiple is typical for businesses with no growth.

The Downside (-15%): The stock drops to $9.35.

Risk Mitigation: Free Cash Flow remains near $150M even at these lower margins. This cash covers the debt service, preventing a solvency issue. The downside is limited compared to the potential gain.

SCENARIO 2: BASE CASE (The “Plan”)

Operating Assumption: This is not a “growth” scenario; it is an “efficiency” scenario. We assume margins expand moderately to 17.5%.

The Math: This target relies on the 32% Gross Margin (vs. 25% legacy) and completing the client pruning strategy. It requires standard operational discipline to hit the margin target.

Valuation (10x): As leverage falls below 2.0x, the discount applied to the debt disappears. A 10x multiple is the historical standard for stable, cash-generative industrial companies (like Stantec or Jacobs).

Reward (+124%): The stock price reaches $24.70. The return is driven by two factors: paying down debt (which transfers value to equity) and margin normalization.

SCENARIO 3: BULL CASE (The “Re-Rating”)

Operating Assumption: The revenue mix shifts. The Engineering unit (NV5) grows at double digits driven by Data Center demand, becoming a larger portion of total revenue. This pushes the consolidated EBITDA margin to 19.0%.

Valuation (13x): The market changes how it classifies the stock. Instead of valuing it as an inspection firm, investors compare it to engineering peers like Tetra Tech or Parsons (which trade at 18x-20x). We assume a conservative re-rating to 13x EV/EBITDA.

Reward (+290%): The stock climbs to $42.90. This upside occurs if the market applies a multiple closer to its technology and engineering peers.

9. FINAL CONCLUSIONS (The Art of Seeing What Others Ignore)
“The stock market is a device for transferring money from the impatient to the patient.” — Warren Buffett

After analyzing the filings and financial data, my conclusion is that the market is mispricing TIC Solutions because of the complex merger and the debt load. While most focus on the leverage, the actual business quality is higher than the valuation implies.

Here is why this thesis works for me:

Alignment of Interest: The most important factor is leadership. I am investing alongside Sir Martin Franklin. He has invested significant personal capital (Preferred Shares) and designed the compensation structure so that management only earns a bonus if the free cash flow targets are met. This ensures total alignment with shareholders.

The Business (Quality at a Fair Price) This is a stable, non-cyclical business. Approximately 90% of revenues are recurring and mandated by regulation. We are buying a company that protects critical infrastructure (Data Centers, Power Grids, Refineries) at a valuation that implies zero future growth. This provides a significant margin of safety.

The Moat (The Unfair Advantage) Charlie Munger always looked for “unfair advantages,” and I see three clear ones here:

Scale: Few competitors can mobilize 4,000 technicians immediately.

Risk Transfer: Clients hire TIC to transfer liability to TIC’s balance sheet.

Retention: The equity packages for employees prevent competitors from poaching key talent.

Asymmetric Risk/Reward: The math is favorable. The Bear Case indicates a limited downside (-15%) because the strong cash flow prevents a solvency crisis. The Bull Case offers significant upside (+290%) if the market re-rates the stock. The potential reward is much higher than the risk.

B. Why now?
The opportunity exists because of the time horizon. The market is focused on the $1.2 billion debt and the short-term quarterly noise.

However, investors must be realistic. This is a high-conviction idea, but it requires a 3-5 year horizon.

The Process: Deleveraging is a mathematical process, but it takes time. It is not exciting.

The Expectation: I expect volatility and quiet quarters, similar to the early days of APi Group ($APG).

If you are looking for a quick trade, this is not the right investment. The “cost” of the high returns in the Bull Case is the patience required to let the debt reduction play out.

We are buying a critical infrastructure service provider at a distressed valuation. The downside is protected by cash flow; the upside depends on the market eventually recognizing the true quality of the business.
