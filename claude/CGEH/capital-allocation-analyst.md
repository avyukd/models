# Capstone Green Energy Holdings, Inc. (CGEH) -- Management & Capital Allocation Assessment

**Date:** February 21, 2026
**Analyst Focus:** Incentive alignment, capital allocation discipline, dilution risk, turnaround credibility
**Ticker:** CGEH (OTCQX) | **Market Cap:** ~$130M | **Price:** ~$5.53 | **1Y Return:** +608%

---

## Verdict: Mixed -- Genuine Operational Turnaround, But Structural Overhang and Promotional Risk Warrant Skepticism

This is a post-bankruptcy turnaround with real operational improvement. Revenue is growing 33-42% year-over-year, gross margins have expanded from 16% to 39% over six quarters, and the company just delivered its second consecutive quarter of GAAP profitability. Management insiders put personal capital into the November 2025 PIPE at $2.00/share -- now worth nearly 3x their entry. These are not the hallmarks of a promotional scheme.

However, the 608% stock appreciation in a micro-cap with limited float, a complex Goldman Sachs preferred unit overhang worth 37.5% of the operating subsidiary, $25.3M in exit notes coming due, a stockholders' deficit of negative $39.4M, and a CEO who has been in seat for less than two years should temper enthusiasm. The turnaround is real but fragile. The corporate structure is unusual. The margin for error is thin.

## 2. Incentive Structure & Alignment

### CEO Compensation Design

Per the CEO employment offer letter (SEC filing):

| Component | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| Base Salary | $550,000 |
| Annual Target Bonus | 100% of base ($550K target) |
| Bonus Floor | 50% of base ($275K minimum) |
| Bonus Cap | 150% of base ($825K maximum) |
| Equity Grant | 450,000 RSUs vesting over 3 years |
| Other | Hotel allowance for relocation |

**What behavior does this compensation encourage?**

The 50% bonus floor is the critical detail. Canino receives at minimum $825,000 annually ($550K salary + $275K minimum bonus) regardless of performance. For a company that only achieved $7.9M of Adjusted EBITDA in its best full year ever, paying the CEO a guaranteed minimum of $825K is disproportionate -- representing approximately 10% of full-year Adjusted EBITDA. The floor eliminates meaningful downside accountability in the compensation structure.

The 450,000 RSU grant, vesting over three years from March 2024, represents approximately 2.3% of pre-PIPE shares outstanding (~19.4M). The vesting schedule aligns with a medium-term horizon, which is appropriate for a turnaround.

**Who benefits most if the stock underperforms?** Canino still collects $825K+ in cash annually. The RSUs would lose value, but his $150K PIPE investment at $2.00 is his most meaningful downside exposure. At current prices (~$5.53), that PIPE investment is worth ~$415K -- a meaningful personal gain that provides some alignment. But his total share ownership of 542,598 shares (~$3.0M at current price) is modest relative to his annual cash compensation.

### Board Compensation

The 2025 proxy proposed an increase of 1,000,000 shares to the 2023 Equity Incentive Plan. Board members received option awards at $0 exercise price in August-September 2025 per Form 4 filings. This is essentially a grant of free shares to directors -- not performance-based and immediately dilutive upon vesting.

### Alignment Assessment

**Positive signals:**
- CEO and five other insiders invested personal capital in the November 2025 PIPE at $2.00/share
- PIPE was priced at an 8% premium to market ($1.85 close on Nov 21, 2025)
- 90-day lock-up for all directors and officers post-PIPE
- CEO RSU grant vests over 3 years, creating medium-term alignment

**Negative signals:**
- CEO guaranteed bonus floor of 50% eliminates meaningful downside in cash comp
- CEO's total equity stake (~2.4% of shares outstanding) is modest for a $130M market cap company
- No disclosed performance metrics for bonus determination -- "Compensation Committee will approve appropriate metrics"
- Board option grants at $0 exercise price are not performance-linked
- Interim CFO and Interim Chairman roles create governance instability
- The most experienced turnaround operator (Flexon) left the board

*Source: EBITDA-to-Adjusted-EBITDA reconciliation tables in earnings releases*

**Assessment:** SBC is remarkably low for a post-bankruptcy company -- $0.3M for all of FY2025 against $85.6M in revenue is negligible (0.4%). The YTD FY2026 run rate of ~$0.8M annualized against trailing twelve-month revenue of ~$110M remains modest. This is NOT a company using SBC as a stealth cost the way many tech companies do. Stock-based compensation is treated as a real (but small) cost.

### Dilution Analysis

**Share Count History:**

| Date | Shares Outstanding | Event |
|------|-------------------|-------|
| Dec 7, 2023 (Emergence) | ~15.4M (est.) | 1:1 conversion from old shares |
| FY2025 WA (ended Mar 2025) | 19,056K | Equity grants, plan issuances |

The November 2025 PIPE was the single largest dilutive event since emergence, adding 7.5M shares to a ~19.4M base -- a 38.7% increase in share count. This is significant dilution.

**However, context matters:**
- The PIPE raised $15M in gross proceeds
- $8M went to mandatory debt repayment (exit notes maturing Dec 7, 2025)
- Remaining funds earmarked for product development, AI/data center expansion, and working capital
- Management priced the PIPE at an 8% premium to market, which is unusual and shareholder-friendly -- most PIPEs are done at discounts
- Management personally participated, eating their own cooking

**Pre-Bankruptcy Dilution History:** Under the CPST/CGRN ticker, this company executed two reverse stock splits (1:20 in 2015, 1:10 in 2019) -- a combined 200:1 reverse split over four years. This is the mark of a company that historically destroyed shareholder value through chronic dilution and operating losses for over two decades.

### Net Dilution Verdict

The post-bankruptcy CGEH is a different entity in terms of SBC discipline. Annual SBC of $0.3-0.8M is trivial. The PIPE dilution was large but purposeful -- debt paydown and growth investment, not management enrichment. The 2023 Equity Incentive Plan was expanded by 1,000,000 shares in August 2025, which at current prices represents ~$5.5M in potential future dilution. For a company generating $12.3M of YTD Adjusted EBITDA, this is manageable but worth monitoring.

## 4. Capital Allocation Track Record

### a) Reinvestment

**Post-Bankruptcy:** Capital expenditures were only $0.9M in FY2025 -- essentially a maintenance-level spend for a company with $85.6M in revenue. The business model (microturbines, EaaS rentals, service contracts) requires investment in the rental fleet (at ~$1.2M per MW) and product development. The DFMA (Design for Manufacturing and Assembly) cost-out initiative has been a standout, reducing component costs by up to 93% on specific parts and mitigating tariff exposure.

**Positive:** Reinvestment appears disciplined. Revenue has grown from $85.6M (FY2025) to a $110M trailing twelve-month run rate with minimal capex. Gross margins expanded from 16% to 39% -- this is reinvestment in process improvement, not in expensive new capacity. The rental fleet runs at 98% utilization.

**Negative:** The very low capex raises a question about underinvestment. A 12.1 MW rental fleet is small, and management plans to grow it by only 2 MW annually through 2028 (~$2.4M/year). This is cautious but may limit growth.

### b) M&A

**Cal Microturbine Acquisition (August 13, 2025):**
- Purchased the Cal Microturbine distributor territory (California, Hawaii, Nevada, Oregon, Washington)
- Described by CFO as "entirely self-funded strategic investment"
- Contributed $0.7M in revenue and $0.2M in earnings in its partial first quarter
- Described as contributing to margin expansion in Q3 FY2026

**Assessment:** This is a vertical integration play -- bringing a distributor territory in-house to capture higher margins and improve customer proximity. It is small, strategic, and self-funded. This is the correct type of M&A for a company at this stage: bolt-on, margin-accretive, and within the existing business model. No empire building evident here.

**Pre-Bankruptcy M&A (for context):** Under prior management, a forced $21M deployment into the rental fleet in October 2020 was mandated by a lender covenant -- not a management decision. The predecessor company's capital allocation was largely dictated by Goldman Sachs as the senior secured lender.

### c) Buybacks & Issuance

There have been no share buybacks. The company is in net issuance mode:
- 7.5M shares issued via PIPE (November 2025)
- Ongoing equity plan issuances under the 2023 Equity Incentive Plan
- S-1 filed to register 7.5M shares for resale by PIPE investors (3.98M already outstanding + 3.52M from pre-funded warrants)

**No buybacks are appropriate at this stage.** The company has $25.3M in exit notes due, a stockholders' deficit of negative $39.4M, and is only beginning to generate positive net income. Buying back shares would be reckless. The correct capital allocation priority is: (1) repay debt, (2) fund organic growth, (3) build a cash buffer. Management appears to be following this sequence.

### d) Debt Usage

**Pre-Bankruptcy Debt:**
- $51M facility from Goldman Sachs/Broad Street Credit at LIBOR + 8.75%
- Interest burden: $6-7M annually
- This debt was the proximate cause of bankruptcy

**Post-Bankruptcy Restructuring:**
- Goldman converted $49M in debt to preferred units (37.5% of OpCo) + $25M in exit notes
- DIP facility: $30M ($12M new money + $18M roll-up)
- Exit notes: $28M aggregate ($7M new money + $20M roll-up of DIP)

**Current Debt (as of December 31, 2025):**
- Exit notes (current): $25.3M
- Exit notes (non-current): $0
- All remaining debt is current -- it is due within 12 months

**PIPE Proceeds Usage:**
- $8.0M used to repay exit notes maturing December 7, 2025
- This is the single best use of proceeds available -- retiring debt that carried high structural cost

**Assessment:** Post-bankruptcy debt management has been rational. Management used PIPE proceeds to deleverage rather than to fund growth fantasies. The remaining $25.3M in current exit notes is the most pressing balance sheet issue. With $14.4M in cash and ~$5M/quarter in Adjusted EBITDA, the company will likely need to refinance or raise additional capital to address this maturity. This creates refinancing risk and potential further dilution.

## 5. The Goldman Sachs Overhang: The Structural Risk Nobody Discusses Enough

The single most complex and consequential element of CGEH's capital structure is the Goldman Sachs preferred unit position:

- Goldman holds 10,449,863 Preferred Units in the Operating Subsidiary
- These represent a 37.5% economic interest in the operating company
- Redeemable at the greater of: (a) share-price-based calculation, or (b) $13.9M (initial recorded value)
- Redemption window: six months following December 7, 2029
- Convertible at any time into common units of the OpCo

**Why this matters for capital allocation:**

1. **HLBV Accretion Destroys Reported EPS:** The HLBV accounting method for these preferred units creates enormous non-cash charges. In Q3 FY2026, operational EPS was $0.06, but reported EPS was negative $1.79. Year-to-date operational EPS was $0.07, but reported EPS was negative $2.79. This makes the company uninvestable for any screen-based or passive investor looking at GAAP EPS.

2. **Asymmetric Value Capture:** As CGEH's stock price rises, the preferred unit redemption value increases proportionally. At a $5.53 share price and ~23M shares outstanding (~$127M equity market cap), Goldman's 37.5% claim on the OpCo is worth approximately $47.6M -- a 3.4x return on their initial $13.9M book value. Goldman benefits from every dollar of value management creates without bearing operating risk or contributing additional capital.

3. **2029 Redemption Risk:** When the preferred units become redeemable in 2030, CGEH will face an obligation to repurchase them at what could be a very large number if the stock price continues to appreciate. This creates a deferred capital allocation constraint that limits management's future flexibility.

4. **Conversion Risk:** Goldman can convert to common units at any time. If Goldman converts, it would effectively create a 37.5% dilution event for public shareholders at the OpCo level. Whether or how this flows through to the public equity depends on complex structural provisions.

**This is the hidden tax on CGEH shareholders that the 608% stock chart does not reflect.** Public shareholders own 100% of the public holding company, which owns only 62.5% of the operating subsidiary that generates all the revenue and cash flow.

## 6. Learning vs. Repetition

### Evidence of Learning

- **Financial discipline is new:** In 37 years, the predecessor company never delivered a positive full-year Adjusted EBITDA. FY2025 delivered $7.9M. YTD FY2026 has already surpassed that at $12.3M. This represents a genuine behavioral change.
- **Margin expansion is real:** Gross margins went from 16% (FY2024) to 39% (Q3 FY2026). This is not revenue growth masking cost problems -- costs are actually being managed.
- **DFMA initiative shows operational rigor:** Reducing component costs by 93% on specific parts is evidence of systematic cost engineering, not just volume leverage.
- **Debt repayment prioritized over growth:** Using $8M of PIPE proceeds for mandatory debt paydown was the correct and disciplined choice.
- **Inventory management improving:** Predecessor had 300 days of inventory turnover. Current inventory of ~$21M against $110M trailing revenue is ~70 days -- a dramatic improvement.
- **Pricing discipline on PIPE:** Issuing at an 8% premium to market rather than the typical PIPE discount shows management was negotiating on behalf of existing shareholders.

### Evidence of Repetition Risk

- **This company has a 37-year history of failing to generate sustained profitability.** Two consecutive profitable quarters, while encouraging, are statistically insufficient to declare the pattern broken.
- **The company is still completing a restatement of previously issued financial statements.** Material weakness in internal controls is a legacy problem that has not been fully resolved.
- **Management turnover continues:** Interim CFO, Interim Chairman, and the departure of Flexon (the turnaround architect) all within the past six months.
- **AI/data center pivot language is emerging.** Management has begun referencing "expansion into the AI data center market" and "800 VDC systems." This is exactly the type of narrative that attracts promotional interest in micro-caps and may signal a shift from operational discipline to growth narrative.

## 7. Forward-Looking Capital Allocation Risk

### What Management Is Likely To Do Next

1. **Refinance or repay $25.3M in exit notes:** This is the most pressing capital need. Options include: (a) internally generated cash flow ($5M+/quarter Adjusted EBITDA), (b) asset sales, (c) additional equity issuance, or (d) replacement debt facility. Given the company's current profitability trajectory, a combination of (a) and (d) is most likely. Another equity raise would be further dilutive.

2. **Pursue Nasdaq/NYSE uplisting:** Management has stated this as a goal. Uplisting would require completing the financial restatement, maintaining OTCQX listing standards, and meeting exchange listing requirements. An uplisting would be genuinely value-accretive by expanding the investor base and improving liquidity.

3. **Continue bolt-on distributor territory acquisitions:** The Cal Microturbine playbook (buy distributor, bring in-house, capture higher margins) is likely to be repeated. These are small, self-funded, and margin-accretive -- the right kind of M&A at this stage.

4. **Grow the EaaS rental fleet:** Plans call for 2 MW annually through 2028 at ~$1.2M/MW. This is capital-intensive but generates recurring revenue at 98% utilization rates. This is disciplined capital deployment if utilization holds.

### Where Capital Is Most Likely To Be Misallocated

1. **AI/data center narrative-chasing:** The mention of "AI data center applications" and "800 VDC systems" is a flag. Every micro-cap company in 2025-2026 wants to claim AI relevance. If management begins allocating capital to chase this narrative rather than focusing on the core microturbine/EaaS business, it would signal a pivot from operational discipline to promotional behavior.

2. **Overpaying for distributor territories:** If the Cal Microturbine acquisition was priced correctly, future acquisitions done at higher multiples (because the stock price is up 600%) would represent management paying more because they can, not because they should.

3. **Management retention spending:** With an interim CFO, interim chairman, and a relatively new CEO, the compensation committee may feel pressure to issue larger equity grants to attract and retain talent. This would accelerate dilution.

### Free Cash Flow at Risk

YTD operating cash flow through Q3 FY2026 was only $2.0M despite $12.3M in Adjusted EBITDA. The gap reflects working capital absorption (accounts receivable nearly doubled from $7.0M to $11.4M with revenue growth) and non-cash adjustments. If revenue growth continues at 30-40%, working capital will continue to absorb cash, limiting the company's ability to self-fund debt repayment and growth simultaneously.

## 8. The One Thing That Determines Value Creation

**The single most important factor for predicting management's future capital allocation behavior is whether the $25.3M in exit notes can be refinanced without significant further equity dilution.**

This dominates all other considerations because:

- The entire exit note balance is now classified as current (due within 12 months)
- Cash on hand of $14.4M is insufficient to cover it from existing resources
- Operating cash flow generation has been modest ($2.0M YTD through nine months) due to working capital needs
- If management is forced into another equity raise at any price lower than the current ~$5.53, it signals the market that the company cannot self-fund -- and the stock's 608% gain becomes a financing mechanism rather than a reflection of value creation
- If they can refinance into a traditional credit facility at reasonable terms, it validates the turnaround and preserves shareholder value

Everything else -- the AI data center opportunity, the Cal Microturbine playbook, the rental fleet expansion -- is secondary to this refinancing event.

## 9. What Consensus May Be Missing

**The market is likely underestimating the economic impact of Goldman's 37.5% preferred unit overhang on per-share value creation.**

The stock chart shows a company that went from $0.25 to $5.53 -- a story of turnaround success. And at the operational level, the turnaround is real. But public shareholders do not own 100% of the economics of this turnaround. They own a holding company that controls 62.5% of the operating subsidiary. Goldman Sachs owns a preferred position with a redemption right in 2030 that will grow proportionally with the company's value.

Here is the math that most retail investors in this OTC micro-cap likely have not done:

- Current market cap: ~$130M (based on ~23M public shares at $5.53)
- Goldman's 37.5% OpCo claim at current implied valuation: ~$49M
- Total implied enterprise value being claimed: ~$179M
- Plus $25.3M in exit notes: ~$204M total claims
- Against trailing twelve-month revenue of $110M and ~$16M annualized Adjusted EBITDA

The 608% return looks less impressive when you realize public shareholders bear 100% of the governance risk and operating volatility but capture only 62.5% of the economic upside. Goldman's preferred position is structurally senior, redeemable, and growing in value as the stock rises.

This makes me neither more nor less trusting of management -- Canino inherited this structure and did not create it. But it makes me more skeptical that the current stock price fully reflects the diluted economic reality of public shareholders' position.
