# Capstone Green Energy Holdings, Inc. (OTCQX: CGEH) -- Financial Analysis

**Date:** February 21, 2026
**Price:** ~$5.53 | **Market Cap:** ~$120M | **Shares Out:** ~22.9M (common) + 0.5M (non-voting) + 3.5M (prefunded warrants)
**Fiscal Year End:** March 31 | **OTC-traded (OTCQX)**

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

CGEH presents a paradox: two consecutive quarters of GAAP net income, accelerating revenue growth, and dramatic margin expansion -- yet the company's own auditors flag "substantial doubt" about its ability to continue as a going concern. The stock trades at ~$120M market cap against a business that generated $1.3M in net income over the last nine months. The $25.3M Exit Roll Up Notes maturing December 7, 2026 are the single most important variable. Everything else is noise until that refinancing is resolved.

The "turnaround" is real at the operating level. But normalized earnings power is far below what the current valuation implies. The market is pricing hope -- specifically, a successful debt refinancing, an uplisting to Nasdaq/NYSE, and sustained high-30s gross margins driven by the AI/data center narrative. If any of those legs break, the stock has substantial downside.

## 1. BUSINESS FINANCIAL PROFILE

| Attribute | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Capital intensity | Low-moderate (CapEx ~$0.8M-$0.9M/yr; asset-light relative to revenue) |
| Cost structure | Mixed; significant fixed costs in R&D/overhead, variable COGS on product sales |
| Operating leverage | High -- demonstrated by 42% revenue growth driving swing from operating loss to operating income |
| Cyclicality | Moderate -- tied to energy infrastructure spending, partially offset by recurring service/rental revenue |
| Revenue quality | Mixed -- 55% product sales (lumpy), 30% parts/service (recurring), 15% rentals (semi-recurring) |
| Post-restructuring | Emerged from Chapter 11 in December 2023; still carrying reorganization-era debt and accounting complexity |

**Critical structural point:** CGEH is not the operating entity. It is the public holding company that sits above an Operating Subsidiary (Capstone Green Energy LLC). The subsidiary issued Series A Redeemable Preferred Units representing 37.5% equity ownership to reorganization creditors. These preferred units have a liquidation preference and are redeemable starting in the six-month period following December 7, 2029. This structure means the common equity sits below ~$70.9M of temporary equity on the balance sheet.

### Key Income Statement Observations

**Revenue growth is real but requires context.** The +42% YTD FY2026 growth is measured against a depressed FY2025 base, which was itself down 6% from FY2024 due to post-restructuring disruption. Revenue is still below the FY2024 run rate on an annualized basis ($110.7M annualized from 9-month FY2026 vs. $91.2M in FY2024). The growth story is partly recovery, partly organic.

**Gross margin expansion is the most important positive signal.** The jump from 16% (FY2024) to 27% (FY2025) to 32% (YTD FY2026) -- and 39% in Q3 FY2026 -- is striking. Drivers cited: favorable product mix (shift toward higher-capacity units), DFMA cost-out initiatives, Cal Microturbine acquisition (margin accretion from bringing a distributor in-house), and pricing improvements. The Q3 39% margin is likely above sustainable levels -- it coincided with a particularly favorable product mix. Management's own estimate for FY2027 revenue of $124.8M at $0.26 EPS implies margins closer to the low 30s on a full-year basis.

**SBC is immaterial.** At $0.6M YTD ($0.3M in FY2025), stock-based compensation is not a meaningful drag. This is one of the few micro-cap turnarounds where SBC is genuinely not distorting earnings.

**The gap between "EPS from operations" and "reported EPS" is enormous and not well understood.** Q3 FY2026 reported $0.06 EPS from operations but $(1.79) reported EPS after HLBV accretion. YTD: $0.07 vs. $(2.79). This is because the Preferred Units' redemption value is marked to the stock price. As the stock rises, the preferred unit accretion charge increases, creating a larger per-share loss to common holders despite improving operations. This is a non-cash accounting artifact, but it reflects a real economic claim on the business.

### Critical Balance Sheet Issues

**1. Working capital deficit: $(22.9)M.** Current liabilities exceed current assets by $22.9M. The $25.3M Exit Roll Up Notes are classified as current because they mature within 12 months (December 7, 2026). Excluding the notes, working capital is roughly breakeven -- but you cannot exclude the notes. They are real and due.

**2. The December 2026 maturity wall is existential.** The $25.3M Exit Roll Up Notes (comprising $21.1M principal + $4.3M accrued PIK interest) mature December 7, 2026. The company has $15.2M in cash. Even assuming positive operating cash flow of $5-8M over the remaining ~11 months, the company cannot repay these notes from internal cash generation. It must refinance, raise equity, or negotiate an extension.

**3. The November 2025 PIPE primarily went to debt repayment.** Of the $15.0M gross PIPE proceeds, $8.3M was immediately used to repay the Exit New Money Notes (which matured December 7, 2025). Only ~$5.5M of net proceeds flowed to the balance sheet for operations after fees.

**4. Covenant risk is real.** The company must maintain $3.5M-$4.0M minimum liquidity (rolling 7-day average) and meet minimum Adjusted EBITDA thresholds. It has already needed two covenant waivers (June 2024, June 2025). Compliance is "probable" per management but not guaranteed.

**5. Stockholders' deficit of $(63.3)M.** This is driven by the reclassification of the $70.9M Redeemable Noncontrolling Interests (Preferred Units) into temporary equity. The preferred units' redemption value is marked to the higher of the stock price or the aggregate purchase price plus unpaid dividends. As the stock has risen from sub-$1 to $5+, this accretion charge has exploded. From March 31, 2025 ($13.9M) to December 31, 2025 ($70.9M), temporary equity increased by $57M -- almost entirely driven by stock price appreciation being reflected in the preferred unit redemption value.

**Enterprise Value:** ~$120M equity + $25.3M debt - $15.2M cash + $70.9M preferred units = ~$201M. However, whether the preferred units should be included in EV depends on how you treat their redemption -- they are not callable until 2029 and their value fluctuates with the stock price. A conservative EV range is $130M-$201M.

### What Must Be True for This Valuation to Make Sense

1. **The $25.3M Exit Roll Up Notes must be refinanced** at or before maturity (Dec 7, 2026). Without refinancing, the company faces insolvency. The market is pricing in near-certainty of a successful refinancing.

2. **Gross margins must sustain at 30%+.** FY2024's 16% margin was the trough. The market is pricing in structural improvement, not a return to historical averages. If margins revert to mid-20s, normalized earnings collapse to near-zero.

3. **Revenue growth must continue.** Consensus expects $124.8M in FY2027 (+18% vs. FY2026E). This requires continued strength in product demand and data center pipeline conversion. A slowdown to $100-105M range would make the stock expensive.

4. **The preferred unit overhang must resolve favorably.** The $70.9M (and growing) preferred unit claim represents a potential future cash outflow or dilution event. If redeemed in 2029 at the then-current stock price, it could be devastating to common equity holders.

### What Breaks Financially

- **Refinancing failure:** If the company cannot refinance the $25.3M notes, it faces a liquidity crisis. The stock could trade toward zero.
- **Margin reversion:** If Q3's 39% was a peak and margins normalize to 25-28%, the business barely breaks even at the operating level.
- **Working capital squeeze:** Growing revenue requires growing inventory and receivables. If the business scales to $125M in revenue, it may need $25M+ in working capital that it does not currently have.
- **Dilution:** The company has already diluted from ~18.6M to ~26.9M fully diluted shares in 9 months. Further equity raises to fund the debt refinancing would dilute further. A $25M equity raise at current prices would add ~4.5M shares, or ~17% dilution.

### Market-Implied Expectations

At $5.53 per share, the market implies:
- Successful refinancing of Exit Roll Up Notes (near certainty)
- Sustained gross margins of 30%+ (above the company's entire pre-2025 history)
- Revenue continuing above $100M annually
- Eventual resolution of the preferred unit overhang without destroying common equity value
- Potential uplisting to a major exchange, unlocking institutional investor access

## KEY FINANCIAL DRIVERS

**1. Gross margin trajectory (most important).** This is the swing factor. At 30%+ gross margins, the business generates meaningful operating income and FCF. At 25%, it breaks even. At 20%, it loses money. The entire turnaround thesis rests on whether the margin improvement is structural (DFMA cost-outs, distributor acquisition, higher-capacity product mix) or cyclical (favorable one-time mix). Monitor quarterly gross margins as the single most important leading indicator.

**2. Debt refinancing outcome.** The $25.3M Exit Roll Up Notes maturing December 7, 2026 are a binary catalyst. Success unlocks continued operations and likely uplisting. Failure triggers a solvency crisis. There is no middle ground.

**3. Revenue scale.** The business needs to stay above ~$95-100M in annual revenue to generate positive operating income at normalized margins. Below that level, the fixed cost base ($22-24M in normalized opex) consumes all gross profit.

## WHAT CONSENSUS MAY BE MISSING

**The HLBV / Preferred Unit accretion is not just an accounting artifact -- it represents a real, growing claim on the business that the market may be underestimating.**

The Series A Redeemable Preferred Units represent 37.5% ownership of the operating subsidiary and carry a liquidation preference. The redemption value is marked to the higher of the stock price (with adjustments) or the aggregate purchase price plus unpaid dividends. As of December 31, 2025, this stood at $70.9M -- up from $13.9M just nine months earlier, driven almost entirely by the stock's rise from ~$1 to $5+.

Here is the paradox most investors likely miss: **the more the stock rises, the larger the preferred unit liability becomes, which increases the accretion charge, which worsens reported EPS, and which increases the potential future cash claim on the business.** At the current stock price, the preferred unit holders could theoretically demand redemption of $70.9M+ starting in mid-2030. That is roughly 59% of the current market cap. If the stock were to reach $10, this figure could approximately double.

Most retail investors focused on the "two consecutive profitable quarters" headline are likely not modeling the preferred unit dynamics at all. The $0.06 quarterly EPS from operations is what gets cited. The $(1.79) reported EPS, which includes the preferred unit accretion, is what actually matters for common equity holders' economic interest in the business.

Additionally, the Q3 FY2026 39% gross margin may be creating a false expectation of the business's earning power. One quarter does not make a trend. The first half of FY2026 ran at 29% gross margin. The full FY2025 was 27%. A realistic mid-cycle gross margin is more likely 29-32% than the 39% that the market may be extrapolating.

## OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**The operating turnaround is genuine. The valuation and capital structure are fragile.**

CGEH has delivered real improvements: gross margins have expanded meaningfully from post-restructuring lows, adjusted EBITDA has turned positive, and the business has posted two consecutive quarters of net income. These are not trivial achievements for a 37-year-old company that had never before posted a full year of positive EBITDA.

However, the stock at $5.53 (~$120M market cap) is pricing in a successful resolution of every outstanding risk simultaneously: debt refinancing, margin sustainability, revenue growth continuation, and a favorable preferred unit outcome. The normalized earnings power I estimate ($3.4M-$5.5M after tax, or $0.13-$0.20/share) does not support the current price on fundamentals alone. Even the consensus FY2027 EPS of $0.26 implies a 21x forward P/E for an OTC-traded, going-concern-flagged, micro-cap industrial company with a complex capital structure. Comparable small industrial/power companies typically trade at 10-15x earnings.

**The risk/reward is asymmetric to the downside from here.** The upside case requires everything to go right. The downside cases -- margin reversion, refinancing failure, or dilutive equity raise -- each have significant probability and would impair the stock substantially.

**Verdict: Financially improving but fully priced (or overpriced) relative to normalized earnings power. The going concern risk and preferred unit overhang create a risk profile inconsistent with the current valuation.**
