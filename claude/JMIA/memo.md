## Key Drivers (The 1-3 Things That Actually Matter)

1. **Nigerian Naira stability** - Not GMV growth, not logistics efficiency, not customer acquisition. Currency is the load-bearing variable. Nigeria is 47% of business. Naira lost 70% since June 2023, 41% in 2024 alone. Local operating performance is irrelevant if FX destroys USD value.

2. **Structural logistics cost problem** - Last-mile delivery costs 28% of GMV vs 5-10% in developed markets. This is not a scale problem that solves with volume. It is an infrastructure problem that requires decades and billions of capex outside Jumia's control.

3. **Cash runway vs. path to breakeven** - $134M liquidity, burning $75-85M annually = 5-7 quarters. Company will need to raise again. At 53% dilution since 2020, this is a serial equity destruction machine.

---

## Thesis: Why This Is A Pass

1. **No variant perception available.** The bull case (Africa demographics, rising middle class, logistics moat) is well-known and priced into $1.5B market cap. The bear case (structural unprofitability, FX destruction, social commerce competition) is equally visible. There is no edge here - just a binary bet on whether Africa e-commerce ever works.

2. **The "Amazon of Africa" narrative is structurally flawed.** Social commerce (WhatsApp/Instagram at 32%+ of digital commerce) may be the superior model - zero take rates, solves trust via personal relationships, flexible logistics. Jumia is not competing with Konga; it is losing to informal retail and chat-based selling.

3. **Unit economics have not improved in 5+ years.** CAC was $63 at IPO vs LTV of $5-11. Active customers declining (10M to 8.3M). 40% 90-day repurchase rate. Operating leverage has not materialized despite years of "investment."

4. **Currency risk is unhedgeable at company level.** Even if local operations improve, USD financials will collapse on next Naira crisis. This is a bet on Nigerian monetary policy - not on Jumia's execution.

5. **Competition is not attacking because market is unattractive.** Jumia is "protected by unattractiveness." This is not a moat; it is a warning sign about the opportunity itself.

## The Setup

**What This Business Is:** Jumia operates an e-commerce marketplace and logistics network across 11 African countries, with Nigeria (47%) and Egypt as primary markets. $720M GMV, $167M revenue, ~23% take rate.

**Why It Exists at $1.5B Market Cap:** Pure optionality on African consumer growth + scarcity premium as only liquid African e-commerce play. This is a "hope" stock, not a "value" stock.

**Structural Forces Against It:**
- Informal retail (70-90% of spending) has trust, credit extension, and relationship advantages
- Social commerce is growing faster at zero take rate
- Infrastructure requires government investment Jumia cannot control
- FX translation destroys any local gains for USD shareholders

## Forward Path & Catalysts

**What Would Need to Happen for Long Thesis:**
1. Nigerian Naira stabilizes for 6+ quarters (reserves at $40B is constructive but fragile)
2. GMV growth in USD terms turns positive and accelerates
3. Logistics cost as % of GMV declines materially
4. Operating losses narrow toward breakeven
5. No equity raise for 12+ months

**Realistic Assessment:** Items 1-5 have never occurred simultaneously. Betting on all five is not investing; it is speculation.

**Near-term Events:**
- Q4 2024 / Q1 2025 earnings - will show continued FX headwinds
- Potential equity raise in 2025 (5-7 quarter runway)
- Nigerian monetary policy decisions

## Risks & Kill Switches

**For Any Long Position (Hypothetical):**
- Another 30%+ Naira devaluation - thesis dead
- Equity raise at distressed prices - permanent dilution
- Customer count continues declining - model not working
- Social commerce share gains accelerate - structural disruption

**For Any Short Position (Hypothetical):**
- Nigerian currency stabilization / oil price spike - could rally 50-100%
- Strategic acquisition (Alibaba, Amazon interest) - takeout premium
- African governments crack down on Temu/Shein - local player advantage

**Why Neither Side Is Attractive:** Asymmetry is unclear. Longs face FX/dilution risk; shorts face squeeze risk on any positive narrative. This is a coin flip with expensive carry on both sides.

---

## Positioning & Portfolio Logic

**Decision: No Position**

**Why This Does Not Deserve Capital:**
1. No identifiable edge - both bull and bear cases are consensus
2. Key drivers (FX, logistics infrastructure, consumer behavior) are outside company control
3. Binary outcome dependent on macro factors with no clear timing
4. Opportunity cost is high - capital deployed here cannot be deployed in situations with clearer edges
5. Position sizing would need to be tiny given volatility, making even a "win" immaterial

**What Would Move This Out of Too Hard Pile:**
1. Two consecutive years of USD GMV growth with margin improvement
2. Logistics cost declining below 20% of GMV
3. JumiaPay penetration above 50%
4. Nigerian Naira stability for 8+ quarters
5. Path to profitability visible within existing cash

Until multiple of these conditions are met, this remains a speculative vehicle unsuitable for capital allocation in a disciplined portfolio.

## Final Word

Jumia is not a bad company pursuing a bad strategy. It is a reasonable company pursuing a potentially impossible objective in a structurally challenging environment. The "Amazon of Africa" narrative assumes African e-commerce will follow the Western playbook. The evidence suggests it may not - social commerce, informal retail, and cash-based economies may represent a different equilibrium, not a temporary state.

**The honest assessment:** We do not know if Jumia will succeed, and neither does anyone else. The difference between a good investor and a bad one is recognizing when "I don't know" is the right answer and acting accordingly.

**Decision: NEUTRAL (Too Hard).** High conviction in the pass. Revisit if structural conditions change.
