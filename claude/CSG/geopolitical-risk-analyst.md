# CSG N.V. (Czechoslovak Group) - Geopolitical Risk Assessment

**Geopolitical Risk Level:** High
**Primary Risk Vector:** Ukraine revenue concentration; peace-driven demand cliff
**Tail Risk Severity:** Material (not existential - diversification underway)
**Key Monitoring Signals:** Ukraine peace negotiations; EU/NATO defense budget commitments; Serbia sanctions developments

---

## The One Geopolitical Risk That Matters

**Ukraine revenue concentration is the thesis risk.** 42% of 2024 revenue came from a single wartime customer whose ability to pay depends entirely on Western financial support. A negotiated peace or Western fatigue could compress this revenue stream by 50-80% within 12-24 months.

The market prices CSG as a structural European defense play. It is actually a Ukraine war derivative with optionality on European rearmament. If Ukraine demand normalizes before NATO restocking fully materializes, there is an air pocket.

---

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**Consensus view:** CSG is a prime European defense beneficiary; Ukraine exposure is a feature, not a bug; peace would hurt short-term but NATO 5% GDP target provides floor.

**Non-consensus insight:** The market underestimates the timing mismatch risk. Ukraine revenues are here now; NATO restocking is a 2027-2032 story. The December 2025 Slovak framework agreement (up to EUR 58B) looks impressive but is a 7-year maximum capacity figure, not contracted revenue. Actual EU ammunition procurement via SAFE has been slow to operationalize. A peace deal in H1 2026 could create 12-18 months of revenue uncertainty before structural NATO demand fills the gap.

**Second non-consensus point:** Serbia exposure may be underappreciated. CSG owns 50% of ZVS Holding (Slovakia) and 100% of IMK 14 Oktobar (Serbia). Serbia suspended arms exports in June 2025 under Russian pressure. Serbian entities appear on EU sanctions circumvention watchlists. While CSG's Serbian operations appear clean, regulatory scrutiny of Serbia-based defense suppliers is intensifying. This is headline risk waiting to happen.

## Sovereign & Regime Risk Assessment

### Czech Republic (Home Jurisdiction)
- **Political stability:** High. NATO/EU member. Strong rule of law.
- **Intervention risk:** Low. Defense exports are government-supported policy.
- **Concern:** Historical Strnad family ties to former President Zeman and figures connected to Russian interests (pre-2022). These appear to have been severed post-invasion but could resurface in opposition attacks.

### Slovakia
- **Political stability:** Moderate. Fico government has shown more Russia-sympathetic tendencies.
- **Intervention risk:** Low-moderate. ZVS Holding is 50% state-owned; Slovak government is contractual partner.
- **Concern:** Political volatility could complicate EU defense procurement coordination.

### Serbia
- **Political stability:** Moderate. Vucic balancing EU accession with Russia ties.
- **Intervention risk:** Material. Serbia suspended all arms exports June 2025 under Russian pressure.
- **Concern:** Serbian subsidiary IMK 14 Oktobar produces rocket launchers and ammunition. Serbia is not subject to EU sanctions compliance. Serbian entities have appeared on EU circumvention watchlists. Reputational and operational risk if Serbia-Russia tensions escalate.

### United States
- **Political stability:** High.
- **Intervention risk:** Low. CFIUS already cleared Kinetic acquisition (November 2024).
- **Concern:** Foreign ownership of U.S. ammunition production could face political scrutiny if U.S.-Czech relations deteriorate.

## Regulatory & State Intervention Risk

**Export control exposure:**
- Czech Ministry of Industry and Trade controls all military equipment exports. CSG requires ongoing export licenses.
- EU dual-use regulations (Regulation 2021/821) apply to technology transfers.
- U.S. ITAR does not directly apply to Czech production but affects any U.S.-origin components.

**Forced divestment risk:** Low in NATO jurisdictions. The Kinetic acquisition passed CFIUS review.

**License revocation risk:** Low under current political alignment. Would require major shift in Czech/EU policy toward Russia.

**Key dependency:** Czech government actively supports CSG's Ukraine deliveries through the Czech Ammunition Initiative. This is policy, not commercial, support. A change in government or EU policy could alter this framework.

---

## Conflict & Sanctions Exposure

**Primary exposure: Ukraine conflict**
- 42% of revenue is directly tied to an active war.
- CSG has delivered 2.7M+ artillery shells to Ukraine since 2022.
- Planned production JV in Ukraine creates direct physical exposure to conflict zone.

**Sanctions risk:**
- **Direct:** None. CSG is not on any sanctions list and is a NATO supplier.
- **Indirect/reputational:** Serbian subsidiary operates in jurisdiction that refuses EU Russia sanctions. Serbia suspended arms exports June 2025 after Russian pressure over munitions appearing in Ukraine.
- **Circumvention risk:** Low but non-zero. EU has sanctioned Serbian entities for Russia sanctions evasion. CSG must ensure Serbian operations are not supplying sanctioned parties.

**Export control risk:**
- U.S. Entity List: Not applicable currently. CSG is aligned with NATO interests.
- BIS/OFAC: No exposure identified.

---

## Currency & Capital Controls

**FX exposure:**
- Revenue in EUR, USD, CZK, and various emerging market currencies.
- Ukraine payments are in hard currency (likely EUR/USD) backed by Western aid packages.
- No significant capital controls in primary jurisdictions (Czech Republic, Slovakia, EU, U.S.).

**Repatriation risk:** Low. Operations primarily in NATO/EU jurisdictions with free capital movement.

**India exposure:** Rail subsidiary (DAKO-CZ INDIA) operates in jurisdiction with historical capital controls. Immaterial to overall business.

## Scenario & Tail Risk Framing

### Base Case: Status Quo Continuation
- Ukraine conflict continues at current intensity through 2026.
- NATO restocking accelerates under 5% GDP commitment (target 2035).
- CSG executes on EUR 14B backlog; Kinetic integration proceeds.
- Revenue continues 20-30% growth trajectory.

### Adverse Case: Peace Agreement + Delayed NATO Procurement
- Ukraine peace deal in H1 2026 sharply reduces immediate ammunition demand.
- Ukraine revenues decline 60-70% over 18 months.
- NATO restocking slower than projected (bureaucratic delays, budget constraints).
- Revenue flat to down 15-25% in 2026-2027 before recovering.
- Stock de-rates from current defense premium.

### Severe Tail Case: Multiple Adverse Developments
- Ukraine peace + Western fatigue on defense spending.
- Serbia subsidiary caught in sanctions controversy.
- Strnad family legacy connections become political liability.
- Customer concentration (42% Ukraine) becomes permanent impairment as Ukraine lacks resources to maintain equipment.
- Revenue down 40%+; valuation compression of 50%+.

## Mitigants & Offsets

**Geographic diversification underway:**
- Kinetic acquisition (November 2024) adds EUR 1.3B U.S. commercial ammunition revenue, reducing Ukraine concentration.
- KNDS partnership provides entry to core European defense OEM relationships.
- EUR 58B Slovak framework agreement (7-year maximum) provides optionality on EU demand.

**Structural tailwinds:**
- NATO 5% GDP target (2035) is treaty-level commitment.
- EU SAFE program provides EUR 150B in defense financing.
- European ammunition production capacity still below wartime requirements.

**Strategic importance:**
- CSG is #2 medium/large-caliber and #1 small-caliber ammunition producer in Europe.
- Critical supplier status provides some protection against adverse regulatory action.

**Contractual protections:**
- Framework agreements with Slovak MoD and other governments provide visibility.
- However, Ukrainian purchases depend on Western funding, not contractual guarantees.

## Final Assessment

CSG is a well-positioned European defense company riding structural tailwinds, but the stock is priced for perfection with minimal margin of safety against its primary risk: Ukraine revenue concentration.

The 42% Ukraine revenue share creates binary exposure to peace negotiations. Market consensus treats this as manageable because NATO restocking will fill any gap. This ignores timing risk. Ukraine demand is monetizing now; NATO procurement operates on multi-year budget cycles.

The Serbia exposure is a sleeper risk. While immaterial to financials, it creates headline vulnerability in a sector where reputational alignment with NATO/EU is paramount.

**This is not a "too-hard" pile situation, but position sizing should reflect the Ukraine timing mismatch risk. The Kinetic acquisition materially improves the risk profile by adding diversified U.S. revenue, but the stock's 2026 performance will be dominated by Ukraine peace negotiations, not European defense fundamentals.**
