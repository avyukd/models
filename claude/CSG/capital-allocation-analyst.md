# CSG N.V. - Management & Capital Allocation Analysis

## Key Findings

**The core question: Can Michal Strnad be trusted with incremental capital?**

The answer is cautiously positive, with important caveats. Strnad is a genuine owner-operator with 85% ownership, a 360-day lock-up (double the standard), and a track record of value-creating acquisitions. However, this is a founder who just extracted EUR 3 billion in personal liquidity from the IPO while simultaneously asking public shareholders to fund "general corporate purposes" including more acquisitions. The misalignment is subtle but real: he de-risked personally while keeping control and the acquisition machine running.

**Non-Consensus Insight:** The market views CSG as a disciplined acquirer riding a defense tailwind. The deeper pattern is that Strnad has demonstrated exceptional timing but at progressively larger scale. The Kinetic Group deal saw CSG raise its bid four times (from $1.91B to $2.225B) in a competitive auction. This is not the behavior of a disciplined capital allocator - it is the behavior of someone determined to win at any price. Whether the defense supercycle justifies paying up remains to be seen.

## 2. Incentive Structure & Alignment

### Ownership Alignment

Strnad's 85% ownership creates strong structural alignment - he bears 85 cents of every euro of value destroyed. This is the single most important alignment mechanism.

**Lock-up:** 360 days (double standard 180-day period). This signals commitment but also means potential selling pressure in January 2027.

### Compensation Design

No public disclosure of Strnad's compensation. As sole owner pre-IPO, he could extract value through salary, dividends, or related-party transactions. Post-IPO, the company targets 30-40% dividend payout from 2027.

**What the incentive structure encourages:**
- Growth through acquisition (Strnad explicitly focuses on "new acquisitions and key business projects")
- Empire building is personally rational when you own 85% and are valued on revenue multiples
- No disclosed return-on-capital hurdles or ROIC targets in compensation

**Who benefits if stock underperforms?**
- Strnad already extracted EUR 3 billion in the IPO - his downside from this point is substantially reduced
- Future acquisitions create larger enterprise value even if returns are subpar

### Critical Question: Why Did Strnad Sell EUR 3 Billion?

The IPO structure is notable:
- Primary proceeds (new shares): EUR 750 million to company
- Secondary proceeds (Strnad selling): EUR 2.55+ billion to Strnad personally

Strnad sold 4x what the company raised. The stated rationale is diversification and liquidity. The behavioral interpretation: even a committed owner saw the current valuation as attractive enough to monetize 15% of his stake.

## 3. Stock-Based Compensation Discipline

**No disclosed SBC program.** As a recently IPO'd company that was 100% founder-owned, there is no legacy equity compensation to evaluate.

**Forward risk:** Post-IPO companies typically implement management equity plans. Watch for:
- Size of equity pool created
- Whether buybacks materialize to offset dilution
- Executive option grants at current elevated prices

**Current dilution picture:** Clean. 1 billion shares outstanding. No options overhang disclosed.

## 4. Capital Allocation Track Record

### a) M&A Track Record (Dominant Activity)

CSG is fundamentally an acquisition platform. Key deals:

| Year | Target | Price | Outcome |
|------|--------|-------|---------|
| 2013 | Tatra Trucks (65% stake) | CZK 176M (~$8M) | **Home run.** Acquired distressed asset at foreclosure auction. Pre-tax profit of CZK 435M within 18 months. Production tripled by 2022. |
| 2022 | Fiocchi Munizioni (70%) | Undisclosed (Fiocchi had ~EUR 380M revenue, ~EUR 109M EBITDA) | Created Ammo+ division. Subsequently acquired remaining 30%. |
| 2024 | Kinetic Group (Federal, Remington, CCI, Speer) | $2.225B | **Price escalated four times** during competitive process (started at $1.91B). Strategic logic sound (Western ammunition dominance) but paid up significantly. |
| 2025 | Various (nitrocellulose plant, ZVI, MUST Solutions, Greek JV) | Undisclosed | Vertical integration and capacity expansion. |

**Pattern analysis:**
- Early deals (Tatra): Distressed acquisition, minimal capital at risk, enormous upside
- Recent deals (Kinetic): Strategic premium, competitive auction, significant capital commitment
- Strnad has moved from opportunistic value buyer to strategic premium payer

**M&A concerns:**
1. The Kinetic Group bid escalation (four price increases in 6 months) suggests emotional commitment to winning, not disciplined capital allocation
2. Deal became politically contentious in US (CFIUS review, Congressional criticism)
3. Scale and complexity increasing rapidly - 100+ subsidiaries, 14,000+ employees across multiple continents

### b) Organic Reinvestment

- Tatra Trucks: CZK 7 billion investment planned (largest in company history)
- Consistent capacity expansion across defense systems
- Revenue growth: EUR 1B (2022) to EUR 4B (2024) to EUR 6.4B+ (2025E) - mix of organic and acquired

EBIT margins expanded ~800bps (2022-2024 pro forma), suggesting operational improvement not just revenue growth. This is a positive signal.

### c) Debt Usage

**Current position:** Net debt/EBITDA of 2.1x as of September 2025 (post-Kinetic). Investment grade adjacent (Fitch BBB-, Moody's Ba1, S&P BB+).

**Recent financing:**
- June 2025: $1B + EUR 1B dual-tranche bond issuance (4x oversubscribed)
- June 2025: CZK 10B domestic bond (largest Czech issuance ever)
- IPO proceeds: EUR 750M primary

**Assessment:** Leverage is moderate for an acquisition-driven defense company. Financing costs are reasonable (5.25-6.5%). Strnad has demonstrated ability to access capital markets.

**Risk:** Defense spending tailwinds could reverse. If EBITDA contracts while debt remains, leverage ratios could deteriorate quickly.

### d) Capital Returns

- No dividends to date (100% founder-owned)
- Target: 30-40% of net profit from 2027
- No buyback history or stated program

## 5. Learning vs. Repetition

**Evidence of learning:**
- Operational turnarounds (Tatra) suggest management capability, not just deal-making
- Margin expansion indicates cost discipline
- Vertical integration (ammunition components) shows strategic thinking

**Evidence of concerning repetition:**
- Kinetic Group price escalation suggests willingness to overpay to "win"
- Acquisition pace accelerating, not moderating, despite increasing complexity
- Pattern of larger and larger deals (classic empire-building trajectory)

**Key uncertainty:** Does Strnad have a limit? At what point does he decline an acquisition? There is no public evidence of deals walked away from.

## 6. Forward-Looking Capital Allocation Risk

**Most likely capital deployment:**
1. Further ammunition/defense acquisitions (stated priority)
2. Capacity expansion in existing facilities
3. Dividends from 2027 (though founder with 85% stake could change policy)

**Primary risk vectors:**

1. **Overpaying for acquisitions:** The Kinetic precedent suggests Strnad will pay strategic premiums. In a hot defense M&A market, this creates value destruction risk.

2. **Integration complexity:** 100+ subsidiaries across 9 countries. Each acquisition adds operational burden. No evidence of post-merger integration issues yet, but complexity compounds.

3. **Geopolitical dependency:** 26% of sales to Ukraine. Defense spending is government-driven. Policy changes (peace negotiations, budget cuts) could rapidly impact backlog quality.

4. **Lock-up expiration (January 2027):** Strnad selling more shares would be negatively perceived even if economically rational.

---

## 7. The One Thing That Determines Value Creation

**The single most important factor:** Whether Strnad maintains acquisition discipline as deal sizes increase.

The Tatra acquisition at CZK 176M in 2013 was a masterclass in opportunistic value investing. The Kinetic Group acquisition at $2.225B in 2024 - after four price increases in a competitive auction - looks more like strategic necessity overpaying for assets.

If Strnad's M&A behavior follows the Tatra template (opportunistic, distressed, contrarian), CSG will compound value. If it follows the Kinetic template (strategic premium, competitive bidding, price escalation), the next cycle's acquisitions will destroy value.

**What would change behavior:** An acquisition that visibly fails. A leveraged deal that strains the balance sheet. A recession in defense spending. None of these have occurred yet.

---

## 8. What Consensus May Be Missing

**Consensus view:** CSG is a disciplined acquirer with a phenomenal track record, riding the defense spending supercycle with an aligned founder at the helm.

**What consensus may miss:**

1. **Strnad already de-risked personally.** He extracted EUR 3 billion in the IPO. His marginal dollar at risk is now far lower than pre-IPO. Incentives shift when founders monetize.

2. **The acquisition pattern is escalating, not stabilizing.** Deal sizes are growing. Competitive auctions are being won by raising bids. This is classic late-cycle serial acquirer behavior.

3. **Governance is effectively absent.** There are no meaningful checks on Strnad. The Board is his management team. Minority shareholders have no protection beyond his 85% ownership alignment.

4. **Political risk is real and underappreciated.** The Kinetic deal drew Congressional scrutiny. Allegations of Russian connections (subsequently denied) surfaced. Operating in defense across NATO and selling to Ukraine creates headline risk.

**Net assessment:** The market may be over-trusting this management team based on historical returns without accounting for the behavioral shift that often accompanies IPO liquidity and increasing scale.
