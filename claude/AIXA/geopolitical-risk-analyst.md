# Aixtron SE (AIXA) -- Geopolitical & Sovereign Risk Assessment

## Geographic Exposure Map

Asia accounts for approximately 60% of Aixtron's revenue, with Europe at ~20% and Americas at ~20%. In 9M 2024, Asia was 62% of revenue, up from 44% in the prior year. Aixtron does not disclose China as a separate line item, but China is the dominant component of Asia revenue -- likely 35-50% of group revenue based on SiC order commentary and the company's stated 50-60% win rate on 200mm SiC opportunities in China. All manufacturing is in Germany (Herzogenrath), with a US subsidiary and Asian service operations. Key customers are compound semiconductor fabs (GaN, SiC, InP) concentrated in China, Taiwan, Japan, South Korea, Europe, and the US.

## Sovereign & Regime Risk Assessment

Germany is Aixtron's domicile -- stable democracy, strong rule of law, member of Wassenaar Arrangement. The primary sovereign risk vector is China, where Aixtron sells to state-linked and private compound semiconductor fabs. China has no history of seizing foreign equipment supplier relationships but does have a track record of accelerating domestic substitution when threatened. The 2016 blocked acquisition by Fujian Grand Chip Investment (CFIUS/Obama executive order) established precedent that the US views Aixtron's technology as strategically sensitive, even though it is a German company.

## Regulatory & State Intervention Risk

This is where Aixtron's risk profile diverges meaningfully from consensus framing.

**MOCVD is not lithography.** The 2025 EU dual-use control list update explicitly added atomic layer deposition, lithography, EUV pellicles, and etching equipment. MOCVD equipment is not specifically enumerated in the updated Wassenaar-derived lists. MOCVD deposits epitaxial layers of compound semiconductors -- it is upstream of the logic/memory fabrication chokepoints that US/EU export controls target. The BIS Entity List and October 2022/2023 semiconductor rules focus on advanced logic (sub-14nm), DRAM, NAND, and the equipment enabling those nodes. Compound semiconductors (GaN, SiC, InP) serve power electronics, RF, optoelectronics -- different end markets from the AI chips driving the export control escalation.

**However, dual-use risk is real.** GaN has direct military applications: the US Navy's AN/SPY-6 radar and Marine Corps' AN/TPS-80 G/ATOR radar use GaN components. MOCVD is required to produce these. The 2016 CFIUS block cited "military applications of the overall technical body of knowledge" of Aixtron. Germany already requires export licenses for some Aixtron shipments to China -- management has referenced delays to export license approvals in prior earnings calls.

**The mechanism that matters:** EU/German export licensing on a case-by-case basis, not a blanket ban. The risk is not binary prohibition but progressive tightening -- longer approval timelines, more end-use scrutiny, potential denial of specific customers with military linkages.

**US secondary pressure:** The US has pressured Germany, the Netherlands, Japan, and South Korea to restrict semiconductor equipment exports to China. ASML and Tokyo Electron faced explicit restrictions. Aixtron has so far avoided equivalent treatment because compound semiconductor equipment is not in the same strategic tier as EUV lithography or advanced etch tools. But this distinction could narrow if US-China tech competition extends to power semiconductors and RF/defense applications.

## Conflict & Sanctions Exposure

Direct sanctions exposure is low -- Aixtron is not selling to sanctioned entities. The risk is indirect: if the US escalates semiconductor controls to encompass compound semiconductor equipment, or if Germany independently restricts sales under national security authority, Aixtron's China revenue could face impairment. A Taiwan contingency would disrupt Asian demand broadly but would not specifically target Aixtron's business model.

China's retaliatory export controls on gallium and germanium (July 2023, escalated December 2024 with a functional ban on US-bound gallium) create a second-order risk. GaN and SiC substrates require gallium and silicon carbide feedstock -- if China restricts gallium exports more broadly, it paradoxically could impair non-Chinese compound semiconductor production while benefiting Chinese fabs that have domestic gallium access.

## Currency & Capital Controls

Revenue is primarily EUR-denominated with USD and CNY exposure. China capital repatriation risk is minimal since Aixtron ships equipment from Germany and collects payment -- it does not have trapped capital in China. FX volatility (EUR/CNY) affects competitiveness but is not an existential risk.

## Scenario & Tail Risk Framing

**Base case:** Status quo continues. Germany issues export licenses with increasing scrutiny and occasional delays. China remains 35-50% of revenue. Domestic Chinese MOCVD competitors (AMEC, others) gain share gradually in commoditized LED MOCVD but cannot match Aixtron in SiC and advanced GaN.

**Adverse case:** EU aligns with US pressure and restricts compound semiconductor equipment exports to China. Aixtron loses 50-70% of China revenue over 12-24 months. Revenue impact: 15-30% of group revenue. Manageable but painful, especially given Aixtron's operating leverage.

**Severe tail case:** Comprehensive export ban on all semiconductor equipment to China, or a Taiwan military contingency. Aixtron loses most of its 60% Asia revenue. This would be an existential revenue shock requiring complete business model reorientation. Low probability but non-zero.

## Mitigants & Offsets

- MOCVD's position outside the current export control perimeter provides a structural buffer
- 70-90% market share means customers have limited alternatives even if controls tighten (switching costs are high)
- Aixtron's German domicile gives it some insulation from US unilateral action -- requires EU/German alignment
- Growing Western demand from AI/data center power (800V architectures requiring GaN/SiC) diversifies away from China dependence over time
- The 2016 CFIUS block paradoxically preserved Aixtron's independence and Western alignment

## The One Geopolitical Risk That Matters

If EU export controls expand to encompass compound semiconductor MOCVD equipment for China, Aixtron loses its largest single market. The trigger would be US pressure on Germany to restrict compound semiconductor equipment, likely catalyzed by evidence of Chinese military end-use of GaN produced on Aixtron tools. The impact would be 15-30% revenue loss in the adverse case, with limited near-term substitution from Western demand.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

Consensus treats Aixtron's China exposure as a generic "China risk" discount. This is imprecise in both directions:

**The risk is lower than headlines suggest** because MOCVD is not in the same strategic tier as lithography/etch equipment. The 2025 EU dual-use update did not add MOCVD. The technology serves power electronics and optoelectronics, not advanced logic -- the actual target of export controls. Aixtron has operated through multiple rounds of semiconductor export control escalation without being directly restricted.

**But the risk is more path-dependent than appreciated.** The distinction between "strategic" and "non-strategic" semiconductor equipment is political, not technical. If GaN's military applications become a political flashpoint, MOCVD could move from "tolerated" to "restricted" quickly. The 2016 CFIUS block shows US national security apparatus already views Aixtron's technology as sensitive. The gap between "not currently restricted" and "could be restricted" is narrower than the market assumes.

The real non-consensus insight: **China's gallium export controls may actually benefit Aixtron's China business in the medium term.** Chinese fabs with domestic gallium access become relatively more competitive, driving more MOCVD tool demand within China while non-Chinese compound semiconductor producers face feedstock constraints. This is a perverse outcome where Chinese retaliation against Western semiconductor controls strengthens demand for Western equipment inside China.

---

**Geopolitical Risk Level:** Moderate

**Primary Risk Vector:** EU/German export control expansion to compound semiconductor equipment

**Tail Risk Severity:** Material (adverse case impairs 15-30% of revenue; severe tail case is existential)

**Key Monitoring Signals:**
- EU dual-use list updates adding MOCVD or compound semiconductor deposition equipment
- German BAFA export license denial rates and processing times for Aixtron China shipments
- US diplomatic pressure on Germany regarding compound semiconductor equipment
- Evidence of Chinese military procurement of GaN/SiC produced on Aixtron tools
- Chinese domestic MOCVD competitor capability in SiC and advanced GaN (AMEC progress)
- China gallium/germanium export control scope changes
