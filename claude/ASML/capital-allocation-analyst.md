# ASML Holding N.V. (NASDAQ: ASML) - Capital Allocation Analysis

## The One Thing That Determines Value Creation

**R&D discipline during cyclical downturns.** ASML spends 14-16% of revenue on R&D regardless of cycle position. This counter-cyclical R&D commitment enabled EUV monopoly and now High-NA. The question is whether new CEO Christophe Fouquet maintains this discipline under investor pressure during inevitable cycle troughs. Wennink's decade of 1,300% returns was built on never cutting R&D when others would have. If Fouquet blinks, the moat erodes.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**The buyback program is real, not SBC offset theater.** Unlike most tech companies where "buybacks" merely neutralize dilution, ASML's share count has declined from ~410M shares (2018) to ~387M (2025) - a genuine 5-6% reduction over seven years. With SBC at only $187M (0.5% of FCF), buybacks are additive to per-share value. The market may underestimate how much of the EUR 12B buyback programs actually accrues to shareholders vs. management.

## 2. Incentive Structure & Alignment

### Compensation Mix (CEO - 2024)
| Component | Amount (EUR) | % of Total |
|-----------|--------------|------------|
| Base Salary | ~979K | 18% |
| Equity (LTI) | ~4,279K | 79% |
| Pension/Other | ~174K | 3% |
| **Total** | **5,432K** | 100% |

### Performance Metrics

**STI (Short-Term Incentive):**
- Target: 80% of base salary
- Mix: 60% financial / 40% qualitative
- Financial metrics not publicly specified in detail

**LTI (Long-Term Incentive):**
- Target: 110-120% of base salary (CEO), 100-120% (other BoM)
- Metrics per prior policy:
  - ROAIC: 40% weight
  - Relative TSR vs. SOX Index: 30% weight
  - Technology Leadership Index: 20% weight (removed for 2025)
  - Sustainability: 10% weight

**What This Structure Encourages:**
- ROAIC emphasis (40%) is unusual and positive - incentivizes returns on capital, not just growth
- Relative TSR (30%) ensures management cares about stock performance vs. peers
- Removal of Technology Leadership Index in 2025 is concerning - may signal shift toward shorter-term metrics

**Who Benefits If Stock Underperforms:**
- 82% of CEO comp is equity-linked, so significant alignment
- However, Fouquet's 0.002% ownership is trivially small for a EUR 250B+ company
- No meaningful insider buying beyond token purchases

### Red Flags
- Supervisory Board explicitly chose NOT to match US tech comp norms for LTI - this is disciplined
- 2025 policy increased STI/LTI levels citing "market alignment" - the eternal creep begins
- New hire policy changes may signal loosening of comp discipline

## 4. Capital Allocation Track Record

### 4a. Reinvestment (R&D)

| Year | R&D Spend | % of Revenue |
|------|-----------|--------------|
| 2022 | EUR 3.25B | 15.4% |
| 2023 | EUR 3.98B | 14.4% |
| 2024 | ~EUR 4.3B | ~15.2% |

- Consistent 14-16% of revenue regardless of cycle - counter-cyclical discipline
- R&D enabled EUV monopoly and now High-NA development
- No evidence of cutting R&D during 2023 downturn

**Capacity Expansion:**
- Netherlands: 357,000 sqm expansion (50 football fields), 20,000 new employees by 2028
- Germany: EUR 100M/year investment in Berlin facility
- Dutch government committed EUR 2.5B infrastructure support ("Operation Beethoven")
- Target: 600 DUV + 90 Low-NA EUV + 20 High-NA EUV systems annually by 2027-28

**Risk:** Expansion assumes demand materializes. If AI capex cycle disappoints, ASML will have overcapacity. However, expansion is customer-funded through long lead time orders - reduced balance sheet risk.

### 4b. M&A History

| Year | Target | Price | Strategic Logic | Outcome |
|------|--------|-------|-----------------|---------|
| 2001 | Silicon Valley Group | - | DUV capability, Intel access | Wilton now major R&D center |
| 2007 | Brion Technologies | - | Computational lithography | Core to Holistic Lithography |
| 2012-13 | Cymer | EUR 1.95B | EUV light source | Critical for EUV monopoly |
| 2016 | Hermes Microvision (HMI) | $3.1B | E-beam metrology | Integrated into portfolio |
| 2017 | Carl Zeiss SMT (24.9%) | EUR 1B + EUR 760M | EUV optics alignment | Strategic vertical integration |
| 2020 | Berliner Glas Group | - | Technical glass capability | Integrated |

**Assessment:**
- Every acquisition was strategically coherent - vertical integration for EUV monopoly
- No empire-building or diversification folly
- Cymer and Zeiss stakes were mission-critical for EUV success
- Timing was reasonable - bought when needed, not at cycle peaks
- No evidence of overpayment or subsequent write-downs

**Grade: A-tier M&A discipline.** Rare to see this coherence in a tech company.

## 5. Learning vs. Repetition

**Evidence of Learning:**
- EUV development took 20+ years - management stayed course despite skepticism
- M&A became more targeted over time (from SVG platform acquisition to surgical Zeiss stake)
- Capacity expansion phased with customer commitments, not speculation

**No Evidence of Repetition of Mistakes:**
- No write-downs from acquisitions
- No dividend cuts
- No R&D cuts during downturns
- No cycle-peak empire building

**CEO Transition Risk:**
- Wennink built the playbook over 25 years
- Fouquet promoted internally (16 years at company) - culture continuity expected
- However, Fouquet's track record is unproven at CEO level
- Watch for: comp creep, R&D discipline under pressure, M&A temptation

---

## 6. Forward-Looking Capital Allocation Risk

**What Management Will Likely Do:**
1. Continue EUR 12B buyback through 2028
2. Maintain 14-16% R&D intensity
3. Execute Netherlands expansion on schedule
4. Grow dividend ~5% annually

**Where Capital Could Be Misallocated:**
1. **Overcapacity risk** - If AI capex cycle disappoints, excess capacity in 2028+
2. **M&A temptation** - Adjacent market expansion (metrology, advanced packaging) could dilute focus
3. **Comp creep** - 2025 policy changes signal potential Dutch-to-US compensation drift

**Probability Assessment:**
- R&D cut risk: Low (embedded culture)
- Bad M&A risk: Low (no pattern, Fouquet is internal)
- Overcapacity risk: Moderate (partially mitigated by customer pre-payments)
- Comp creep risk: Moderate (2025 policy is warning sign)
