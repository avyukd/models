# Semiconductor Equipment Industry Analysis

## The One Industry Factor That Matters

**AI-driven WFE (Wafer Fab Equipment) spending concentration.** The semiconductor equipment cycle has decoupled from traditional PC/smartphone demand. AI-related capex now dominates the industry's growth trajectory. TSMC alone plans $52-56B in 2026 capex — more than Intel and Samsung combined. ~70% flows to advanced logic and HBM. This single customer's spending trajectory is the industry's marginal driver.

**What would cause it to inflect:** Hyperscaler capex slowdown, AI training efficiency gains reducing GPU demand, or TSMC capacity utilization dropping below 80% at leading-edge nodes.

**How to monitor:** TSMC monthly revenue data, hyperscaler capex guidance (MSFT, GOOG, META, AMZN quarterly calls), HBM pricing and lead times.

---

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**Bearish insight the market underweights:** The semiconductor equipment industry is experiencing an unprecedented demand concentration. AI-related spending now dominates, but traditional end markets (PC, smartphone, automotive) are flat-to-declining. This creates a single-point-of-failure risk that the current valuation does not reflect.

**The variant view:** Consensus treats this as a "structural growth" story. The reality is more nuanced — this is cyclical peak demand from a single use case (AI training infrastructure), not broad-based semiconductor demand recovery. Memory orders at 56% of ASML bookings in Q4 2025 reflect HBM buildout, not DRAM/NAND recovery. If AI capex stalls, there is no second wave of demand behind it.

**China's 50% domestic equipment mandate** is underappreciated. China's localization rate rose from 23% to 35%+ in 18 months and may exceed 50% by end-2025. This is structural demand destruction for Western equipment vendors, not a temporary headwind. ASML's China revenue falling from 41% (2024) to 20% (2026 guidance) is the visible symptom; the underlying cause is permanent share loss in the world's largest equipment market.

## Cycle Position: Late Cycle / Peak

| Indicator | Reading | Interpretation |
|-----------|---------|----------------|
| ASML book-to-bill | 1.4x (Q4 2025) | Strong, but booking surge concentrated in memory/HBM |
| Backlog | EUR 38.8B (1.2x 2025 revenue) | Orders booked into 2027 — visibility high, but backlog often peaks before cycle turns |
| Gross margin | 52.8% (2025) | Near top of 10-year range (45-53%) |
| Operating margin | 34.6% (2025) | Record levels; 35.3% in Q4 |
| WFE growth | +11.9% in 2025, +9% in 2026E | Decelerating from 2024 peak |
| Customer concentration | TSMC 37% capex increase; Samsung/Intel cutting | Demand increasingly narrow |
| Memory vs Logic mix | 56% memory in Q4 2025 bookings | First time memory > logic; HBM-driven |

**Assessment:** The industry is in late-cycle or at peak. Margins are at record highs. Backlog is extended but concentrated. Growth is decelerating. The telltale sign: memory capex surging while logic customers ex-TSMC are cutting. This historically precedes cycle turns.

## Mid-Cycle Definition

| Metric | Mid-Cycle Normal | Current (2025) | Gap |
|--------|------------------|----------------|-----|
| ASML gross margin | 47-49% | 52.8% | +400-600 bps |
| ASML operating margin | 28-32% | 34.6% | +250-650 bps |
| WFE growth | 3-5% | 11.9% | Well above trend |
| Customer capex concentration | 50% top 3 | 65-70% top 3 | Elevated |
| Memory share of bookings | 30-40% | 56% | Elevated |

Mid-cycle for semiconductor equipment means: (1) Capacity utilization at leading fabs around 75-80%, (2) capex growing with end-market semiconductor demand (~5% CAGR), (3) balanced demand across logic and memory, (4) gross margins in the mid-to-high 40s for ASML.

**Current conditions exceed mid-cycle across every metric.** Margins, utilization, and growth rates are all above normalized levels.

## Industry Structure

| Dimension | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| Structural trajectory | **Growth** (but slowing; AI extends cycle, doesn't create new paradigm) |
| Competitive structure | **Oligopoly** (top 5 = 70%+ share; ASML monopoly in EUV) |
| Barriers to entry | Extreme — decades of R&D, EUV development took 20+ years, $10B+ investment |
| Customer concentration | High — TSMC, Samsung, Intel = ~60% of industry capex |
| Pricing power | Strong — limited alternatives for leading-edge equipment |

## Historical Cycle Patterns

| Cycle | Peak-to-Trough Duration | Peak-to-Trough Decline (SOX) | Key Trigger |
|-------|-------------------------|------------------------------|-------------|
| 2008 | 14 months | -55% | Financial crisis |
| 2018 | 12 months | -30% | Trade war, memory oversupply |
| 2022 | 12 months | -40% | Post-COVID demand normalization |

**Pattern:** Semiconductor equipment downturns last 12-18 months and typically see 30-50% revenue declines peak-to-trough for cyclical vendors. ASML has been less cyclical due to EUV monopoly, but still participates in industry turns with 15-25% revenue declines historically.

**Mean reversion drivers:**
- Capacity additions outpacing demand growth
- Memory oversupply (historically the trigger)
- Fab utilization dropping below 70%
- Customer capex cuts cascading (typically starts with Intel/Samsung, TSMC follows 2-4 quarters later)

---

## Key Risks to Industry Economics

1. **AI capex stall:** If hyperscalers pause or slow datacenter buildout, there is no offsetting demand. Traditional PC/smartphone/auto are flat.

2. **China localization:** Domestic Chinese equipment vendors (Naura, AMEC) gaining share rapidly. China's 50% domestic mandate accelerates this. Western vendors face permanent structural demand loss in world's largest market.

3. **Memory oversupply:** HBM capacity being added aggressively. SK Hynix, Samsung, Micron all expanding. HBM pricing will compress as supply catches demand — this historically triggers memory equipment cuts.

4. **EUV capacity overbuild:** ASML capacity booked through 2027. If AI demand disappoints, customers will defer or cancel orders (Samsung Taylor fab already delayed).

5. **Geopolitical disruption:** Export controls continue tightening. If extended to DUV or mature nodes, could create sharp demand cliff.

## Supply Response Analysis

**Time to add capacity:** New fab construction = 2-3 years. Equipment lead times = 12-24 months for EUV.

**Current capacity additions:**
- TSMC Arizona: ramping 2025-2026
- Samsung Taylor: delayed to 2026+
- Intel foundry: struggling with utilization
- SMIC/Chinese fabs: aggressively adding mature capacity

**Supply elasticity:** Low in the short-term (equipment production constrained), but high in the medium-term as fabs are added. The 2-3 year buildout cycle is what creates semiconductor equipment cyclicality.

---

## ASML-Specific Industry Position

**Monopoly economics:** ASML has 100% EUV market share and ~85% total lithography share. This provides unusual stability relative to the broader equipment industry. In past downturns, ASML revenue declined 15-20% while peers declined 30-40%.

**Book-to-bill at 1.4x in Q4 2025** suggests continued strength, but elevated memory orders (56%) are a warning sign — memory customers add capacity aggressively at peaks, then cut sharply.

**Backlog of EUR 38.8B** provides 12+ months visibility. However, backlog is a lagging indicator and has historically peaked before cycle turns.

**Guidance for 2026:** EUR 34-39B revenue (flat to +19% YoY). Wide range reflects uncertainty. Midpoint (+12%) implies deceleration from 2025's +15%.

---

## Second-Order Considerations

1. **HBM is a bubble within a cycle.** Memory companies are prioritizing HBM over commodity DRAM/NAND. If HBM pricing compresses (supply catching up), equipment spending will reverse sharply. Memory capex historically overshoots in both directions.

2. **The AI buildout has a completion date.** Unlike smartphones or PCs, AI training infrastructure may reach sufficiency. Once major models are trained, the marginal GPU demand declines. This is not priced into "structural growth" narratives.

3. **ASML's China exposure decline is not voluntary.** The drop from 41% to 20% revenue share reflects export controls, not diversification. This is structural demand destruction, not rotation.

4. **Customer concentration is extreme.** TSMC's capex trajectory is now the single most important variable for the entire equipment industry. If TSMC disappoints, there is no second-order demand to absorb it.
