# ASML Geopolitical Risk Assessment

**Geopolitical Risk Level:** High
**Primary Risk Vector:** Export controls / China revenue cliff
**Tail Risk Severity:** Material (not existential)
**Key Monitoring Signals:** Further DUV restrictions, China rare earth retaliation, Taiwan Strait escalation

---

## The One Geopolitical Risk That Matters

**Progressive export control tightening that eliminates ASML's China business entirely.**

The ratchet only turns one direction. EUV has been blocked since 2019. Advanced DUV (NXT:1970i/1980i) was restricted in late 2024. If the US-Netherlands-Japan trilateral coordination expands to all DUV immersion systems, ASML loses its entire China revenue (~20-25% of sales going forward).

**Trigger:** Chinese retaliation via rare earths, Taiwan Strait escalation, or US policy shift toward comprehensive decoupling.

**Impact:** China contributed €10.2B of €28.3B revenue in 2024 (36%). Even at the "normalized" 20% level, a complete ban would eliminate €6-7B in annual revenue. Stock would re-rate significantly lower on both earnings and multiple compression.

---

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**The market is pricing China decline—but may underappreciate the upside optionality and overweight the downside tail.**

1. **China revenue is already declining by design.** ASML guided to 20% China mix in 2026 vs 36% in 2024. The "stockpiling" is over. The market knows this. Analysts have already modeled the decline.

2. **Export controls are actually a positive for ASML's moat.** By blocking China's access to advanced lithography, the US is ensuring China cannot develop competitive fabs. This protects ASML's pricing power with non-China customers (TSMC, Samsung, Intel) who will remain captive buyers.

3. **SMEE is a decade away from being competitive.** China's domestic lithography champion just achieved 90nm production in 2025. They claim 28nm DUV capability but have not demonstrated high-volume manufacturing. EUV prototypes reportedly exist but are nowhere near commercial viability. Morningstar analysts estimate China is "years, if not decades" from economically viable EUV production. The SMEE threat is real at legacy nodes but irrelevant for leading-edge.

4. **Tariff risk may be overstated.** Initial panic over 20-25% EU tariffs on semiconductor equipment has moderated. Semiconductor manufacturing equipment appears likely to receive exemptions given strategic importance to US fab buildout (Intel, TSMC Arizona). ASML's monopoly position means tariffs would be passed through to customers anyway.

**Non-consensus view:** The geopolitical discount in ASML's stock may be appropriate or even excessive given that the China revenue decline is known, SMEE is not a near-term threat, and export controls actually reinforce ASML's monopoly with Western customers.

## Scenario Analysis

### Base Case (60% probability)
- China revenue settles at 20% of sales as guided
- No further major restrictions on remaining DUV systems
- SMEE remains non-competitive at leading edge
- ASML continues to dominate Western fab buildout

**Impact:** Earnings continue to grow driven by EUV demand. Stock trades at 25-35x forward earnings. Modest multiple compression from peak.

### Adverse Case (30% probability)
- Comprehensive DUV ban extends to all immersion lithography
- China retaliates with rare earth export restrictions causing supply chain disruption
- US tariffs applied without semiconductor exemption

**Impact:** Revenue declines 15-25% from peak China exposure. 6-12 month supply chain disruptions. Stock trades at 20-25x on reduced earnings. 30-40% downside from current levels.

### Severe Tail Case (10% probability)
- Taiwan Strait conflict or invasion
- TSMC operations disrupted or halted
- Global semiconductor supply chain collapse

**Impact:** ASML loses access to its largest customer (TSMC is ~25-30% of EUV demand). Revenue could decline 30-50%. Stock would face existential repricing. However, ASML would likely be needed for post-conflict reconstruction.

## China's Domestic Alternatives: SMEE Assessment

| Capability | SMEE Status (2025) | ASML Equivalent | Gap |
|------------|-------------------|-----------------|-----|
| i-line (legacy) | Mass production | N/A (ASML exited) | Closed |
| 90nm ArF | Just entering production | Decades-old tech | 15+ years |
| 28nm DUV immersion | Prototype stage | NXT:1970i restricted | 10+ years |
| EUV | Lab prototype (100-150W) | High-NA (500W+) | Decades |

**Key insight:** SMEE's 4% global market share is concentrated in i-line legacy equipment. Their 28nm claims are unproven at scale. EUV development—even with 3,000+ researchers—faces fundamental physics and engineering challenges that took ASML decades and billions to solve.

**The SMEE threat is to legacy nodes (40nm+), not to leading-edge where ASML's monopoly lies.**

---

## Rare Earth Dependency Risk

China controls ~90% of high-purity rare earth production. ASML's wafer stage motors use neodymium-iron-boron magnets.

**October 2025 China restrictions:** Case-by-case licensing required for exports to companies involved in 14nm-and-below chip production.

**ASML's mitigation:** Long production lead times and forward material planning provide buffer. CFO Dassen stated company is "well prepared." ASML has deployed €1.2B in strategic equity investments to secure supply chain.

**Assessment:** This is a harassment tool, not an existential threat. China needs rare earth export revenue. Weaponizing rare earths would trigger broader Western decoupling that hurts China more. ASML has inventory buffers.

---

## Taiwan Concentration Risk

TSMC is ASML's largest customer (~25-30% of EUV demand). Taiwan accounts for 39% of global wafer production.

**Risk:** Taiwan Strait conflict would be catastrophic but is also a global tail risk beyond ASML-specific analysis.

**Mitigants:**
- TSMC diversifying to Arizona, Japan, Germany
- ASML's installed base creates recurring service revenue
- Post-conflict reconstruction would require ASML equipment

**Assessment:** This is a systemic risk to the entire semiconductor industry, not an ASML-specific geopolitical risk. If Taiwan falls, ASML's stock price is not your primary concern.

## Tariff Risk Assessment

| Scenario | Probability | Impact |
|----------|-------------|--------|
| Full semiconductor equipment exemption | 50% | Negligible |
| Partial exemption (EUV only) | 30% | Moderate—DUV margins compressed |
| No exemption (20-25% tariff) | 20% | Material—passed to customers but could delay orders |

**Key insight:** ASML's monopoly position means tariffs are largely pass-through. Intel/TSMC/Samsung have no alternative supplier for EUV. Short-term order timing could be affected but not long-term demand.

---

## Mitigants & Offsets

1. **Monopoly position in EUV.** No alternative exists. Export controls reinforce, not undermine, this moat with Western customers.

2. **Geographic customer diversification.** TSMC Arizona, Intel Ohio, Samsung Texas reduce Taiwan concentration over time.

3. **Service and installed base.** 30%+ of revenue from recurring service. Installed base of 5,000+ systems globally creates annuity stream.

4. **Strategic importance to all parties.** US needs ASML for domestic fab buildout. Netherlands derives significant economic value. Even China benefits from service relationships on existing installed base.

5. **No credible alternative supplier.** SMEE, Canon, and Nikon cannot replicate ASML's EUV capability within a decade.

## Final Assessment

ASML faces genuine geopolitical risk, but the market narrative may overweight it:

1. **China revenue decline is known and guided.** This is not a surprise risk—it's priced.

2. **Export controls strengthen ASML's moat** by blocking Chinese domestic alternatives from catching up.

3. **SMEE is not a credible threat** at leading-edge nodes for at least a decade.

4. **Tail risks (comprehensive DUV ban, Taiwan conflict) are real** but difficult to hedge and represent systemic semiconductor risk, not ASML-specific risk.

5. **Tariff risk is manageable** given monopoly position and likely exemptions.

**Bottom line:** Geopolitical risk is material but may be appropriately priced or even over-discounted at current levels. The single factor that could permanently impair this investment is a Taiwan Strait conflict—but that's a global macro event, not an ASML-specific thesis risk. For ASML-specific geopolitical risk, the key question is whether comprehensive DUV restrictions occur before China revenue "normalizes." Current trajectory suggests ASML will navigate this transition without permanent impairment.
