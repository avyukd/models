# VIC Posting — Kinx Inc. (093320 KS)

## Description
Kinx Inc. ("Kinx") is the largest independent and publicly traded data center company in South Korea. We think Kinx trades at 6x-7x stabilized EBITDA upon the ramp of its new Gwacheon data center ("Gwacheon"). This ramp up is expected to take place in the next 12-24 months and is already being derisked per public disclosures. On our assumptions, Kinx trades at a large discount to the ~20x EBITDA multiple of local market transactions of individual data centers, as well as valuations of publicly traded peers across the world. Kinx and its parent company, Gabia, also have two prominent Korean activists activist investors as large holders in their shares, Miri Capital and Align. We think these activists and other shareholders desiring better governance practices recently gained a majority of parent Gabia's voting shares in December 2025, just in time for the record date for the March 2026 annual meeting. While we don't know how shareholders will vote, or what proposals there will be to vote on, we think it is likely that activists in these companies could successfully advocate for Gabia to adopt measures that increase the value of Kinx as it is Gabia's largest and most easily monetizable asset. The largest shareholder of Gabia, Miri, also owns nearly 13% of Kinx and has near equal economic interest in both companies. They furthermore have a track record of successfully realizing value in similar situations in Japan and Korea. We believe Kinx and Gabia are Miri's largest combined holding.

We believe this opportunity exists because Kinx has historically been controlled by Gabia. Despite owning just 37% of Kinx, South Korean law allows Gabia to consolidate Kinx as a subsidiary. This has led to Gabia siphoning off some value to itself at the expense of Kinx. As we invest more abroad, we are increasingly interested in situations where a company's value doesn't fully appreciate the extent of potential governance changes. We think this is the case for Kinx and believe this is emphasized by Miri continuing to accumulate shares in Kinx in recent months, even at materially higher prices than their initial purchases, post activists gaining the upper hand in voting last month.

We think the upcoming inflection in profits related to Gwacheon, other future data center projects Kinx is working on, and the prospect of improved governance could result in 130%-230% upside (50%-80% IRR) by the end of 2027. The fact that this is an AI/Data Center play in a market with active retail speculation adds optionality, on top of several fundamental and governance drivers, to realize upside.

## Company Overview

Kinx has several reported product lines/segments for its core business, but 80%+ of unconsolidated sales and almost all future growth comes from the company's Data Center (IDC) business, which will be the focus of this overview. For the sake of this analysis, we use the company's unconsolidated financials as consolidated results include minority stakes in other entities, such as publicly traded SPSoft, which aren't material to the value of the business.

### Data Centers (IDC) & Related Services - 80%+ of unconsolidated revenue

This portion of revenue is derived from Kinx's owned and leased data centers, as well as related revenue from networking services and its CloudHub product that are sold to its data center customers. In total, Kinx has 13.5MW of IT load split into the following categories:

- 10MW fully owned
- 2.5MW leased from other owners and released to smaller customers
- 1MW ownership of just networking services to customers, excludes rack rentals

Historically the company's data center business primarily involved it subleasing space in other companies' data centers to smaller customers and providing additional services (networking and CloudHub) to these customers. This materially changed with the opening of Gwacheon in October 2024 where it could sell its own IT capacity. Gwacheon increased the company's IT capacity by over 2.5x and also marked its entry into selling IT capacity to mid-sized "wholesale" customers.

Beyond the 13.5MW of IT load already described, Kinx is currently in negotiations to manage and sublease another 20MW-30MW of IT load from an upcoming data center that may begin construction soon. Such a project would more than 2x-3x the company's owned/leased IT load.

The company's data center business also monetizes data center customers by selling them additional services. These include:

- **CloudHub**: Provides customers with access to a multi-cloud environment hosted on servers in Kinx's data center space
- **Networking**: Provides infrastructure for customers to send data to/from their servers inside one of Kinx's data centers to any of its other data centers, or third-party end locations

Networking is a unique strength for Kinx vs comparable data center operators, in that it is "carrier-neutral", i.e. not part of the three main telcos in South Korea. In South Korea, these telcos have historically dominated the data center market and often require captive service where a customer cannot easily send/receive data outside of that telco's network. Kinx's "carrier-neutral" status, however, allows its data center customers to send/receive data without restricting which network they're using. This is very valuable to certain customer use cases, i.e. streaming or financial services, which send data to/from their servers frequently, often to places with differing ISPs/telcos providing coverage.

Kinx is the largest "carrier-neutral" provider in South Korea, and its unique position within the market has enabled it to have 70% of its pre-Gwacheon data center sales and 33% of Gwacheon's estimated revenue come from its networking services. This is far above the 15%-20% share of sales from similar services at other data center companies and enables Kinx to achieve higher revenue and EBITDA per MW of IT capacity than other public data center businesses. Growth in networking revenues occurs at a strong rate even after a data center is fully ramped, so we believe Kinx's networking mix gives their facilities a higher terminal growth rate than comps.

## Thesis Points

### Gwacheon facility already "derisked" and may be worth today's market cap on its own

The company has guided to KRW 53B-64B/yr of revenue from the facility when it's ramped which would translate to KRW 25B-30B+/yr of EBITDA, excluding leases. This compares to KRW 28B of ex lease EBITDA for the entire company in 2024.

Key derisking evidence:
- As of January 2026, Gwacheon is already 80%+ contracted which makes the facility effectively "full" per the company's definition to meet publicly disclosed guidance.
- Includes 3MW-4MW taken up by one of the largest tech companies in the country and another 3MW taken up by another larger customer.
- These contracts are accessible in financial filings on DART.
- In the monthly IR calls Kinx hosts at 1am-3am ET, the company has communicated that it has met its initial guidance and that there's another prospective customer which may be signed in the coming months which would make Gwacheon near 100% filled.
- South Korea's data center market is incredibly short on supply with vacancy rates at just ~5%.
- Power capacity constraints from the utility monopoly KEPCO mean that it is exceedingly difficult for additional data centers to be built in the Seoul metro.
- The Gwacheon facility is just a few miles from the middle of Seoul, and several industry experts viewed it as a location likely to see sustained high demand.

Publicly available contract terms and disclosures from the company in its December monthly IR call indicate rack rental revenue for Gwacheon at least in-line with the company's initial guidance of KRW 35B-40B/yr, with possible upside as capacity shortages in the country have led to a significant increase in rack rental rates recently. Kinx expects Gwacheon to also generate KRW ~20B/yr of networking revenue when first ramped by the end of 2027. This may increase to KRW 35B-40B/yr further in the future per company commentary.

### Activists & upcoming shareholder vote may catalyze value realization opportunity

In the past 18-24 months, two well-known activists with strong track records in Korea and Japan, Miri Capital and Align Capital Partners, have acquired approximately 40% of Gabia's stock, inclusive of the impact treasury stock has on voting shares.

- Miri has acquired ~25% of Gabia and ~13% of Kinx over the past two years and has aggressively bought Kinx shares on the open market in recent months, even at much higher share prices. Miri has a materially above average hit rate in Korea and Japan, including success with Dream Incubator which was a similar situation to that of Kinx. Via public disclosures, Miri's combined stake in Kinx and Gabia is its largest position and is over 20% of their currently disclosed investments.
- Align has also aggressively built to a 14% stake in Gabia, including via a recent tender offer. Align has also filed the Korean equivalent of a 13D for its position in Gabia. Align has also had significant success in Korea, most notably with JB Financial which has tripled since their investment started in mid-2022. Gabia is Align's third largest position that is publicly disclosed.

The timeframe for a board "flip" to outside shareholders could be as soon as the end of March 2026 when the 2026 shareholder vote occurs, to as late as spring 2027 when another vote could give a majority of the board to shareholders in favor of better governance.

### Extra "ways to win" from data center pricing and a new "capital-light" data center business

- Kinx has publicly communicated that it's in negotiations to be the third-party operator and master lessee of a new 40MW-60MW data center.
- Kinx would hold a master lease for up to 50% of the new data center's capacity (20MW-30MW) and profit from reselling this space to smaller customers, as well as providing networking services. This would be a more than 2x-3x increase in the IT load managed/owned by Kinx vs today's amount.
- Such an agreement would require minimal capital investment from Kinx (KRW 5B-20B).
- South Korea is very short on supply for data centers with a lack of power availability from KEPCO preventing enough new supply from coming online.
- Excluding the pass-through impact of power costs, pricing published by Gabia's data centers has increased by 30%-43% from December 2022 to September of 2025.

## Valuation

We think Kinx has 130%-230% upside through the end of 2027.

**Legacy Data Center/IX Businesses:**
- Should contribute KRW 33B-36B of ex lease EBITDA in FY26
- Valued at 15x-20x EBITDA

**Gwacheon Data Center:**
- Rack rental revenue should ramp to KRW 35B-40B/yr initially. KRW 16B/yr of cash costs, translating to KRW 19B-24B of ex lease EBITDA.
- Networking should initially ramp to KRW 18B-24B of annual revenue and KRW 5.5B-7B of ex lease EBITDA.
- Valued at 20x EBITDA/5% cap rate.
- Korean data center comps are cited as having 5% cap rates by several global real estate firms.
- A recent single-data center acquisition by Macquarie in the Seoul Metro occurred at a 20x EBITDA multiple.
- Publicly traded global data center comps trade at a median ~20x EBITDA multiple/5% cap rate.

**Platform value for "cap-light" data center business:**
- Present value on KRW 19.5B-27.5B 2030E ex lease EBITDA, discounted at 10% annualized rate.
- Valued at 15x-20x EBITDA.

**Target:** KRW 250k-350k/share at the end of 2027, a double to triple from current value.

## Risks

- Lack of governance changes at Gabia resulting in the company's true value being locked away or siphoned to Gabia.
- Outside shareholders gaining control of Gabia but siphoning value away from Kinx (mitigated by Miri's substantial ownership in both).
- South Korea's supply/demand environment for data centers turning negative (assessed as minimal risk given current supply shortage).

## Catalysts

- Ramp of Gwacheon data center
- Activist activity and shareholder votes
- New data center contracts
