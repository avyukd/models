## The Setup

South Korea's data center market is at ~5% vacancy in Seoul metro, constrained by KEPCO's power allocation bottleneck. Kinx is the largest independent, publicly traded DC operator in the country and the only carrier-neutral IX provider. The Gwacheon facility (opened October 2024) expanded capacity by 2.5x and is ramping with named anchor tenants.

The business is operationally sound. The problem has always been governance: Gabia owns 37% of Kinx but consolidates 100%, creating structural incentives for value extraction rather than value maximization. Two credible activists (Miri and Align) have accumulated combined ~40% of Gabia's shares and are pushing for board change through formal legal channels -- tender offers, 13D filings, AGM proposals including an independent director nominee (former Morgan Stanley M&A banker).

The Korean government is cooperating, albeit indirectly: the Value-Up reform wave has delivered two transformative legislative changes in seven months that directly benefit this activist campaign.

## Forward Path and Catalysts

**Near-term (weeks):** March 2026 Gabia AGM. If activists elect even one independent director, it signals the beginning of real oversight. This is the highest-information-content event in the near term.

**Medium-term (6-12 months):** Gwacheon revenue ramp toward guided KRW 53-64B. Quarterly results through 2026 will show whether the 80%+ contracted capacity converts to revenue on schedule.

**Longer-term (12-24 months):** Capital-light expansion (20-30MW for KRW 5-20B). If executed, this would be transformatively accretive but is currently speculative -- no counterparty or timeline confirmed.

**Spring 2027 AGM:** If March 2026 delivers only partial success, the second vote could deliver board majority at Gabia.

## Risks and Kill Switches

**Kill switch 1: March 2026 AGM fails entirely.** If activists cannot elect a single independent director despite apparent voting majority, the governance thesis is broken. Would force a full re-evaluation.

**Kill switch 2: KEPCO materially expands Seoul metro power.** HVDC grid completion or explicit policy reversal on supply restrictions would erode pricing power and compress achievable multiples from 15-20x toward 8-12x.

**Kill switch 3: Gwacheon anchor tenant loss.** Two tenants at 6-7MW combined represent ~20% of total capacity. Losing either creates a binary earnings event.

**Tail risk: KEPCO power reallocation.** KEPCO has statutory authority to revoke power from existing contracts. Low probability, severe impact. Unhedgeable.

**What I am NOT worried about:** Balance sheet (1.1x leverage), competitive entry (KEPCO constraints protect near-term), or traditional dilution (SBC is immaterial).

## Positioning and Portfolio Logic

**Position size: 1-2% (tactical).** This is a catalyst-driven position, not a core holding. The governance catalyst has a defined timeline (March 2026, spring 2027) which limits the duration of dead money risk.

**Why now:** The March 2026 AGM is weeks away. The treasury share cancellation law passed yesterday. The governance reform momentum is at its highest point. Waiting for "more certainty" means paying a higher price if the AGM goes well.

**What I would watch post-entry:**
- March 2026 AGM vote results (the single most important data point)
- Quarterly Gwacheon revenue trajectory
- KEPCO announcements on HVDC completion or power policy changes
- Any Align or Miri public communications signaling escalation or de-escalation

**Hedge consideration:** No direct hedge available for a Korean KOSDAQ small-cap. Portfolio-level, this is uncorrelated to US/EU equity beta, which is itself a form of diversification. Size discipline is the primary risk management tool.

## Resolving Key Tensions

**VIC claims 130-230% upside vs. financial analyst's 50-100%:** At the current price of ~KRW 124K (not the VIC's implied ~KRW 86K entry), both estimates compress. Realistic upside is 15-60%. The VIC's high-end requires 20x multiples which are aspirational for a Korean small-cap. I underwrite the financial analyst's more conservative framework but note that even 15-30% upside with defined catalysts and limited downside is an attractive risk/reward.

**Moat is IX/networking, not the building:** Correct and important. This means Kinx's durability is higher than a commodity colocation provider but its addressable market is narrower (peering-sensitive workloads, not general compute/AI training). The moat is moderate and narrow -- enough to sustain pricing, not enough to dominate.

**KEPCO risk vs. supply thesis:** The supply constraint is real today but policy-dependent. I weight it as a 2-3 year tailwind, not a permanent moat. The IX ecosystem is the durable advantage; KEPCO is the cyclical kicker.

**Miri dual ownership alignment:** This is the most underappreciated structural feature. An activist who owns both parent and subsidiary cannot play the value extraction game. This is genuinely unusual and mitigates the most common risk in Korean parent-sub situations.

## Bottom Line

Kinx is a moderate-quality business (IX moat is real but narrow) entering a high-conviction catalyst window (March 2026 AGM, governance reform legislation). At ~10-11x stabilized EBITDA, the stock is not cheap enough to own on fundamentals alone, but the governance catalyst creates asymmetric upside with defined timing. If the AGM delivers even partial success, the re-rating path to 13-16x is credible. If it fails, downside is limited because the underlying business is operationally sound at a non-demanding multiple.

This is a tactical buy with a 12-18 month horizon, sized at 1-2%, with the March 2026 AGM as the primary proof point. The variant is on governance timing, not business quality. If the AGM produces nothing, I would exit or reduce to a monitoring position.
