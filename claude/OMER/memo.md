## Key Drivers (The 3 Things That Matter)

1. **Yartemlea is the only approved therapy for a disease that kills 90% of patients.** There is no standard of care — prior treatment was supportive care and off-label eculizumab with poor outcomes. This isn't a "better mousetrap" drug; it's a monopoly filling a medical void. 61% complete response rate vs. death.

2. **NTAP reimbursement gap is a timing issue, not a risk.** CMS approved 54 technologies for NTAP in FY2026. For an FDA-approved orphan drug with no alternatives that meets cost thresholds, NTAP is formulaic. Omeros has already filed and presented at CMS's December town hall. Expected effective October 2026. The 9-month gap is real friction, but it resolves mechanically.

3. **The stock prices in the bear case.** At ~$730M EV, the market implies ~$150-200M peak sales — the low end of the outcome range. If Yartemlea works (500-750 patients globally at $350K avg), you get $175-260M peak revenue, 70-80% gross margins, and a 2-3x re-rating.

## Competitive Landscape

| Drug | Company | Mechanism | Stage | TA-TMA Response Rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Yartemlea (narsoplimab)** | Omeros | MASP-2 (lectin pathway) | **Approved** | 61% CR |
| **Ravulizumab (Ultomiris)** | AstraZeneca/Alexion | C5 inhibitor (long-acting) | **Phase 3** (peds data out, adult ongoing) | 17% CR (peds) |
| Eculizumab (Soliris) | AstraZeneca/Alexion | C5 inhibitor | Off-label | Poor outcomes, high infection risk |
| Pegcetacoplan | Apellis | C3 inhibitor | Early/investigational | No TA-TMA data |

**Key takeaway:** Ravulizumab is the only credible near-term competitor, but the clinical data favors narsoplimab by a wide margin (61% vs 17% CR). Different complement pathways — MASP-2 vs C5 — meaning they could coexist. AstraZeneca's commercial muscle is a risk, but orphan exclusivity through 2032 provides protection. Narsoplimab has also shown efficacy in eculizumab-refractory patients, establishing it as the superior agent in the pathway.

## Risks and Kill Switches

- **Launch stall pre-NTAP:** If Q1-Q3 2026 revenue is near-zero, it signals reimbursement friction worse than expected. Watch March 31 earnings closely.
- **Dilution:** Any equity raise below $10 damages the thesis. Convertible note conversions add pressure.
- **EMA rejection (mid-2026):** Eliminates European optionality (~$50-100M peak sales).
- **Ravulizumab approval in TA-TMA:** Would create a well-resourced competitor, even if clinically inferior. Timeline: likely 2027-2028 at earliest.
- **Adverse safety events post-launch:** Catastrophic for a single-product company.
- **Addressable market proves smaller than modeled:** If only 100-200 patients/yr are treated, peak sales ceiling drops to $50-100M and the stock is overvalued here.

## Why Buy (Changed from Neutral)

The original Neutral call was driven by "we have no data." That's true but misses the asymmetry:

1. **The stock already prices in the bear case.** At $730M EV, you're paying for ~$150M peak sales. The market is not giving credit for a successful launch.
2. **The primary "risk" — NTAP — is a formality, not an uncertainty.** CMS approves these mechanically. The question is timing (October), not probability (~100%).
3. **SOC is death.** 90% mortality untreated. Clinicians want this drug. The adoption barrier is economic (reimbursement), not clinical — and that barrier has an expiration date.
4. **2-3x upside vs ~40% downside** on base case assumptions. You don't need to be right about everything — you need Yartemlea to work in a disease with no alternatives, which is exactly what the clinical data shows.
5. **Short squeeze optionality.** 20% SI + positive revenue data = potential forced covering.

The risk you're taking is that the market is too small and/or the cash bridge doesn't hold. Both are real. But the expected value calculation favors a position at this price, sized appropriately for the binary risk profile.
