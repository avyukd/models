## 2. Key Drivers (The 3 Things That Matter)

**1. RWWI is structurally underearning, and the market is valuing it on trough economics.** The agency model transition required maintaining legacy infrastructure (billing, collections, fulfillment) while the new commission model ramped. OpEx has not yet been cut to reflect the lighter business model. As 3-year prepaid deals fully run off by end of CY2026, OpEx declines and cash flow normalizes. The Buyback Capital initiation valued the company at 10x LTM / 8x EBIT as of Sept 2025 -- if we use that earnings base and apply the current market cap, the stock has re-rated but is still pricing in trough margins, not normalized margins.

**2. Rand Simulation is a material, undiscovered second growth engine.** At ~$40M in revenue growing 20-30% annually as an ANSYS VAR, this segment diversifies away from pure ADSK dependency. The market almost certainly treats RWWI as a single-vendor ADSK play. Simulation operates the same outsourced model (consulting, implementation, resale) but for ANSYS -- a completely separate vendor with its own growth trajectory. This segment alone could be worth $150-250M at maturity.

**3. Channel consolidation is concentrating share in Rand at an accelerating pace.** Revenue +14.6% YoY, operating income +28%. Bronze/silver VARs are dying or selling. Addnode made two North American acquisitions in 2025. ADSK is explicitly redirecting incentives from renewals (now automated) to new sales -- exactly what Platinum VARs with real sales forces and implementation teams do best. RWWI controls ~1/3 of all North American ADSK seats, making it the dominant survivor.

The company is carrying redundant infrastructure costs that disappear as the agency transition completes. On normalized earnings, the multiple is likely 12-18x EBIT.

- **Rand Simulation diversifies the single-vendor risk that defines the bear case.** A ~$40M segment growing 20-30% as an ANSYS VAR means the ADSK dependency, while still dominant, is diminishing. This segment was invisible in our initial analysis and is invisible to most investors.

The playbook -- buy, delist, consolidate, compound -- is working. But this comes with governance risk (see below).

Addnode (European peer) has guided to the same normalization timeline. Once clean FCF quarters appear, the "is this business actually generating cash?" question disappears.

## 5. The Setup

Rand Worldwide operates IMAGINiT Technologies (largest North American ADSK Platinum VAR, ~1/3 of all NA seats) and Rand Simulation (ANSYS VAR, ~$40M revenue). Controlled by Peter Kamin's 3K LP, a ValueAct founder who delisted the company and runs it as a private compounder -- acquiring competitors at 20-30% IRRs and paying out all FCF as dividends.

The agency model transition (June 2024) moved RWWI from gross to net revenue recognition on ADSK sales. This is the source of all data confusion. Gross profit dollars were unchanged; only reported revenue dropped. The company is currently carrying excess OpEx from the pre-transition infrastructure that has not yet been rationalized. This creates temporary underearning.

The competitive landscape is consolidating rapidly. ADSK wants "fewer, more solution-focused channel partners." Smaller VARs are shutting down or selling. Every displaced customer is a potential Rand customer. The question is not whether Rand gains share -- it is how much and how fast.

## 8. Risks & Kill Switches

**Kamin/3K LP governance risk (NEW -- this is the primary risk).** Kamin executed a take-private at Calloway's Nursery using a reverse stock split that squeezed minority shareholders at an undervalued price. RWWI shareholders face the same risk. Mitigant: RWWI's ~$500M market cap makes this mechanically harder, and the more likely exit is an ADSK acquisition or uplisting. But this is a real risk that caps how large a position I would take.

**ADSK commission rate compression.** If ADSK squeezes Platinum VAR take rates beyond renewal adjustments, Rand's margins compress with no recourse. The Buyback Capital reader question about this was left somewhat open. Monitor ADSK incentive program changes closely.

**Kill switch:** If LOC has not begun declining by March 2026 quarter (Q4 FY2026), the temporary bridge thesis is wrong. Exit.

**Kill switch:** Any evidence of Kamin initiating a take-private transaction at below-market terms. Exit immediately.

**Re-evaluation trigger:** Addnode or other Platinum VARs reporting deteriorating economics, or ADSK building a direct mid-market sales team.

## 9. Positioning & Portfolio Logic

**Position: 2-3% of portfolio (upgraded from 1-2%).** The conviction upgrade reflects three new inputs: (1) Rand Simulation as a diversifying growth engine, (2) the underearning thesis providing a more favorable true valuation than our initial analysis suggested, and (3) Kamin's track record as a capital allocator, despite the governance tail risk.

**Why not larger:** The Kamin governance risk is real and unhedgeable. OTC liquidity constrains exit options. And the ADSK dependency, while diversifying via Simulation, remains dominant. A 2-3% position captures the upside without exposing the portfolio to unacceptable concentration in an illiquid, governance-challenged name.

**Entry approach:** Scale in over 2-3 months. OTC liquidity demands patience.

**Post-entry monitoring:** (1) LOC balance -- primary KPI; (2) ADSK earnings calls for channel strategy language; (3) Any 3K LP/Kamin filings suggesting structural transactions; (4) Addnode quarterly results as global VAR read-through; (5) Rand Simulation growth rate if any disclosure becomes available.

**What would make this a full-size position:** Governance clarity (uplisting, independent board representation, or ADSK acquisition interest becoming public) combined with one or two clean FCF quarters post-transition. Without governance improvement, 2-3% is the ceiling.
