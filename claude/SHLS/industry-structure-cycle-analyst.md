# Solar EBOS Industry Structure & Cycle Analysis

**Date:** 2026-02-24

## What Drives Profitability in Solar EBOS

Solar EBOS (electrical balance of system -- wiring harnesses, combiners, disconnects, monitoring) is a niche within the broader utility-scale solar value chain. EBOS represents roughly 5-8% of total project costs but is critical-path equipment. Profitability is driven by: (1) volume of utility-scale solar MW installed annually, (2) product mix and engineering complexity, (3) copper and commodity input costs, and (4) competitive intensity among a small number of suppliers. The North American EBOS market is approximately $2.1B, growing at a 12% CAGR through 2030.

Gross margins for EBOS leaders historically range 33-40%, reflecting moderate engineering differentiation and some switching costs (custom harness designs per project). This is meaningfully higher than commodity solar components but lower than software-like margins -- it is fundamentally a manufactured electrical product.

## Competitive Landscape -- Consolidating but Intensifying

The EBOS market was historically fragmented with Shoals as the dominant integrated player. That is changing fast:

- **Nextracker/Nextpower** acquired Bentek ($78M) and launched NX PowerMerge, directly targeting Shoals' core trunk EBOS market. Nextracker has the distribution relationships and can bundle EBOS with trackers.
- **Voltage LLC** is a direct competitor currently in patent litigation with Shoals (Shoals won an initial ITC ruling in Feb 2026).
- **GameChange Energy** acquired Terrasmart's EBOS division, pursuing a "module to grid" integrated offering.

The competitive dynamic is shifting from Shoals-as-monopolist to oligopoly with bundling pressure. Tracker companies integrating EBOS is the structural threat -- it reduces Shoals' ability to price independently. The Q4 2024 revenue miss was explicitly attributed to "competitive dynamics and volume discounts," confirming price competition is real and intensifying.

## Where Are We in the Solar Installation Cycle

US utility-scale solar installations hit approximately 41 GWdc in 2025 (per SEIA), with 2026 forecasts at 36-43 GW depending on source. This is the most important context: **the US is at or near peak annual solar installation rates driven by a policy-induced pull-forward.**

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (signed July 2025) created an aggressive phase-out: solar/wind projects beginning construction after July 4, 2026 receive only 60% of ITC/PTC, declining to 20% for 2027 construction starts, and zero for 2028+. Projects must be placed in service by end of 2027 to receive full credits if construction began before the cutoff.

This creates a textbook demand pull-forward through mid-2026, followed by a cliff. The record $747.6M backlog at Shoals reflects this pull-forward perfectly -- developers are racing to begin construction before the July 2026 deadline. The 2026 guidance of $560-600M likely captures this rush.

**The critical question is what happens to 2027-2028 installation rates.** Without ITC/PTC support, utility-scale solar economics depend entirely on merchant power prices and state mandates. Solar is cost-competitive in many regions on an unsubsidized basis, but the removal of 30% ITC meaningfully changes project IRRs and will reduce volumes. I estimate 2027-2028 installations could decline 25-40% from 2025 peak levels before stabilizing.

## Tariff Headwinds -- Structural, Not Transient

AD/CVD tariffs on Southeast Asian solar cells (Cambodia, Malaysia, Thailand, Vietnam) are now final, with combined rates of 50-80%+ for major manufacturers. Section 301 "reciprocal" tariffs add further costs. This is compressing margins across the solar supply chain. Shoals' gross margin compression to 31.6% (from 37.6%) reflects tariff-driven input cost increases and competitive pressure preventing full pass-through.

Module costs flowing through to higher project costs create a secondary demand headwind -- higher all-in project costs reduce the pipeline of economically viable projects, particularly post-ITC phase-out.

## BESS -- The Structural Growth Offset

US BESS installations hit 28 GW/58 GWh in 2025, with 2026 forecasts at 35 GW/70 GWh. Critically, **storage ITC was preserved through 2032** under the OBBBA, even as solar/wind credits phase out. This creates a divergence: BESS is the policy-favored growth vector while solar faces headwinds.

Shoals' $67M BESS backlog is early-stage but directionally important. EBOS for storage systems is a natural adjacency. However, BESS EBOS is a different competitive market with different incumbents, and Shoals' share is unproven. The BESS opportunity partially offsets the solar installation cliff but is unlikely to fully compensate in the 2027-2028 window.

## Mid-Cycle Definition

Mid-cycle for US solar EBOS means: 25-30 GW annual utility-scale installations, EBOS industry gross margins of 34-37%, steady but not frenzied backlog growth, and a stable policy environment. We are currently **above mid-cycle on volume** (41 GW in 2025) and **below mid-cycle on margins** (31.6% vs. 34-37% normal) due to tariff distortion. This is a late-cycle condition with a policy cliff ahead.

## The One Industry Factor That Matters

**The ITC/PTC phase-out timeline dominates everything.** The July 2026 construction-start deadline is the single most important variable for solar EBOS demand over the next 3 years. Current backlog and 2026 revenue guidance reflect the pull-forward. The question is entirely about the depth and duration of the 2027-2028 trough. Monitor: monthly utility-scale solar interconnection queue additions, SEIA quarterly installation forecasts, and any legislative attempts to extend or modify the phase-out.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

Consensus treats the record backlog and 2026 guidance as evidence of durable demand recovery. **This is likely wrong.** The backlog is inflated by a policy-driven pull-forward that creates a 2027-2028 air pocket. Simultaneously, Nextracker's EBOS entry is structurally compressing Shoals' pricing power in a way that may not mean-revert -- bundled tracker+EBOS is a superior value proposition for developers. The combination of a demand cliff and permanent competitive intensification is more bearish than the market's "growth stock at a discount" framing suggests. The partial offset from BESS is real but too small to bridge the gap.
