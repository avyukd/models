# California First Leasing Corporation (CFNB) - Management & Capital Allocation Analysis

**Price:** ~$27.50 | **Market Cap:** ~$244M | **Shares Outstanding:** ~8.9M

---

## Executive Summary

CFNB is a founder-controlled entity approaching the 90% insider ownership threshold required for a short-form merger. The Paddon family has demonstrated disciplined capital return via repeated tender offers at increasing prices, buying back shares below NAV. This is textbook aligned capital allocation. The primary investment thesis here is not operating fundamentals but rather a governance event: a likely going-private transaction at or near NAV (~$34/share) within 12-24 months. Alignment is structural, not contractual.

## 2. Incentive Structure & Alignment

### What the Structure Reveals

This is not a typical public company compensation problem. With ~69% direct ownership and family members holding another ~20%, Patrick Paddon's economic interests are overwhelmingly tied to per-share value, not salary.

**What behavior is this compensation encouraging?**
- Paddon benefits from share price appreciation and capital returns
- No meaningful incentive to dilute or empire-build
- Strong incentive to maximize NAV and eventually take the company private at favorable terms

**Who benefits if the stock underperforms?**
- Only outside shareholders. Paddon family has ~$200M+ exposure to stock price

**Key Compensation Observations:**
- Total non-interest expense for FY 2024: $2.7M (covering all operating costs for ~13-26 employees)
- This is remarkably lean for a $250M entity
- No evidence of stock-based compensation programs or option grants
- Paddon's last Form 4: purchased 24,991 shares at ~$17.85/share (February 2024) - buying, not selling

### Assessment

The compensation structure is effectively irrelevant here. Paddon owns the company. His "compensation" is equity appreciation and eventual liquidity at NAV. This is founder alignment at its most direct.

## 4. Capital Allocation Track Record

### A. Reinvestment: Minimal Organic Reinvestment Required

The leasing business is in managed runoff. Capital is being redeployed into:
- Liquid large-cap equities (MSFT, AAPL, NVDA per reports)
- Maintaining lease portfolio without growth

This is rational. A 13-employee operation has no reason to reinvest aggressively. The strategy is to hold liquid assets and return capital.

### B. M&A: None (Positive Signal)

No acquisition activity. The 2021 sale of CalFirst Bank to DMG Bancshares was a divestiture, not an acquisition. Paddon sold the bank, retained the leasing business, and registered as a closed-end fund.

**Assessment:** Zero empire-building behavior. Discipline maintained.

### C. Buybacks & Tender Offers: Excellent Execution

CFNB has conducted multiple tender offers to repurchase shares below NAV:

| Date | Price | Shares Sought | Shares Accepted | % of Outstanding |
|------|-------|---------------|-----------------|------------------|
| Nov 2022 | $16.00 | 375,000 | ~375,000 | ~4% |
| Oct 2023 | $16.50 | 200,000 | 394,069 | ~4% |
| May 2025 | $18.50 | 330,000 | 330,000 | ~3.5% |

**Key Observations:**
- All tender offers completed below estimated NAV (~$34/share)
- Offers oversubscribed each time (2023: 575K tendered for 200K offer; 2025: 669K tendered for 330K offer)
- Insider ownership percentage increases with each tender (insiders not tendering)
- Pricing disciplined: offers at or below market, well below NAV

**Assessment:** Capital allocation A+. Buybacks at 50-55 cents on the dollar of NAV. Insiders accumulating without buying in open market (would be redundant given tender strategy).

### D. Dividends: None

No dividend currently paid. This is appropriate given:
- Concentrated ownership (dividends would go 90% to insiders)
- Tax inefficiency vs. buybacks below NAV
- Goal is going-private, not income distribution

### E. Debt Usage: Conservative

Net cash position: ~$25M or ~$2.65/share. Minimal debt ($14K per one source). Balance sheet is clean.

## 6. Forward-Looking Capital Allocation Risk

### Most Likely Path (Base Case)

Insiders currently own ~88-92%. California short-form merger threshold is 90%. At current pace:
- 2026 tender offer likely at ~$20-22
- 90% threshold achieved 2026
- Short-form merger at NAV (~$34/share) possible by late 2026 or 2027

### Primary Risks

1. **Timing Risk:** Paddon is 70+. No clear succession. If he becomes incapacitated before going-private, minority shareholders face uncertainty.

2. **Take-Private Price Risk:** Will Paddon offer NAV or a discount? His track record suggests fair dealing, but no legal obligation exists to offer full NAV in short-form merger (must offer "fair value" which could be negotiated).

3. **Liquidity Trap:** With ~90% insider ownership, float is ~$20-25M. Difficult to build meaningful position; difficult to exit if thesis breaks.

4. **Equity Portfolio Risk:** NAV is sensitive to large-cap equity prices. Correction in MSFT/AAPL/NVDA reduces takeout price.

## The One Thing That Determines Value Creation

**The dominant factor is whether Patrick Paddon executes a going-private transaction at or near NAV (~$34/share).**

Everything else is noise. The leasing business is in runoff. The investment portfolio is generic large-caps. The 13 employees are maintenance. The only question that matters: when Paddon reaches 90%, will he pay NAV?

His track record (tender offers at escalating prices, open market purchases, no self-dealing) suggests he will. But this is a bet on character, not contractual protection.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**Non-Consensus Insight:** Consensus views CFNB as an illiquid OTC oddity. What consensus misses is that the tender offer sequence is a deliberate march toward a short-form merger. Each oversubscribed tender increases insider ownership. At 90%, Paddon can take the company private without a shareholder vote.

The market prices CFNB at ~$27.50 vs. estimated NAV of ~$34. This 20% discount reflects liquidity concerns and OTC status. It should reflect the probability-weighted outcome of a take-private at NAV within 12-24 months.

**This makes the analysis more favorable:** The path to value realization is observable and in progress, not speculative.
