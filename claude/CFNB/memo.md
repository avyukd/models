## THE BOTTOM LINE

This is a going-private arbitrage that looks attractive on the surface (30% discount to liquid NAV) but fails on the one dimension that matters: **you cannot force convergence, and the party who can is actively working against you.**

The Paddon family owns ~90% and is systematically squeezing out minorities via below-NAV tender offers ($16-18.50 vs. ~$39-40 NAV). They are approaching the 90% threshold for a short-form merger in California, which requires no shareholder vote. At that point, minorities are at the mercy of a "fair value" determination in which Paddon's interests are directly adverse.

The expected value math is unfavorable:
- **Bull case (NAV takeout at $39-40):** 45% upside
- **Bear case (below-NAV squeeze at $20-25):** 10-25% downside
- **Probability weighting:** Paddon has every incentive to minimize takeout price

This is not a situation where minorities have leverage, catalysts, or legal protection beyond appraisal rights (expensive, uncertain, illiquid). The "edge" here requires betting that a 90% owner with complete control will voluntarily pay fair value when he has no legal obligation to do so.

**Pass.**

## POSITIONING & PORTFOLIO LOGIC

### Why NOT This Position

1. **No Edge:** We are betting on Paddon's generosity, not on information or catalyst we control

2. **Adverse Selection:** Every tender offer is oversubscribed. Smart money is tendering. We would be among the last minorities standing.

3. **Illiquidity:** Cannot size meaningfully, cannot exit if wrong

4. **Opportunity Cost:** Capital tied up waiting for event we cannot influence

5. **Asymmetric Downside:** Bull case is +45% (NAV); realistic bear case is +0-15% (below-NAV squeeze). Not attractive risk/reward given lack of control.

### What Would Move This Out of "Too Hard"

1. **Credible regulatory pressure:** SEC enforcement action or formal investigation of 1940 Act compliance
2. **Activist involvement:** Someone with capital and legal resources to force action (unlikely given float size)
3. **Paddon signaling fair value intent:** Public statement, board resolution, or tender at 80%+ of NAV
4. **Succession clarity:** Named successor with stated intent to liquidate or distribute

None of these are present today.

## WHAT CONSENSUS MAY BE MISSING (Honestly, Not Much)

**The bull case** (from capital allocation analyst): The tender offer sequence is a deliberate march to short-form merger. Each oversubscribed tender increases insider ownership. The discount should reflect probability-weighted takeout at NAV.

**The bear case** (from financial analyst): Management is systematically extracting value from tendering minorities. The 30% discount prices minority powerlessness correctly. There is no reason to believe takeout will be at NAV.

**Our view:** The bear case is more honest. Paddon has every incentive to minimize takeout price. His actions (below-NAV tenders, increasing control) are consistent with squeeze-out at minimum viable price, not fair value distribution.

The one potential miss: Regulatory catalyst under 1940 Act could force NAV-level buyout. This is low probability but would be highly positive. We have no visibility into SEC intentions.

## FINAL DECISION

**Neutral (Too Hard)**

This is a situation where the math looks attractive but the structure is unfavorable. A 30% discount to liquid NAV with a going-private catalyst sounds like a great setup. But minorities have no leverage, no forcing mechanism, and a controlling shareholder with demonstrated willingness to transact at half of NAV.

We would be betting that:
1. Paddon offers fair value when he has no obligation to do so
2. OR: SEC forces action (low probability, unpredictable timing)
3. OR: Paddon dies/becomes incapacitated and heirs liquidate (morbid and uncertain)

None of these represent an analytical edge. This is a lottery ticket, not an investment.

**The right trade here was buying at $16-18 before the tender offers and tendering into them.** At $27.50, you are buying what remains after the easy money has been made.

Pass.
