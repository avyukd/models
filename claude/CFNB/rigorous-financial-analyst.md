# California First Leasing Corporation (CFNB) - Financial Analysis

**Stock Price:** ~$27.50 (OTC: CFNB)
**Market Cap:** ~$244M
**Fiscal Year End:** June 30

---

## Executive Summary: What This Really Is

CFNB is NOT primarily a leasing company anymore. It has transformed into a **de facto closed-end fund** holding a liquid equity portfolio, with a small legacy lease operation. The company registered under the Investment Company Act of 1940 in February 2022.

**Key Finding:** The stock trades at ~70% of NAV (~30% discount) on highly liquid assets. Insider ownership approaches 90%, and management is actively working to reduce the public float through tender offers. This creates a peculiar situation: a family-controlled investment vehicle with a shrinking minority public float.

## Normalized Earnings Power

### The Problem With Reported Earnings

Reported EPS of ~$3.31-3.54 is **NOT repeatable normalized earnings.** It includes:
- Unrealized gains on equities (~$40M+ in FY2024)
- One-time lease transaction gains
- Market beta on ~$200M+ equity portfolio

### Reconciliation: GAAP to Economic Earnings

| Line Item | Amount | Notes |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| Reported Net Income (FY2024) | $33.6M | Includes unrealized gains |
| Less: Unrealized equity gains | ~$(30M+) | Mark-to-market, non-cash |
| Plus: Realized gains/dividends | ~$3-5M | Estimated cash income |
| Operating Lease Income | ~$5M | Actual business operations |
| Operating Expenses | ~$(3M) | G&A overhead |
| **Economic Operating Earnings** | ~$5-7M | Pre-tax estimate |
| After-tax (~25%) | ~$4-5M | |
| **Normalized EPS** | ~$0.45-0.55 | On ~9M shares |

**Implication:** At $27.50/share, the stock trades at ~50-60x normalized operating earnings. The "cheap" 8x reported P/E is an illusion created by unrealized equity gains.

## Credit Quality & Residual Value Risk

### Lease Portfolio Assessment

The lease portfolio is **small and declining** (~$20M, or 8-9% of assets):

- Target sectors: Universities, healthcare, K-12, government
- Credit profile: Primarily investment-grade or government/non-profit obligors
- Credit loss provision: $80K (FY2024), $19K (FY2025) - negligible
- Historical charge-offs: Not material to financial results

**Residual Value Risk:** Minimal. The company retains ~90% of leases, but:
1. Portfolio is in runoff mode
2. Size is immaterial to total assets
3. New bookings ($13.6M in FY2023) are small relative to equity portfolio

**Bottom Line:** Credit and residual value risks are NOT the key drivers for this stock. The equity portfolio dominates.

## Capital Allocation Assessment

### Recent Actions

1. **Tender Offers:** June 2025 - purchased 330,000 shares at $18.50/share (~$6.1M). Offer was 2x oversubscribed (669K shares tendered).

2. **Share Count Reduction:** Shares outstanding declined ~4.5% YoY to ~9.3M shares.

3. **Dividend:** Historically ~2% yield, now suspended (0% payout FY2025).

### What This Signals

Management is systematically reducing the public float through below-NAV tender offers. This benefits insiders (~90% ownership) at the expense of minority shareholders who tender.

**Tender Offer Math:**
- Tender price: $18.50
- Estimated NAV: ~$39-40
- Discount captured by remaining shareholders: ~50%

This is value transfer from tendering minorities to insiders. Classic closed-end fund discount arbitrage, except management is on the receiving end.

## Key Financial Drivers

### The 1-3 Metrics That Matter

1. **Equity Portfolio Returns:** This is a levered bet on the equity market. A 20% equity decline could wipe out multiple years of "earnings."

2. **NAV Discount/Premium:** The spread between stock price and NAV is the key valuation metric, not P/E.

3. **Insider Actions (Tender Offers, Buybacks):** Management's capital allocation telegraphs their view of intrinsic value.

### Why Other Metrics Don't Matter

- **Lease income:** <10% of value
- **Operating margins:** Overwhelmed by portfolio mark-to-market
- **Revenue growth:** Meaningless when 80%+ is unrealized gains

## What Consensus May Be Missing

### Non-Consensus Insight: Potential Forced NAV Realization

Per Seeking Alpha analysis: If CFNB's attempt to reduce regulatory compliance burdens by squeezing out small shareholders fails, they could face **forced buyout at NAV** under 1940 Act provisions.

**Potential Outcome:** $5-6/share uplift (~20-25% upside) if forced to close the NAV discount.

**Probability:** Uncertain - depends on regulatory interpretation and management's strategy success.

### What Bulls Miss

The ~8x P/E is fake. Normalized operating earnings are ~$0.50-1.00/share, implying 27-55x true P/E. You're buying equity market beta at a premium wrapped in a leasing company shell.

### What Bears Miss

The NAV discount is real (~30%), and assets are highly liquid. In a liquidation scenario, shareholders would receive ~$39-40/share. The question is whether management ever allows that to happen.

## Conclusion

CFNB is a family-controlled closed-end fund masquerading as a leasing company. The ~$3.31 TTM EPS is almost entirely equity market beta from unrealized portfolio gains.

**Financial Assessment:** Solid but fully priced on NAV-discount basis. Weak on normalized operating earnings basis.

**Key Risk:** Minority shareholder interests are subordinate to 90% insider control. Management is actively working to reduce external oversight and squeeze out public shareholders.

**Investment Implication:** This is NOT a cheap stock at 8x "earnings." It's a ~30% NAV discount on a liquid portfolio with governance risk and potential regulatory catalyst. The variant bet is on whether the NAV discount closes (voluntarily or by force) or management successfully converts this into a private family office.
