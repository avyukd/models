# Equipment Leasing Industry: Structure & Cycle Analysis

## Lead Finding: Late-Cycle With Margin Compression Accelerating

The equipment leasing industry is in late-cycle territory with clear warning signs: ROA collapsed from 1.7% to 1.1% in 2024, ROE dropped from 11.1% to 7.9%, and interest expense rose 33% while bad debt provisions jumped nearly 50%. New business volume grew just 3.1%—barely matching inflation. Banks are pulling back, creating temporary market share opportunities for independents, but this is a cyclical handoff, not a structural tailwind.

The consensus view that "equipment demand remains resilient" misses the deteriorating unit economics. Volume growth without margin growth is value destruction.

---

## Industry Structure

### Market Size & Composition
- $1.3 trillion U.S. equipment finance market (ELFA data)
- 82% of equipment acquisitions use some form of financing
- Approximately 57% of E&S investment is financed

### Competitive Structure: Consolidating (Banks Dominant)
Market share by organization type (2024):
- **Banks:** >50% market share, 1.3% NBV decline
- **Captives (OEM finance arms):** Growing, 5.9% NBV increase
- **Independents:** Small base, 17.7% NBV increase

Banks dominate with low funding costs but are pulling back from equipment finance exposure. Captives leverage point-of-sale advantages. Independents are gaining share by default—banks retreating creates opportunity, but independents face structural funding disadvantages.

### Segment Economics
| Segment | Ticket Size | Characteristics |
|---------|------------|-----------------|
| Small Ticket | <$250K | App-only underwriting, higher yields, higher losses |
| Middle Market | $250K-$5M | Bank-dominated, spread compression acute |
| Large Ticket | >$5M | Relationship-driven, competitive |

**Municipal/Government/Education (EGNP):** Tax-exempt lease purchase (TELP) represents >$10 billion annually. Near-zero default rates (<0.5% non-appropriation). Lower yields but superior credit quality. Counter-cyclical demand—government spending often increases during recessions.

## What Drives Industry Profitability

1. **Net Interest Spread:** Yield on leases minus cost of funds. Pre-tax yields ~7.4%, cost of funds ~4.8%, spread ~2.6% (2024). Spread compression is the dominant profitability driver.

2. **Credit Losses:** Charge-offs at 60 bps in 2024—highest since 2018. Delinquencies stable at ~2.0% but rising at banks.

3. **Operating Efficiency:** Employment down 2.5% in 2024. Firms managing costs as revenue/margin pressure intensifies.

4. **Volume Growth:** Only matters if margins hold. Volume +3.1% with margins compressing = declining profitability.

### Mid-Cycle Industry Economics (Reference Point)
Based on historical data:
- **ROA:** 1.4-1.7% (currently 1.1%—below mid-cycle)
- **ROE:** 10-12% (currently 7.9%—below mid-cycle)
- **Spreads:** 2.3-2.6% (currently ~2.6%—at mid-cycle)
- **Charge-offs:** 30-50 bps (currently 60 bps—above mid-cycle)
- **Delinquencies:** 1.8-2.2% (currently 2.0%—at mid-cycle)

## Cyclicality Assessment: HIGH

### Capex Correlation
Equipment leasing demand is a direct function of business capital expenditure. Historical pattern:
- E&S investment typically declines 3-5x more than GDP during recessions
- 2020: New business volume fell 7%—first decline in a decade
- 2021: Rebounded 7.4% as capex recovered

### Cycle Amplifiers
1. Credit availability tightens during downturns (double hit)
2. Equipment residual values decline, increasing loss severity
3. Delinquencies spike with lag to economic weakness
4. Funding costs can spike while yields remain fixed on existing portfolio

### Historical Cycle Evidence
- **2008-2009:** Industry suffered material losses; independents lost significant market share to banks
- **2020:** Sharp but brief downturn; government stimulus cushioned impact
- **Post-2021:** Strong rebound, now normalizing

## Current Cycle Position: LATE CYCLE

### Evidence of Late-Cycle Dynamics

**Deteriorating Profitability:**
- ROA: 1.7% (2023) → 1.1% (2024)
- ROE: 11.1% (2023) → 7.9% (2024)
- Interest expense +33% YoY
- Bad debt provisions +50% YoY
- Pre-tax income -14.5% YoY

**Credit Quality Weakening:**
- Charge-offs at 60 bps—highest since 2018
- Delinquency rate stable but masking bank-level volatility

**Volume Growing But Slowing:**
- 2022: +6.3%
- 2023: +1.1%
- 2024: +3.1%
- Growth barely keeps pace with inflation

**Employment Contracting:**
- Headcount -2.5% in 2024
- Banks -3.7%, Captives -4.7%
- Only independents hiring (+4.3%)

### Confidence Level: MODERATE-HIGH
The profitability deterioration is unambiguous. The uncertainty is timing—how long before credit losses normalize and rates stabilize? Could be 12-24 months or longer depending on Fed trajectory.

---

## Interest Rate Sensitivity: HIGH

### Direct Impacts
1. **Cost of Funds:** Rising rates directly increase funding costs; not all can be passed through to customers
2. **Spread Compression:** Fixed-rate legacy portfolios earn less relative to current funding costs
3. **Demand Destruction:** Higher rates reduce equipment investment (capex sensitive)
4. **Credit Quality:** Higher rates stress borrowers, increasing defaults

### Recent Rate Environment
- Fed hiked aggressively 2022-2023
- Rates remained elevated through 2024
- Industry absorbed 33% increase in interest expense in 2024
- Spreads relatively flat at 2.59%—margin pressure absorbed elsewhere

### Duration Mismatch Risk
Equipment leases typically 3-7 year terms. Funding can be shorter duration. Rising rate environments create negative carry on existing portfolios.

## Competitive Dynamics

### Banks: Structural Advantages, Cyclical Pullback
- Lowest funding costs (deposit funding)
- Regulatory capital constraints limiting appetite
- Currently retreating from equipment finance
- Will return when cycle improves

### Captives (OEM Finance Arms): Growing Influence
- Point-of-sale advantage (embedded financing)
- Product knowledge enables better residual value forecasting
- Use financing as strategic tool for equipment sales
- Willing to subsidize rates to move equipment

### Independents: Opportunistic Growth, Structural Disadvantage
- Higher funding costs than banks
- Flexible, can specialize in niches banks won't serve
- Gaining share as banks retreat
- Vulnerable when banks return aggressively

### Implication
Bank pullback creates temporary opportunity for independents and captives. This is NOT a permanent structural shift—banks will return when cycle turns. Independents celebrating market share gains are likely harvesting temporary cyclical dynamics.

## Municipal/Healthcare/Education Segment

### Characteristics
- **Tax-Exempt Status:** Municipal leases offer tax-exempt interest, lowering borrower costs
- **Superior Credit:** <0.5% non-appropriation rate historically
- **Counter-Cyclical:** Government/education spending often increases during recessions
- **Lower Yields:** Trade-off for credit quality

### Addressable Market
- 90,000+ government entities in U.S.
- >$10 billion annually in tax-exempt lease purchase (TELP)
- Equipment: vehicles, technology, medical equipment, infrastructure

### Competitive Position
Specialized knowledge required (non-appropriation clauses, budget cycles, procurement processes). Relationship-driven. Less price-competitive than commercial market.

## The One Industry Factor That Matters

**Interest Rate Trajectory**

Everything else is noise. The equipment leasing industry's profitability in the next 2-3 years will be determined almost entirely by:
1. Where Fed funds rate settles
2. Yield curve shape
3. How quickly legacy portfolios roll off

If rates decline materially, spreads widen on new originations while funding costs drop—profitability rebounds. If rates stay elevated, continued margin pressure and credit deterioration.

**Monitoring:** Fed funds rate, 2-10 spread, SOFR swap rates, industry cost of funds data (ELFA MLFI)

---

## What Consensus May Be Missing

### Consensus View
"Equipment demand is resilient; industry weathering rate environment well; independents gaining structural market share."

### Non-Consensus View (Bearish Industry Economics)
**The bank pullback is cyclical, not structural.** Independents are gaining share because banks are retreating—not because independents have better economics. When the cycle turns and rates normalize, banks will return aggressively with their funding cost advantage. Independents celebrating share gains today may find themselves squeezed on both volume AND margins in 2-3 years.

**ROA collapse is worse than it looks.** The drop from 1.7% to 1.1% ROA in one year suggests industry economics are fragile. This isn't "modest normalization"—it's a 35% decline in asset-level returns. Another bad credit year or sustained rate pressure could push ROA below 1%.

**The 2020 cycle didn't test the industry.** Government stimulus masked what should have been a severe credit cycle. The industry hasn't faced a true stress test since 2008-2009. Investors may be underestimating cycle risk.

### CFNB Context (California First Leasing)
CFNB operates in municipal/government, education (K-12 and higher ed), and healthcare—segments with superior credit quality and counter-cyclical characteristics. This positioning partially insulates CFNB from the worst of commercial equipment leasing cyclicality. However:
- Tax-exempt yields are lower, limiting upside
- Still exposed to interest rate sensitivity on funding
- Niche focus reduces competitive pressure but also limits growth
- Registered as investment company (1940 Act) due to equity securities as % of assets—adds regulatory complexity

The EGNP focus is a defensive positioning in a late-cycle environment, but does not eliminate industry-level risks.
