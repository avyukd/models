# Namib Minerals (NAMM): Management Quality and Capital Allocation Analysis

## Executive Summary

Namib Minerals presents a textbook case of SPAC structural misalignment compounded by extreme capital needs in a maximum-risk jurisdiction. The company went public via SPAC (Hennessy Capital VI) in June 2025, is incorporated in the Cayman Islands, operates gold mines in Zimbabwe, and requires $300-400M in external capital for expansion. The claim of pursuing "non-dilutive funding" is contradicted by the reality of an already-dilutive SPAC structure with 18.6M warrants outstanding, up to 30M earnout shares, and a massive equity incentive plan reserve representing 100%+ of the current float. Management's 74% ownership (via Greenstone shareholders) appears aligned, but the SPAC sponsor's track record is mixed, and the capital requirements dwarf the company's current market capitalization.

## Incentive Structure & Alignment

### SPAC Sponsor Economics - The Hidden Misalignment

The sponsor, Hennessy Capital Partners VI LLC, acquired 11.5M founder shares for $25,000 - a cost basis of $0.002 per share versus the $10 SPAC IPO price. This creates a structural incentive problem:

**What this compensation structure encourages:**
1. Closing ANY deal, regardless of quality - the sponsor profits at virtually any price above zero
2. No downside risk for the sponsor while public shareholders bear all price risk
3. Time pressure to close before SPAC deadline rather than finding optimal deals

**Who benefits if the stock underperforms:**
- The sponsor already captured massive value at the $25,000 entry point
- SPAC directors received founder shares "prior to the initial public offering"
- Management (Greenstone shareholders) received shares at their negotiated basis, not the SPAC price

### Management Compensation - Limited Transparency

Specific salary, bonus, and equity compensation details for CEO Ibrahima Tall and other executives are not readily available from public sources. This opacity is concerning for a recently-public company.

What is disclosed:
- **CEO ownership**: 3.4% (~$7.6M at ~$4/share; ~$50M at peak $55)
- **Insider holdings total**: ~$101M worth of shares (per Simply Wall St, though timing unclear)
- **Equity incentive plan**: Company has 53.9M shares reserved for the equity incentive plan, warrants, and earnout shares

### Alignment Assessment

**Positive signals:**
- Greenstone shareholders (including management) retained 74% ownership - significant stake
- CEO has 24 years of operating experience in African gold mining
- Management team has operated assets for 19+ years (CFO tenure)
- CEO has M&A training (Columbia Business School certificate)

**Negative signals:**
- SPAC sponsor incentives are fundamentally misaligned with public shareholders
- Cayman Islands domicile limits shareholder legal protections
- Compensation disclosure is limited
- Board chair and several executives only joined at SPAC closing (June 2025) - limited track record together

## Stock-Based Compensation Discipline

### The Dilution Math - This Is Where The Problem Is

**Current share count (per 20-F, June 2025):**
- Ordinary shares outstanding: 53.7M

**Reserved for future issuance:**
- Warrants outstanding: 18.6M (exercisable at $11.50)
- Earnout shares: Up to 30M (tied to operational milestones)
- Equity incentive plan: Remainder of 53.9M reserved shares

**Total potential dilution:**
- Current: 53.7M shares
- Fully diluted potential: 53.7M + 18.6M + 30M + equity plan = **potentially 130M+ shares**
- This represents **140%+ potential dilution** from current share count

### Warrant Dilution Analysis

The 18.6M warrants at $11.50 strike price are currently out-of-the-money (stock at ~$4). However:
- If stock recovers to $15+, warrants become exercisable
- Warrant exercise would bring in ~$214M cash but dilute shareholders 35%
- This creates perverse incentive: company may pursue strategies that boost stock price above $11.50 to enable warrant exercise and cash raise, regardless of intrinsic value

### Earnout Share Triggers

The 30M earnout shares are tied to "operational milestones" - specific triggers not fully detailed in search results. This represents:
- 56% additional dilution from current share count
- Management/Greenstone shareholders are the primary earnout beneficiaries
- Incentivizes hitting milestones regardless of capital efficiency

### "Non-Dilutive Funding" Claim vs. Reality

Management claims to be "pursuing a balanced funding mix including structured solutions that minimize dilution and prioritize project debt."

**Why this claim is suspect:**
1. $300-400M funding need vs. ~$100M market cap - cannot be achieved without massive dilution or debt
2. Zimbabwe country risk demands punitive lending terms (per industry analysis)
3. Project debt for a single-asset gold producer in Zimbabwe is not "non-dilutive" - it's secured against the only asset
4. Gold streaming/royalty deals (common "non-dilutive" structures) permanently give away future production upside
5. Current ratio of 0.14 indicates short-term obligations already exceed liquid assets

**What "non-dilutive" likely means in practice:**
- Streaming agreements that mortgage future gold production
- Secured debt with physical gold collateral (standard for Zimbabwe)
- Convertible debt that becomes equity in distress
- Strategic partnerships that involve equity stakes

## Capital Allocation Track Record

### Historical Context - Limited Track Record as Public Company

Namib Minerals has been public for only ~7 months (June 2025 to January 2026). There is no meaningful public company capital allocation track record to evaluate. Pre-SPAC decisions were made by Greenstone as a private company.

### Capital Allocation Plans - Forward Looking

**The $300-400M Expansion Plan:**

| Allocation | Amount | Purpose |
|------------|--------|---------|
| How Mine expansion | Minority share | 40,500 to 55,000 tpm by H2 2026 |
| Redwing restart | Majority share | 8-month dewatering, restart February |
| Mazowe restart | TBD | Feasibility studies underway |
| DRC exploration | TBD | 13 copper/cobalt permits |

**Red Flags in the Capital Plan:**

1. **Scale vs. capacity**: $300-400M need against ~$100M market cap and minimal cash. This will require either massive dilution or debt that subordinates equity holders.

2. **Expansion at cycle peak**: Current gold prices (~$4,500/oz per industry analysis) are at historical highs. Expansion economics modeled at peak prices may not hold at mid-cycle $1,600/oz.

3. **Multiple simultaneous projects**: How Mine expansion + Redwing restart + Mazowe restart + DRC exploration. This is empire-building behavior, not disciplined capital allocation.

4. **Feasibility studies incomplete**: "WSP engaged to deliver S-K 1300 compliant feasibility studies for Redwing and Mazowe over 12-18 months." Capital needs are "preliminary and subject to completion of feasibility studies."

5. **"Majority allocated to Redwing"**: The mothballed mine restart is consuming most of the capital. Restarting a mothballed mine in Zimbabwe with external capital at gold cycle peak is high-risk.

### The Zimbabwe Capital Trap

Per the industry analysis already completed, Zimbabwe presents:
- 30% export surrender requirement at overvalued local currency
- Electricity costs USc14.21-30/kWh
- Financiers demanding physical gold collateral and escrow accounts
- De-dollarization fears limiting long-term financing past 2030
- 80% of mining executives expect forex access to deteriorate in 2026

**The math at mid-cycle gold prices:**
- At $4,500 gold with $1,500 AISC: ~$3,000/oz margin
- At $1,600 gold with $1,300 AISC: ~$300/oz margin
- Margin compression of 90% in a downturn
- $300-400M debt cannot be serviced at mid-cycle margins

## SPAC Sponsor Track Record - Daniel Hennessy

### The Mixed Record

Daniel Hennessy has sponsored 12 SPAC transactions representing over $7B in enterprise value. However, outcomes vary dramatically:

| Deal | Company | Outcome |
|------|---------|---------|
| HCAC I (2015) | Blue Bird (BLBD) | **SUCCESS**: Stock up ~85% from SPAC price; $1.4B market cap; profitable |
| HCAC II (2017) | Daseke (DSKE) | **MIXED**: ~$200M market cap; negative recent returns |
| HCAC IV (2020) | Canoo (GOEV) | **CATASTROPHIC FAILURE**: Chapter 7 bankruptcy; stock from $22.75 to $0.007 |
| HCIC V | Plus | **FAILED**: Merger cancelled; SPAC liquidated |
| HCVI (2025) | Namib Minerals | **TBD**: Stock down 65%+ from peak |

**Pattern recognition:**
- 1 clear success (Blue Bird - industrial, US-based)
- 1 mediocre outcome (Daseke)
- 1 total loss (Canoo - EV, speculative)
- 1 failed deal (Plus)
- 1 in progress (Namib Minerals)

**What this tells us:**
The sponsor has no track record with African mining, commodity exposure, or high country-risk assets. The successful deal (Blue Bird) was a US industrial company with existing revenue and profitability. The failed deals were speculative growth stories. Namib Minerals is a speculative growth story in a high-risk jurisdiction.

### Cannot Assess - Insufficient History

As a newly public company, there is no track record of mistakes being corrected or repeated. However, the SPAC structure itself suggests the company has NOT learned from the broader market's SPAC failures:

1. **Standard 20% promote**: No reduction despite post-2021 SPAC backlash

## Forward-Looking Capital Allocation Risk

### Where Capital Is Most Likely To Be Misallocated

1. **Expansion capex at cycle peak**: The $300-400M will be deployed into projects whose economics are modeled at $4,500 gold. If gold normalizes, these projects destroy value.

2. **DRC exploration**: 13 permits in the DRC is empire-building. Diversifying into copper/cobalt with untested management in a second high-risk African jurisdiction stretches capital and focus.

3. **Multi-asset strategy before proving single asset**: How Mine is the only producing asset. Capital should be returned or used to optimize How Mine before pursuing restarts and exploration.

4. **Streaming/royalty deals**: To achieve "non-dilutive" funding, management will likely sell future production at discounted prices, permanently transferring value from shareholders to financiers.

### Free Cash Flow Risk

How Mine generated $86M revenue from 36.6 koz production in 2024. At current gold prices, this is highly profitable. However:

- FCF must service existing obligations (current ratio 0.14)
- FCF will be consumed by expansion capex
- FCF at mid-cycle prices may not cover sustaining capex
- There is no scenario where FCF is returned to shareholders in the near term

**The single most important factor: Whether management can fund $300-400M expansion without catastrophic dilution or debt terms that subordinate equity holders.**

This factor dominates because:
1. The capital need ($300-400M) exceeds market cap (~$100M) by 3-4x
2. Zimbabwe country risk limits funding sources
3. Gold cycle risk means expansion economics may not hold
4. SPAC structure already embeds significant dilution (warrants, earnouts)

**What would change this assessment:**
- Securing project debt at reasonable terms without streaming/royalty giveaways
- Gold prices remaining elevated through expansion completion (2026-2027)
- Successful Redwing restart proving management execution capability
- Completion of feasibility studies confirming economic reserves

## What Consensus May Be Missing

### The Non-Consensus Insight

**Consensus view**: Namib Minerals is a high-risk/high-reward gold play with significant dilution risk but potential upside from Zimbabwe gold assets at current prices.

**What consensus may be missing**: The "non-dilutive funding" narrative is mathematically impossible without permanent value transfer from shareholders.

Here's the specific insight:
- $300-400M need vs. ~$100M market cap
- Zimbabwe requires gold collateral and punitive terms
- "Non-dilutive" structures (streaming, royalty) permanently alienate future production
- Warrants (18.6M at $11.50) only become useful if stock triples - management may pursue price manipulation over value creation
- Earnout shares (30M) incentivize hitting operational milestones regardless of capital efficiency or returns

**What this means:**
- Equity holders face dilution from every direction (warrants, earnouts, equity plan, capital raises)
- The only "non-dilutive" path involves selling future gold production to streamers/royalty companies at discounted prices
- Either path results in less value accruing to current shareholders than the assets would generate under optimal capital allocation

**This makes me less trusting of management**, not because of bad intent, but because the capital structure makes value-creating capital allocation nearly impossible.
