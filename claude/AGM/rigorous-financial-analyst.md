# AGM (Federal Agricultural Mortgage Corp / Farmer Mac) - Financial Analysis

## Key Finding: Infrastructure Pivot Is the Story, But the Market May Be Right to Discount It

Farmer Mac trades at 10-11x earnings and 1.6x book value despite a 17% ROE and 14 consecutive years of dividend increases. The apparent cheapness reflects two things: (1) a structural shift toward higher-margin infrastructure lending that the market hasn't fully priced, and (2) legitimate agricultural credit concerns that could materialize in 2025-2026. The key question is whether the infrastructure transformation offsets potential farm credit deterioration.

## Spread Income & Net Interest Margin Analysis

### Net Effective Spread Decomposition

Farmer Mac's key profitability metric is net effective spread - the difference between yield on earning assets and funding costs. As a GSE, funding costs are materially lower than commercial lenders.

| Segment | Q3 2025 Net Effective Spread | Business Volume | % of Total |
|---------|------------------------------|-----------------|------------|
| Farm & Ranch | 104 bps | $18.2B | 58% |
| Power & Utilities | ~120 bps est. | $7.3B | 23% |
| Corporate AgFinance | ~100 bps est. | $2.0B | 6% |
| Renewable Energy | 175 bps | $2.3B | 7% |
| Broadband Infrastructure | 230 bps | $1.3B | 4% |
| **Consolidated** | **~120 bps** | **$31.1B** | 100% |

*Source: Q3 2025 investor presentation, press releases*

**Critical Insight**: The highest-margin segments (Renewable Energy at 175 bps, Broadband at 230 bps) are growing fastest. Broadband doubled YoY to $1.3B. This mix shift is the primary driver of spread expansion from 102 bps (2022) to ~120 bps (2025).

### Spread Trend

| Year | Net Effective Spread | Commentary |
|------|---------------------|------------|
| 2022 | 1.02% (102 bps) | Pre-infrastructure pivot |
| 2023 | 1.18% (118 bps) | Infrastructure ramping |
| 2024 | 1.15% (115 bps) | Slight compression |
| 2025 YTD | ~1.17-1.20% | Record spread dollars |

The 2024 compression warrants attention - despite favorable mix shift, basis spread compressed slightly. This may reflect competitive dynamics or timing. 2025 has recovered.

## Credit Quality Analysis

### Portfolio Credit Metrics

| Metric | Q3 2025 | Historical Average |
|--------|---------|-------------------|
| 90-Day Delinquency Rate | 1.35% | 1.0% (15-year avg) |
| Allowance for Losses | 0.12% of volume | 0.09% (2020) |
| Average LTV Ratio | 55% | Conservative |
| Provision for Credit Losses (Q3 2025) | $7.4M | Idiosyncratic issues |

*Source: Q3 2025 earnings call, press releases*

**Assessment**: Delinquencies are above the 15-year average but management characterizes this as "normalization, not deterioration." The $7.4M Q3 provision related to: (1) three agricultural storage/processing borrowers, (2) California groundwater regulation affecting permanent plantings, (3) one solar project with equipment failures.

**Red Flag Consideration**: Delinquencies trending up while farm income is under pressure. Farm bankruptcies on pace to exceed 2024. However, 55% LTV provides substantial collateral cushion - Farmer Mac has historically experienced minimal actual losses even when delinquencies spike.

### Agricultural Sector Stress Indicators

- Farm debt at record $386B in 2025
- Farm bankruptcies: 181 in H1 2025, on pace to exceed 2024's 216
- 70% of ag lenders "very worried" about grain profitability (up from 15% two years ago)
- Net farm income forecast to increase 41% in 2025 due to $40B government payments (quadrupling YoY)

**Judgment**: The agricultural sector is stressed, but government support is providing a backstop. If government payments normalize and commodity prices remain depressed (tariff impacts on soybean exports to China), credit losses could materially exceed provisioning. However, Farmer Mac's infrastructure diversification (now 40%+ of portfolio) provides meaningful offset.

## Key Financial Drivers

### The 1-3 Metrics That Actually Matter

**1. Net Effective Spread (THE key driver)**
- Every 10 bps of spread on $31B portfolio = ~$31M pretax = ~$24M after-tax = ~$2.20 EPS
- Spread has expanded from 102 bps (2022) to 120 bps (2025) primarily via infrastructure mix shift
- Upside: Higher-margin infrastructure segments grow from 40% to 50%+ of portfolio
- Downside: Competitive pressure, rate environment, or Farm & Ranch spread compression

**2. Credit Losses / Provision Expense**
- Currently running $20-30M annually in provisions
- Agricultural stress could push this to $50-75M in a severe scenario
- Mitigated by 55% LTV and infrastructure diversification
- Every $25M of incremental provision = ~$2.30 EPS hit

**3. Business Volume Growth**
- Growing at ~8-10% annually
- Infrastructure segments growing 15-25%
- More volume at stable spreads = more spread dollars
- At 120 bps spread, every $1B of new volume = ~$12M pretax = ~$0.90 EPS

## What Consensus May Be Missing

### Non-Consensus Insight: The Infrastructure Transformation Is Real and Underappreciated

**The Bull Case the Market Underweights:**

Farmer Mac has quietly transformed from a pure agricultural lender (2019: 100% ag exposure) to a diversified rural infrastructure finance platform (2025: 40% non-ag). The key insight:

1. **Broadband at 230 bps spread vs Farm & Ranch at 104 bps** - Infrastructure segments generate 2x the spread per dollar of assets
2. **Government tailwinds** - Rural broadband subsidies (BEAD program), renewable energy tax credits, utility infrastructure spending create secular demand
3. **Competitive moats** - GSE funding advantage is unmatched; commercial lenders cannot compete on spread

**The math**: If infrastructure grows from 40% to 50% of portfolio over 3 years while legacy ag stays flat, consolidated spread could expand another 10-15 bps purely from mix - worth $2-3/share in EPS.

**What skeptics say**: "It's still an ag lender with tariff/commodity risk." The market appears anchored on the legacy ag business while underweighting the infrastructure optionality.

### Non-Consensus Concern: Government Payment Dependency

The bullish farm income outlook ($180B net farm income in 2025) is entirely driven by government payments quadrupling to $40B. This is not organic profitability improvement. If political winds shift and ag subsidies decline while commodity prices remain depressed (China trade tensions), farm credit quality could deteriorate faster than the infrastructure pivot can offset.

---

## Conclusion

Farmer Mac trades at a modest valuation (10-11x core earnings, 1.6x book) for a business generating 17% ROE with clean financials, minimal SBC dilution, and a structural transformation story. The infrastructure pivot is real and financially impactful, but agricultural credit stress is also real and mounting.

**Investment thesis depends on**:
- Belief that infrastructure tailwinds outpace ag headwinds (bull)
- Concern that ag credit losses could spike if government support wanes (bear)

The financials are solid. The transformation is genuine. Whether it's enough to offset sector-wide agricultural stress is the key judgment call.
