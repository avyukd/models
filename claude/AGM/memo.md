## Key Drivers (The 1-3 Things That Matter)

1. **Infrastructure Mix Shift** - The composition of the portfolio is moving from 100bp spread ag loans toward 175-230bp infrastructure assets. This is the earnings growth engine. Every 10 bps of consolidated spread improvement on a $31B book equals ~$2.20 in EPS.

2. **Agricultural Credit Quality** - Delinquencies at 1.35% are elevated but not systemic. The 55% average LTV provides substantial collateral cushion. If farmland values hold (they are, +4.3% in 2025), credit losses stay manageable.

3. **GSE Funding Moat** - The 50-100bp funding advantage is permanent as long as the charter exists. This is not a competitive moat that can erode - it requires an act of Congress to eliminate.

## The Setup

Farmer Mac is a government-sponsored enterprise providing secondary market liquidity for agricultural and rural infrastructure loans. The GSE charter (1988) grants a permanent funding cost advantage that competitors cannot replicate.

The business has quietly transformed over 5 years:
- 2019: 100% agricultural exposure
- 2025: 60% agricultural, 40% infrastructure (renewable energy, broadband, utilities)

The infrastructure segments are not just diversification - they generate materially higher spreads (175-230bp vs 104bp) and have secular tailwinds (IRA subsidies, rural broadband expansion, data center buildout). This mix shift is the primary driver of spread expansion from 102bp (2022) to ~120bp (2025).

Agricultural credit concerns dominate the narrative. But the "stress" is normalization from pandemic peaks, not a downturn:
- Net farm income at $180B (above 20-year real average)
- Debt-to-asset at 13.4% (equals 20-year average)
- Farmland values rising 4.3% (collateral is strengthening)

The 1980s farm crisis required rates >15%, farmland collapse, and overleveraged operators. None of these conditions exist.

## Risks & Kill Switches

**Primary Risk: GSE Charter Modification**
- Probability: Very low
- Impact: Existential - would eliminate funding advantage
- Mitigant: Agricultural policy has bipartisan support; Farmer Mac avoided conservatorship; no political momentum for reform
- Kill Switch: Any serious Congressional discussion of GSE reform

**Secondary Risk: Agricultural Credit Deterioration**
- Probability: Moderate
- Impact: EPS compression of $2-4 in severe scenario
- Mitigant: 55% LTV, farmland values holding, government payment backstop
- Kill Switch: Farmland values declining >5% for 2+ consecutive years; delinquencies exceeding 3%

**Tertiary Risk: CEO Transition**
- Probability: Low-moderate
- Impact: Behavioral shift toward growth over capital efficiency
- Mitigant: Historical discipline (1.8% dilution over 5 years), board dividend priority
- Watch: Infrastructure segment returns vs capital consumption in first year under new CEO

**Quaternary Risk: Infrastructure Execution**
- Probability: Low
- Impact: Growth story weakens if expansion stalls
- Mitigant: Secular tailwinds (IRA, rural broadband programs), doubling annual volume

## Bottom Line

AGM is a modestly cheap ($10-11x earnings) franchise-quality business (17% ROE, insurmountable moat) undergoing a transformation that the market has not priced. The infrastructure pivot is real, financially impactful, and secular. Agricultural credit concerns are legitimate but manageable given collateral strength and government backstop.

The variant view: this is not an ag lender entering a stressed cycle - it is a diversified rural infrastructure finance platform trading at ag lender multiples. The infrastructure transformation is worth 10-15bps of additional spread over 3 years ($2-3/share EPS), which alone justifies the current valuation even if ag credit merely normalizes rather than improving.

Medium conviction reflects: (1) CEO transition creates behavioral uncertainty, (2) agricultural tail risk is non-zero, (3) levered financials require respect. But the setup offers attractive asymmetry - downside protected by GSE charter and dividend yield, upside from infrastructure re-rating.

**Buy at current levels. Size at 1-2%. Review at CEO transition.**
