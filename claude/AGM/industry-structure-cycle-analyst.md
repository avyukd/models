# Agricultural Credit Industry Analysis

## The One Industry Factor That Matters

**Farmland values are the collateral base for agricultural credit. Everything else is noise.**

Farm sector debt-to-asset ratios remain at 13.4%—the same level as the 20-year average—because farmland values have risen in lockstep with debt. This is fundamentally different from the 1980s crisis when farmland values collapsed 60% while debt remained elevated, pushing debt-to-assets to 22%.

The critical question is not "will farm income recover?" but "what would cause farmland values to decline materially?" Three potential catalysts:
1. Sustained interest rates above 5% making farmland yields uncompetitive with treasuries
2. A structural break in commodity prices (not cyclical weakness, but permanent demand destruction)
3. Forced selling from distressed operators overwhelming thin bid-side liquidity

None of these are imminent. Farmland values rose 4.3% in 2025 despite farm income pressure—demand from investors, estate buyers, and neighbor consolidation absorbs supply.

**Monitoring threshold:** If farmland values decline >5% nationally for two consecutive years, industry credit metrics would deteriorate rapidly. This has not happened since the 1980s.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**Consensus view:** Agricultural credit is entering a stressed period as farm incomes decline from 2022 peaks.

**What's actually happening:** This is a normalization from unsustainable highs, not a cycle downturn.

Key facts:
- Net farm income of $180B in 2025 is above the 20-year average in real terms
- Debt-to-asset ratio of 13.4% is identical to 2005-2024 average
- Delinquencies at 1.45% are elevated from pandemic lows (0.6%) but well below historical averages (~3-4% mid-cycle)
- Farmland values continue rising despite income normalization

**The variant insight:** The agricultural credit industry is mid-cycle, not early-cycle stress. Consensus is anchored to 2021-2022 peak conditions and interpreting normalization as deterioration.

Bullish implication: Secondary market participants like Farmer Mac face benign credit conditions with rising demand for liquidity as primary lenders manage balance sheet risk. The "credit stress" narrative creates attractive entry points.

Bearish caveat: If commodity prices remain at/below breakeven (corn at $4.00-4.20, soybeans at $10.00) for 3+ years, stressed operators will exit. But this takes time to flow through—farmland values are sticky on the downside due to estate/investor demand.

## Current Cycle Position: Mid-Cycle (High Confidence)

### Evidence for Mid-Cycle:
| Metric | Current | 1980s Crisis | Mid-Cycle Range |
|--------|---------|--------------|-----------------|
| Debt-to-Asset | 13.4% | 22.2% | 12-15% |
| Delinquency Rate | 1.45% | 7-12% | 1.5-3% |
| Farmland Value YoY | +4.3% | -15% annual | +2-5% |
| Net Farm Income | $180B | Negative real | $150-200B |

### Why This Is Not Early-Cycle Stress:
- Farm sector equity at $3.83 trillion—record high
- Interest expense at $29.5B is elevated but serviceable at current income levels
- Chapter 12 bankruptcies at 259 (Q1 2025)—elevated from pandemic lows but below 2019 levels
- Lenders report manageable credit quality; major/severe repayment problems at 2.9%—elevated from 2.2% but historically benign

### Key Uncertainty:
Commodity prices at/below production cost for row crops. If sustained 3+ years, cycle position shifts to late-cycle stress. But 2025 farm income forecast includes $40B government payments (up from $10B in 2024)—policy is the shock absorber.

## Industry Structure Assessment

### Structural Trajectory: Mature (Slight Growth)
- Total farm debt growing 5% annually ($592B in 2025)
- Real growth of 2-3% after inflation
- Farmland values appreciating 2-4% real—creates organic collateral growth
- Rural infrastructure (energy, broadband) provides new demand vectors

### Competitive Structure: Oligopolistic
| Lender Type | Market Share | Trend |
|-------------|--------------|-------|
| Farm Credit System | 46% | Gaining |
| Commercial Banks | 35% | Stable |
| USDA Direct/Guaranteed | 12% | Stable |
| Insurance/Life Cos | 7% | Stable |

Farm Credit System's cooperative structure and patronage refunds (effectively 100-125 bps rate reduction) create structural advantage vs. banks. Farmer Mac serves secondary market—not competing for origination.

### Farmer Mac's Niche:
- Secondary market for ag mortgages and USDA-guaranteed loans
- GSE status provides 50-100 bps funding advantage
- Primary customers: smaller banks lacking securitization capability
- Diversification into renewable energy (doubled to $2.3B) and broadband ($1.3B) at higher spreads (175-230 bps vs. 104 bps for farm loans)

## Mid-Cycle Definition for Agricultural Credit

**"Normal" conditions:**
- Net farm income: $150-180B (real, adjusted for current production scale)
- Debt-to-asset ratio: 12-14%
- Farmland value growth: 2-4% nominal
- Delinquency rates: 1.5-2.5%
- Loan demand growth: 3-5% annually
- Interest coverage: 2-3x at sector level

**We are currently in the upper half of mid-cycle:** Income at $180B, delinquencies at 1.45%, farmland values +4.3%. Conditions are good but normalizing.

## Historical Credit Cycle Reference

Agricultural credit cycles are long—average 14.5 years upturn, 7 years downturn per University of Illinois research.

| Period | Phase | Duration | Trigger |
|--------|-------|----------|---------|
| 1973-1982 | Boom | 10 yrs | Commodity supercycle, export demand |
| 1983-1991 | Bust | 9 yrs | Rate shock, farmland collapse |
| 1992-2013 | Recovery/Boom | 22 yrs | Land appreciation, ethanol mandate |
| 2014-2020 | Stagnation | 7 yrs | Commodity price normalization |
| 2021-2022 | Peak | 2 yrs | Pandemic supply shock, Ukraine |
| 2023-Present | Normalization | Ongoing | Supply recovery, demand normalization |

**Key observation:** Current "normalization" is not a downturn—income remains above long-run average. The 1980s crisis required rates >15% AND farmland collapse AND overleveraged operators. None of these conditions exist.

## Industry Risk Assessment

### Primary Risk: Interest Rates Sustained Above 5%
- Farm real estate loans at 6-7% rates compress operating margins
- Farmland capitalization rates already near historic lows (~3%)
- If treasuries yield 5% sustainably, farmland faces repricing risk

### Secondary Risk: Trade Policy Shock
- China soybean demand highly policy-sensitive
- 2018-2019 tariffs required $23B in emergency payments
- USMCA/China tensions create tail risk

### Tertiary Risk: Input Cost Persistence
- Fertilizer, fuel, labor costs remain elevated
- Crop prices at/below breakeven
- Margin squeeze sustainable for 2-3 years with balance sheet strength; beyond that, exits accelerate

### Risk NOT Priced: Farmland Bubble
- Institutional investors (TIAA, pension funds) have increased ag allocations
- Estate buyers insensitive to yield
- Bid-side depth untested in downturn
- Farmland is not marked-to-market by most lenders—stress could emerge suddenly

## Rural Housing Segment (Farmer Mac's USDA Line)

**Structural trend:** Rural housing affordability deteriorating faster than urban.
- Median rural price up 60.5% since 2019 vs. 48.9% suburban
- Minimum income to afford median home: $75K (up from $36K)
- Appraisal challenges in thin markets
- USDA loan program provides liquidity backstop

**Cycle position:** Mid-to-late. Demand remains elevated post-pandemic but affordability ceiling approaching. Limited new supply due to construction economics.

## Renewable Energy/Broadband Infrastructure (Farmer Mac Growth Drivers)

**FACT:** 40% of Farmer Mac's $31.1B portfolio now in infrastructure segments.

**JUDGMENT:** This is the variant opportunity. Consensus views Farmer Mac as ag credit; reality is diversified rural infrastructure finance.

Key metrics:
- Renewable energy: $2.3B (doubled YoY), 175 bps spread
- Broadband/data centers: $1.3B (doubled YoY), 230 bps spread
- Combined: 18% of core earnings vs. 11% of volume (margin accretive)

Growth drivers:
- IRA subsidies for renewable projects
- Rural broadband expansion programs
- Data center buildout for AI/cloud

**This is secular, not cyclical.** Even if ag credit stagnates, infrastructure finance provides organic growth.
