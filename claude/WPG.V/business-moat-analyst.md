## 1. Business Model Summary (Economic, Not Narrative)

**How it makes money:** It does not. West Point Gold is a pure exploration-stage company burning cash to drill and define a gold resource. Revenue is zero. The "product" being manufactured is geological information -- drill results that de-risk an orebody and create option value on future gold production.

**Who pays:** Shareholders fund operations through equity issuances. The company raised C$25M in a private placement (closed February 19, 2026 at C$1.10/share) and C$13M through financings, warrants, and option exercises in 2025. This is the business model: sell equity, spend on drilling, create geological value, repeat until resource definition triggers a re-rating or acquisition.

**Cost structure:** Overwhelmingly variable in theory (exploration spend is discretionary), but functionally semi-fixed during active drill campaigns. Three drill rigs running simultaneously in Q1 2026 represents a significant burn rate. G&A overhead is modest relative to exploration spending, as is typical for TSX-V explorers.

**Where value accrues:** Value accrues (or destroys) at the resource definition stage. The critical value inflection points are: (1) maiden NI 43-101 resource estimate (targeted 2026), (2) preliminary economic assessment (PEA), (3) pre-feasibility study (PFS), and (4) acquisition or development decision. Per Deloitte's 2024 Tracking the Trends report, projects advancing through PFS can see 3-5x valuation re-ratings as technical and permitting risk declines.

**Current share structure:** Approximately 129-131M shares outstanding post-February 2026 placement, plus ~23M warrants outstanding. Treasury estimated at C$30M+ (C$7.4M cash at year-end 2025 plus ~C$23.5M net placement proceeds). This is a well-funded junior by sector standards.

## 2. Source(s) of Advantage -- Adapted for an Explorer

For a pre-revenue explorer, "moat" analysis must be reframed. There are no customers, no revenue streams, no pricing power, and no switching costs in the traditional sense. The relevant question becomes: **Does this company control something scarce and difficult to replicate, and does it have structural advantages in converting geological potential into economic value?**

### 2a. Land Position Quality and Consolidation (Primary Advantage)

**Mechanism:** West Point Gold has systematically assembled the dominant land position in the northern Oatman Mining District over several years. The Gold Chain Project now comprises:

- **15 patented mining claims** covering 282 acres (114 hectares)
- **492 unpatented lode claims** on BLM land covering 9,840 acres (3,982 hectares)
- **Total: ~10,122 acres** across a 15-km mineralized trend

The patented claims are the structural keystone. Unlike unpatented BLM claims (which grant only mineral rights and require ongoing maintenance and federal permitting), **patented claims convey full fee-simple ownership of both surface and mineral rights**. This is a critical distinction because:

1. **Permitting simplification:** Development on patented land avoids the lengthy Bureau of Land Management environmental review process (NEPA, EIS) that can add 5-10 years to project timelines on federal land.
2. **Development optionality:** The entire Tyro Main Zone exploration target (19.5-31.2 Mt at 2.0-3.0 g/t Au) sits on just 3 patented claims. This means the core of any future mine could be permitted and developed under state jurisdiction rather than federal.
3. **Scarcity:** Patented mining claims are a finite, non-renewable asset. The U.S. government stopped issuing new mineral patents in 1994 under a Congressional moratorium. You cannot create new ones. The only way to acquire them is to buy existing ones from current holders.

CEO Derek Macpherson has explicitly stated that the exploration target "has the potential to be moved into production quickly given that it is on patented claims and given the existing underutilized infrastructure in the surrounding district" (per September 2025 press release). The permitting advantage of patented claims is real and structural.

**Replicability:** A competitor cannot assemble a comparable package in the immediate area. West Point Gold has been aggressively staking and acquiring claims since the Gold79 days, prompted by "increased exploration and staking activity in the region" (per company filings). The company expanded the land package by 30% in early 2025 alone, staking 113 new lode claims along a structural corridor. The ground is now spoken for.

**Limitation:** The unpatented BLM claims (97% of the land area) remain subject to federal permitting requirements and annual maintenance fees. Only the patented claims (~3% of area) carry the full development advantage. However, the fact that the core orebody sits on patented ground is disproportionately important.

### 2b. Geological Quality (Secondary Advantage -- Genuinely Differentiated Grades)

**The grade question is the central analytical issue for this company.** Assessed objectively:

**Context -- What constitutes high-grade gold?**
- Global average mine grade: approximately 1.0-1.5 g/t Au (declining over decades)
- Low-sulphidation epithermal deposits range from <0.5 g/t (bulk disseminated, e.g., Hycroft) to 40+ g/t (bonanza veins, e.g., Hishikari)
- The Oatman District historically produced ~2 million ounces at grades typical of mid-grade epithermal systems

**Assessment:** These are legitimately exceptional intercepts for an exploration-stage project. The NE Tyro results -- particularly 21.3m at 13.48 g/t Au, 27.4m at 9.56 g/t Au, and 36.6m at 7.35 g/t Au -- represent bonanza-grade mineralization over significant widths. For context:

- **Fruta del Norte** (Lundin Gold, Ecuador), one of the highest-grade underground gold mines currently operating globally, has reserves at ~8 g/t Au. West Point's best intercepts exceed this grade over meaningful intervals.
- **Prime Mining's Los Reyes** (recently acquired by Torex Gold for C$450M) had intercepts in the 1-5 g/t range over similar widths.
- The combination of grade AND width is what matters. Narrow high-grade veins (0.5m at 50 g/t) are common in epithermal systems and often uneconomic. Broad high-grade zones (20-45m at 5-13 g/t) are genuinely rare.

The NE Tyro zone is showing **coalescing veins** -- multiple vein structures merging into a single wide, high-grade zone at depth. This is characteristic of the "boiling zone" in low-sulphidation epithermal systems, where gold deposition is most concentrated. The geological team (VP Exploration Rob Johansing, with 45 years of epithermal experience including Kinross El Salvador) and the Colorado School of Mines/CASERM research partnership are specifically studying vein textures and gold deposition mechanisms to vector toward the highest-grade zones.

**Critical caveat:** These are individual drill intercepts, not a resource. The exploration target (19.5-31.2 Mt at 2.0-3.0 g/t Au) is explicitly conceptual and may not convert to a defined resource. Drill results are inherently selective -- the company reports its best holes. The maiden resource estimate, expected in 2026, will be the definitive test of whether these grades hold across the entire deposit.

**Why this matters for structural advantage:** If the maiden resource confirms anything close to 2+ g/t Au over meaningful tonnage, this becomes one of the higher-grade open-pit-amenable gold deposits being defined globally. High grade is the ultimate cost advantage in mining -- it means less rock moved per ounce recovered, lower strip ratios, smaller processing plants, and faster payback. Grade cannot be engineered; it is geological endowment.

### 2c. District-Scale Optionality (Tertiary Advantage)

The Gold Chain Project is not a single deposit. It is a **district-scale system** with multiple identified targets across a 15-km mineralized trend:

- **Tyro Main Zone**: The bulk tonnage target, exploration target of 19.5-31.2 Mt at 2.0-3.0 g/t Au
- **NE Tyro**: The high-grade zone, now 300m+ strike length, open at depth and along strike
- **Tyro South**: 1.2-km extension of the Tyro vein, untested
- **Sheep Trail / Banner Trend**: Part of the ~10-km North Oatman trend, surface samples up to 51.9 g/t Au
- **Frisco Graben**: Large-scale structural target
- **Union Pass Corridor**: Newly staked structural corridor with rhyolite domes and gold occurrences
- **Black Dyke, Gold Chain Hill**: Additional untested targets

Additionally, the company holds:
- **Jefferson Canyon** (Nevada): JV with Kinross Gold, 7km from Round Mountain Mine; Kinross operating and funding exploration with option to acquire 70-80% for US$10M
- **Baxter Spring** (Nevada): Acquired October 2025, 137 claims covering 2,829 acres in Manhattan Mining District, ~40km from Round Mountain, with 128 historical drill holes
- **Tip Top** (Nevada): Additional early-stage project

**Mechanism of advantage:** Multiple discovery targets on consolidated ground create asymmetric upside. Each new target tested is an independent "lottery ticket" on a new discovery, but the cost of each ticket is low because the infrastructure, team, and permitting are shared. A single new discovery at Sheep Trail or Frisco Graben could double the project's resource base for incremental drilling cost.

### 2d. Kinross JV as Third-Party Validation

The Jefferson Canyon JV with Kinross Gold (US$16.7B market cap, 2M+ oz annual production) is a meaningful signal. Kinross is spending real money (US$600K minimum exploration commitment plus US$75K/year lease payments, with option to pay US$5M for 70% and additional US$5M for 80%) to explore a property 7km from its operating Round Mountain mine.

This is not a "moat" per se, but it serves as **credible third-party geological validation** and creates an embedded call option on a potential acquisition. If Kinross exercises its option, WPG receives US$5-10M in cash while retaining 20-30% interest. If Kinross walks away, the geological data it generated becomes WPG's property.

AZS has signed three confidentiality agreements with potential acquirers. AZS is a legitimate competitor for capital and acquirer attention.

**Mako Mining Corp. (TSXV: MKO)** -- Owns the Moss Mine in the Oatman District, a producing open-pit heap-leach operation. Moss has a defined resource of 646,000 oz Au (M+I) at 0.35 g/t Au. This is a fundamentally different business: low-grade, bulk tonnage, heap-leach. It validates the district's productive geology but is not directly competitive with WPG's high-grade vein model.

### The Critical Distinction: Grade Separates WPG from the Pack

Among the 500+ junior gold explorers on TSX-V, **the combination of grade, width, depth, and continuity that WPG is demonstrating at NE Tyro is in the top percentile**. Most junior explorers report either:
- Narrow high-grade veins (1-2m at 10+ g/t) that are geologically interesting but economically marginal
- Broad low-grade systems (50-100m at 0.5-1.0 g/t) that require enormous scale to be economic
- A few scattered high-grade hits with no continuity

WPG is consistently hitting 20-45m widths at 3-13 g/t Au across a 300m+ strike zone, with the zone open in multiple directions. This is the geological sweet spot: wide enough for low-cost mining methods, high enough grade for robust economics, and continuous enough to suggest a coherent deposit rather than random high-grade pockets.

### Barriers to Entry

1. **The ground is staked.** You cannot enter the northern Oatman District without acquiring claims from existing holders. WPG controls the dominant position.
2. **Geological knowledge is cumulative and proprietary.** WPG has drilled 9,898m to date, funded a Colorado School of Mines research partnership, and has 45 years of epithermal expertise on the team. This geological model is not public.
3. **Patented claims are not acquirable from the government.** The 15 patented claims at Gold Chain are a finite, irreplaceable asset.
4. **But:** An acquirer with capital (mid-tier or major producer) could simply buy WPG itself. This is not a barrier -- it is the intended exit pathway.

## 6. Fragility Analysis (How the Moat Breaks)

This section identifies the structural vulnerabilities that could destroy value regardless of management quality.

### 6a. The Grade Does Not Hold (Most Dangerous Risk)

**The risk:** Individual drill intercepts can be misleading. Epithermal vein systems are notoriously "nuggetty" -- gold distribution can be extremely heterogeneous, with bonanza-grade pockets surrounded by barren rock. The NE Tyro intercepts are spectacular, but the maiden resource estimate could reveal:
- A smaller volume of high-grade material than intercepts suggest
- Significant grade variability that lowers the average
- Geometric constraints that make mining difficult (thin veins at unfavorable orientations)
- The "coalescing veins" interpretation is wrong and the wide intercepts are an artifact of drill angle

**Probability assessment:** Moderate. The consistency across multiple holes (10+ holes, all hitting mineralization over 300m of strike) reduces the probability of a "nugget effect" illusion. The 86% metallurgical recovery from preliminary testing is encouraging. But until a NI 43-101 resource is defined with proper geostatistical analysis, this risk cannot be retired.

**What to watch:** The maiden resource estimate (expected 2026). If the resource grade comes in below 1.5 g/t Au at meaningful tonnage, the geological thesis is impaired. If it confirms 2+ g/t over 1M+ ounces, the thesis is significantly de-risked.

### 6b. Permitting Failure or Delay

**The risk:** Even with patented claims covering the core orebody, development will likely require permits for infrastructure, water, waste disposal, and access roads that extend onto BLM or state land. Arizona's permitting environment is favorable (Fraser Institute top 5) but not immune to challenge. Environmental opposition, endangered species findings, or water rights disputes could delay development by years.

**Mitigating factor:** The Plan of Operations (POO) for expanded drilling is expected in early March 2026. This is a lower-bar permit than a mining permit, but its timely receipt will signal regulatory cooperation.

### 6c. Gold Price Collapse

**The risk:** WPG's entire thesis is leveraged to gold price. A sustained decline below US$1,500/oz would likely make any future mine uneconomic, halt exploration funding, and collapse the share price. At current gold prices (~US$2,900+/oz), even a 2.0 g/t deposit is highly economic. At US$1,200/oz, the calculus changes dramatically.

**Mitigating factor:** This is a macro risk, not a company-specific fragility. All gold explorers share it. WPG's high grade provides a margin of safety -- a 2+ g/t open-pit deposit is economic at much lower gold prices than a 0.5 g/t heap-leach operation.

### 6d. Dilution and Capital Destruction

**The risk:** Exploration companies destroy shareholder capital more often than they create it. The statistical base rate for junior explorers advancing to production is extremely low (estimated <5% of TSX-V explorers ever reach production). Each additional financing dilutes existing shareholders. If the project stalls, the C$38M+ raised will have been destroyed.

**Current status:** Mitigated by strong treasury (C$30M+), but the risk resets with every new program or acquisition. The Baxter Spring acquisition (October 2025) and planned 5,000m drill program there represent additional capital requirements beyond Gold Chain.

### 6e. Management Exits or Key-Person Risk

**The risk:** VP Exploration Rob Johansing (45 years of epithermal experience) is the geological brain of the operation. His departure would be a material loss. Similarly, the recently assembled board (Bowering, Paterson) could lose interest if results disappoint.

### 6f. Competitor Discovery Outperformance

**The risk:** Arizona Gold & Silver's Philadelphia Project is producing comparable high-grade intercepts in the same district. If AZS defines a larger or higher-grade resource first, acquirer attention and capital could flow there instead. The Oatman District may support multiple discoveries, but acquirer dollars are finite.

## 7. Moat & Durability Classification

### Moat Strength: Moderate

**Justification:** West Point Gold controls a genuinely scarce asset -- a consolidated, district-scale land position anchored by patented claims in a top-5 global mining jurisdiction, with drill results indicating bonanza-grade gold mineralization over significant widths. The patented claims are irreplaceable (no new patents since 1994), the ground position cannot be replicated, and the geological endowment appears to be in the top percentile of current TSX-V exploration projects.

However, the "moat" is incomplete because:
1. No defined resource exists yet. The geological promise is exceptional but unproven at the resource-estimate level.
2. The ultimate value realization depends on factors outside the company's control (gold price, permitting, acquirer appetite).
3. Competitors in the same district (AZS) are producing comparable results, meaning WPG does not have a monopoly on Oatman District geology.
4. There are no customers, no revenue, and no demonstrated economic returns -- only geological potential.

The moat rating reflects the structural advantages (patented claims, consolidated ground, grade quality, district scale) tempered by the fundamental uncertainty inherent in all pre-resource exploration companies.

### Earnings Durability: Not Applicable / Low

**Justification:** There are no earnings. There is no revenue. There is no production. Assigning "earnings durability" to an exploration company is a category error. What can be assessed is **the durability of the geological option value**, which is moderate-to-high: the gold is in the ground, the claims are secure, and the jurisdiction is stable. But converting geological option value into durable earnings requires successfully navigating resource definition, permitting, feasibility, financing, construction, and operations -- a multi-year, multi-billion-dollar gauntlet that most junior explorers never complete.

The more relevant question for WPG is: **Is this asset likely to be acquired before the company needs to build a mine itself?** Given the board composition (Bowering, Paterson -- serial company builders/sellers), the Kinross JV, and the grade quality, the answer is: more likely than the base rate for TSX-V explorers, but far from certain.

**The single most important factor determining West Point Gold's competitive durability is the confirmation of grade in the maiden NI 43-101 resource estimate.**

Everything else -- the patented claims, the land consolidation, the board quality, the Kinross JV, the district-scale optionality -- is derivative of whether the geological grade holds up under rigorous geostatistical analysis. If the resource comes in at <1.0 g/t Au or <500K ounces, the market capitalization will contract significantly regardless of all other advantages. Grade is destiny in gold mining, and no amount of land position, board pedigree, or jurisdictional advantage can compensate for inadequate grade.

This factor dominates because:
- It determines whether the asset is economically viable at a range of gold prices
- It determines the level of acquirer interest (majors want high-grade ounces, not marginal deposits)
- It is the one variable that cannot be managed, negotiated, or marketed around -- it is geological reality

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**Non-consensus insight (bullish for durability):** The market is likely pricing WPG primarily on the Tyro Main Zone exploration target (~1.3-3.0 Moz at 2.0-3.0 g/t Au conceptual), but **may be underweighting the NE Tyro high-grade zone as a separate, potentially standalone economic target.**

Here is the second-order logic:

1. **First-order view (consensus):** WPG has a promising exploration target at Tyro Main Zone. The NE Tyro high-grade intercepts are exciting but represent a small portion of the overall system. The company needs to define a resource and advance toward a PEA.

2. **Second-order view (potentially non-consensus):** The NE Tyro zone -- with its 300m+ strike length, 200m+ depth extent, and consistent grades of 5-13 g/t Au over 20-45m widths -- may be developing into a **high-grade underground mining target** that is distinct from and incremental to the bulk-tonnage Tyro Main Zone. If so, the project economics could support two extraction methods simultaneously: open-pit on the broader, lower-grade Tyro Main Zone, and selective underground mining on the high-grade NE Tyro core. This dual-approach model (seen at deposits like Round Mountain, where both open-pit and underground operations extract different grade profiles) could dramatically improve project economics versus a single open-pit scenario.

3. **Why this matters:** A project with both a 1-2 Moz open-pit component at 2+ g/t AND a high-grade underground component at 5+ g/t is a fundamentally different proposition than a 2 g/t open-pit alone. The underground component provides cash flow acceleration, improved average feed grade, and a "starter mine" option that reduces initial capital requirements. This is the kind of project configuration that mid-tier producers with underground expertise (Lundin Gold, Agnico Eagle) specifically seek in acquisitions.

4. **The bear case this challenges:** Bears may argue that NE Tyro intercepts are "cherry-picked" bonanza hits in a system that averages 1-2 g/t. If the geological model (coalescing veins in the boiling zone) is correct and the high-grade core is structurally coherent, this characterization would be wrong.

5. **What could invalidate this:** The maiden resource estimate. If NE Tyro's high grades do not survive the transition from individual drill intercepts to a block-modeled resource, the "dual extraction" thesis collapses.

**Additional non-consensus observation (bearish nuance):** The project is in Arizona, not Nevada. While Arizona is still highly ranked (Fraser Institute top 5 overall, 7th on policy alone), it does not carry the same "Nevada premium" that some investors may be assuming. If market participants are assigning a Nevada-tier jurisdictional premium to an Arizona asset based on imprecise geographic framing, there may be a modest perception adjustment as this becomes widely understood. This is a minor point but represents a gap between perception and reality in how the company is sometimes discussed.

**Board composition as a forward signal:** The November 2025 board reconstitution -- adding Andrew Bowering (serial company builder/seller with Prime Mining, Millennial Lithium exits) and elevating Anthony Paterson (Prime Mining early-stage financier) to Chairman -- is not typical of a company planning to operate a mine. This is the board you assemble when you intend to de-risk an asset and sell it. If the geological thesis holds, the M&A exit path has been deliberately engineered by people with a demonstrated track record of executing exactly that playbook.
