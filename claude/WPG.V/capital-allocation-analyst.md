# WPG.V (West Point Gold Corp.) -- Management & Capital Allocation Analysis

## Key Finding

**A capital markets-led management team running a genuine exploration story, but with low insider ownership, aggressive dilution, and structural conflicts that demand scrutiny.** Insider ownership sits at approximately 6.4% -- thin for a junior explorer at this stage. The share count has grown ~586% in 22 months (post-consolidation). CEO Derek Macpherson simultaneously serves as Executive Chairman of Olive Resource Capital (TSXV: OC), a merchant bank that holds WPG shares. The company has executed four financings and two acquisitions in 16 months, issuing approximately 112 million shares and warrants against a post-consolidation base of ~19 million.

The drill results are real. The geological story at Gold Chain (Tyro zone) is increasingly compelling -- today's 13.48 g/t over 21.3m is an exceptional intercept. But the question for this analysis is not geology -- it is whether this management team is structurally incentivized to create value for shareholders or to maintain a financing and promotional cycle that benefits insiders disproportionately.

## Incentive Structure & Alignment

### Cash Compensation (per Q1 FY2025 Financial Statements)

Management service agreements provide the following annual compensation equivalents:
- Executive Chairman / CEO: C$240,000/year (12-month termination payment = C$240K)
- CFO: C$120,000/year (lump sum termination = C$120K)
- VP Exploration: C$120,000/year (lump sum termination = C$120K)

CFO's controlled company also charges bookkeeping/administrative fees: C$13,012 in Q1 2025 (up from C$4,036 in Q1 2024 -- a 222% increase year-over-year).

Total management service compensation: approximately C$480K/year. This is lean relative to market cap and appropriate for an explorer.

### Stock-Based Compensation

**December 2025 grant:** 4,200,000 options at C$1.18 exercise price (5-year expiry). 3,800,000 vest 50% immediately, 25% at 12 months, 25% at 24 months. 400,000 vest immediately.

**February 2025 grant:** 450,000 options at C$0.33 (to a director and consultant), vested immediately.

**March 2025 grant:** 250,000 options at C$0.49 (to a director), partial immediate vesting.

**Q1 2025 SBC expense:** $170,618 total ($117,763 expensed, $52,855 capitalized to exploration assets).

### What Behavior Does This Compensation Encourage?

The combination of lean base salary and large option grants creates a classic junior explorer incentive: management benefits from share price appreciation. The December 2025 grant of 4.2M options at $1.18 is material -- at the current $1.60 price, these options are approximately $1.76M in the money (on paper). For a CEO making $240K/year in cash, this is a meaningful multiple of annual salary.

**However:** The 50% immediate vesting on 3.8M options is aggressive. Half the options are in the money from day one with no lock-up. This creates incentive to promote near-term catalysts rather than optimize long-term value.

**Who benefits if the stock underperforms?** Management still collects base salary and the CFO collects administrative fees across multiple listed vehicles. The option grants become worthless, but base comp continues. With ~6.4% aggregate insider ownership and an estimated ~2.6% for the CEO, management has limited personal downside relative to outside shareholders.

### The Warrant Overhang Is the Real Story

24 million warrants represent 18% potential dilution on the basic count. Many carry exercise prices of $0.40-$0.55 -- deeply in the money at $1.60. This creates an ongoing dilution cascade as warrants are exercised, which management frames as "proceeds from warrant exercises" (i.e., non-dilutive financing) but which is deferred dilution from prior financings priced at much lower levels.

In 2025, 6.27 million warrants were exercised for C$3.0M in proceeds between September 30 and December 15 alone. These exercises brought in cash but created selling pressure and dilution at warrant prices far below market.

## Capital Allocation Track Record

### a) Exploration Reinvestment -- Credible

This is the company's primary capital allocation decision, and the track record is credible.

- 9,131m drilled at Gold Chain in 2025
- 9,898m completed of 15,000m program as of Feb 25, 2026
- Second drill rig added January 2026; third rig commencing March 2026
- Updated exploration target at Tyro Main Zone: 19.5-31.2 Mt at 2.0-3.0 g/t Au
- Preliminary metallurgical recoveries up to 86%
- Total capital raised in 2025: C$13M (financings + warrant/option exercises)
- Cash position Dec 31, 2025: C$7.4M; post-Feb 2026 PP: ~C$30M+

**Rough exploration capital efficiency:** C$13M raised in 2025, ~9,100m drilled. Implied all-in cost of ~C$1,400/m (including G&A). Reasonable for RC/core drilling in Arizona, though without segmented cost data, the G&A load cannot be precisely isolated.

The expansion from 10,000m to 15,000m, and the addition of a second and third rig, reflects a team that is accelerating into strong drill results. This is appropriate behavior. Capital is going into the ground.

### b) M&A -- Mixed

**Bullet Exploration Amalgamation (November 2024):**
- 3-for-1 exchange ratio (1 WPG share per 3 Bullet shares)
- 19,112,940 shares issued + 6,400,000 replacement warrants at $0.45
- Bullet shareholders received ~46% of combined entity (fully diluted, pre-financing)
- Added Jefferson Canyon (Kinross JV) and Jefferson North projects

**Assessment:** At prevailing share prices (~$0.25), the implied consideration was ~C$4.8M in shares for largely early-stage Nevada properties. The 46% ownership split to Bullet holders for pre-resource exploration assets is generous. This was primarily a corporate combination to create a larger vehicle -- common on TSX-V but not purely value-driven. The Kinross JV on Jefferson Canyon has some strategic value.

**Baxter Spring Acquisition (October 2025):**
- 13,500,000 shares at $0.435/share (implied value: ~C$5.9M)
- 2-year staggered contractual hold periods through October 2027
- 128 historic drill holes, ~12,850m of historical drilling
- Manhattan District, ~40km south of Kinross Round Mountain Mine

**Assessment:** Better structured. The staggered lock-up is shareholder-friendly. Acquiring historical drilling data for ~C$5.9M in locked-up shares is defensible. CEO's claim that "replacement cost far exceeds share consideration" is plausible given 12,850m of historical drilling.

## Learning vs. Repetition

### Positive Signals (Evidence of Learning)
- Financing pricing has consistently improved (C$0.25 to $0.45 to $1.10) -- management is not selling shares at depressed prices
- February 2026 PP was common shares only (no warrants) -- a structural improvement over unit deals
- Baxter Spring acquisition included meaningful contractual lock-ups on vendor shares -- an improvement over the Bullet deal structure
- Board additions (Bowering, Paterson) bring credible mine-building and transaction experience
- Bowering personally invested $471K in February 2026 PP -- skin in the game
- Three directors invested combined C$725K in February 2026 PP

### Concerning Patterns (Evidence of Repetition)
- The consolidate-then-dilute cycle (191M to 19M to 131M) is a pattern seen in lifestyle/promotional juniors
- CEO tenure instability: two CEO rotations in 12 months
- CFO holds concurrent roles at two other public companies -- common on TSX-V but limits attention
- CEO holds concurrent Executive Chairman role at Olive Resource Capital, a merchant bank that has held Gold79/WPG shares
- Immediate vesting on 50% of December 2025 options suggests urgency rather than long-term alignment
- 4 employees for a company managing 4+ project areas and running a 15,000m drill program with 3 rigs

## Forward-Looking Capital Allocation Risk

### What Management Will Likely Do Next

1. **Continue aggressive drilling:** The 15,000m program with three rigs through 2026 is well-funded. Post-PP cash position is approximately C$30M+. This funds 12-18 months of aggressive exploration without additional financing.

2. **Deliver maiden resource estimate:** Targeted for 2026. The exploration target of 19.5-31.2 Mt at 2.0-3.0 g/t Au (1.25-3.0 Moz range) at Tyro Main Zone provides the framework.

3. **Potential additional acquisitions:** Baxter Spring and Bullet both demonstrate appetite for portfolio building. With cash and a rising share price, management may pursue further bolt-on acquisitions.

4. **Another financing within 12-18 months if resource is strong:** Expect management to raise again at a higher price to fund a PEA or pre-feasibility study. Dilution will continue.

### Where Capital Is Most Likely To Be Misallocated

- **Over-diversification across too many targets.** Gold Chain (multiple Tyro zones), Baxter Spring, Jefferson Canyon, Jefferson North, and numerous step-out targets (Sheep Trail, Black Dyke, Gold Chain Hill, Union Pass Corridor, Frisco Graben) -- for a 4-person company, this is an expansive portfolio. Capital spread across too many targets reduces the probability of a definitive outcome at any one.
- **Related party transactions through Olive Resource Capital.** The CEO's dual role creates an information and deal-flow asymmetry.
- **Conference and promotional spending.** The company is participating in multiple investor conferences (PDAC 2026, Investor Summit). Without granular G&A disclosure, promotional spend cannot be quantified.

## The One Thing That Determines Value Creation

**Whether the maiden resource estimate at Tyro Main Zone is large enough and high-grade enough to attract a strategic partner or acquirer before management returns to the financing window with further dilution.**

This dominates because:
- The geological results are genuinely strong (13.48 g/t over 21.3m today, prior hits of 36.6m at 7.35 g/t, 30.5m at 9.05 g/t)
- But management's low insider ownership (~2.6% CEO on current count) and demonstrated willingness to issue shares aggressively means the per-share value equation is fragile
- If the resource is 1.5-2+ Moz at 2+ g/t in a Tier-1 jurisdiction, a major producer will notice, and the path to value crystallization is clear
- If the resource disappoints or is delayed, the company returns to the financing cycle with a bloated share count and limited insider alignment
- The Bowering/Paterson board additions are consistent with positioning for a sale or major JV -- these are people who know how to structure exits
- **The key behavioral tell:** Watch whether management pursues a strategic JV at Gold Chain (value-crystallizing) or finances internally and retains 100% (dilution-maximizing). The decision will reveal whether the board is building for shareholders or for itself.

**What would change behavior:** A significant strategic investment from a mid-tier/major producer at Gold Chain would de-risk the financing dependency, validate the geological thesis, and reduce dilution risk. A Kinross-style JV at Gold Chain (similar to Jefferson Canyon) would be the highest-signal event for management alignment.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**The market sees a hot explorer with exceptional drill results and a rising share price. What it may underweight is the structural dilution embedded in the warrant book and the thinness of insider alignment.**

Specifically:

1. **24 million warrants outstanding** (18% dilution on basic count) at deeply in-the-money prices ($0.40-$0.55 vs. $1.60 share price) will be exercised over the next 12-24 months, creating persistent selling pressure and dilution even without another financing. This is ~C$10.5M in cash inflows but 24M shares of dilution.

2. **The CEO's ownership has been structurally diluted from ~7.7% (pre-Bullet) to an estimated ~2.6% (current).** At $1.60/share, Macpherson's estimated ~3.4M shares represent approximately C$5.4M in value. This is meaningful to a person making C$240K/year, but it does not represent the kind of concentrated ownership (>10%) that structurally prevents bad behavior.

3. **The consolidate-then-dilute pattern is underappreciated.** Pre-consolidation: 191M shares. Post-consolidation: 19M shares. Current: 131M shares. The company consolidated specifically to enable equity financing, then issued shares at a rate that has nearly returned to the pre-consolidation count. On a per-share basis, existing shareholders at the time of consolidation have been diluted by approximately 586%.

4. **The CEO's concurrent Olive Resource Capital role** creates an information and deal-flow asymmetry that benefits Olive shareholders (who get exposure to WPG deal flow via a merchant bank vehicle) at the potential expense of direct WPG shareholders. This is not disclosed prominently.

5. **The CFO serving three public companies simultaneously** is common on TSX-V but means WPG does not have a dedicated finance executive during its most capital-intensive phase (running three drill rigs and a C$30M+ treasury).

**Net assessment:** These factors make me less trusting at the margin than the headline narrative suggests. The drill results are real and exceptional, but the share structure and insider alignment are weaker than what the story deserves. The risk of this becoming a vehicle that raises capital, drills holes, generates excitement, and dilutes holders into the next cycle is non-trivial -- even with good geology.
