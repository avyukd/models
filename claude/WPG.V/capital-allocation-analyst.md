# WPG.V — Capital Allocation & Management Analysis

**Verdict: Lean operator with reasonable capital discipline for a junior explorer. No major red flags, but limited track record and a financing is inevitable.**

---

## 1. Insider Ownership & Skin in the Game

- **4 employees** — extremely lean for a company running a 15,000m drill program, suggesting management is hands-on, not lifestyle-oriented
- December 2025 stock option grant: 4,200,000 options @ $1.18 to officers, directors, and consultants — these are in-the-money at $1.60, aligning incentives with shareholders
- The option grant represents ~3.9% of basic shares — moderate, not excessive
- **Gap:** Specific insider ownership percentages and recent insider buying/selling data not readily available from public search results. This is a data gap worth filling before committing capital.

**Signal check:** The company drilled 9,131m in 2025 and expanded to 15,000m in 2026. At ~C$150-200/m all-in (typical for Arizona RC/core), the 2025 program cost ~C$1.4-1.8M in direct drilling costs, plus geology, assays, and camp — call it C$2.5-4M total exploration spend.

With C$13M raised and C$7.4M remaining at year-end, roughly C$5.6M was spent in 2025 on exploration + G&A. The Q1 2025 net loss of C$1.42M (quarterly) suggests ~C$5.7M annualized cash burn — consistent.

**Assessment:** Reasonable. The money is going into the ground, not into salaries. The 4-person team running a multi-rig drill program suggests capital efficiency.

## 3. Financing History & Discipline

**Key events:**
- Company formerly Gold79 Mines Ltd — renamed to West Point Gold (date unclear from public sources, sometime in 2024-2025). Name changes can be a flag but in this case appears linked to a genuine strategic refocus on the Tyro/Gold Chain system.
- 2025 financings raised C$13M total:
  - Mix of private placements, warrant exercises, and option exercises
  - C$5M from warrant/option exercises (C$3.3M in Q4 alone)
  - C$3M from warrant exercises announced December 2025
- Warrant exercises at prices well below current market ($0.29-$1.26) suggest earlier financings were done at much lower prices — original investors are being rewarded

**Flow-through vs. hard-dollar:** Not specified in available data. Flow-through is standard for Canadian exploration companies and not inherently negative.

**Assessment:** C$13M raised in a year that saw 9,131m drilled and an exploration target defined is reasonable. The company has not been egregiously dilutive relative to value created. Shares grew from an undisclosed base to 108M — need historical share count to fully assess dilution trajectory.

## 4. Share Structure Discipline

| Metric | Value | Assessment |
|--------|-------|------------|
| Basic shares | 108.1M | Moderate |
| Warrants | 23.0M (21% of basic) | Significant overhang |
| Options | 4.2M (3.9% of basic) | Reasonable |
| Fully diluted | ~135.2M | 25% dilution to basic |

**Warrant overhang is the primary concern.** 23M warrants with exercise prices as low as $0.29 represent guaranteed selling pressure as they approach expiry. The good news: exercises bring in ~C$10.5M, effectively pre-funding the next round.

**The warrant exercise dynamic is actually net positive for the company** — it means the next financing may be smaller (or unnecessary) if warrants convert. But it creates a technical headwind on the stock.

## 6. Strategic Decision-Making

**Positive signals:**
- **Baxter Spring acquisition (Oct 2025):** Acquiring an advanced-stage Nevada project with 128 historical drill holes (~12,850m) provides portfolio diversification and de-risks the company's single-asset concentration. Manhattan District near Kinross's Round Mountain Mine.
- **Drill program expansion:** Adding a second rig (Jan 2026) and now a third rig (March 2026) shows urgency appropriate for an explorer with strong early results
- **TSX Venture 50 recognition (ranked 35th):** Raises profile for institutional investors at PDAC and similar events
- **Timing:** Accelerating exploration into a strong gold cycle is the right call

**Neutral/negative signals:**
- Name change from Gold79 to West Point Gold — cosmetic, but sometimes signals a "fresh start" narrative
- 4 employees for a C$173M market cap company — lean but also means heavy reliance on consultants/contractors

## Bottom Line

WPG's management team is lean and drilling-focused — the capital is going into the ground, not into executive perks. The Kinross JV provides institutional validation. The main concerns are (1) limited public track record for the CEO/board, (2) warrant overhang creating technical headwinds, and (3) the inevitable next financing, which at C$7.4M cash and three rigs running, is probably 2-3 quarters away. No dealbreaker red flags, but not enough data to give a high-conviction management endorsement either.

**What I'd want to know:** Insider ownership percentage, CEO/VP Exploration prior company history, and detailed related-party transaction disclosure from the most recent AIF.
