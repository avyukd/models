## The 1-3 Key Drivers That Actually Move This Stock

**1. Operating Leverage / Margin Expansion (Primary)**
At 84% adjusted gross margins, every incremental dollar of revenue generates ~$0.50+ of incremental EBITDA. The company hit 39% EBITDA margins in Q3 2025 against a 40% target. This operating leverage inflection is the core earnings story. If margins expand while revenue grows 15%+, EPS growth will significantly outpace revenue growth.

**2. Revenue Growth Sustainability**
The stock's multiple is predicated on 15-20% growth continuing. FY2024 delivered 25% growth; YTD 2025 tracking 21-26%. Customer count acceleration (304 IDI adds in Q3 vs. 183 in Q4 prior year) suggests momentum intact. Any deceleration below 15% compresses the multiple materially.

**3. Strategic Value / M&A Optionality**
Industry is consolidating aggressively (First Advantage/Sterling $2.2B, LexisNexis/IDVerse, Entrust/Onfido). RDVT's cloud-native platform, FOREWARN distribution network, and proprietary data fusion layer make it an attractive acquisition target. This optionality is not priced explicitly.

## Thesis in 3-5 Bullets

1. **RDVT is a high-quality, capital-light data analytics business** with 84% adjusted gross margins, zero debt, $45M cash, and accelerating FCF ($14.4M FY2024, on pace for $25M+ FY2025).

2. **Operating leverage is hitting an inflection point** - Q3 2025 EBITDA margin of 39% nearly achieved the 40% target, meaning earnings growth will outpace revenue growth going forward.

3. **Management capital allocation is exceptional** - bought back shares at $19.36 average, switched to dividends when stock hit $40+. This price discipline is rare and underappreciated.

4. **The problem is the price** - at $39.26, the stock trades at 64x forward GAAP P/E and 24x normalized earnings power (~$1.65). This assumes flawless execution of the base case with no room for disappointment.

5. **No clear variant perception at current price** - the market understands this is a quality growth company. The operating leverage story is partially priced after the stock doubled from $19 to $39.

## The Setup

**Business Model:** RDVT operates a data fusion platform (CORE) that transforms licensed data into identity intelligence for collections, fraud prevention, financial services, and law enforcement. Two products: IDI/idiCORE (B2B identity platform, ~9,800 customers) and FOREWARN (B2B2C real estate agent safety app, 575+ REALTOR Associations, 400K+ users). 77% contractual revenue provides visibility.

**Industry Structure:** The identity verification market (~$430B data broker TAM) is an oligopoly at the top (Experian, Equifax, TransUnion, LexisNexis control ~45-50%) with a fragmented middle tier. RDVT competes by serving customers that giants serve poorly - SMB and mid-market where cloud-native delivery and usage-based pricing create advantages.

**Why Now:** The company is at an inflection point where it has achieved profitable scale (31% EBITDA margins), is approaching target profitability (40%), and generates meaningful FCF ($14M+ annually). The question is whether the stock price already reflects this.

## Summary: Why Neutral Is the Right Call

RDVT is that frustrating situation: **a genuinely good business that I would want to own, but not at this price.**

The management is exceptional. The business model is attractive. The operating leverage story is real. But at $39.26:

1. **No variant perception is sufficiently mispriced** - the market has correctly identified this as a quality growth company and priced it accordingly
2. **The margin of safety is insufficient** - fair value is $41, downside to $24 on growth disappointment
3. **Risk/reward is approximately symmetric** - not the asymmetric setup required for conviction

This is NOT a "too hard" classification. The business is understandable, the management is trustworthy, and the industry dynamics are clear. It is simply not cheap enough to justify a Buy recommendation.

**The discipline of passing on a quality business at a fair price is itself a form of edge.** Capital should be allocated to opportunities with asymmetric payoffs, not to "good companies at reasonable prices" where the best case is modestly positive returns.
