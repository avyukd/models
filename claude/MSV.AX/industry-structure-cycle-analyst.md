# Australian Drilling Services: Industry Structure & Cycle Analysis

*Date: 2026-02-24*

## What Drives Profitability in This Industry

Drilling contractors operate a capital-intensive, operationally leveraged business. Profitability is a function of rig utilization first, day rates second. A contractor running 90%+ utilization on its fleet earns 20%+ EBITDA margins; at 65-70% utilization, margins compress to low-teens or worse. The key mechanism: rigs carry fixed depreciation, maintenance, and crew costs whether they turn or not. Incremental rigs deployed above breakeven drop through at high margins.

Pricing power is determined entirely by the supply/demand balance for rigs in specific disciplines (diamond core, RC, underground). Contracts are typically 1-3 years for production drilling, shorter for exploration. When utilization is tight, contractors negotiate rate escalations; when loose, they bid aggressively to cover fixed costs.

## Where We Are in the Cycle

**The exploration cycle is inflecting upward, but unevenly.** ABS data shows total mineral exploration expenditure at A$1,068M in the September 2025 quarter, up 5.7% QoQ and 5.0% YoY. This follows a soft patch through calendar 2024 where quarterly spend dipped from A$991M (Jun-24) to A$933M (Mar-25) before recovering. We are not yet at the 2022-23 highs but the trajectory has turned.

The composition matters enormously. Gold exploration expenditure surged 45% YoY to A$432M in Sep-25, now representing 40% of all mineral exploration spend. This is the dominant driver. Meanwhile, coal exploration has contracted and nickel has collapsed (drill holes down 53% in recent quarters per ABS data).

**Current position: Early Recovery, transitioning from Trough.** The trough was calendar Q1 2025. Gold-driven recovery is real but narrow. Broad-based recovery has not yet materialized.

## The Gold-to-Drilling Lag

Historical pattern: gold exploration spending lags the gold price by 6-12 months. Gold crossed A$3,000/oz in mid-2024 and has continued to new highs. The exploration response began in late 2024 and accelerated through 2025. Critically, we are seeing mostly brownfield/near-mine spending so far. Greenfield exploration expenditure actually fell 17% YoY through mid-2025 (to A$253M from A$306M), which suggests juniors remain capital-constrained despite record gold prices.

This creates a two-speed recovery: major miners are spending on gold exploration near existing operations (benefiting contractors like Mitchell with mine-site relationships), while the junior-driven greenfield boom that would drive a full cycle upturn has not yet arrived. Junior capital raises typically lag further -- 12-18 months from sustained high commodity prices.

## Supply Side: Consolidation Is Real and Underappreciated

The Australian drilling services industry has undergone significant consolidation:

- **DDH1 acquired Swick** (2022), creating the largest Australian fleet at ~170 rigs
- **Perenti acquired DDH1** (2023) for A$410M, forming Perenti Drilling Services (DDH1 + Swick + Ausdrill + Ranger + Strike)
- **Boart Longyear went private** (2023-24) after years of restructuring, reducing fleet in volatile regions

Perenti Drilling Services now dominates with 2.5M+ diamond drilling meters annually. Boart Longyear is the other major player at ~2.2M meters. Mitchell Services operates as the largest remaining independent with ~90 rigs.

This consolidation structurally tightens supply response. In prior cycles, fragmented competitors would add rigs aggressively at cycle peaks, destroying pricing. With Perenti now controlling the dominant share, the industry has moved from fragmented to consolidating. Capital discipline should be better this cycle -- Perenti has no incentive to flood the market. New rig delivery lead times of 6-12 months provide an additional buffer.

**Industry fleet utilization currently sits around 65-70%** (Mitchell reports 62 of 88 rigs operating). This is below mid-cycle but above trough. Full cycle peaks see 85-90%+ utilization.

## Coal: Structural Decline, Not Cyclical Weakness

Australian thermal coal exploration is in structural decline. Government forecasts project thermal coal export volumes declining 14Mt by FY2029-30. China expects thermal coal imports to drop 22% by 2025 and over one-third by 2030. Coal power plant utilization in China has fallen to ~50% and is forecast to halve again by 2050.

Coal exploration was ~6% of total Australian mineral exploration in 2023 (A$322M). For drilling contractors, coal provided steady, high-utilization production drilling work. Its decline permanently removes a base of demand. Contractors exposed to Queensland coal (Mitchell's home market) face headwinds that gold strength must overcome. This is not coming back.

## What a Full Cycle Upturn Looks Like

Based on the 2017-2022 experience and earlier cycles:

| Metric | Trough | Mid-Cycle | Peak |
|--------|--------|-----------|------|
| Fleet Utilization | 55-65% | 75-80% | 85-95% |
| EBITDA Margins | 10-14% | 17-20% | 22-28% |
| Day Rate Growth | Flat/declining | CPI + 2-3% | CPI + 5-10% |
| Revenue Growth | Negative | Mid-single digit | Double digit |

We are currently at the low end of this range, with utilization at ~70% and margins at ~20% (Mitchell's recent result). The margin resilience at low utilization reflects mix improvement toward higher-value underground and gold work.

## The One Industry Factor That Matters

**Gold exploration expenditure trajectory.** With gold now 40% of Australian mineral exploration spend and rising, the entire drilling cycle hinges on whether gold exploration continues to accelerate. The key inflection to watch is greenfield exploration -- currently still declining. When junior miners begin raising capital for new gold projects (not just near-mine extensions), that signals the broadening of the cycle that drives utilization from 70% toward 85%+. Monitor ABS quarterly exploration data by commodity and by type (greenfield vs. brownfield).

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**Consensus underestimates the structural supply tightening.** The market views drilling as a commodity service with easy entry. But the Perenti mega-consolidation (absorbing DDH1, Swick, Ausdrill, Ranger, and Strike into one entity) has fundamentally changed industry structure. Mitchell Services is now the largest independent -- not by choice but by default. In a demand upturn, the supply response will be slower than historical patterns suggest. Remaining independents have limited balance sheet capacity to rapidly expand fleets, and Perenti has rational incentive to maintain discipline. This is **bullish** for industry margins at mid-cycle and above.

The offsetting risk: coal's structural decline removes ~6-10% of industry demand permanently. If gold exploration plateaus before utilization tightens, the industry could get stuck in a "good but not great" equilibrium.
