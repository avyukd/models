# Mitchell Services (ASX:MSV) -- Financial Analysis

**Price: A$0.585 | Market Cap: ~A$123M | ~210M shares | Feb 24, 2026**

## Business Financial Profile

Contract drilling services -- high fixed-cost fleet model with significant operating leverage. Revenue is labor + equipment-intensive, largely variable with rig utilization, but the fleet carries substantial fixed depreciation. ~45% gold exposure, remainder coal/base metals/geotech. Cyclical, tied to mining capex cycles. Revenue quality is moderate: multi-year contracts but subject to volume variation and client-directed stand-downs.

FY2025 was the trough. Revenue fell 17% on coal weakness and project transitions. EBITDA declined ~36% -- demonstrating the operating leverage that cuts both ways. NPAT collapsed 94% to near-zero as depreciation and interest consumed almost all operating profit.

H1 FY2026 represents a sharp inflection: EBITDA of A$21.4M (up 69% HoH) on only 3% revenue growth. This is the operating leverage working in reverse -- modest utilization improvements driving outsized profit recovery. EBITDA margins expanded to 19.7% vs. the ~13% trough in FY25.

## Balance Sheet and Financial Risk

Net cash of A$7.2M at Dec 2025, a A$15.6M swing from A$8.4M net debt at June 2025. This is a clean balance sheet for a cyclical industrial. The company went through the trough year while still generating enough cash to deleverage meaningfully.

Fleet of 88 rigs. Book value likely A$60-80M (estimate). No refinancing risk apparent. The balance sheet provides capacity to increase fleet deployment or acquire during the upturn without equity dilution.

## Capital Allocation

The A$0.04/share dividend (A$8.4M total) represents a ~55-75% payout on normalized H1 earnings, which is aggressive but signaling management confidence. At 6.9% yield, this is meaningful capital return. Capex of A$17.5-18M appears to be maintenance-level given fleet size, suggesting most incremental revenue from utilization gains is free cash flow.

Loop Decarbonisation JV valued at ~A$24M by Sumitomo (25% stake acquisition). This is an option on a growth vertical that the market may not be pricing -- capital-light expansion funded by a strategic partner.

## Valuation Framework

At A$0.585, the market is pricing:
- ~8-11x normalized mid-cycle NPAT (A$11-15M)
- ~3.0-3.4x normalized mid-cycle EBITDA (A$36-42M)
- ~8-11% normalized FCF yield

For a cyclical services business with moderate growth, these multiples are reasonable-to-cheap. Comparable ASX drilling peers (DDH1 was acquired at ~4-5x EBITDA; Perenti trades at 3-4x). MSV at 3x mid-cycle EBITDA is at the low end of peer range.

**What must be true**: Gold exploration spend remains at or above current levels; fleet utilization sustains 65-70+ rigs; EBITDA margins hold 17%+. If utilization reaches 75+ rigs (still well below fleet capacity), earnings could overshoot -- A$18-20M NPAT is achievable at peak utilization with current fleet.

**What breaks it**: Gold price correction reducing exploration budgets; coal remains structurally impaired and those rigs become stranded; customer concentration risk (45% gold-linked).

## Key Financial Drivers

1. **Rig utilization rate** -- This is the single most important variable. With 88 rigs and 62 operating, every incremental rig deployed drops almost entirely to EBIT given the fixed cost structure. Moving from 62 to 75 rigs could nearly double NPAT.

2. **Gold exploration spending** -- 45% revenue exposure. Record gold prices are driving exploration budgets higher. This is the macro tailwind.

3. **EBITDA margin trajectory** -- Whether margins stabilize at 17-19% (mid-cycle) or push toward 20%+ (as H1 FY26 suggests) is the difference between a fairly valued and an undervalued stock.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

The H1 FY2026 result demonstrates that MSV has significant embedded operating leverage that is only beginning to manifest. The stock has tripled from its 52-week low of A$0.21, so the market has noticed the turnaround -- but the annualized H1 earnings run-rate (~A$16M NPAT) is already above what I estimate as mid-cycle. If this is early-cycle rather than mid-cycle, the stock is cheaper than it appears.

The specific non-obvious factor: **fleet capacity headroom**. With only 62 of 88 rigs operating (70% utilization), MSV has 30% spare capacity requiring minimal incremental capex to deploy. Each additional rig generates revenue with high incremental margins. If gold exploration demand continues accelerating, MSV can grow earnings substantially without capital raises or significant capex -- a dynamic that standard earnings-multiple analysis understates because it doesn't capture the convexity of the operating leverage.

The Loop Decarbonisation business (valued at A$24M by Sumitomo, ~20% of MSV market cap) is also likely receiving zero value in most analyses of the core drilling business.
