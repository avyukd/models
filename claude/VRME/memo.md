## 2. Key Drivers (The 1-3 Things That Matter)

**1. Carrier Dependency Is the Entire Business**
PeriShip is not a technology company. It is a reseller of FedEx/UPS services with a thin software layer. When FedEx terminated the relationship in August 2025, the business immediately deteriorated. The new UPS deal is a 3-year promotional rate agreement - temporary by definition. If UPS changes terms, the business model breaks. This is not a moat; it is a commercial arrangement that has already failed once.

**2. Revenue Trajectory Is Structurally Negative**
Revenue declined in 7 of 8 quarters YoY. Q1 2025 down 22%, Q2 2025 down 17%, Q3 2025 down 7%. This is not cyclical softness - it is customer attrition from contract losses. Management explicitly stated "not all customers will transition" to the new UPS partnership.

**3. The Merger Signals No Standalone Path**
VerifyMe signed a letter of intent to merge with Open World (a Web3/tokenization company) where VRME shareholders would own only 10% of the combined entity. If the standalone business had genuine prospects, why accept 90/10 dilution? Management is telling you the answer.

---

## 3. Thesis (Why This Is a Pass)

- **No durable competitive advantage exists.** The FedEx termination proved that carrier relationships are temporary and terminable without recourse. The same risk now exists with UPS.

- **Normalized earnings power is zero.** Adjusted EBITDA of ~$0.9M becomes breakeven-to-negative after SBC and maintenance capex. The business is not earning a return on capital.

- **Customer concentration creates binary risk.** Top 5% of customers generate 60%+ of Proactive revenue. Loss of 2-3 customers is existential. This has already happened multiple times in 2024-2025.

- **M&A track record is poor.** Trust Codes: acquired in 2023, divested for NZD 1 in 2024. PeriShip: $3.9M impairment in Q3 2025. Two deals in 18 months, both value-destructive.

- **The merger with Open World is the tell.** Management sees no viable standalone future and is diluting shareholders 90% into a speculative Web3 vehicle. This is not a strategic combination; it is an exit.

## 5. The Setup

VerifyMe operates two segments:

**Precision Logistics (PeriShip Global) - ~95% of revenue:** A logistics services intermediary for perishable goods (food, pharmaceuticals). The business monitors time-sensitive shipments and provides intervention services. It does NOT own trucks, warehouses, or logistics infrastructure. Value capture comes from the spread between what PeriShip charges customers and what it pays carriers. This spread depends entirely on negotiated carrier rates - a factor outside the company's control.

**Authentication - Commercially irrelevant:** Patented invisible ink technology and track-and-trace software. Per 10-K: "did not experience growth in 2024." Trust Codes Global divested for NZD 1 in December 2024. This segment has failed after years of investment.

**Industry dynamics:** The US cold chain logistics market is $91B+ and growing, but is dominated by scale players (Americold, Lineage Logistics, FedEx, UPS). The software/analytics layer includes well-funded players like FourKites and project44, which have raised hundreds of millions in VC and offer superior AI-powered, carrier-agnostic solutions. PeriShip is sub-scale and single-carrier-dependent in a consolidating market.

## 9. Positioning & Portfolio Logic

### Why This Does Not Deserve Capital Now

1. **No edge exists.** The "cheap" valuation is not mispriced - it reflects genuine business fragility. There is no variant perception that the market is missing.

2. **Binary outcome with negative expected value.** Either UPS works and the stock recovers modestly, or it doesn't and the business continues to deteriorate. The asymmetry is unfavorable: upside case is a subscale, no-moat business trading at fair value; downside case is further impairment and dilution.

3. **The merger complicates everything.** If the Open World merger proceeds, shareholders receive 10% of a Web3 entity with unclear prospects. This is not a thesis anyone can underwrite.

4. **Too many assumptions required.** To believe the stock works, you must assume: (a) UPS relationship is durable, (b) customers transition without defection, (c) margins hold during transition, (d) no dilutive financing needed, (e) management resists M&A temptation. Five assumptions, zero margin for error.

### What Would Move This Out of the "Too Hard" Pile

- **Evidence of revenue stabilization:** Two consecutive quarters of flat or positive YoY revenue
- **Customer additions disclosed:** Net new logos, not just retention
- **Margin expansion without carrier subsidy:** Gross margins sustained above 40% post-promotional period
- **Merger cancelled:** Demonstrates management sees standalone path
- **Insider buying at scale:** CEO's 95k share purchase in March 2025 was notable but small ($66k); would need 5-10x that to signal real conviction

## 10. Reconciling Conflicting Inputs

### Financial Analyst View
"Speculative / High Risk - Insufficient normalized earnings power to justify fundamental investment."

### Moat Analyst View
"Moat Strength: NONE. This is a structurally fragile business masquerading as a technology company."

### Capital Allocation Analyst View
"Insider alignment is genuine and unusual for a company of this size... different problems require different evaluations."

### Reconciliation

The capital allocation analyst correctly identifies that management alignment is real - the CEO has made 30 open-market purchases with zero sales, and board members took pay cuts. This is a genuine positive and unusual for a microcap.

However, the moat analyst's finding dominates: high alignment did not prevent bad acquisitions (Trust Codes sold for NZD 1), did not prevent customer concentration risk from manifesting, and did not prevent the FedEx relationship from terminating. Alignment prevents looting but does not fix structurally broken businesses.

The financial analyst's conclusion is correct: normalized earnings power is zero, and the investment is binary speculation on the UPS transition.

**Weighted conclusion:** All three analyses point the same direction. The positives (insider alignment, clean balance sheet, modest cash flow) are insufficient to offset the structural negatives (carrier dependency, customer concentration, no moat, declining revenue). This is a pass.

## 11. Summary

VerifyMe is a microcap logistics services reseller masquerading as a technology company. The core business depends on a single carrier relationship that has already failed once (FedEx) and is now unproven (UPS). Revenue has declined in 7 of 8 quarters. Normalized earnings are zero. M&A track record is poor. The announced merger with Open World signals management sees no standalone path.

Insider alignment is genuine but has not prevented value destruction. The "cheap" valuation at 0.25-0.35x EV/Revenue reflects genuine business fragility, not market inefficiency.

There is no variant perception here. The market is correctly pricing a structurally impaired business with binary risk. This is too hard to underwrite.

**Decision: Neutral (Too Hard)**
