# American Integrity Insurance Group (AII) -- Business Quality & Moat Analysis

## Business Model Summary

AII is a Florida-focused residential property insurer writing $885M in gross premiums earned across 421,866 policies. Revenue comes from net premiums retained after ceding roughly 73% of gross premiums to reinsurers ($642M ceded in FY2025). The company distributes through 1,000+ independent agents. Cost structure is heavily variable: reinsurance is the largest expense, followed by loss costs and agent commissions. AII reduced its quota share from 40% to 25% in 2025, retaining more premium and risk. Net investment income ($21.7M) is a secondary but growing revenue stream post-IPO capital infusion.

The business model is straightforward: underwrite Florida residential risk, buy catastrophe protection, and keep the spread. Profit accrues from underwriting discipline, not from investment leverage or fee income.

## Source(s) of Advantage

**Proprietary Loss Data (19 Years)**: AII's longest-standing advantage is granular loss data accumulated over 19 years of writing Florida residential business. This informs risk selection and pricing at the individual property level. Competitors entering Florida face an adverse selection problem -- they lack the claims history to distinguish good risks from bad. However, this advantage is shared by other incumbents (UVE, HCI, Heritage) and erodes as third-party data vendors improve.

**Agent Relationships as Controlled Distribution**: AII selectively appoints agents believed capable of producing profitable business. This is not a switching cost -- agents represent multiple carriers simultaneously. The mechanism is softer: AII provides fast quoting, consistent market presence, and reliable capacity. In Florida's volatile market, where carriers regularly enter and exit, *reliability itself* becomes valuable. Agents route business toward carriers they trust to renew policies, not just whoever offers the lowest rate. This is a weak but real advantage.

**Regulatory Familiarity**: Florida's property insurance market is uniquely complex -- OIR rate approvals, Citizens depopulation mechanics, assignment-of-benefits reforms, reinsurance compliance. AII's institutional knowledge of this regulatory environment creates friction for out-of-state entrants. National carriers have largely avoided Florida for years due to this complexity combined with cat exposure.

**Scale Within a Niche**: As 3rd largest Florida residential writer (ex-Citizens/nationals), AII has purchasing power in reinsurance markets. Its $1.97B cat tower for a single event reflects scale that smaller Florida domestics cannot replicate cost-effectively.

None of these advantages are individually decisive. Together, they create moderate friction against new entry but do not prevent competition from established Florida peers.

## Competitive Landscape

The Florida residential property market is moderately concentrated among domestic specialists. Key competitors:

- **UVE**: Largest pure-play Florida domestic; FY2025 combined ratio ~87.5% (per Q4 2025 earnings). More established but less profitable than AII currently.
- **HCI Group**: Technology-forward; targets sub-70% combined ratio. Spinning off its Exzeo tech platform. Most direct comp on underwriting quality.
- **Heritage (HRTG)**: Resumed writing new policies Aug 2024; combined ratio ~94% in 2024, improving. Weaker underwriting history.
- **FNHC**: Went through rehabilitation; not a meaningful current competitor.

Differentiation among these players is limited. All write the same Florida homeowners peril, buy from overlapping reinsurance panels, and distribute through many of the same independent agents. The primary differentiator is underwriting discipline -- which is a management quality, not a structural moat.

Price competition is re-emerging. Multiple Florida carriers filed for rate decreases in late 2025, and Citizens recommended rate cuts. As the market normalizes post-reform, the favorable pricing environment that produced AII's 63.7% combined ratio will compress.

## Customer Behavior & Revenue Quality

**Switching costs are near-zero for policyholders.** Homeowners insurance is an annual contract. Switching requires only a new quote and a phone call to an agent. There is no lock-in, no data portability issue, no integration cost. Retention is driven by inertia and agent recommendation, not structural barriers.

**Growth sources are shifting favorably.** Q4 2025 voluntary policies written: 86,818 (+16.9% YoY). Citizens take-out: only 7,972 (down from 68,198 in Q4 2024). This is critical -- AII is transitioning from depopulation-driven growth to organic competitive wins. Depopulation is a one-time tailwind now largely exhausted (Citizens down to ~385K policies from 936K at start of 2025).

**Customer concentration risk is low** -- 421K policies, no single policyholder material. Revenue concentration is geographic, not customer-based.

## Evidence of Moat in the Financials

FY2025 combined ratio of 63.7% is exceptionally strong but misleading as a baseline. Q1 2025 was 42.9% (no storms, plus large Citizens take-out bolus with favorable loss characteristics). Q3 was 78.9%. The annual figure benefits from a benign hurricane season. One active season could push the combined ratio above 100%.

The quota share reduction (40% to 25%) mechanically improved net profitability by retaining more premium. This is a capital deployment decision enabled by IPO proceeds, not an operating improvement.

ROE of 39.9% reflects a period of favorable loss experience, reduced cessions, and policy growth. This is not a sustainable run-rate -- it reflects the best possible confluence of factors.

Historical margin stability cannot be assessed from one year of public financials. This is a significant analytical limitation.

## Fragility Analysis

**Hurricane concentration is the existential risk.** AII writes almost exclusively in Florida. A major hurricane hitting the I-4 corridor or Tri-county region would test the reinsurance tower and potentially impair capital. The $1.97B cat tower provides coverage, but reinsurance is priced annually -- a bad loss year dramatically increases future reinsurance costs, compressing margins for multiple subsequent years.

**Tri-county expansion increases tail risk.** Miami-Dade, Broward, and Palm Beach are the highest cat-exposure zones in the continental US. AII exited these markets a decade ago for good reason. Re-entering now, while reinsurance is available and affordable, is a bet that the favorable conditions persist. This is pro-cyclical risk-taking.

**Reinsurance dependency is structural.** AII cedes 73% of gross premiums. A hard reinsurance market (triggered by a major global cat event) would compress margins immediately. AII has no control over reinsurance pricing.

**Competitive convergence.** With Citizens depopulation largely complete, all Florida domestics are now competing for the same organic growth pool. Rate decreases are already being filed. The supernormal profitability of 2025 is a function of the cycle, not structure.

## Moat & Durability Classification

**Moat Strength: Weak.** AII operates in a commodity insurance market with near-zero customer switching costs, limited product differentiation, and cyclical profitability. Its advantages (data, agent relationships, regulatory knowledge) create friction but not durable excess returns. Competitors HCI and UVE have identical structural positions.

**Earnings Durability: Low.** Current earnings reflect a benign cat year, favorable reinsurance pricing, a one-time Citizens depopulation tailwind, and quota share optimization from fresh IPO capital. Multiple factors would need to persist for current profitability to be sustained. One major hurricane resets the entire picture.

## The One Thing That Matters for Durability

**Hurricane frequency and severity in Florida.** Every other factor -- agent relationships, technology, underwriting discipline -- is secondary to whether Florida experiences major cat losses. A bad hurricane season simultaneously increases AII's direct losses, raises reinsurance costs for years, and can trigger regulatory intervention on rates. AII's competitive position relative to peers matters far less than its absolute exposure to Florida cat risk. This is a business where the most important variable is entirely outside management's control.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**Bearish insight**: The market is likely anchoring on AII's 63.7% combined ratio and 39.9% ROE as evidence of underwriting excellence. The non-obvious point is that *the Citizens depopulation wave handed AII a book of pre-selected, rate-adequate policies with favorable loss characteristics*. As this tailwind fades (Citizens take-outs collapsed from 68K to 8K per quarter), AII must now compete organically in a market where peers are filing rate decreases. The transition from "harvesting Citizens" to "competing for market share" is a fundamentally different and harder game, and the Tri-county re-entry is the clearest expression of the pressure to find growth as the easy growth disappears.
