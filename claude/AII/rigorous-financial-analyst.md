# American Integrity Insurance (AII) -- Financial Analysis

**Date:** 2026-02-24 | **Price:** ~$18.10 | **Market Cap:** ~$354M | **Trailing P/E:** 3.2x | **FY25 EPS:** $5.65

## Business Financial Profile

Florida-focused residential property insurer. 7th largest Florida writer, 3rd excluding Citizens and nationals. 421,866 policies in-force. Capital-light model heavily reliant on quota share and cat XOL reinsurance -- the company cedes ~72% of gross premiums earned ($642M ceded vs $885M gross in FY25). Revenue is recurring (annual policy renewals) but highly exposed to catastrophe risk and Florida regulatory/legal environment. Fixed cost base creates significant operating leverage on retained premium.

The combined ratio improvement is stunning but requires decomposition. Two drivers: (1) zero catastrophe losses in FY2025 -- Florida had essentially no significant hurricane activity, and (2) quota share reduction from 40% to 25% increased retained premium, spreading fixed expenses over a larger base (expense ratio down 8pts). Prior year reserve development was only $(1.8)M favorable -- minimal distortion.

**SBC:** Not explicitly broken out in earnings release. As a recent IPO, this will become material. Must monitor in 10-K.

## Earnings Quality & Combined Ratio Sustainability

The 63.7% combined ratio is NOT sustainable. This is a peak-cycle result driven by an anomalously benign hurricane year. Florida's long-run average cat load is substantial.

**Building a normalized combined ratio:**
- FY2025 loss ratio of 38.7% included zero cat losses. A normal cat year for a Florida-focused writer adds 10-20pts to the loss ratio. Using 12-15pts as a mid-cycle cat load assumption.
- Underlying non-cat loss ratio: ~38.7% (FY2025 was essentially attritional-only)
- Normalized loss ratio: 38.7% + 13pts cat load = ~52%
- Normalized expense ratio: 25% is likely representative post-quota-share reduction
- **Normalized combined ratio: ~77%**

This compares to UVE's FY2025 combined ratio of 94.1% (also a benign cat year), suggesting AII runs structurally leaner. However, AII cedes far more premium (72% vs UVE's ~32%), so its net results are more leveraged to reinsurance terms.

## Quota Share Reduction Impact

The reduction from 40% to 25% is the single most important earnings driver going forward. On ~$885M gross premiums earned:
- Old retention: 60% = $531M net before cat reinsurance
- New retention: 75% = $664M net before cat reinsurance
- Incremental retained premium: ~$133M
- At a 77% normalized combined ratio, incremental underwriting profit: ~$31M pre-tax
- After-tax (~21% rate): ~$24M incremental earnings

This alone represents ~25% earnings uplift on the current base, before any policy growth. The offset: greater catastrophe exposure on the retained book. One bad hurricane season now hits the P&L harder.

## Balance Sheet & Capital Position

- Cash & Investments: $348.6M total investments + significant cash
- Total Equity: $337.0M (book value ~$17.30/share)
- Debt: $721K -- essentially zero leverage
- Cat Reinsurance Limit: $2.59B (increased 45% YoY)

The balance sheet is a fortress. IPO proceeds of $93M net sit largely undeployed. Price/book is ~1.05x. For a company earning 40% ROE, trading at book value is optically cheap -- but the ROE is peak-cycle.

## Key Financial Drivers

1. **Hurricane season outcomes** -- This is fundamentally a binary bet on weather. A single major Florida landfall can produce a 30-40pt combined ratio swing. Everything else is secondary.

2. **Retained premium via quota share structure** -- The 40% to 25% reduction is a structural earnings step-up. Any further reduction directly flows to the bottom line. Conversely, if reinsurance markets harden, they may need to increase cessions.

3. **Policy growth trajectory** -- 18.5% growth is exceptional but Citizens depop-driven. Sustainable organic growth of 8-12% in a hardened Florida market is more realistic going forward.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**The trailing P/E of 3.2x is misleading in both directions.** Bulls see $5.65 EPS and a 3.2x multiple and think it's absurdly cheap. Bears know a bad cat year zeros out earnings. Both are right.

The non-obvious insight: **the quota share reduction creates a permanent earnings step-up that the market may be underpricing.** The shift from 40% to 25% cession is structural, not cyclical. Even in a normalized cat environment, AII should earn ~$3.85/share -- implying a 4.7x normalized P/E with zero debt and 18% policy growth. For context, UVE trades at 7.4x trailing in a benign cat year with slower growth. If AII re-rated to even 6x normalized earnings, the stock would be ~$23 (27% upside).

The risk that consensus may be UNDERpricing: AII retains more risk now. The next major hurricane will test whether the market has correctly priced the asymmetric downside of higher retention.

**Valuation framework:** At $18.10, the market implies normalized earnings of ~$2.60/share at a 7x P/E (peer average), or ~$3.60 at a 5x P/E (catastrophe discount). My normalized estimate of $3.85 suggests the stock is modestly cheap even with a cat-risk discount, but not egregiously so. The margin of safety comes from the balance sheet -- $337M equity with no debt provides significant catastrophe absorption capacity.
