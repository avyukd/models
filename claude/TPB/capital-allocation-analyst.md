**Date:** February 8, 2026
**Stock Price:** ~$130 | **Market Cap:** ~$2.5B | **Shares Outstanding:** ~19.1M

This is a company where the largest owner *is* the boardroom.

## 2. Incentive Structure & Alignment

### CEO Compensation (Graham Purdy)

| Component | 2023 | 2024 |
|-----------|------|------|
| Total Compensation | $3.0M | ~$3.0M |
| Base Salary | $750K (25.5%) | $750K |
| Equity + Bonus | ~$2.25M (74.5%) | ~$2.25M |

**Per 2024 proxy / employment agreement:** Purdy's base is $750K with eligibility for annual bonuses and equity awards with minimum annual grant date value of $500K.

### Performance Metrics

- **Annual Incentive:** Adjusted EBITDA targets. Per the 2022 proxy, the Compensation Committee sets EBITDA bonus goals and has "never altered or changed" them once set. In FY 2022, TPB hit >maximum EBITDA goal, resulting in 200% payout.
- **Long-Term Incentive:** Performance-based RSUs tied to net sales and Adjusted EBITDA over multi-year periods.
- **No TSR component.** None of TPB's long-term compensation is subject to any total shareholder return test (per shareholder criticism cited in proxy materials).

### What Behavior Does This Encourage?

The compensation structure rewards **EBITDA growth, not returns on capital or per-share value creation.** This is a critical distinction for a company actively issuing equity via ATM and investing heavily in FRE. Management gets paid for growing the absolute EBITDA number, regardless of how many shares were issued to get there. The absence of any TSR or ROIC metric means management bears no direct compensation penalty for dilutive growth.

### Who Benefits If the Stock Underperforms?

With a 45% controlling shareholder and insiders using forward financing arrangements (effectively monetizing shares without selling), insiders have partially derisked their downside while maintaining ownership optics. Purdy's $12.3M forward financing pledging 140K of his 227K shares means **62% of his stake is economically hedged** -- he keeps upside but has locked in a floor. This is not the behavior profile of a concentrated, aligned owner.

*Note: Precise annual SBC figures not available from press releases alone. Q2 2024 SBC was $1.9M vs $2.1M prior year. Q4 2023 SBC was $1.9M vs $1.2M. Annualized run rate appears to be $7-8M.*

**SBC as a percentage of revenue is low** (~2%), which is good for a small-cap. SBC as a percentage of FCF is moderate and rising due to declining FCF in 2024. This is not a red flag in isolation.

**However, the real dilution story is the ATM program, not SBC.** TPB issued ~$100M of equity at ~$98.59/share in 2025 (roughly 1.0M new shares, or ~5.5% dilution from the ATM alone). The company has expanded ATM capacity to $200M. Share count currently at 19.1M is up from ~17.7M in mid-2024. **The dilution risk at TPB comes from deliberate equity issuance, not creeping SBC.**

## 4. Capital Allocation Track Record

### a) Reinvestment: FRE/Modern Oral

This is the dominant capital allocation decision at TPB right now.

| Metric | FY 2024 | Q3 2025 LTM Est. |
|--------|---------|-----------------|
| Modern Oral Revenue | ~$16M (est.) | $125-130M guided |
| SG&A Growth (Q4 2025 YoY) | -- | +50.5% to $44.5M |
| Modern Oral as % of Revenue | ~4-5% | ~31% |

FRE/ALP pouch sales grew **628% YoY** in Q3 2025 to $36.7M. FRE is in 14,000 stores. Revenue guidance raised multiple times. SG&A is surging to support this (+38-50% YoY increases).

**Assessment:** The reinvestment into Modern Oral appears to be working at the top line. The question is whether spending discipline holds or whether growth-at-all-costs mentality takes over. The ATM issuance was explicitly tied to funding "high return opportunities to accelerate growth of Modern Oral." Management is issuing equity at $98/share to fund marketing and distribution expansion in nicotine pouches -- a market where ZYN (Swedish Match / Philip Morris) dominates and regulatory risk is elevated.

### b) M&A History

| Deal | Year | Price | Outcome |
|------|------|-------|---------|
| Zig-Zag NA license | 1997 | N/A (license) | Core brand, excellent long-term outcome |
| Zig-Zag full ownership | ~2020-2021 | Not fully disclosed | Consolidated ownership of crown jewel asset |
| Durfort Holdings (cigar wraps) | 2020 | $46M ($36M cash + $10M note) | Guided +$5M revenue, +$7M EBITDA -- if accurate, paid <7x EBITDA |
| Unitabac (cigar portfolio) | 2021 | Undisclosed | Cigarillo bolt-on |
| dosist (cannabis) | 2020 | $15M convertible | Cannabis bet; dosist appears to have struggled |
| Old Pal (cannabis) | 2021 | $8M convertible note | Cannabis lifestyle brand; uncertain outcome |

**Assessment:** The core tobacco M&A (Durfort, Unitabac, Zig-Zag consolidation) was disciplined and strategically sound -- buying adjacent brands at reasonable multiples to consolidate a niche. The cannabis investments ($23M+ in dosist, Old Pal, CASH) were a different animal: speculative venture bets that appear to have generated minimal or negative returns. Management deserves credit for keeping these bets small relative to enterprise value, but they were still capital misallocated into a sector where TPB had no edge. The CDS divestiture cleaned up the portfolio at a loss.

**Pattern:** Good at bolt-on tobacco deals. Poor at adjacent-category ventures.

But the framing matters: management presents buybacks and ATM as parallel capital allocation tools, when in reality the net effect is dilution. The $200M expanded ATM authorization signals more equity issuance ahead.

## 5. Learning vs. Repetition

**Mistakes corrected:**
- Cannabis investments (dosist, Old Pal) were small and appear to have been written down or abandoned. Management did not double down.
- CDS segment was divested, simplifying the business. Management recognized it was non-core.
- Debt refinancing was proactive (extended maturity well ahead of 2026 wall).

**Concerns about repetition:**
- The "growth investment" playbook has shifted from small cannabis bets to a much larger bet on FRE/Modern Oral. The dollar amounts are an order of magnitude larger ($100M+ ATM, 50% SG&A increases vs. $23M in cannabis).
- Management's instinct when it sees an opportunity is to issue equity and spend aggressively. The question is whether FRE is dosist-at-scale or Zig-Zag 2.0.
- Insiders hedging their positions (forward financing) while the company issues equity to fund growth is a behavioral mismatch.

## 6. Forward-Looking Capital Allocation Risk

**Where capital is most likely to be misallocated:**

1. **FRE/ALP overspend.** The $200M ATM authorization signals management may issue substantially more equity. If Modern Oral growth decelerates, the company will have diluted shareholders to fund SG&A that generated negative returns. SG&A is already up 50% YoY -- this spending rate cannot persist indefinitely without margin pressure.

2. **M&A risk re-emerges.** With FRE generating momentum, management may seek to acquire distribution assets, competing pouch brands, or adjacent nicotine delivery companies. Incentives rewarding EBITDA growth (not ROIC) create bias toward deals.

3. **Insider monetization.** Both the CEO and Executive Chairman have used forward financing arrangements to extract value from their stakes. If the stock declines below their financing strike prices ($91-$126 range), they face margin-call-like dynamics. This creates personal incentive to support the stock price through narrative management rather than capital discipline.

---

## The One Thing That Determines Value Creation

**The single most important factor is whether FRE/Modern Oral justifies the equity issuance required to fund it.**

TPB has issued ~$100M of equity and authorized another $200M, while simultaneously growing SG&A by 50% to support pouch distribution. If Modern Oral becomes a durable, high-margin segment generating $150M+ in revenue at 20%+ EBITDA margins, the dilution will have been value-creative. If pouch growth stalls due to regulatory action (FDA), competitive response (ZYN price cuts), or market saturation, shareholders will have absorbed 8-15% dilution to fund operating losses in a segment that never reached profitability.

This dominates all other considerations because: (1) it is the primary use of incremental capital, (2) the equity issuance is the largest single change to shareholder economics, and (3) the compensation structure actively rewards this behavior regardless of outcome.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**Consensus view:** TPB is a "great capital allocator" with an aligned controlling shareholder and a transformative growth opportunity in Modern Oral/FRE. The stock's move from $51 to $130 in roughly one year reflects enthusiasm for this narrative.

**What consensus may underweight:**

1. **The CEO has economically hedged 62% of his stake.** Purdy's $12.3M forward financing secured by 140K of his 227K shares means his downside exposure is far less than headline ownership suggests. Executive Chairman Glazek has similar arrangements. The "insider alignment" story is weaker than it appears.

2. **No compensation metric penalizes dilution.** Adjusted EBITDA targets reward absolute growth. Management can issue unlimited equity, spend it on SG&A, grow EBITDA, hit their bonus, and shareholders eat the dilution. There is no TSR gate, no per-share earnings metric, and no ROIC hurdle. This is a structural flaw, not a nitpick.

3. **The ATM is the real capital return story, not the buyback.** TPB authorized $100M in buybacks and $200M in ATM issuance. The buyback program has spent $5.1M. The ATM has already deployed ~$100M. Follow the actual capital flows, not the press releases.

4. **Standard Diversified's 45% stake is a governance ceiling.** Minority shareholders cannot effect change. Board composition, compensation design, and capital allocation priorities are set by the controlling holder. Whether this is a feature or a bug depends entirely on whether you trust David Glazek's judgment -- and he just sold $3.3M of stock while the company was issuing equity to fund growth.
