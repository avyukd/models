# Industry Structure & Cycle Analysis: TPB-Relevant Industries

**Date:** February 8, 2026
**TPB Stock Price:** ~$130 (as of Feb 6, 2026 close: $129.97; 52-week range $51.48-$129.35; market cap ~$2.3B)

---

## Executive Summary

TPB straddles three distinct industry sub-segments with sharply divergent structural trajectories. The analytical challenge -- and where consensus likely errs -- is that the market is pricing TPB as a nicotine pouch growth story (NewGen/FRE), while underappreciating both the durability of Zig-Zag's economics and the accelerating structural decline in MST. The critical industry question is whether nicotine pouches will follow a winner-take-most dynamic (bad for FRE) or sustain multi-brand shelf space (good for FRE). The PMTA regulatory moat is real but narrowing as the FDA fast-tracks authorizations.

## I. Rolling Papers, Wraps & Cones (Zig-Zag Segment)

### Industry Structure

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| US Market Size | ~$220M (US share of $790M global, 2025) |
| Growth Rate | 4-5% CAGR (2026-2033) |
| Structure | Oligopoly: Zig-Zag/OCB (Republic Technologies) ~40-45% share, RAW (HBI) ~15%, long tail of small brands |
| Zig-Zag Paper Share | ~43% of US cigarette paper market; ~80% of flavored wraps |
| Key Growth Driver | Cannabis pre-roll adoption, state-level legalization expansion |

### Structural Assessment: **Favorable -- Stable Oligopoly with Secular Tailwinds**

This is TPB's most misunderstood segment from an industry perspective. Key dynamics:

- **Duopoly pricing power.** Zig-Zag and RAW collectively control ~55-60% of the US market. Barriers to entry are high (brand loyalty in a habitual-use product, massive retail distribution, and the cost of building c-store/head-shop shelf space). This is a razor/razorblade model: low unit costs, high repurchase, minimal switching.

- **Cannabis is a structural tailwind, not a cycle.** Schedule III reclassification (Dec 2025 executive order) and continued state-level legalization expand the addressable market. Pre-rolls are the fastest-growing cannabis format, directly driving demand for papers, cones, and wraps. This is not dependent on federal legalization -- state-by-state expansion continues.

- **Low capital intensity.** Paper/wrap manufacturing is not capacity-constrained. Margins are driven by brand and distribution, not supply scarcity.

- **Mid-cycle normal:** Low-to-mid single digit volume growth, stable pricing, operating margins in the 35-45% range for leading brands. The industry rarely experiences true cyclicality -- demand is tied to habitual consumption, not economic cycles.

### Cycle Position: **Not cyclical -- steady-state secular grower**

This industry does not have meaningful cyclicality. Demand is inelastic (habitual use, low absolute cost). The relevant risk is regulatory (wraps/flavored paper bans), not economic.

## II. Moist Smokeless Tobacco / Chewing Tobacco (Stoker's Segment)

### Industry Structure

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| US MST Market Size | ~$3.4B (2025) |
| Volume Trend | Declining 5-8% annually (accelerating from ~2% historical decline) |
| Structure | Highly concentrated: Altria (Copenhagen/Skoal) + Reynolds (Grizzly) = ~85-90% share |
| Stoker's Position | Value/discount tier; #2 in chewing tobacco; fast-growing in MST (small base) |
| Margin Structure | Industry operating margins 60-70% for leaders (Altria oral segment: 68.7% in 2025) |

### Structural Assessment: **Secular Decline -- but Managed Decline with Pricing Power**

This is the critical nuance consensus may be getting wrong on the bearish side:

- **Volume decline is accelerating.** Copenhagen volumes -7.7%, Skoal -8.8% in Q2 2025. The historical ~2% annual decline has tripled. Nicotine pouches are the direct cannibalization vector. This is not reversible.

- **But price-over-volume works.** The tobacco industry's proven playbook: raise prices faster than volumes decline. Altria has consistently grown oral tobacco revenue despite volume declines. Industry pricing discipline is extremely strong in this oligopoly.

- **Stoker's occupies a unique position.** As a value/discount brand, Stoker's actually benefits from trade-down dynamics when premium brands (Copenhagen, Skoal) raise prices aggressively. The value tier often gains share during periods of aggressive premium pricing.

- **The endgame question.** MST is not going to zero in the next decade, but it is in structural decline. The rate of decline depends on the pace of nicotine pouch adoption and whether MST users (who skew older, rural, price-sensitive) switch at the same rate as younger users.

- **Mid-cycle normal:** For the MST industry broadly, mid-cycle means flat-to-declining volumes offset by 4-6% pricing, delivering low-single-digit revenue growth and stable-to-expanding margins. For Stoker's specifically, its share gains within a declining category could deliver above-market growth for several more years.

### Cycle Position: **Structural decline, early-to-mid stage**

MST is not cyclical -- it is in secular decline. The "cycle" is how fast it declines. Current decline rate (~5-8% volume annually) is above the long-term structural trend, partly because nicotine pouch supply constraints (ZYN shortage) have now resolved with PMI's capacity additions.

## III. Modern Oral Nicotine Pouches (NewGen/FRE Segment)

### Industry Structure

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| US Market Size | ~$4-5B (2024-2025, growing rapidly) |
| Growth Rate | ~30% CAGR (2025-2030); monthly sales up 207% from Jan 2023 to Apr 2025 |
| Structure | Dominant leader + challengers: ZYN ~55-65% share, On! (Altria) ~10-15%, VELO (BAT) ~5-8%, Rogue ~3-5%, FRE (TPB) <5% |
| ZYN 2025 Sales | >$3.24B |
| FRE Traction | In 14,000 stores; $36.7M quarterly sales (Q3 2025), up 628% YoY |
| PMTA Status | ZYN authorized (Jan 2025, 20 products); On! PLUS authorized (Dec 2025, 6 products); FRE still pending |

### Structural Assessment: **Hypergrowth Phase of a New Category -- Critical Question is Market Structure at Maturity**

This is where the most important industry analysis lies for TPB:

**Supply dynamics are shifting dramatically:**
- ZYN shortage (2024) is over. PMI invested $830M+ in capacity expansion (Owensboro KY expansion + $600M Aurora CO facility, regular production starting 2026). ZYN shipped 202M cans in Q1 2025 vs 132M in Q1 2024 -- a 53% increase.
- This is the single most important industry development. The ZYN shortage temporarily created shelf space for challengers like FRE. As ZYN supply normalizes and the Colorado facility ramps, the competitive intensity will increase sharply.

**Regulatory moat is real but narrowing:**
- PMTA authorization creates a meaningful barrier to entry. Only ZYN and On! PLUS have marketing granted orders as of early 2026.
- FDA pilot program is accelerating reviews. FRE is in the pilot program. If authorized, it gains legitimacy and potentially locks out non-PMTA brands. If denied, it faces existential risk.
- State-level taxes on nicotine pouches are expanding rapidly (10 states passed new taxes in 2025; WA imposing 95% wholesale tax in 2026; GA raising to 75%). This creates headwinds but also barriers for subscale players.

**Competitive dynamics at maturity:**
- The key industry question: does this market consolidate toward ZYN dominance (like Copenhagen in MST) or sustain 4-5 viable brands (like energy drinks)?
- ZYN's first-mover advantage, FDA authorization, and PMI's distribution muscle suggest the former. But: category growth of 30%+ means there is room for challengers to grow even as ZYN maintains dominance.
- Online data (Nicokick) shows Rogue growing 136% and On! growing 77% even as ZYN declined 8% on that platform -- suggesting brand experimentation is ongoing.

**Mid-cycle definition:** This category has no "mid-cycle" yet -- it is pre-maturity. A hypothetical mid-cycle state (perhaps 2030+) would feature: category growth slowing to single digits, ZYN with 50-60% share, 2-3 authorized challengers with 10-15% each, stable pricing, operating margins of 25-35% for scaled players.

### Cycle Position: **Pre-maturity hypergrowth -- supply normalization phase**

The category is transitioning from supply-constrained growth (which benefited challengers) to supply-abundant growth (which will test challenger stickiness). This is the most important inflection point.

## The One Industry Factor That Matters

**ZYN supply normalization is the single most important variable across all three of TPB's industry exposures.**

- For Zig-Zag: Irrelevant (different market)
- For Stoker's: Accelerates MST volume decline as ZYN availability improves (bearish for MST volumes but offset by pricing)
- For NewGen/FRE: Directly threatens FRE's growth trajectory. The 2024 ZYN shortage created a window for FRE to gain shelf space and trial. With PMI's $830M+ capacity investment coming fully online in 2026, ZYN will be abundantly available everywhere. FRE's ability to retain and grow its position against a fully-supplied ZYN is the central competitive question.

**How to monitor:** PMI quarterly ZYN shipment data (reported each quarter). Watch for shipments stabilizing above 200M cans/quarter -- that signals full supply normalization. Also watch NielsenIQ c-store scanner data for pouch category share by brand.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**Consensus view:** TPB is a nicotine pouch growth story. FRE's rapid growth proves the brand can compete. The market is so large and growing so fast that multiple brands will thrive.

**What consensus may underappreciate (BEARISH for industry newcomers):**

1. **The ZYN shortage was a one-time gift.** FRE's 628% YoY growth occurred during a period when the dominant brand literally could not supply the market. PMI's capacity is about to roughly double. The competitive environment FRE faces in 2026-2027 will be categorically different from 2024-2025. Historical analogy: many challenger brands in energy drinks, hard seltzer, and e-cigarettes grew rapidly during category booms only to see share erode once the dominant player invested in distribution and availability.

2. **PMTA is simultaneously a moat and a sword.** If FRE gets FDA authorization, it joins a small club of authorized products and gains a durable competitive position. If it doesn't, or if authorization comes with restrictions, the brand faces existential headwinds. The binary nature of this outcome is not well-reflected in a smooth growth extrapolation.

3. **State nicotine pouch taxes are coming fast.** Ten states in 2025 alone. Washington's 95% wholesale tax and Georgia's 75% wholesale tax are punitive. These taxes compress margins for all players but disproportionately hurt subscale brands with less pricing power. This is a structural headwind for the category that the 30% CAGR projections may not fully incorporate.

**What consensus may underappreciate (BULLISH for Zig-Zag):**

The rolling papers/wraps business is the most defensible of TPB's three segments and gets the least analytical attention. It operates in a stable oligopoly with 40%+ market share, benefits from cannabis tailwinds that are structural (not cyclical), has minimal capital requirements, and faces no meaningful substitution threat. Schedule III cannabis reclassification is a genuine positive catalyst that expands the addressable market. This segment alone likely justifies a significant portion of TPB's current enterprise value, yet most TPB analysis focuses on FRE.

Current Position:      Structural decline -- early-to-mid stage (accelerating)
Mid-Cycle Definition:  Flat-to-declining volumes, 4-6% annual pricing, stable margins

```
Industry Structure:    Favorable for category, Uncertain for challengers (ZYN dominance + supply normalization)
Cyclicality:           N/A (pre-maturity growth phase)
Current Position:      Hypergrowth transitioning from supply-constrained to supply-abundant
Mid-Cycle Definition:  Does not yet exist; hypothetical: single-digit growth, 50-60% ZYN share, 25-35% margins
Primary Industry Risk: ZYN supply normalization compresses challenger share; PMTA denial; state tax escalation
```

### Composite View for TPB-Relevant Industries
```
Industry Structure:    Mixed -- one favorable, one declining, one uncertain
Cyclicality:           Low (tobacco/nicotine industries are structurally driven, not cyclical)
Current Position:      Inflection point -- ZYN supply normalization is the key transition
Mid-Cycle Definition:  Varies by segment (see above)
Primary Industry Risk: FRE fails to retain share gains made during the ZYN shortage window
```
