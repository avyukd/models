# LandBridge Company LLC (LB): Capital Allocation & Management Quality Analysis

## Executive Summary

LandBridge presents a complex case study in sponsor-backed public company dynamics. Five Point Energy controls LB through a dual-class structure with ~50M Class B shares (no economic rights, full voting rights) versus ~25M public Class A shares. The sponsor has systematically converted Class B to Class A and sold into the public market, raising over $300M since the June 2024 IPO. The central question: is this aligned PE monetization or value extraction at public shareholders' expense?

**Corporate Structure:**
- LandBridge Company LLC is a holding company; operating assets held by DBR Land Holdings LLC (OpCo)
- Up-C structure: Class A shares trade publicly; Class B shares held by LandBridge Holdings LLC (sponsor vehicle)
- Class B shares have no economic rights but carry 1-for-1 voting rights
- OpCo Units + Class B shares can be exchanged for Class A shares or cash at LB's election
- Five Point has contractual rights to designate majority of board

**Key Governance Facts:**
- Board composition controlled by Five Point through shareholder agreement
- Deloitte is auditor but has NOT audited internal controls over financial reporting (emerging growth company exemption)
- Company formed September 2023; IPO June 2024 (less than 1 year from formation to public markets)

## 2. Incentive Structure & Alignment

### Compensation Design

Per 2024 proxy, Named Executive Officers received:
- **Base Salary:** $0 (paid through Shared Services Agreement with WaterBridge)
- **Bonus:** $800K-$850K cash
- **Equity:** ~$10M+ per executive (Incentive Units + RSUs under LTIP)

The Shared Services Agreement is critical: LandBridge does not directly employ its executives. Management is provided by WaterBridge affiliates. Any cash expense for management incentive units is borne by LandBridge Holdings LLC (the sponsor vehicle), not the public company.

### What Behavior Does This Encourage?

1. **Dual-loyalty problem:** Executives serve both LB (public) and WaterBridge (private), with compensation flowing through WaterBridge affiliates
2. **Incentive Units tied to sponsor returns:** Management's largest equity awards are linked to returns received by investors in LandBridge Holdings LLC, not public shareholders
3. **No disclosed clawback or performance hurdles tied to public shareholder returns:** Compensation appears to reward transaction completion and EBITDA growth, not ROIC or capital efficiency

### Who Benefits If Stock Underperforms?

Five Point and management benefit from:
- Continued fees through Shared Services Agreement
- Incentive Units vesting regardless of public share price (tied to sponsor IRR thresholds)
- Related-party revenue flows from WaterBridge regardless of stock performance

**Assessment:** Management is economically aligned with Five Point's exit timeline, not with long-term public shareholders.

## 3. Stock-Based Compensation Discipline

### SBC as Reported

| Period | SBC Expense | Revenue | SBC % of Revenue |
|--------|-------------|---------|------------------|
| Q3 2024 | $11.6M (non-cash) | $28.5M | 41% |
| Q2 2025 | $11.3M (non-cash) | Est. $44M | ~26% |
| FY2024 | Drove net loss | $110M | Material |

**Critical Nuance:** The company repeatedly states that ~$9M per quarter of SBC relates to "management incentive units issued by LandBridge Holdings LLC" and that "any actual cash expense associated with such incentive units will be borne solely by LandBridge Holdings LLC and not the Company."

This framing is misleading. The public company is booking the non-cash expense while claiming the economic cost is borne by the sponsor. This creates artificial separation between reported profitability and actual value transfer.

### Dilution Reality

| Date | Event | Shares |
|------|-------|--------|
| June 2024 | IPO | 14.5M Class A sold to public at $17 |
| May 2025 | Secondary | 1.9M Class B converted + sold at $75.25 ($143M) |

**Current Share Count:**
- Class A outstanding: ~25.3M
- Class B remaining: ~48.6M (per Nov 2025 Form 4)

**Net Dilution Assessment:** Public float has increased ~70% since IPO (from ~14.5M to ~25.3M), entirely through sponsor conversions and sales. No buybacks. The dividend ($0.40/year, 0.7% yield) is token.

## 4. Capital Allocation Track Record

### a) Reinvestment: 1918 Ranch Acquisition

**Deal Terms:**
- $250M total consideration ($208M cash, $42M LB equity)
- 37,500 acres (Loving, Reeves, Winkler, Ward counties, TX)
- Expected $20M EBITDA contribution in FY2026
- Implied multiple: ~12.5x forward EBITDA

**Assessment:**
- Headline multiple is expensive for royalty-type assets
- Management claims long-term multiple could compress to 3-4x as volumes scale (900k bbl/day pore space potential)
- Acquisition is contiguous to existing acreage (strategic logic present)
- Funded with leverage push: net leverage temporarily to 3.1x vs. 2.0-2.5x target

**Verdict:** Reasonable strategic rationale, but paid full price. Depends entirely on volume ramp thesis proving out.

### b) M&A Discipline: Ecosystem Building

LandBridge is part of Five Point's integrated Permian play:
- **LandBridge:** Surface acreage, royalties, pore space
- **WaterBridge:** Produced water infrastructure
- **PowerBridge:** Data center development ($1B equity commitment announced May 2025)

**Pattern:** Five Point creates related entities, IPOs them or sells stakes to institutional investors (GIC bought WaterBridge minority at $2.8B EV in 2019), and monetizes through public markets.

**Red Flag:** Related-party revenue from WaterBridge was 32% of LB revenue in 2024 (per Gotham City Research), despite management claiming "only 17%." If related-party pricing is at market rates (as the company claims), this is less concerning. If pricing is inflated, this is value extraction.

### c) Buybacks & Issuance

**Buybacks:** Zero. No buyback program announced or executed.

**Issuance:**
- IPO: $247M raised (proceeds to company)
- Subsequent secondaries: $300M+ (proceeds to selling shareholder, not company)

**Assessment:** Capital allocation is entirely focused on sponsor monetization. Public shareholders fund the capital structure; sponsor extracts cash through secondary offerings.

### d) Debt Usage

**Current Capital Structure (post-Nov 2025 refinancing):**
- $500M 6.250% Senior Notes due 2030
- $275M revolving credit facility (new)
- Net leverage: 2.2x (Q3 2025), trending toward 3.1x post-1918 Ranch

**Assessment:**
- Leverage is moderate and within stated targets
- Used debt for acquisition (appropriate) rather than dividends/buybacks
- Successfully refinanced at reasonable rates
- No aggressive financial engineering observed

## 5. Learning vs. Repetition

### Observable Patterns

1. **Secondary timing near earnings:** Multiple secondaries announced within days of quarterly results (Q3 2024, Q3 2025). Pattern suggests sponsor prioritizes liquidity windows over market stability.

2. **Consistent conversion + sale:** Every major conversion has been immediately followed by public sale. No insider has converted and held.

3. **Leverage discipline:** Company is tracking toward stated targets. 1918 Ranch temporarily elevated leverage, but repayment path appears credible.

4. **No course corrections:** Short report from Gotham City Research in July 2025 alleged revenue inflation. Company has not meaningfully changed disclosure practices or related-party arrangements.

### Learning Evidence

None observed. Behavior is consistent with planned PE exit playbook, executed without deviation.

## 6. Related-Party Transaction Analysis

### Key Relationships

| Counterparty | Relationship | 2024 Revenue % |
|--------------|--------------|----------------|
| WaterBridge | Shared ownership (Five Point), shared management | ~32% (per Gotham) |
| PowerBridge | Five Point portfolio company | Early stage |

### Gotham City Research Allegations (July 2025)

1. 16%-55% of revenues "artificially boosted" by related parties
2. $8M data center deposit recognized as revenue upon receipt
3. Powered Land (customer) incorporated same day as deal announcement
4. 2 of 3 audit committee members oversaw prior companies with related-party/accounting issues
5. No internal control audit (Deloitte exemption)

### Counter-Argument (Management/Bulls)

- WaterBridge pricing at market rates (verifiable through volume/rate math)
- Related-party % overstated; organic growth is real
- PowerBridge represents genuine optionality, not circular scheme

### Assessment

The truth likely lies in the middle. Related-party revenue is elevated but may be at arm's-length pricing. The governance structure (shared management, no internal control audit, same-day entity formation) creates conditions for extraction even if not currently exploited. Investors must trust management absent verification mechanisms.

## 7. Forward-Looking Capital Allocation Risk

### What Management Is Likely To Do Next

1. **Continue Class B-to-A conversions and secondary offerings** until sponsor stake is meaningfully reduced (currently ~63% of voting power, target likely 20-30%)
2. **Pursue additional acquisitions** in Delaware Basin to drive EBITDA growth (needed to support leverage and maintain growth narrative)
3. **Announce PowerBridge-related transactions** to create data center optionality narrative (high-multiple revenue potential)
4. **Maintain token dividend** as proof of shareholder friendliness without meaningful capital return

### Where Capital Is Most Likely Misallocated

1. **Overpaying for acquisitions** to meet EBITDA targets (1918 Ranch at 12x sets precedent)
2. **Related-party transactions** at prices beneficial to WaterBridge/Five Point
3. **PowerBridge investments** that prioritize ecosystem value over LB shareholder returns

### Risk of Free Cash Flow Misuse

Moderate-to-High. FCF will likely be used for:
- Leverage repayment (appropriate)
- Acquisitions (quality TBD)
- Token dividends

FCF will NOT likely be used for:
- Meaningful buybacks
- Special dividends
- De-levering below target

## 8. The One Thing That Determines Value Creation

**The single most important factor:** The timeline and pricing of Five Point's exit.

With ~48.6M Class B shares remaining versus ~25.3M Class A public shares, the sponsor has nearly 2x the economic interest still to monetize. Every secondary offering dilutes public shareholders' claim on future earnings. If Five Point exits rapidly (next 2-3 years) through aggressive conversions, public shareholders absorb significant dilution. If exit is gradual and disciplined over 5-7 years, aligned with business growth, dilution is manageable.

**Why this dominates:** LandBridge's operating business (surface acreage, pore space, royalties) appears sound. The question is not whether the business creates value--it's whether public shareholders capture that value or whether it leaks to the sponsor through dilution and related-party transactions.

**What would change behavior:** Five Point completing its exit (reducing stake to <20%) would remove the principal-agent conflict. Alternatively, an activist accumulating a blocking position could force governance improvements.

---

## 9. What Consensus May Be Missing

**Non-consensus insight:** The market may be underpricing the dilution embedded in the Class B overhang.

With 48.6M Class B shares convertible to Class A at the sponsor's discretion, the effective share count is 74M, not 25M. Current market cap of ~$1.9B on 25M Class A shares implies ~$76/share. On a fully converted basis (74M shares), the per-share value is ~$26.

Bulls argue the Class B overhang is "known" and priced in. But at recent prices ($55-75/share), the market appears to value LB on float shares, not fully diluted shares. Every conversion crystallizes this discount.

**This makes me less trusting of management:** The pace and size of secondary offerings (3 offerings in 18 months) suggests sponsor urgency to monetize. Urgency is inconsistent with long-term alignment.
