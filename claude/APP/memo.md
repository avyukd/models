# AppLovin Corporation (APP) -- Investment Memo

## Recommendation

- **Decision:** Sell / Short
- **Conviction:** Medium
- **Time Horizon:** 12-18 months
- **Classification:** Tactical Short (mean-reversion on margins + failed TAM narrative)

---

## Key Drivers (The 3 Things That Decide This Stock)

1. **E-commerce expansion success or failure.** At $406, the stock requires ~$10B revenue to justify valuation on normalized earnings. That requires e-commerce to work at scale. This is the single biggest swing factor and it is unproven, pitting APP against Google, Meta, and Amazon on their home turf without the mediation data moat that made gaming dominant.

2. **Margin normalization.** 82% EBITDA margins are 2x any scaled ad tech platform in history. The Trade Desk runs ~40%, Google Ads ~35%. Whether this compresses toward 45-55% over time -- as competition re-enters (Unity stabilizes, Google invests) and e-commerce carries lower margins -- determines whether current earnings power is real or peak.

3. **Data practices / regulatory outcome.** SEC investigation and short-seller allegations of unauthorized data extraction from Meta, Snap, TikTok, Google. If AXON's performance edge is partly built on data access that gets curtailed, the entire flywheel degrades. Binary tail risk.

---

## Thesis

- APP is one of the best businesses in ad tech inside mobile gaming -- and the stock is priced as if it will become one of the best businesses in all of digital advertising. Those are two very different propositions.
- 82% EBITDA margins at scale are historically unprecedented in ad tech and are a product of one-time competitive dislocation (Unity's self-destruction) plus AXON's AI step-change. Peak margins are being capitalized as sustainable.
- The mobile gaming ad market grew 0.2% in 2025. APP's 68-77% revenue growth is almost entirely share gains in a stagnant pie. Share gains are inherently self-limiting -- the closer you get to 100%, the harder incremental gains become.
- E-commerce TAM expansion -- the critical variable for justifying the current price -- moves APP from niche dominance (strong moat) into a commodity arena (no moat) against the three largest ad platforms on earth.
- Governance structure (61% voting control, no sunset, CEO who attempted $20B hostile M&A at cycle peak) creates asymmetric downside risk from capital allocation errors that shareholders cannot block.

## The Setup

APP is a genuinely excellent business in mobile gaming ad tech. Post-divestiture of the apps segment (mid-2025), it is a pure-play AI-driven ad platform with extraordinary unit economics. The AXON engine, fed by MAX mediation data, creates a legitimate flywheel that competitors cannot easily replicate within gaming.

The problem is not the business. The problem is the price.

At ~$138B market cap, the stock discounts not just continued gaming dominance but successful expansion into e-commerce -- a market where APP's structural advantages evaporate. The valuation embeds peak margins as sustainable and treats share gains in a stagnant market as a durable growth engine.

This is a classic late-cycle setup: an outstanding niche business priced as a platform winner in a much larger arena it hasn't yet entered competitively.

## Risks to the Short / What Could Make Us Wrong

1. **E-commerce actually works.** If AXON's performance algorithms transfer to e-commerce and APP captures meaningful share, the TAM expansion justifies the multiple. This is the primary risk to the short. Early evidence of strong advertiser retention and competitive ROAS in e-commerce would be a clear signal to cover.

2. **Margins prove durable.** If the vertical integration (MAX + AXON) creates a structural cost advantage that sustains 70%+ margins even at scale, normalized earnings are much higher than our estimate. We weight this as unlikely given no historical precedent, but it is possible.

3. **Buyback acceleration.** With $3.4B+ FCF and a clean balance sheet, aggressive buybacks at lower prices could put a floor under the stock and accelerate EPS growth mechanically.

4. **Acquisition of a scaled e-commerce ad platform.** If Foroughi uses the balance sheet and dual-class control to acquire an e-commerce advertising asset (rather than building organically), the TAM expansion thesis could accelerate. This is a double-edged risk -- could work or could be value-destructive.

**Kill switches (cover the short if):**
- Two consecutive quarters of e-commerce ad revenue exceeding $500M with stable/improving margins
- SEC investigation resolved with no material findings
- AXON demonstrating measurable performance advantage in e-commerce (published case studies with verifiable ROAS data)

## Positioning and Portfolio Logic

- **Position size:** 1-2% short. Medium conviction limits sizing. The business quality is real, which caps how aggressively you lean in.
- **Entry:** Current levels (~$406). The stock is at ~28x forward on consensus that embeds aggressive assumptions.
- **Hedge / pair:** Consider pairing with a long in a scaled ad platform benefiting from the same secular tailwinds but at a more reasonable valuation (TTD, META). This isolates the APP-specific overvaluation from the sector.
- **Time horizon:** 12-18 months. The thesis plays out through margin normalization and e-commerce proof points, not an overnight event.
- **Watch list post-entry:** (a) Quarterly EBITDA margin trends, (b) E-commerce revenue disclosures, (c) SEC investigation updates, (d) CEO selling activity, (e) Any M&A announcements.

## Too Hard Assessment

This is NOT too hard. The analytical framework is clear:

- The business is excellent but the stock prices in a TAM expansion that lacks structural support.
- Margins are at historical extremes with no precedent for sustainability at scale.
- The key variables (margin trajectory, e-commerce traction, SEC outcome) are observable and will produce data within the investment horizon.

The reason conviction is Medium rather than High: APP has demonstrated the ability to surprise (AXON's performance gains were underestimated by the market in 2023-2024), and genuinely exceptional businesses sometimes sustain economics that look unsustainable. The short is a bet on mean reversion in margins and the limits of TAM expansion -- both are probabilistically sound but not certainties.
