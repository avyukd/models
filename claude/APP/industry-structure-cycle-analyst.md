# Mobile Ad Tech / In-App Advertising: Industry Structure & Cycle Analysis

*Analysis date: 2026-02-08*

---

## The One Industry Factor That Matters

**The AI-driven mediation flywheel determines who captures value in this industry.** The company with the largest mediation share generates the most signal data, which trains the best ad-matching models, which delivers the highest eCPMs to publishers, which attracts more publishers, which generates more data. This is not a traditional scale economy -- it is a compounding data advantage with increasing returns.

- **Inflection risk:** A rival (Unity Vector, Google, or a new entrant) cracks the model quality gap. Unity's Vector launch in early 2025 reportedly drove 15-20% growth in installs/IAP for its clients -- the first credible competitive response in two years.
- **How to monitor:** Quarterly mediation market share data (Tenjin benchmarks, Sensor Tower), relative eCPM performance by network on iOS and Android, and Unity's disclosed Vector adoption metrics.

---

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**Consensus view:** Mobile ad tech is a secular growth story riding AI + mobile penetration. AppLovin has won the mediation war and will extend dominance into e-commerce. The debate is about valuation, not industry structure.

**Where consensus may be wrong (BEARISH for industry participants at current margins):**

The mobile gaming ad market -- AppLovin's core TAM -- is approaching zero growth. Revenue grew just 0.2% YoY in 2025 (per Sensor Tower/industry data), down from 3% in 2024. Downloads have declined every year since 2021. New title releases fell 43% YoY in 2024. This is not a cyclical pause -- this is a maturing market with saturating engagement in developed markets.

AppLovin's extraordinary revenue growth (68-77% YoY through 2025) has been almost entirely driven by **share gains within a stagnant pie**, not pie expansion. The consensus treats this as sustainable because "AI keeps getting better." But share gains are inherently self-limiting -- you cannot take more than 100% of the market. The question is not whether AXON is good, but what growth looks like once the share gain runway shortens.

The e-commerce TAM expansion is real but unproven. Projected e-commerce revenue of ~$1.45B in 2026 represents significant upside, but the competitive dynamics in e-commerce advertising (vs. Meta, Google, Amazon) are fundamentally different from mobile gaming, where AppLovin had structural advantages via MAX mediation and first-party game data. In e-commerce, AppLovin has no equivalent data moat. **The market is pricing in a successful TAM expansion before the evidence is in.**

This is a **bearish-for-current-valuations** insight, not bearish for the industry broadly. The underlying ad tech infrastructure business is sound; the question is whether peak share + stagnant core TAM + unproven TAM expansion justifies current multiples.

## Industry Structure

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Structure** | Oligopoly in mediation (AppLovin MAX ~50%+, Google AdMob, Unity LevelPlay together >90%). More fragmented in demand-side/ad networks |
| **Trajectory** | Consolidating further. Unity's botched IronSource integration handed share to AppLovin. Smaller independents (Digital Turbine, Liftoff, Mintegral) are niche players |
| **Key players** | AppLovin (mediation + DSP + measurement via Adjust), Google (AdMob + demand), Meta (demand-side giant), Unity (weakened but rebuilding), Mintegral (China/emerging markets) |
| **Competitive dynamics** | Winner-take-most in mediation/optimization layer; more competitive in ad demand. Mediation is the strategic chokepoint |

### Industry Value Chain Position

```
Advertiser (demand) --> DSP/Ad Network --> Mediation Layer --> Publisher (supply)
                                              ^
                                     STRATEGIC CHOKEPOINT
                                     (AppLovin MAX dominates)
```

The mediation layer is where industry economics are determined. The mediator sees all bid data across networks, enabling superior optimization. This creates an information asymmetry that compounds over time.

## Cyclicality Assessment: MODERATE

| Evidence | Detail |
|---|---|
| **2008 recession** | Total US ad spending fell ~13%. Digital fell only ~2%. Mobile was nascent |
| **2020 COVID** | Total ad spend dipped ~4-5%. Mobile/digital recovered within quarters and accelerated. In-app actually benefited from lockdown engagement |
| **2022 downturn** | Mobile gaming ad spend contracted meaningfully after ATT shock + post-COVID normalization. Industry eCPMs fell 15-30% on some formats |
| **Structural offset** | Secular shift from traditional to digital/mobile partially insulates the category in downturns. Advertisers cut TV/print before performance-based digital |

**Key cyclicality characteristics:**
- Digital ad tech is less cyclical than traditional advertising, but NOT acyclical
- Performance-based mobile ads (CPI, CPA) are more resilient than brand/CPM-based spending
- The industry has moderate cyclicality: expect 5-15% revenue declines in a recession, vs. 20%+ for traditional media
- Recovery tends to be fast (2-3 quarters), not prolonged

**Judgment:** Moderate cyclicality. The performance-marketing orientation of in-app advertising provides a floor that brand advertising lacks. Advertisers can measure ROI in real-time and tend to maintain spend where returns are provable.

## Current Cycle Position: LATE CYCLE (for mobile gaming ad tech specifically)

| Indicator | Signal |
|---|---|
| **Industry margins** | AppLovin at 82% adjusted EBITDA margin is unprecedented in ad tech history. The Trade Desk runs ~40%. This is peak-margin territory |
| **Mobile gaming revenue growth** | 0.2% YoY in 2025. Near-zero underlying market growth |
| **eCPM trends** | Rising, but driven by bid density improvements (AI-driven), not underlying demand expansion |
| **Competitive dynamics** | Dominant player taking share in a flat market = late-cycle share consolidation pattern |
| **Capital returns** | AppLovin buying back $571M in Q3 2025 alone. Classic late-cycle capital allocation |
| **New entrant activity** | Low. Barriers rising via AI/data moats. Also a late-cycle signal (no new capacity being built) |

**Confidence: Medium.** The e-commerce expansion could reset the cycle clock if successful. If AppLovin successfully enters a $170B+ e-commerce ad market, the "late cycle in gaming" framing becomes less relevant. But that transition is unproven.

## Mid-Cycle Reference Point

**What "normal" looks like for mobile ad tech:**

| Metric | Mid-Cycle Estimate | Current | Commentary |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Industry revenue growth** | 10-15% | ~12-13% (total mobile ad market) | Total market is mid-cycle; gaming subsegment is late/post-cycle |
| **Leading platform EBITDA margins** | 35-50% | 82% (AppLovin) | Current margins are 2x+ mid-cycle norms. The Trade Desk at ~40% is closer to "normal" for scaled ad tech |
| **Mediation market concentration** | Top 3 at 80%+ | Top 3 at 90%+ | Slightly above normal concentration but directionally consistent |
| **eCPM growth** | Low-to-mid single digits | Higher (AI-driven efficiency gains) | Temporarily elevated by AI optimization step-change |
| **Mobile gaming growth** | 5-8% | 0.2% | Below mid-cycle. Mature market dynamics |

**FACT:** AppLovin's 82% EBITDA margins are not normal for ad tech at any point in history. The Trade Desk, the other scaled pure-play, runs 40-43%. Even Google's ad segment runs ~35% operating margins. AppLovin's margins reflect: (a) the AXON AI engine's efficiency, (b) dominant mediation share creating pricing power, and (c) a period of limited competition post-Unity's stumble.

**JUDGMENT:** These margins are extraordinary and likely to compress over time as: competition responds (Unity Vector), the law of large numbers constrains share gains, and e-commerce expansion requires investment with lower initial margins. Mid-cycle sustainable margins for a dominant ad tech platform are probably 45-55% EBITDA.

## Secular Trends

### Tailwinds

| Trend | Impact | Timeframe |
|---|---|---|
| **Mobile time spent increasing** | More inventory, more ad opportunities | Ongoing but decelerating |
| **Shift from brand to performance advertising** | Favors measurable in-app formats | Multi-year secular trend |
| **AI/ML in ad optimization** | Step-function improvement in matching efficiency. Raises eCPMs industry-wide | 2023-2027 (current wave) |
| **Emerging market smartphone penetration** | New users, new inventory. Turkey +28%, Mexico +21%, India +17% spending growth in 2024 | 5-10 year runway |

### Headwinds

| Trend | Impact | Timeframe |
|---|---|---|
| **Mobile gaming saturation in developed markets** | Core TAM growth approaching zero. Downloads declining since 2021 | Structural, already underway |
| **Apple ATT (enacted)** | ~87% opt-out rate. Shifted ad revenue from iOS to Android. Forced industry to rebuild targeting without IDFA | Structural shift, largely absorbed |
| **Privacy regulation broadly** | GDPR, state-level US laws, potential federal legislation. Raises compliance costs, constrains targeting | Ongoing regulatory overhang |
| **New title release decline** | 43% fewer new mobile games in 2024. Fewer new advertisers entering the ecosystem | Concerning leading indicator |

### Resolved/Non-Factor

| Trend | Status |
|---|---|
| **Google Privacy Sandbox** | Effectively dead. Google retired most Sandbox APIs in Oct 2025. Third-party cookies remain enabled by default in Chrome. This was a major overhang that has now been removed -- **bullish for status quo ad tech** |
| **ATT disruption** | Largely absorbed. Industry adapted via contextual targeting, probabilistic attribution, and AI-based optimization. Companies with first-party data (like AppLovin via MAX) were net beneficiaries |

## Industry Economics

### Profitability Drivers

1. **Data scale and model quality** -- More signal data = better ad matching = higher eCPMs = more publisher adoption = more data. This is the core profit engine.
2. **Mediation control** -- The mediator sees all bids and can optimize allocation. This is an information advantage that translates directly to pricing power.
3. **Low marginal cost of serving** -- Ad tech platforms are software businesses. Incremental ad impressions cost nearly nothing to serve once infrastructure exists. This drives operating leverage at scale.

### Capital Intensity: LOW

- Primary costs are engineering talent and compute/cloud infrastructure
- No physical plant, inventory, or heavy capex requirements
- R&D is the critical investment -- AI model development and infrastructure
- Capex/revenue ratios for scaled ad tech platforms typically run 3-8%
- **This is a high-ROIC industry for winners**, with returns on invested capital commonly exceeding 30%+ for scaled platforms

### Winner-Take-Most Dynamics: STRONG

| Factor | Strength |
|---|---|
| Data network effects | Strong. More data improves models non-linearly |
| Switching costs (publisher side) | Moderate-High. Mediation SDK integration + revenue optimization makes switching costly |
| Switching costs (advertiser side) | Low-Moderate. Advertisers can and do use multiple networks |
| Economies of scale | Strong in model training. Moderate in infrastructure |
| Brand/trust | Moderate. Publishers care about eCPM results, not brand |

**JUDGMENT:** This industry exhibits strong winner-take-most characteristics in the mediation/optimization layer, but NOT in the demand layer (ad networks). The strategic question is whether mediation dominance translates to demand-side dominance. AppLovin's vertical integration (MAX mediation + AppLovin ad network + Adjust measurement) attempts this, but it is not guaranteed.

## Supply/Demand Dynamics

### Supply Side (Ad Inventory)

- Mobile ad inventory is effectively unlimited and growing (more apps, more time spent, more ad placements per session)
- Supply is NOT the constraint in this market
- The shift toward hybrid monetization (IAP + ads) in mobile gaming has increased high-quality ad inventory
- **Oversupply is the default state.** The value is in matching/optimization, not in raw inventory

### Demand Side (Advertiser Spend)

- Total mobile ad spend growing ~15-18% annually (broad market)
- Mobile gaming advertiser spend near-flat (0-3% growth)
- E-commerce and retail media represent fastest-growing demand categories
- Performance advertisers (CPI/CPA) are the most valuable demand -- they bid based on measurable outcomes

### Pricing Dynamics

- eCPMs have been rising despite flat underlying mobile gaming growth -- driven by AI optimization improving bid efficiency
- This is a **temporary dislocation**: AI is extracting more value per impression, but this gain has a ceiling
- Long-run pricing in ad tech trends toward advertiser-ROI parity: eCPMs rise until advertiser returns normalize
- **The pricing power is in the optimization layer, not in the inventory itself**

## Structural Shifts Assessment

### Shifts Favoring Incumbent Ad Tech Platforms (AppLovin-relevant)

1. **AI as barrier to entry.** The compute and data requirements to build competitive ad optimization models have risen dramatically. This favors incumbents with existing scale.
2. **Privacy changes consolidated power.** ATT eliminated third-party tracking, which hurt smaller ad networks disproportionately. Platforms with first-party data and large-scale contextual signals gained share. This is structural and permanent.
3. **Google Privacy Sandbox death.** The removal of this overhang preserves the current competitive landscape. No forced industry re-architecture.

### Shifts Threatening Incumbent Positions

1. **Mobile gaming TAM saturation.** The core market is ex-growth. This is structural, not cyclical. Developed market smartphone penetration is ~85%+. Time spent on mobile games is plateauing.
2. **Platform risk.** Apple and Google control the mobile OS layer. Any change to app store policies, ad frameworks, or privacy rules can restructure the industry overnight. ATT was a demonstration of this power.
3. **E-commerce ad market has different competitive dynamics.** Meta, Google, and Amazon have massive first-party purchase data. AppLovin's advantages in gaming (mediation data, publisher relationships) do not directly transfer. The e-commerce expansion is a bet on AI model transferability, not structural advantage.

## Historical Cycle Context

| Period | Industry Conditions | Margins (scaled platforms) | Key Driver |
|---|---|---|---|
| **2019-2020** | Pre-ATT era. Strong growth. COVID lockdowns boosted mobile engagement | 25-35% EBITDA | Mobile engagement surge |
| **2021** | ATT launches. Industry shock. Facebook loses ~$10B in ad revenue. Smaller networks devastated | Compressed for most; winners gained share | Privacy disruption |
| **2022** | Post-COVID normalization + macro slowdown. Mobile gaming revenue declined ~5%. eCPMs fell sharply | Trough margins for many ad tech players | Demand correction + ATT aftershock |
| **2023** | Recovery begins. AI-driven optimization (AXON 2.0) drives step-function improvement. Share consolidation accelerates | Rapid margin expansion for AppLovin specifically | AI + competitive consolidation |
| **2024-2025** | AppLovin revenue +68-77% YoY. EBITDA margins reach 82%. Mobile gaming market grows 0-3% | Peak margins. Unprecedented in ad tech | Share gains in flat market + AI leverage |

**JUDGMENT:** The 2021-2022 period was a genuine trough driven by the ATT shock and macro weakness. The 2023-2025 recovery has been real but has overshot mid-cycle norms, particularly for the dominant platform. Current conditions represent peak profitability driven by a one-time competitive dislocation (Unity's stumble) intersecting with a technology step-change (AXON AI). Neither of these tailwinds is infinitely repeatable.
