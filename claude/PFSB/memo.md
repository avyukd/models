## THE SETUP

**What This Is:** A 138-year-old community thrift in rural Illinois that converted from mutual to stock ownership in October 2023, raising $17.3M at $10/share. The conversion created an overcapitalized balance sheet (18.6% equity/assets) with excess capital seeking deployment.

**Business Profile:**
- $197.6M total assets
- $96.3M loans (65% residential mortgages)
- $159.6M deposits
- Two branches, 25 employees
- LaSalle County, Illinois (population ~110,000)

**What Makes This Interesting:** Management is not sitting on the excess capital. They have authorized repurchase of 20% of the float and are executing at a 31% discount to tangible book. This is unusual discipline for a micro-cap thrift conversion.

**What This Is Not:** A growth story. Peru, IL has declining population (-0.48% annually). The addressable market is not expanding. The moat is weak - this is a price-taker in a competitive local market earning low returns. None of that matters for the thesis.

## RISKS & KILL SWITCHES

**What Invalidates the Thesis:**

1. **Management Stops Buybacks:** If capital deployment slows and cash accumulates unproductively, the mechanical value creation stops. Monitor quarterly share count changes.

2. **Credit Deterioration:** 65% residential mortgage concentration in rural Illinois. A local housing market downturn would pressure book value. Monitor NPAs quarterly - current 0.43% is excellent.

3. **Premature Sale at Inadequate Premium:** The biggest risk is management accepting an acquisition offer at 1.0x TBV (premium to market but discount to intrinsic value). Any deal below 1.1x TBV destroys value.

4. **Deposit Outflows:** If the deposit franchise erodes, the strategic value to acquirers declines. Monitor deposit trends quarterly.

**Kill Switches (Exit Triggers):**

- NPAs rise above 2% of assets
- Management announces slowdown/halt of buybacks without clear rationale
- Deposit base declines more than 5% in any 12-month period
- Acquisition announced at less than 1.05x TBV

**What Would Cause Permanent Loss:**
Significant credit losses that impair tangible equity. Given 18.6% equity/assets and pristine current credit quality, this would require a severe local recession. The overcapitalization provides substantial buffer.

## FINAL WORD

This is not a great business. It is a mediocre business trading at a substantial discount to tangible book, run by management that is doing the right thing with excess capital. The buyback math works. The M&A optionality is real. The downside is protected by overcapitalization.

The bear case (sub-scale, declining market, low ROE, permanent illiquidity) is obvious and priced in. What the market is missing is that none of those factors matter if management continues buying back stock at 69 cents on the dollar. The variant view is that this management team, 14 months into their public company tenure, has demonstrated unusual discipline for a micro-cap thrift conversion.

This is not a high-conviction position. The liquidity constraints limit sizing. But as an optionality trade with defined downside and asymmetric upside, it earns a place in the portfolio.

**Decision: BUY at 0.5-1.0% position size, with patience on entry given liquidity constraints.**
