# US Community Banking Industry Analysis

## Industry Context for PFS Bancorp (NASDAQ: PFSB)

---

## Executive Summary

The US community banking industry is in structural decline masked by cyclical recovery. The number of community banks has fallen from 8,300+ in 2000 to approximately 4,050 today - a 50%+ decline. This is NOT primarily driven by failures but by voluntary M&A and the crushing economics of being sub-scale in a business where regulatory compliance costs 11-15% of personnel expenses regardless of size.

The current moment (late 2025/early 2026) represents a rare cyclical bright spot: NIMs have recovered above pre-pandemic averages (3.73% vs. 3.63% historical), the regulatory environment has shifted dramatically in favor of small banks, and M&A activity is surging with faster deal approvals. Valuations for micro-cap community banks remain at GFC-era discounts (20% below tangible book value on average).

**The non-obvious insight:** Consensus treats community bank consolidation as a headwind. I believe it is the primary source of shareholder value for well-run small banks. With 4,487 banks remaining and an expected 4% annual consolidation rate, roughly one in every 25 banks will be sold each year. Shareholders in acquired banks have historically enjoyed 30%+ premiums. For a $25M market cap bank like PFSB, the M&A exit multiple is likely the primary driver of equity returns - not operating performance.

---

## The One Industry Factor That Matters

**For micro-cap community banks, the single most important variable is M&A takeout probability, not operating performance.**

This may seem contrarian for an "industry economics" analysis, but the math is stark:

| Factor | Impact on Small Bank Equity Returns |
|--------|-------------------------------------|
| NIM improvement | Modest (1-2 bps = small EPS impact) |
| Loan growth | Constrained by size/geography |
| Operating leverage | Limited at sub-$500M scale |
| M&A premium | 30%+ premium to TBV = transformational |

For a bank trading at 0.8x TBV, an M&A exit at 1.3x TBV represents 60%+ upside. No amount of operating improvement delivers that return at this scale.

**What would cause M&A to inflect:**
- Regulatory approval timelines (currently accelerating - deals approved in half the time vs. Biden era)
- Interest rate stability (makes deal math more predictable)
- Credit union acquisition activity (record 22 whole-bank deals proposed by CUs in 2024)
- Buyer motivation: 43% of bank leaders say they're "very or somewhat likely" to buy another bank by end of 2025, up from 35%

**How to monitor:**
- FDIC bank merger filings
- Deal announcement volumes (already 150+ deals in 2025, exceeding all of 2024)
- Price-to-TBV multiples on announced deals
- Credit union bank acquisition announcements

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**Consensus view:** Community banks are in secular decline due to technology disruption from neobanks/fintechs, inability to compete on rates, and crushing regulatory burden. Small banks are dinosaurs waiting to die.

**What consensus may be missing:** The regulatory climate has fundamentally shifted, and small bank valuations have overshot to the downside.

Three underappreciated dynamics:

1. **Regulatory burden is actually improving for the first time in history.** The Community Bank Sentiment Index regulatory burden component hit 130 in Q1 2025 - above neutral (100) for the first time ever. The removal of "reputational risk" from CAMELS ratings, rescission of the 2024 merger review guidelines, and proposed Community Bank Leverage Ratio framework changes all reduce friction.

2. **The neobank threat is real but overstated for small bank customers.** Fintechs captured 44% of new checking account openings in 2024 - but primarily from Gen Z/Millennial customers in urban areas. Community banks serve relationship-based SMB lending and rural markets where neobanks have minimal penetration. The customers leaving weren't profitable for community banks anyway.

3. **Micro-cap community bank valuations have overcorrected.** These banks trade at 20% discounts to TBV - near pandemic trough and GFC-era levels. Yet fundamentals are far better: NIMs are above pre-pandemic averages, credit quality remains strong (delinquency at 3.99% is below historical averages), and M&A exit multiples remain healthy.

**This is moderately bullish for small community bank equity holders** - not because operating performance will surge, but because valuations can re-rate toward historical norms while M&A activity provides an exit path.

## Industry Economics: The Community Bank Business Model

### Net Interest Margin - The Primary Profit Driver

Community banks are fundamentally spread businesses. NIM is ~80% of revenue for most small banks.

| Period | Community Bank NIM | Notes |
|--------|-------------------|-------|
| Pre-pandemic average | 3.63% | Historical benchmark |
| Q4 2023 (trough) | 3.34% | Deposit competition + wholesale funding costs |
| Q3 2025 (current) | 3.73% | Above pre-pandemic, driven by asset repricing |

**The NIM recovery is real.** Asset yields have repriced higher while deposit costs are now declining as rate cuts flow through. Q3 2025 NIM increase was driven by a 10 bp increase in earning asset yield that more than offset a 1 bp increase in cost of funds.

**Key insight on deposit betas:** Community banks faced deposit betas (how much of Fed rate increases they had to pass through) of roughly 50-60% on the way up. On the way down, they may retain more margin as deposit rates prove "sticky" downward. This is favorable for NIM in a cutting cycle.

### The Scale Problem

The fundamental challenge for sub-$500M banks is that many costs are fixed regardless of size:

| Cost Category | Small Bank Burden |
|---------------|------------------|
| Regulatory compliance | 11-15% of personnel expense |
| Core banking systems | Fixed vendor fees |
| Cybersecurity | Increasing minimums |
| Audit requirements | Step up at $500M and $1B thresholds |

**At $200M in assets, you face the same BSA/AML compliance requirements as a $2B bank but have 1/10th the revenue to cover it.** This is the fundamental reason for consolidation.

### Asset/Liability Sensitivity

Most community banks are liability-sensitive - their deposits reprice faster than their loan portfolios. This means:
- Rising rates: NIM compression (experienced 2022-2023)
- Falling rates: NIM expansion (experiencing now)

The current environment of gradually declining rates is favorable for community bank NIMs. The caveat: many banks hold unrealized losses on securities portfolios purchased at peak rates. At Q3 2025, unrealized losses on securities remain a focus for regulators.

## The Consolidation Imperative

### Historical Context: 50%+ Decline in 25 Years

| Year | Approximate Number of Community Banks |
|------|--------------------------------------|
| 2000 | 8,300+ |
| 2014 | 6,100 |
| 2019 | 4,750 |
| 2024 | ~4,050 |

**This is not primarily a story of bank failures.** Voluntary M&A and the virtual halt of de novo bank chartering are the drivers. From 2009-2018, new bank charters averaged near zero, compared to 100-200+ annually before the GFC.

### Current M&A Environment

2024 saw ~130 completed bank deals worth $16.3B - a tripling from the 2023 trough ($4.2B). 2025 is dramatically stronger:
- 150+ deals announced through mid-year (exceeding all of 2024)
- October 2025 alone saw 21 deals totaling $21.4B
- Median seller asset size: $265M (up from $146M in 2014)

**The typical seller is a $200-300M asset bank.** PFSB's ~$200M asset size puts it squarely in the target zone for acquirers.

### Who's Buying

| Buyer Type | 2024 Activity | Notes |
|------------|---------------|-------|
| Other community banks | 70%+ of deals | Median buyer: $1.9B assets |
| Credit unions | 17 of 59 announced CBO deals | Tax advantage enables higher bids |
| Regional banks | Select deals | Sub-$10B staying below regulatory thresholds |

**Credit unions are an increasingly important buyer.** Their tax-exempt status allows them to bid higher, particularly for community banks in attractive deposit markets.

### Deal Multiples

Historical M&A premiums for community banks have averaged 30%+ above tangible book value. Current deal activity suggests similar levels as:
- Regulatory approval timelines have shortened dramatically (half the time vs. 2024)
- Rate stability has improved deal pricing certainty
- Strategic value of core deposits remains high

## Competitive Dynamics

### The Fintech/Neobank Question

Headlines overstate the threat for most community banks:

| Segment | Fintech Threat Level | Reason |
|---------|---------------------|--------|
| Consumer deposits (urban) | High | 44% of new checking to digital banks |
| Consumer deposits (rural) | Low | Physical presence, relationship banking still valued |
| SMB lending | Low | Relationship-based, complex underwriting |
| Commercial real estate | Low | Local market knowledge essential |

**The customers fintechs are taking are the least profitable customers for community banks anyway** - rate-sensitive, transaction-oriented, low balance. Relationship-based SMB and CRE lending is defensible.

### Technology Investment Burden

Community banks face a difficult choice:
- Invest heavily in digital ($2-5M+ for meaningful upgrades) with uncertain ROI
- Partner with fintech vendors (gives up margin)
- Accept deposit attrition from younger demographics

For a $25M market cap bank, meaningful technology investment is nearly impossible to justify independently. This is another driver toward consolidation.

### The Branch Economics Question

National branch count has declined from ~100,000 (2009) to 76,000 (2025). But:
- Branch closures are concentrated in urban/suburban markets
- Rural branches remain economically viable
- Community banks with single-market focus face less pressure

## Regulatory Environment

### The 2025 Shift

The regulatory environment has shifted dramatically under the new administration:

| Change | Impact |
|--------|--------|
| Merger review guidelines rescinded | Faster deal approvals |
| Expedited review process restored | Deals approved in half the time |
| "Reputational risk" removed from CAMELS | Less subjective examination pressure |
| Community Bank Leverage Ratio framework changes proposed | More capital flexibility |
| Regulatory burden sentiment | First positive reading ever (130 vs. 100 neutral) |

**This is the most favorable regulatory environment for community banks in decades.** The combination of faster M&A approvals and reduced examination friction is material for small bank operations and exit optionality.

### Key Thresholds to Monitor

| Asset Size | Regulatory Step-Up |
|------------|-------------------|
| $402M | Small Bank CRA threshold (2025) |
| $500M | Enhanced audit requirements |
| $1B | Additional audit/governance requirements |
| $10B | Durbin amendment, CFPB oversight, stress testing |

Banks just below these thresholds face a "grow or be sold" decision. This drives M&A activity among banks approaching $500M or $1B.

## Cycle Assessment

### Where We Are: Early-to-Mid Recovery

| Indicator | Reading | Signal |
|-----------|---------|--------|
| NIM | 3.73% (Q3 2025) | Above pre-pandemic, healthy |
| NIM trajectory | Improving for 3 consecutive quarters | Favorable |
| Credit quality | 3.99% delinquency | Below historical average |
| Regulatory sentiment | 130 (first positive ever) | Tailwind |
| M&A activity | 150+ deals in 2025 | Surging |
| Valuations | ~0.8x TBV for micro-caps | Depressed |

**Interpretation:** Operating fundamentals have recovered to above-normal levels (NIM), while valuations remain at trough levels. This is the classic setup for mean reversion.

### Historical Cycle Context

| Period | Community Bank Environment | Key Driver |
|--------|---------------------------|------------|
| 2008-2012 | Severe stress | Financial crisis, CRE losses |
| 2013-2019 | Steady recovery | Consolidation, improving credit |
| 2020-2021 | Abnormally strong | PPP fees, near-zero funding costs |
| 2022-2023 | NIM compression | Rapid rate increases, deposit competition |
| 2024-2025 | Recovery | NIMs improving, M&A accelerating |

**Mid-cycle definition for community banks:**
- NIM: 3.50-3.70%
- Loan growth: 3-5% annually
- Delinquency: 1.5-3.0%
- M&A activity: ~4% of banks sold annually
- Price-to-TBV: 1.0-1.3x

Current NIMs are above mid-cycle. Valuations are below mid-cycle. This disparity suggests upside.

## Industry Risk Assessment

### Primary Industry Risk: Technology Obsolescence Without M&A Exit

The existential risk for micro-cap community banks is being stranded without an exit:
- Unable to invest in technology at scale
- Slow deposit attrition to digital competitors
- Regulatory burden consuming ever-larger share of expenses
- No acquirer interested at any reasonable multiple

**This risk is mitigated when:**
- Bank has defensible deposit franchise (loyal customer base, rural/underserved market)
- M&A environment remains active
- Bank maintains clean credit quality (problem assets scare acquirers)

### Secondary Risks

1. **Credit cycle turn:** Community banks have elevated CRE concentration. A CRE downturn (particularly office/retail) would pressure asset quality and reduce M&A multiples.

2. **Prolonged rate volatility:** Makes deal pricing difficult, slows M&A.

3. **Regulatory reversal:** If regulatory environment tightens again, deal timelines extend and valuations compress.

4. **Deposit competition resurgence:** If rates rise again, funding costs could pressure NIMs.

## Industry Favorability Assessment

**Structural factors: Unfavorable**
- Long-term decline in community bank count
- Scale economics work against small players
- Technology investment requirements rising
- Fintech competition for commodity banking services

**Cyclical factors: Favorable**
- NIMs above pre-pandemic averages
- Credit quality sound
- M&A environment strongest in years
- Regulatory sentiment most positive ever

**Net assessment for micro-cap community banks:** The industry structure is unfavorable for long-term independent operation, but the M&A exit environment is highly favorable. This creates a situation where equity returns depend more on takeout probability than operating performance.
