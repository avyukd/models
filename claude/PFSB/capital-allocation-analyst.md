## Incentive Structure & Alignment

### CEO Compensation (Eric Heagy)

**Base Salary:** $205,000/year (per employment agreement)
**Total Compensation:** ~$260K (81.2% salary, 18.8% bonus/stock)
**Direct Ownership:** 0.72% of shares (~$180K)

**Employment Agreement Terms:**
- Three-year term with annual renewal (board approval required)
- Salary reviewed annually; can be increased but not decreased (except company-wide cuts)
- Eligible for incentive compensation and bonuses in any bank/company plan
- Salary Continuation Agreement filed March 2024 (deferred compensation/severance)

**What Behavior Does This Compensation Encourage?**

The compensation structure is conservative and appropriate for a $200M community thrift:
- Base salary of $205K is modest for a bank CEO (approximately 1% of equity capital)
- Heavy salary weighting (81%) with modest bonus component (18.8%)
- Direct ownership of 0.72% creates alignment but is not dominant wealth exposure
- No evidence of excessive stock option grants or aggressive SBC programs

This is a caretaker compensation structure - it does not encourage aggressive risk-taking or empire-building. The 20-year tenure suggests Heagy is a career community banker, not a hired-gun turnaround CEO.

**Who Benefits If Stock Underperforms?**

Heagy's downside is limited - the salary provides stability regardless of stock performance. However, his ownership stake (~$180K) represents meaningful wealth exposure relative to his salary. The ESOP (138,000 shares, 8% of total) further aligns bank employees with shareholders.

**Assessment:** Compensation is modest and appropriate. No red flags. The structure favors stability over growth-at-all-costs.

## Capital Allocation Track Record

### Context: Post-Conversion Capital Deployment

The conversion raised ~$16M in net proceeds, creating an overcapitalized thrift with ~17% common equity to total assets. The central question for any thrift conversion: **Will management deploy excess capital wisely or will it sit idle?**

### a) Share Repurchases (Primary Capital Return Mechanism)

**December 2024 - Initial Program:**
- Authorized repurchase of up to 172,500 shares (10% of outstanding)
- Open market purchases via Rule 10b5-1 plan

**December 2025 - Additional Authorization:**
- Authorized additional 155,693 shares
- Combined authorization: 328,193 shares (~19% of original float)

**Shares Repurchased to Date:**
- ~125,000 shares already retired (based on share count decline from 1.725M to 1.6M)
- This represents ~7% of original shares in approximately 14 months post-IPO

**Assessment:** This is aggressive, shareholder-friendly capital return for a thrift conversion. Management is not sitting on excess capital - they are actively shrinking the share count at prices well below book value.

**Buyback Timing:**
- IPO price: $10.00
- Current price: ~$15.79
- Book value per share: ~$23 (based on $36.7M equity / 1.6M shares)
- Price to book: ~0.69x

Buying back shares at 69% of book value is highly accretive. Each dollar spent on buybacks creates ~$1.45 of book value per share for remaining shareholders.

### b) Dividends

**Current Policy:** No dividend declared.

For a newly-converted thrift trading at 69% of book value, this is the correct decision. Dividends would be tax-inefficient compared to buybacks at these valuations. Management is prioritizing the highest-return use of capital.

### c) Organic Reinvestment

**Loan Portfolio:**
- Loans: $96.3M (49% of assets)
- Primarily 1-4 family residential mortgages
- Conservative, bread-and-butter community bank lending

**Deposit Base:**
- Deposits: $159.6M
- Two branch locations in Peru, IL

**Earnings:**
- 2024 Revenue: $5.75M (+9.4% YoY)
- 2024 Net Income: $905K (-10.2% YoY)
- EPS: ~$0.57 (on average shares)

The earnings decline is concerning but context matters: post-conversion accounting adjustments, ESOP expense, and public company costs all impact near-term earnings. The core community bank franchise appears stable.

### d) M&A

**Acquisitions Made:** None.

**Acquisition Target Potential:** High. Small Illinois thrifts are attractive acquisition targets for regional banks seeking deposits and branch presence.

Management has made no statements about acquisition activity. The focus on buybacks suggests they are not pursuing growth-through-acquisition.

### e) Debt Usage

**Leverage:** Minimal. The bank is overcapitalized at ~17% equity-to-assets. This is typical for a recent thrift conversion.

### Evidence of Learning/Discipline

1. **Immediate Buyback Authorization:** Within 14 months of IPO, management authorized repurchases of 19% of shares. This is faster than many thrift conversions.

2. **Execution at Discount to Book:** Repurchases occurring at ~69% of book value demonstrate price-aware capital allocation.

3. **No Dividend at Sub-Book Valuation:** Correct prioritization of buybacks over dividends.

4. **No Empire-Building:** No acquisitions announced. Management appears content to run the existing franchise.

5. **Long Tenure:** Heagy's 20+ years at Peru Federal suggests deep local knowledge and conservative orientation.

### Concerns

1. **New Public Company:** Only 14 months of post-IPO history. Track record is limited.

2. **Earnings Decline:** 2024 net income down 10% YoY. Needs monitoring.

3. **Lack of Insider Buying Disclosure:** No Form 4 filings found showing director/officer purchases. In a stock trading at 69% of book, insider buying would be a strong signal of confidence.

## Forward-Looking Capital Allocation Risk

### What Management Is Likely To Do

1. **Continue Buybacks:** The additional 155,693 share authorization signals ongoing commitment to capital return.

2. **Maintain Conservative Lending:** No indication of growth-at-all-costs mentality.

3. **Potentially Initiate Dividend:** Once stock trades closer to book value, a modest dividend may be introduced.

4. **Remain Independent (Near-Term):** No acquisition activity or merger discussions disclosed.

### Where Capital Could Be Misallocated

1. **Excess Cash Sitting Idle:** The biggest risk is that management slows buybacks and lets capital accumulate unproductively.

2. **Ill-Timed Acquisition:** If a larger thrift or regional bank approaches with an acquisition, management could be tempted by premium but sell at less than intrinsic value.

3. **Reaching for Yield:** Pressure to improve ROE could lead to riskier lending or securities purchases.

### Primary Risk: Premature Sale

The most likely value destruction scenario is not misallocation but **selling the company too cheaply**. At 69% of book value, any acquisition at even 100% of book would leave substantial value on the table. Management must resist the temptation to sell at a premium to current market but discount to intrinsic value.

## The One Thing That Determines Value Creation

**The single most important factor:** Whether management continues aggressive buybacks until the stock trades at or above book value.

At 69% of book, every share repurchased creates ~45% immediate accretion to remaining shareholders. If management executes the full 328,193 share authorization (~19% of original float) at current prices, book value per share would increase materially.

The math is simple: management does not need to grow loans, cut costs, or make acquisitions. They merely need to keep buying back stock at a discount to book value.

**Why this dominates:** Peru Federal is a stable, well-capitalized community bank with a 137-year history. The franchise is not going to grow rapidly, but it does not need to. The excess capital from the conversion, deployed into buybacks at discounted prices, creates shareholder value without operational risk.

**What would cause a change:**
- Stock price rising above book value (changes buyback math)
- Management becoming complacent and halting buybacks
- Accepting an acquisition offer at inadequate premium

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**Non-Consensus Insight:** The market appears to be ignoring this stock entirely - PFSB trades on OTCQB with minimal coverage. The variant perception is that **management's actions in the first 14 months suggest unusual discipline for a micro-cap thrift conversion.**

Most thrift conversions trade at discounts to book because:
1. Management is passive and fails to return capital
2. Insiders extract value through compensation
3. The bank gets acquired at modest premiums before value is realized

PFSB is doing the opposite:
- Aggressive buyback authorization (19% of float)
- Modest CEO compensation ($260K vs $905K net income)
- No premature sale discussions
- Execution at significant discount to book

**What Analysts Typically Miss:** The BauerFinancial 5-Star rating for 65 consecutive quarters (16+ years) is meaningful for a bank this small. It indicates consistent safety and soundness that predates the conversion.

**Implication:** If management continues current capital allocation approach, PFSB shareholders are effectively buying dollars for 69 cents through the company's buyback program. The question is whether management will maintain discipline or succumb to typical thrift conversion pitfalls.
