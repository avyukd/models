**Bottom line: A 40% cut gets DOCU to ~$500M economic FCF ($2.50/share). At 15-20x, that is $37-$50 vs. the current $43. The stock is already priced for significant efficiency gains. Upside only works if revenue holds.**

## 5. Risks to the Cost-Cutting Thesis

**Revenue attrition (the big one).** Cutting 50% of S&M risks losing the enterprise motion. If revenue declines 10-15% from cuts, economic FCF drops to ~$800M and the stock is worth $60 at 15x -- still up from here, but the margin of safety narrows. The counter-argument: DocuSign's NRR has been 100%+ and the product is embedded in workflows. Churn risk is moderate, not catastrophic.

**Can you actually cut 40%?** Meta cut ~21% in 2022-23 and saw revenue re-accelerate. But Meta had a revenue growth engine to fall back on. DOCU's growth is decelerating. The right comp is more like Autodesk or Dropbox -- mature SaaS that ran lean and traded at premium FCF multiples. Dropbox cut to ~25% FCF margins with a much smaller revenue base.

**SBC may not shrink as modeled.** Remaining employees may demand larger grants. New CEO could get a large equity package. SBC could settle at 12-14% of revenue instead of 10%, reducing economic FCF by $60-120M.

**AI disruption.** Cutting R&D by 25% while AI-native competitors (e.g., Ironclad, contract AI startups) are emerging is a gamble. The product moat is switching costs, not technology.

**Execution risk.** No current activist pressure or management signaling toward this magnitude of cuts. This is a hypothetical, not a base case.

---

## Key Takeaway

DOCU at $43 is already pricing in mediocrity but not pricing in aggressive restructuring. The gap between headline FCF ($920M) and economic FCF ($250M) is the entire story -- $610M in annual SBC on a $8.7B market cap company is extraordinary. A 40% cut would compress SBC to $305M and unlock $1B+ in economic FCF, justifying $60-108/share. The stock is interesting below $40 where you get the restructuring option for free.
