# DocuSign (DOCU) - Investment Committee Memo

**Date:** January 2026
**Recommendation:** NEUTRAL (Too Hard / No Edge)
**Conviction:** N/A
**Classification:** Too Hard - Structurally Challenged Asset Without Compelling Entry Point

---

## Decision

**NEUTRAL - No Position**

DocuSign presents as a classic value trap: optically cheap on headline metrics, expensive on economic reality, with a core business facing structural headwinds and a strategic pivot (IAM) that is unproven. There is no variant perception that gives us an edge here. We are not bearish enough to short, not bullish enough to own.

---

## Key Drivers (The 1-3 Things That Matter)

### 1. SBC-Adjusted Economics Are Far Worse Than Headlines Suggest
- Headline FCF: ~$920M (31% margin)
- SBC: ~$610M (20% of revenue)
- Economic FCF: ~$310M
- Despite $1.4B+ in buybacks over two years, share count increased from 201M to 210M
- True FCF yield to shareholders: 2-3%, not the 7% headline suggests

**This is the core insight.** The market prices DocuSign as if $920M FCF accrues to shareholders. It does not. Management captures ~$600M annually through equity compensation. The buyback program is a SBC offset mechanism, not capital return.

### 2. Core E-Signature Business Is Commoditizing
- NRR declined from 110% to 98-102% in 18 months
- Microsoft introduced native e-signature in SharePoint (April 2024)
- AI-driven document automation (OpenAI's DocuGPT announcement caused 12% single-day drop)
- Customer reports show 40-50% discounts achievable when citing alternatives
- No network effects - recipient signs without needing DocuSign account

### 3. IAM Pivot Is Unproven and Time-Constrained
- DocuSign is trying to become a platform (IAM) before e-signature becomes a feature
- Management guidance: "revenue lags billings by 6-7 quarters" for IAM impact
- CLM market is crowded (Icertis, Agiloft, Ironclad)
- AI disruption operates faster than enterprise software pivots
- The transition window may close before the pivot completes

---

## Thesis: Why We Are Passing

1. **Valuation is not cheap on SBC-adjusted basis.** At $14B market cap and ~$310M economic FCF, stock trades at ~45x economic earnings for a 5-7% grower. On SBC-adjusted FCF of $620-740M at 15x multiple, fair value is $50-58/share vs. current ~$90.

2. **No variant perception.** Both bulls and bears understand the e-signature commoditization risk. The IAM optionality is priced in by optimists. We do not have differentiated insight into whether IAM succeeds.

3. **Management alignment is weak.** CEO owns <0.1% of company. No ROIC metrics in compensation. 36 insider sales vs. zero purchases in past year. 16% say-on-pay approval in 2023.

4. **Structural headwinds are accelerating.** Microsoft bundling, AI commoditization, and declining NRR are happening now. The fix (IAM adoption) is 2-3 years away.

For current valuation to make sense, you must believe:
- IAM successfully expands TAM and drives NRR back to 110%+

## Risk Assessment

### Why Not Short?

1. **Balance sheet is pristine.** Zero debt, $1.1B cash. No financial stress catalyst.
2. **Subscription revenue is sticky.** 97% subscription, annual contracts. Revenue does not collapse quickly.
3. **IAM optionality is real.** If it works, stock re-rates materially higher.
4. **Short interest already elevated.** Crowded trade.

### Why Not Long?

1. **Valuation not compelling on economic basis.** 45x economic FCF for a 5-7% grower.
2. **Structural headwinds accelerating.** Microsoft bundling, AI disruption.
3. **Management alignment poor.** Excessive SBC, no insider buying.
4. **No clear catalyst for re-rating.** IAM proof points are 2-3 years away.

---

## What Would Move This Out of the Too Hard Pile

**To consider going LONG:**
- Stock at $50-55 (30%+ below current), creating genuine margin of safety
- NRR inflecting to 105%+ for two consecutive quarters
- IAM showing measurable revenue contribution (5%+ of billings growth)
- SBC declining to 15% of revenue with clear trajectory to 10-12%
- Insider purchasing at meaningful levels

**To consider going SHORT:**
- Stock rallying to $100+ on narrative momentum
- Microsoft announcing e-signature bundled free with M365
- Major enterprise customer loss announced
- IAM traction metrics clearly disappointing

---

## Portfolio Decision

**Action:** No position

**Rationale:** This is a structurally challenged business that appears cheap but is expensive on economic metrics. The IAM pivot may work, but we have no edge in predicting it. Management is not aligned with shareholders. There is no catalyst that creates urgency.

Pass. Revisit if valuation becomes compelling ($50-55) or if IAM shows traction.

---

## Summary

DocuSign is a high-quality business facing structural headwinds, run by management whose interests are poorly aligned with shareholders, trading at a valuation that appears cheap on headline metrics but is expensive on economic reality. The IAM pivot is the key variable, and we lack differentiated insight into its success probability.

When the key variable is binary and unforecastable, the correct decision is to pass. This is not cowardice - it is discipline. Capital should go where we have an edge.

**Decision: NEUTRAL (Too Hard)**
