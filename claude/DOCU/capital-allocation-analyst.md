## Incentive Structure & Alignment

### CEO Compensation (Allan Thygesen - FY2025)

| Component | Amount | % of Total |
|-----------|--------|------------|
| Base Salary | $1.0M | 3.8% |
| Stock Awards | $23.8M | 91.4% |
| Non-Equity Incentive | $1.2M | 4.6% |
| **Total** | **$26.0M** | 100% |

### Performance Metrics

**Annual Cash Incentive:**
- Non-GAAP Operating Income: 50% weight
- Net New MRR: 25% weight
- Revenue: 25% weight

**PSU Awards:**
- 50% tied to Relative TSR vs. Nasdaq Composite
- 50% tied to Subscription Revenue growth + Free Cash Flow

### What This Compensation Encourages

75% of cash bonus tied to revenue/NNMRR metrics incentivizes growth over profitability. **No ROIC or return on capital metric anywhere in the compensation structure.** Executives can meet targets through aggressive M&A that grows revenue without creating value.

### 2023 Say-on-Pay Crisis

**16% approval** - among the worst in corporate America. Driven by controversial Stockholder Value Creation (SVC) award structure. FY2024 improved to 45% - still a failing grade.

## Stock-Based Compensation Discipline

### SBC Trend

| Year | SBC | Revenue | SBC/Revenue |
|------|-----|---------|-------------|
| FY2022 | ~$409M | $2.1B | ~19.4% |
| FY2023 | $539M | $2.5B | ~21.4% |
| FY2024 | $617M | $2.8B | ~22.3% |
| FY2025 | $609M | ~$2.9B | ~21% |

### The Dilution Reality

**Gross SBC Dilution (2023):** 8.2% of shares outstanding
**Buyback Offset:** 1.5%
**Net Dilution:** 6.7%

This is approximately **3x the median dilution for tech companies (~2.6%)**.

### Share Count Trajectory

Despite $1.4B+ in buybacks over FY2024-FY2025, diluted share count increased from 201M to 210M.

**Critical Assessment:** The buyback program is NOT returning capital to shareholders - it is merely offsetting (and failing to fully offset) SBC dilution.

## The One Thing That Determines Value Creation

**The single most important factor:** Whether management addresses the SBC/dilution problem or continues to pretend buybacks are capital returns.

At current levels:
- SBC: ~$600M annually
- FCF: ~$1B annually
- Buybacks: ~$700M annually

This leaves only **~$300M of true free cash flow** after accounting for SBC as a real cost. Shareholders receive roughly 30% of what headline FCF suggests.

---

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**Consensus view:** DocuSign has become a disciplined, mature software company returning capital to shareholders through buybacks.

**What consensus misses:** The buyback program is almost entirely a SBC offset mechanism. Net dilution remains 5-7% annually. Shareholders own less of the company each year despite $700M+ in annual "capital returns."

**The math:**
- Headline FCF: $1.0B
- Less: True cost of SBC: ~$600-700M
- Real FCF to shareholders: ~$300-400M

At $13-14B market cap, true FCF yield is 2-3%, not the 7%+ headline numbers suggest.

## Final Classification

**Incentive Alignment:** Weak
- CEO owns <0.1% of company
- No ROIC metrics in compensation
- Consistent insider selling

**Capital Allocation Discipline:** Moderate
- M&A measured and coherent
- But buyback framing is misleading

**Dilution Risk:** High
- 5-7% annual net dilution
- 20%+ SBC/Revenue in slow-growth business

**Primary Risk:** Value leakage through excessive stock-based compensation that buybacks fail to offset, leaving shareholders with shrinking ownership while management captures majority of FCF through equity compensation.
