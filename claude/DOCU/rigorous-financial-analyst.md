# DocuSign (DOCU) Financial Analysis

**Analysis Date:** January 2026
**Fiscal Year End:** January 31

---

## Executive Summary

DocuSign is a mature SaaS business with dominant e-signature market share (~56-67%), generating ~$3B in annual revenue with 80%+ gross margins and strong free cash flow conversion (~$920M FCF on ~$3B revenue, ~31% margin). The company is transitioning from a high-growth phase (revenue growth decelerated from 10%+ to 7-8%) to a cash-generative, mature software company. Balance sheet is pristine (zero debt, ~$1.1B cash). However, stock-based compensation remains substantial (~$610M annually, ~20% of revenue), and share buybacks are merely offsetting dilution rather than reducing share count.

**Variant Perception:** Consensus views DocuSign as a low-growth, commoditizing e-signature business. The non-obvious insight is that normalized after-SBC free cash flow is significantly lower than headline FCF suggests. The company's ~$920M headline FCF should be viewed as ~$310M economic FCF after accounting for SBC. At current valuation (~$14B market cap), the stock trades at ~45x economic earnings, not the ~15x implied by headline FCF.

---

## Key Financial Drivers (The 1-3 Things That Actually Matter)

### 1. Stock-Based Compensation as % of Revenue (~20%)
- SBC at $610M annually represents real economic dilution
- Despite $684M in buybacks (FY25), share count still increased by ~3M
- Until SBC normalizes to 10-12%, economic earnings remain depressed relative to headline FCF
- Positive inflection: SBC declining to 12-15% would add ~$200M to economic earnings
- Negative inflection: If SBC remains elevated, buybacks continue to merely offset dilution

### 2. Dollar Net Retention Rate (100-102%)
- At 100-102%, existing customers are barely expanding
- Caps organic growth at low-single-digits absent new customer acquisition
- Below the 120%+ benchmark of high-performing SaaS
- Positive inflection: IAM platform driving expansion to 110%+ would signal renewed growth
- Negative inflection: Drop back to 98% would signal customer churn accelerating

### 3. Revenue Growth Rate (7-8%, decelerating to 5-6%)
- At ~$3B revenue and 7-8% growth, transitioning from growth to value
- Growth rate determines appropriate valuation multiple
- Positive inflection: IAM driving re-acceleration to 10%+ would justify premium multiple
- Negative inflection: Deceleration to <5% would compress multiple

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**The SBC/Dilution Dynamic Is Worse Than It Looks:**
- DocuSign generated $920M in FCF in FY25
- Spent $684M on buybacks
- Net share count still increased by ~3M shares
- Meaning buybacks only neutralize ~60% of SBC dilution

**Adjusted FCF Calculation:**
- Headline FCF: $920M
- Less: Net dilution cost (~$180M, 3M shares x $60)
- True Economic FCF: $620-740M range

At $740M economic FCF and 15x multiple: Fair value ~$58/share
At $620M economic FCF and 15x multiple: Fair value ~$50/share

Current price (~$60) is at or above fair value on SBC-adjusted basis.

## Financial Assessment

**Solid but fully priced.** High-quality business with dominant market share, recurring revenue, and strong cash generation. However, expensive when properly accounting for SBC as real economic cost.

- Balance sheet: Excellent (no debt, $1.1B cash)
- Cash generation: Good headline numbers, but SBC erodes economic value
- Growth: Decelerating (7-8% to 5-6%)
- Valuation: Reasonable on headline metrics, expensive on SBC-adjusted basis

**The market may be missing:** The extent to which SBC dilutes economic returns. Headline FCF overstates true cash generation available to shareholders by $180-300M annually.
