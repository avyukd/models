## Why This Is Too Hard

IWG is a mid-transition business caught between two models. The legacy lease-arbitrage business generates most of today's revenue but carries $6.2bn of lease liabilities against $93m of equity. The asset-light franchise model is the future but accounts for only $79m of fee income on $3.7bn of revenue. The stock at 241p prices in substantial success on the transition without offering adequate compensation for the execution risk or the balance sheet fragility.

The core problem: **I cannot underwrite either path with confidence.**

If the asset-light pivot succeeds, IWG becomes a high-ROIC franchise royalty stream and the stock is cheap. If it stalls or traditional office markets recover (reducing landlord desperation), IWG is a structurally impaired legacy operator with thin equity and $6bn+ of lease obligations that cannot be easily unwound. The current price sits in the middle, demanding I make a precise call on a transition that is 2-3 years from proof.

---

## Key Drivers (The 1-3 Things That Actually Matter)

1. **Fee Income Trajectory** - The entire bull case depends on growing fee income from $79m to $300m+ over 3-5 years. This is the single metric that determines whether IWG transforms or stagnates. Current run rate is insufficient to offset any deterioration in the owned portfolio.

2. **Landlord Partnership Availability** - IWG's asset-light growth depends on landlords wanting to outsource flex operations. This is counter-cyclical: high office vacancy (currently 18-20%) creates partnership supply. If traditional office recovers, landlord desperation fades and IWG's growth engine stalls.

3. **True Free Cash Flow** - Not EBITDA, not pre-IFRS 16 EBITDA. The $140m FCF target for FY25 is the real measure of cash available after $1.2bn+ of annual lease servicing. This is a 4.5% FCF yield on a leveraged, cyclical business - inadequate margin of safety.

## The Setup

IWG is the survivor of a brutal industry consolidation. WeWork destroyed itself with reckless expansion. COVID culled weak operators. CBRE verticalizing into Industrious signals the endgame: landlords want to own flex economics directly.

IWG responded by pivoting to asset-light franchising - essentially becoming a licensor of the Regus/Spaces brands rather than an operator. This is strategically rational. But it creates a 3-5 year transition period where the company generates neither the high margins of a fully-owned portfolio nor the high returns of a pure franchise model.

The balance sheet is the constraint. $6.2bn of lease liabilities against $93m of equity means IWG cannot absorb much adversity. The SPV structure provides some liability isolation, but wholesale defaults would destroy the franchise value. Management is running buybacks ($130m for FY25) on a balance sheet this levered - aggressive.

## Positioning and Portfolio Logic

**Why not now:** The risk/reward at 241p does not compensate for the execution uncertainty. I am being asked to pay 29x normalized earnings for a cyclical, leveraged business mid-transition, with no margin of safety if the pivot stalls.

## Summary

IWG is a plausible long-term compounder if the asset-light transition succeeds. It is also a potential value trap if the transition stalls while the legacy business atrophies. At 241p, the market is pricing in meaningful success without offering compensation for the very real possibility of failure.

The specialist analyses were thorough but did not surface a variant view that would give me edge. The bull case is coherent (scale + asset-light = high ROIC), but so is the bear case (commoditization + landlord disintermediation + balance sheet fragility = margin compression). Without a differentiated insight into which path is more likely, and without a price that offers margin of safety, this is a pass.

**Final assessment: Too Hard.** The investment case requires precision on execution timing that I cannot deliver. Revisit when either (a) price offers margin of safety below 150p, or (b) fee income trajectory provides 2+ quarters of proof at 40%+ growth.
