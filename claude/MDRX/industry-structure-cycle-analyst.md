# MDRX (Veradigm) — Industry Structure & Cycle Analysis

## Health IT / Health Data Analytics Industry

---

## The 1-3 Things That Matter

### 1. The Industry Is Consolidating Around Scale Players — Veradigm Is a Target, Not a Consolidator

The EHR and health data market has seen massive consolidation:
- Oracle acquired Cerner for $28B (2022)
- Bain/Hellman & Friedman acquired athenahealth for $17B
- Microsoft acquired Nuance for $19.7B
- Epic dominates hospitals (~38% share) and is expanding to ambulatory

Veradigm at ~3.6% ambulatory EHR share is subscale. The strategic alternatives process attracted 30+ parties but zero final offers. This suggests the asset is either: (a) too messy to buy right now (restatement), or (b) not compelling enough at the ask.

### 2. Real-World Data Is a Secular Growth Market — But Veradigm Is Losing Ground

The RWD/RWE market is growing rapidly as pharma companies increasingly use real-world clinical data for drug development, regulatory submissions, and commercial analytics.

| Player | RWD Position | Scale |
|--------|-------------|-------|
| IQVIA | Dominant (33% health analytics share) | $15.7B+ revenue, 24% EBITDA margins |
| Optum | Large (25% share) | UHG ecosystem, claims + clinical |
| HealthVerity/Datavant | Growing | Data connectivity platforms |
| Veradigm | Niche | ~$585M total revenue, declining |

Veradigm's clinical data from EHR workflows is genuinely differentiated vs. claims-based data. But the company can't capitalize while consumed by governance issues.

### 3. TEFCA Is Structurally Reducing EHR Switching Costs

The Trusted Exchange Framework and Common Agreement (TEFCA) has exchanged nearly 500M health records as of early 2026 (vs. 10M in Jan 2025). HHS projects $19.2B in administrative cost savings over the next decade.

**Impact on Veradigm:**
- **Negative for provider moat:** Easier data portability = easier to switch EHR vendors
- **Mixed for data business:** More data flowing could benefit analytics, but also commoditizes the data connectivity that was Veradigm's edge
- 55% of health information networks report EMR vendor information blocking — regulatory pressure is actively dismantling vendor lock-in

## Industry Economics

| Metric | EHR/Provider | Health Data/Analytics |
|--------|-------------|---------------------|
| Growth rate | Low single-digit (mature) | High single-digit to low double-digit |
| Margins | Moderate (15-25% EBITDA) | Higher (25-35% EBITDA for leaders) |
| Switching costs | High but declining (TEFCA) | Moderate (data integrations) |
| Cyclicality | Low (healthcare = non-discretionary) | Low-moderate (pharma R&D budgets) |
| Capital intensity | Moderate (software development) | Low (data assets) |

Healthcare IT spending growing at ~14% CAGR through 2030 (market expected to reach $397B). But 85%+ of health systems are prioritizing cybersecurity and EHR modernization — benefiting Epic/Oracle more than Veradigm.

---

## Cycle Position

This is not a cyclical industry in the traditional sense. Healthcare IT spending is relatively insulated from economic cycles. However:
- **Pharma R&D budgets** (which drive data/analytics revenue) can be cyclically sensitive
- **Post-COVID digital health spending surge** has normalized
- Hospital IT budgets for 2026 are mixed (some expanding, some flat/cutting)

Veradigm's current weakness is company-specific (governance, attrition), not cycle-driven.

---

## Structural Risk: AI Disruption

Oracle's new AI-powered EHR (built from scratch on Oracle Cloud Infrastructure, launched Aug 2025) represents a generational threat to legacy EHR vendors. If Oracle succeeds, smaller EHR vendors like Veradigm face existential pressure on their provider business.
