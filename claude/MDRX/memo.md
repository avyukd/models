## Key Drivers (The 1-3 Things That Matter)

1. **Restatement completion and relisting timeline.** FY2022 10-K was filed March 2025. FY2023 and FY2024 are outstanding. Relisting expected 2026. At $4, the stock prices in a meaningful probability that relisting never happens or that the franchise is permanently broken. Any concrete progress on filings is a catalyst.

2. **Customer attrition trajectory.** Large physician group losses drove FY2024 revenue down ~6% and EBITDA down ~38%. If attrition stabilizes -- or even just decelerates -- the operating leverage works in reverse on the way back up. If it accelerates, the data flywheel breaks and the equity is worth materially less. This is the load-bearing variable for the business.

3. **SEC investigation resolution.** Unknown liability, but at $4 the stock is pricing in a very bad outcome. A settlement (even a painful one) removes the tail risk that keeps institutional capital away.

## Thesis in 3-5 Bullets

- **At $4, the market is pricing MDRX for permanent impairment or terminal decline.** 6.4-6.8x EBITDA with net cash on a recurring-revenue healthcare IT business implies the market expects continued revenue erosion, material litigation losses, or both. This is distressed pricing on a business that generates $85M+ of EBITDA.

- **The restatement costs are genuinely temporary -- and they are the primary reason EBITDA collapsed.** The swing from $140M EBITDA (FY2023) to $87M (FY2024) on only ~6% revenue decline is almost entirely restatement-related advisory, legal, and audit costs. Once filings are current, $20-40M of annual costs fall away. This is not an assumption -- it is mechanical.

- **Relisting is the decompression catalyst.** OTC status excludes ~90% of institutional capital. Index inclusion is impossible. Analyst coverage is zero. The moment MDRX relists, it becomes investable for a universe of buyers who literally cannot own it today. This is a structural supply/demand imbalance that resolves in one direction.

- **The failed strategic review at $11 is not the same signal at $4.** Buyers walked at 18-19x EBITDA during a chaotic period with incomplete filings. At 6-7x EBITDA with FY2022 filed and FY2023/24 presumably closer to done, the bid/ask dynamics are entirely different. A re-engagement of M&A interest post-relisting is plausible.

- **The data asset, while eroding, is not yet impaired below replacement cost.** EHR-connected clinical data flowing to life sciences customers is genuinely differentiated vs. claims-based data (IQVIA, Optum). The provider base is shrinking but still generates ~$585M of revenue. This is not a zero.

## The Setup

Veradigm is a subscale (~3.6% ambulatory EHR share) health IT company whose differentiated asset -- clinical data flowing from EHR workflows to life sciences and payer customers -- is genuinely valuable but under stress. A $40M revenue overstatement (FY2020-21) triggered a multi-year restatement, NASDAQ delisting, SEC investigation, management turnover, and a failed strategic review.

The stock has fallen from $11 to $4 since the prior memo, a ~64% decline. The business has not deteriorated proportionally. Revenue is ~$585M and guided flat. EBITDA is $85-90M but depressed by restatement costs. Net cash is ~$45M. The company is run by interim management (CEO Tom Langan, CFO Lee Westerfield) with a refreshed board including activist-appointed directors.

The industry is consolidating (Epic, Oracle Health, athenahealth, IQVIA) and TEFCA is reducing EHR switching costs. Veradigm's competitive position is weakening structurally. But the question at $4 is not "is this a great business?" -- it is "is this a $575M EV business or a $1B+ EV business once the governance overhang clears?"

## Numbers That Matter

- **Revenue:** ~$585M, flat-to-declining. ~80% recurring. Provider attrition is the key risk.
- **Normalized EBITDA:** $85-100M (14-17% margin). Could recover to $110-120M as restatement costs ($20-40M/yr) roll off.
- **True FCF:** $40-60M normalized after capex ($30-40M). 6.5-10% yield at current EV.
- **Cash:** $120M with $75M debt = $45M net cash. Adequate but declining. Monitor closely.
- **Valuation:** 6.4-6.8x EV/EBITDA on depressed earnings. On cost-normalized EBITDA of $110M+, this is 5.2x. Healthcare IT comps trade at 12-20x.
- **Share count:** ~155M (up from 110M in 2022 due to convertible note conversion). Further dilution possible but unlikely near-term.

**What the current price implies:** At 6.5x EBITDA, the market is pricing in either (a) continued EBITDA declines to $60-70M, (b) material litigation losses ($100M+), or (c) a meaningful probability of the company not surviving as a going concern. We think all three of these are too pessimistic.

## Scenario Framework

**Bull (25% probability): $8-12/share (100-200% upside)**
Filings completed H1 2026, relisting by Q3 2026, SEC settles for $20-40M, customer attrition stabilizes, permanent CEO hired. Restatement costs roll off, EBITDA recovers to $110-120M. Stock re-rates to 10-12x EBITDA on a listed basis. Alternatively, strategic buyer re-engages at 8-10x post-relisting.

**Base (45% probability): $5-7/share (25-75% upside)**
Filings drag into late 2026, relisting early 2027. Modest customer attrition continues. SEC investigation lingers but settles within manageable range. EBITDA stabilizes at $85-95M. Stock re-rates modestly as OTC overhang partially lifts with filing progress.

**Bear (30% probability): $1-3/share (25-75% downside)**
SEC enforcement is material ($75M+ or consent decree with operational restrictions). Customer attrition accelerates. Data asset falls below critical mass. Cash position forces dilutive capital raise. Franchise enters terminal decline.

**Expected value: ~$5.50-6.50/share (35-65% upside).** The asymmetry is favorable. The bull case is 2-3x; the bear case is -50-75%. This is a positive expected value bet with a defined downside (the company has net cash and generates positive EBITDA even in the stress case).

## Positioning and Portfolio Logic

**Why this deserves capital now:** The stock has de-rated 64% from $11 to $4 while the business has not deteriorated proportionally. The restatement process is advancing (FY2022 10-K filed). The risk/reward at 6.5x EBITDA with net cash is asymmetric to the upside if any of the catalysts (filing progress, relisting, SEC resolution) materialize. This is a special situation where you are being compensated for governance uncertainty that is time-bounded.

**Size:** 1-2% position. This is an event-driven, illiquid, OTC special situation with meaningful tail risk. It does not warrant a core position. But it warrants capital because the expected value is clearly positive and the catalysts are identifiable.

**Hedging:** No direct hedge available (OTC, no liquid options). Portfolio-level hedge via short exposure to overvalued health IT (e.g., high-multiple SaaS health IT names) could provide partial offset. Size discipline is the primary risk management tool.

**Post-entry monitoring:**
- Filing progress (FY2023 10-K is the next gate)
- SEC investigation disclosures
- Provider count / revenue trends in any operational updates
- Cash balance trajectory
- Management hires
- Any resumed M&A activity or activist filings

## What Changed From the Prior Memo

The prior memo (at $11) rated MDRX as **Neutral (Too Hard)**. The key difference is price.

At $11 (18-19x EBITDA), the relisting catalyst was partially priced, the valuation offered no margin of safety, and the risk/reward was unattractive. Every concern in the prior memo was valid.

At $4 (6.4-6.8x EBITDA), the same concerns exist but you are being compensated for them. The business has not changed; the price has. The prior memo's condition #5 -- "stock trading at 10-12x EBITDA ($7-9/share) where risk/reward compensates" -- has been exceeded to the downside. The market has overshot.

The failed strategic review, SEC risk, customer attrition, and governance chaos are all real. But at $4, they are priced. The question is no longer "is this a good business?" but "is the market right that this is worth less than 7x EBITDA?" We think the answer is no.
