# SNWV Industry Analysis: Low-Frequency Ultrasound Wound Therapy

## The One Industry Factor That Matters

**The CMS skin substitute reimbursement collapse is reshaping the entire wound care competitive landscape—and Sanuwave's CPT 97610 reimbursement is structurally insulated from this disruption.**

The critical industry dynamic: CMS is cutting skin substitute reimbursement by ~90% effective January 2026 (from ASP-based pricing to flat $127/cm2). Medicare skin substitute spending exploded from $256M (2019) to $10B+ (2024)—CMS is now crushing that spend. This creates massive dislocation for Organogenesis, MiMedx, and the ~200+ placental/amniotic products in this market.

**Why this matters for SNWV**: UltraMIST is billed under CPT 97610 (procedural code), not as a skin substitute supply. CMS confirmed 97610 rates for 2026 remain "within $2-4 of 2025 rates"—less than 1% change. While competitors face existential reimbursement pressure, Sanuwave's procedural revenue stream is unaffected.

**What to monitor**:
- Wound care center economics—will HOPDs gain volume as office-based skin substitute providers exit?
- Healogics partnership traction (600+ centers)—distribution into this disrupted channel
- Any CMS reclassification attempts on CPT 97610

---

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**Consensus view**: SNWV is a small wound care device company competing in a crowded market.

**The variant insight**: The skin substitute reimbursement disaster is not a headwind but a **massive channel opportunity**.

The second half of 2025 saw "intense transition in the wound care space as reductions to reimbursement rates for skin-substitutes and allografts profoundly reshaped [the] market" (SNWV CEO). While Organogenesis saw 21-23% revenue declines in H1 2025, SNWV posted record quarters.

**Why the market may miss this**:
1. Most investors bucket all "wound care" together—but reimbursement structures differ dramatically between supplies (ASP-based) and procedures (fee schedule)
2. Healogics iSupply partnership timing (late 2025) positions SNWV exactly when wound centers need alternatives to now-uneconomic skin substitutes
3. The ~$10B in Medicare skin substitute spend doesn't disappear—wounds still need treatment. Some volume shifts to procedural alternatives like UltraMIST.

**Directional impact**: Bullish for SNWV's competitive positioning within wound care, though overall industry economics for small players remain challenging.

## Cyclicality Assessment: LOW

**Justification**: Wound care demand is driven by chronic disease prevalence (diabetes, obesity, vascular disease) and demographics (aging population), not economic cycles.

| Factor | Cyclical Sensitivity |
|--------|---------------------|
| Demand drivers | Non-cyclical (disease prevalence, demographics) |
| Pricing/reimbursement | Regulatory-driven (CMS), not market-driven |
| Capital intensity | Low (consumables model, not heavy equipment) |
| Recession correlation | Minimal—chronic wounds require treatment regardless of economy |

**Historical evidence**: Wound care spending has grown through every recession since 2000. Medicare wound care spending is on secular growth trajectory ($22.5B annually).

---

## Current Cycle Position: N/A (Regulatory Inflection, Not Economic Cycle)

The wound care industry is not experiencing a traditional business cycle—it's experiencing a **regulatory regime change**:

| Phase | Description | Current Status |
|-------|-------------|----------------|
| Skin substitute boom | 2019-2024 | Complete—40x spending growth ($256M to $10B) |
| CMS crackdown | 2025-2026 | Active—90% reimbursement cuts finalized |
| Market restructuring | 2026+ | Beginning—channel and treatment mix shifting |

**For UltraMIST specifically**: Mid-cycle economics. Razor/blade model with growing installed base (155 systems sold Q3 2025, record quarter). Consumables 55-65% of revenue. This is the steady-state business model.
