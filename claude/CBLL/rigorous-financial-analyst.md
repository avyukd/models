# Ceribell (CBLL) -- Financial Analysis

**Date:** 2026-02-24 | **Price:** $19.90 | **Market Cap:** $738M | **Shares:** ~37.1M

## Business Financial Profile

AI-powered point-of-care EEG platform for acute care settings. Revenue model is hybrid: product sales (headsets/electrodes, ~75% of revenue) plus subscription (AI software/cloud, ~25%). High gross margins (88%) suggest strong pricing power and low marginal cost of goods. Capital-light -- FY2024 capex was only $1.3M. Fixed cost base is heavy in S&M and R&D headcount; operating leverage should emerge as revenue scales against a more slowly growing cost base. Low cyclicality (hospital neurology is non-discretionary). Revenue is transactional (per-use consumables) with a recurring subscription layer, creating decent visibility once accounts are onboarded.

## Income Statement Analysis

**Quarterly Revenue Trajectory (2025):**
| Quarter | Revenue | YoY Growth |
|---------|---------|------------|
| Q1 2025 | $20.5M  | ~38%       |
| Q2 2025 | $21.2M  | ~38%       |
| Q3 2025 | $22.6M  | ~31%       |
| Q4 2025 | $24.8M  | ~34%       |
| **FY 2025** | **$89.1M** | **36%** |

FY2024 revenue was $65.5M (+45% YoY). Growth is decelerating from 45% to 36% to guided 25-29% for FY2026. This is natural for a company scaling past $100M but worth monitoring -- the deceleration curve matters for valuation.

**Revenue Mix (Q4 2025):** Products $18.8M (+33%), Subscription $6.0M (+37%). Subscription growing faster is a positive signal for revenue quality.

**Margins:** FY2025 gross margin of 88% is exceptional for medtech and among the highest in the sector. This reflects a consumable/SaaS hybrid with minimal COGS. Total opex of $136.7M grew 42% vs 36% revenue growth -- **negative operating leverage in FY2025**. This is the central financial tension.

**SBC:** $12.2M in FY2025 (13.7% of revenue). Elevated for a company this size but expected post-IPO. Trend: $3.3M in Q4 alone, implying an annualized run-rate of ~$13M+ and likely rising as public company comp structures mature.

**GAAP Net Loss:** $53.4M (FY2025) vs $40.5M (FY2024). Losses are widening in absolute terms even as the loss per share narrowed from $3.39 to $1.46 due to IPO share count increase.

## Cash Flow and Normalized Earnings

**Approximate FY2025 Cash Bridge:**
- Net loss: ($53.4M)
- Add back: SBC $12.2M
- Add back: D&A (est. ~$2-3M)
- Working capital changes: unknown from press release
- **Estimated operating cash burn: ~($38-40M)**
- Capex: minimal (~$1-2M based on FY2024 pattern)
- **Estimated FCF: ~($39-42M)**

Cash declined from $194.4M (year-end 2024) to $159.3M (year-end 2025), a $35.1M decrease. This is consistent with ~$38-40M operating burn offset by interest income on the cash balance (~$5-7M at current rates).

**Normalized earnings are not applicable.** This company has no positive earnings to normalize. The relevant question is: when does the P&L cross over?

## Path to Profitability Model

**Assumptions (conservative):**
- FY2026 revenue: $113M (midpoint of guidance)
- FY2027 revenue: $140M (assumes ~24% growth, continued deceleration)
- Gross margin holds at 87-88%
- Opex growth decelerates to 15-20% as S&M scales more efficiently

**Illustrative P&L:**
| Item | FY2025 | FY2026E | FY2027E |
|------|--------|---------|---------|
| Revenue | $89.1M | $113M | $140M |
| Gross Profit (88%) | $78.4M | $99.4M | $123.2M |
| Opex | $136.7M | $157M | $180M |
| Operating Loss | ($58.3M) | ($57.6M) | ($56.8M) |

Even with opex growth slowing to 15%, breakeven requires revenue closer to $180-200M (likely FY2028-2029). If opex growth can be held to 10%, breakeven accelerates to ~$170M revenue. The company needs roughly 2x current revenue at current cost structure to break even on an operating basis. **Cash-flow breakeven (adding back SBC) could arrive ~$150-160M revenue, potentially late 2027.**

## Balance Sheet and Financial Risk

- Cash/investments: $159.3M. No debt mentioned in filings.
- At ~$35-40M annual burn, runway is approximately 4 years even without revenue growth improvements.
- No near-term dilution risk from capital raises -- the balance sheet can fund operations through expected profitability.
- Dilution from SBC: ~37.1M shares outstanding. At $12M+ SBC annually, this represents ~2-3% annual dilution at current prices.

## Sales Efficiency and Unit Economics

- **Active accounts:** 647 (year-end 2025), up from 529 (year-end 2024). Net adds: 118 accounts in FY2025.
- **Revenue per active account:** $89.1M / ~588 avg accounts = ~$152K/account. This is a strong figure and suggests solid per-hospital monetization.
- **Implied account economics:** At 88% gross margin, each account generates ~$134K gross profit. If fully loaded customer acquisition cost is $200-250K (estimating from the S&M spend), payback is under 2 years -- attractive unit economics.
- **Net revenue retention:** Not disclosed, but revenue growing faster than account growth (36% vs 22%) implies meaningful same-store expansion, likely from increased utilization at existing accounts.

## Rule of 40 Analysis

FY2025: Revenue growth 36% + FCF margin (~-44%) = **Rule of 40 score: approximately -8.** Not passing. However, at the gross margin level: 36% + 88% gross margin is extraordinary -- the issue is entirely in opex discipline, not business model quality.

## Valuation Framework

**Current:** $738M market cap / $89.1M revenue = 8.3x trailing. On FY2026 guided revenue ($113M midpoint) = 6.5x forward.

**Medtech comps context:** High-growth medtech with 85%+ gross margins and 25-35% revenue growth typically trades 8-15x forward revenue. Companies like Shockwave (pre-acquisition), Inspire Medical, and Axonics at similar stages traded 10-15x. CBLL at 6.5x forward is at the lower end of this range, reflecting pre-profitability risk and smaller scale.

**What must be true for current valuation:** At $738M, the market implies this business reaches ~$50-60M in steady-state operating income (8-10% operating margin on ~$500M+ revenue, discounted back, or a nearer-term path to $25-30M operating income at a growth multiple). This requires: (1) continued 20%+ growth for several years, (2) opex leverage emerging by FY2027, and (3) no significant competitive disruption. These are plausible but not certain.

**What breaks:** If growth decelerates below 20% before profitability, the stock re-rates sharply lower. The 42% opex growth in FY2025 vs 36% revenue growth is the wrong direction -- one more year of this pattern and the market will lose patience.

## Key Financial Drivers

1. **Opex leverage is the single most important variable.** Gross margins are already best-in-class. The entire path to profitability depends on whether S&M and G&A can grow at 10-15% while revenue grows 25%+. FY2025 showed negative operating leverage (42% opex growth vs 36% revenue growth). This must reverse.

2. **Account growth and same-store revenue expansion.** 118 net new accounts in FY2025 on a base of 529 is solid. Revenue per account expanding implies the land-and-expand model works. If net adds slow or per-account revenue plateaus, the growth story breaks.

3. **New product pipeline (delirium, neonatal, stroke).** These represent optionality -- management's $1.5B+ incremental TAM claim is unverifiable but the FDA clearances are real catalysts for expanding use cases per account.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**The same-store expansion signal is underappreciated.** Revenue grew 36% while accounts grew 22%, implying ~14 points of growth came from existing accounts increasing utilization. This is the hallmark of a sticky, expanding platform rather than a one-product company. If Ceribell can layer delirium and neonatal monitoring onto the installed base, the revenue per account ($152K currently) could expand materially without proportional S&M spend -- this is where the operating leverage would actually come from, not from slowing hiring. The Street models likely focus on account additions; the real driver is wallet share expansion within hospitals already using the platform.

The offsetting risk: FY2025 opex growth of 42% included elevated legal costs (mentioned in the earnings release), which may be non-recurring. If legal costs normalize, FY2026 could show a meaningful step-change in operating leverage that the current run-rate overstates.
