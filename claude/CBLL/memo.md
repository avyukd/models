# Ceribell (CBLL) -- Investment Decision Memo

**Date:** 2026-02-24 | **Price:** $19.90 (AH: $20.29) | **Market Cap:** $738M

## 1. Recommendation

- **Decision:** Buy
- **Conviction:** Medium
- **Time Horizon:** 6-12 months
- **Classification:** Tactical (with optionality upgrade path)
- **Position Size:** 1-2% -- tight float and illiquidity cap sizing

## 2. Key Drivers (The 3 Things That Matter)

1. **Same-store revenue expansion.** 14 points of FY2025's 36% growth came from existing accounts increasing utilization. This is the mechanism for operating leverage and the variable the Street is likely underweighting. New FDA indications (delirium, neonatal) amplify this by expanding use cases within hospitals already trained on the platform.

2. **Regulatory exclusivity window.** First-and-only FDA clearances in delirium monitoring and neonatal seizure detection create a 2-3 year head start before competitors can replicate clinical evidence. This is a depreciating asset -- it matters how fast Ceribell converts it into installed base before Natus/Nihon Kohden close the gap.

3. **Opex inflection.** FY2025 showed negative operating leverage (42% opex growth vs 36% revenue). FY2025 included elevated legal costs that may be non-recurring. FY2026 is the year this must reverse. If opex growth drops to 15-20% against 25-29% revenue growth, the path to cash-flow breakeven (late 2027) becomes visible.

## 3. Thesis in 5 Bullets

- The market has not yet priced the triple FDA clearance news (filed at 4:23 PM ET today). On a tight 23.8M share float with 289K average daily volume, the reaction tomorrow could be outsized.
- Same-store expansion (14pp of growth) is the underappreciated driver. Street models likely anchor on account additions. The real story is wallet share per hospital expanding as new indications layer onto existing workflows -- this is where operating leverage actually emerges.
- At 6.5x FY2026 revenue ($113M midpoint), CBLL trades at the low end of high-growth medtech comps (8-15x for 25%+ growers with 85%+ gross margins). The discount reflects pre-profitability risk, which is fair, but the gap is too wide given the quality of the gross margin profile.
- The $1.5B incremental TAM from new clearances is aspirational, but even partial conversion on the delirium and neonatal indications would meaningfully expand revenue per account beyond the current $152K.
- Cash position ($159.3M, no debt) provides 3-4 years of runway with no near-term dilution risk, removing the existential funding overhang that kills many pre-profit medtech stories.

## 5. The Setup

Ceribell operates a razor/blade AI-powered EEG platform in acute care. The business is high-quality at the gross margin level (88%) with attractive unit economics (~2-year payback per hospital). The company just received three FDA clearances simultaneously -- delirium monitoring (first-and-only), neonatal seizure detection (first-and-only across all ages), and Breakthrough Device Designation for stroke. This news dropped after market close today. The stock trades on a tight float (23.8M shares, 289K avg volume) with moderate short interest (6.8%).

The structural tension: this is a strong product in a competitive market. Natus/Persyst (embedded in 97 of the top 100 neuro hospitals) and Nihon Kohden are credible threats in core seizure detection. Ceribell's edge is not that it has a better mousetrap in seizures -- it is that delirium and neonatal are uncontested categories where it is the only FDA-cleared player.

## 6. Numbers That Matter

- **FY2026 guided revenue:** $111-115M (+25-29%). At midpoint, 6.5x forward revenue.
- **Gross margin:** 87-88%. Management flagged potential mid-80s% in 2026 due to tariffs -- monitor but not structural.
- **Revenue per account:** $152K and expanding. This is the number to watch.
- **Cash burn:** ~$35-40M/year. At $159M cash, no capital raise needed before profitability.
- **Cash-flow breakeven:** ~$150-160M revenue (adding back SBC), plausibly late 2027.
- **Valuation range:** At 8-10x FY2026 revenue (mid-range for high-growth medtech), fair value is $24-30/share. Current price implies ~6.5x, offering 20-50% upside to fair multiple.
- **Sensitivity:** If growth decelerates below 20% before breakeven, re-rate to 4-5x revenue ($12-15/share). This is the downside case.

## 8. Risks and Kill Switches

**Kill switch 1: Opex leverage fails to materialize.** If FY2026 shows continued negative operating leverage (opex growth exceeding revenue growth), the path to profitability extends and the cash runway becomes a binding constraint. Exit if Q2 2026 still shows opex growing faster than revenue after normalizing for legal costs.

**Kill switch 2: Competitive clearance in delirium/neonatal.** If Natus or Nihon Kohden receives FDA clearance for delirium monitoring or neonatal seizure detection, Ceribell's primary moat erodes. This would warrant immediate re-evaluation.

**Kill switch 3: Same-store growth decelerates.** If revenue per account flattens or declines, the thesis breaks. The entire operating leverage argument depends on wallet share expansion.

**Permanent loss risk:** Low, given $159M cash and no debt. The downside is multiple compression and a grinding path to lower prices, not bankruptcy.

**Moderate concern: Moat is time-limited.** The moat analyst correctly flagged this as moderate and depreciating. Ceribell is in a race to convert regulatory exclusivity into installed base before incumbents catch up. This is execution-dependent, which caps conviction at medium.

## 9. Positioning and Portfolio Logic

**Why now:** The triple FDA clearance news has not been priced by the market (after-hours). On a tight float with thin volume, the setup is favorable for a tactical entry. The stock is down 19% over the past year and trades at the low end of medtech comps despite best-in-class gross margins.

**Size:** 1-2% position. Illiquidity (289K daily volume) and pre-profitability status constrain sizing. This is not a core position until opex leverage is demonstrated.

**Upgrade path:** If Q1-Q2 2026 show positive operating leverage and same-store expansion accelerating from new indications, this could become a 3-4% core position.

**Hedge:** No direct pair trade needed. The position is self-hedged by the kill switches and tight sizing. If concerned about medtech sector risk, a small short in an overvalued, decelerating medtech name would offset.

**Post-entry monitoring:** (1) Revenue per account trend quarterly, (2) Opex growth rate vs revenue growth rate, (3) Competitor FDA filings in delirium/neonatal, (4) Gross margin trajectory (tariff impact), (5) Cash burn rate.

**Bottom line:** CBLL is a medium-conviction buy on a catalyst setup (unpriced FDA news, tight float) with a credible medium-term thesis (same-store expansion driving operating leverage). The variant view is that the market models this as an account-addition story when it is actually a wallet-share-expansion story -- and the new FDA clearances amplify exactly that mechanism. The moat is moderate and depreciating, which caps conviction and sizing, but the risk/reward at 6.5x forward revenue with 88% gross margins and 3+ years of funded runway is attractive.
