## 2. Key Drivers (The 1-3 Things That Matter)

**1. U.S. multi-listed options volume growth.** This is the single most important variable. MIAX is a pure-play on the volume of multi-listed options contracts traded in the U.S. Industry ADV hit 60.4M contracts in 2025 (+26% YoY), the sixth consecutive record. If volumes normalize toward mid-cycle (45-55M ADV), MIAX's revenue growth rate halves and operating leverage works in reverse. If the secular 15% CAGR persists, the stock works.

**2. GAAP margin inflection -- real or mirage?** MIAX has been approximately breakeven at the GAAP operating level for three consecutive years (2022-2024) despite 19% annual net revenue growth. Adjusted EBITDA margin expanded from 27% to 44% (Q3 2024 to Q3 2025), but the $142M gap between adjusted earnings ($39.9M) and GAAP net loss ($102.1M) in Q3 2025 is among the most extreme I have seen. Until the 10-K reveals the precise SBC burden, capex, and FCF, we cannot determine whether this business earns $1.30/share or $0.70/share on a normalized basis.

**3. Market share durability post-ERP expiration.** MIAX's 15-17% multi-listed share was built through Equity Rights Programs (2013-2024) that gave major market makers equity in exchange for volume commitments. All ERP earning periods expired by June 2024. Share has held at 17%+ through 2025, but this has not been tested in a flat or declining volume environment. The 2025 volume surge masks whether share is genuinely sticky or inertia-driven.

## 3. Thesis Summary

There is no trade here today. The reasons:

- **The normalized earnings power is unknowable with current disclosures.** The GAAP-to-adjusted gap is $142M in a single quarter. SBC magnitude, FCF, and capex are not disclosed in sufficient detail. The first 10-K (expected March 2026) will be the first comprehensive standalone financial disclosure.

- **The stock prices in margin expansion that has never been demonstrated in GAAP results.** At $40/share, the market implies MIAX reaches 30%+ GAAP operating margins and sustains 15%+ revenue growth. GAAP operating margins have been ~0% for three years running.

- **No proprietary products means structurally capped margins.** MIAX competes exclusively in multi-listed options where every contract trades on 17 other venues. Cboe earns 65%+ EBITDA margins because SPX/VIX can only trade there. MIAX's ceiling is structurally lower, probably 35-40% adjusted EBITDA at mid-cycle, vs. the 44% currently being reported at peak volumes.

- **The franchise is real.** Four options exchanges, 17% market share, in-house technology, clean post-IPO balance sheet with $400M+ net cash. This is not a bad business -- it is a good business at an uncertain price with critical data missing.

- **Catalysts resolve the uncertainty quickly.** Q4 earnings (Feb 25) and the 10-K (March) will reveal SBC, FCF, and normalized cost structure. There is no reason to take a position 48 hours before an earnings release when the key unknowns resolve imminently.

## 5. The Setup

MIAX is the fourth-largest U.S. options exchange family, operating four options exchanges, one equities exchange, and a futures exchange. It IPO'd in August 2025 at $23/share and now trades at $40, a 75% gain in six months.

The structural context matters:
- **Industry tailwind is real.** U.S. options volume has compounded at 15% annually since 2016, driven by 0DTE proliferation, retail adoption, and ETF options expansion. This is secular, not cyclical. But 2025's 26% growth is above trend and likely unsustainable.
- **MIAX has no proprietary products.** Every contract that trades on MIAX can trade on 17 other venues. This is the defining structural fact. Cboe's SPX/VIX franchise is an unassailable moat. MIAX's technology advantage is real but depreciating and replicable.
- **Near-zero switching costs.** Market makers route flow via smart order routers that can redirect across venues in milliseconds. MIAX's customer base is concentrated among fewer than 10-15 major market-making firms. If Citadel or Susquehanna shift routing, the impact is immediate.
- **Venue proliferation never reverses.** 18 venues today, IEX targeting a 19th by late 2026. Incremental entrants compress per-contract economics for everyone.

## 8. Risks & Kill Switches

**Thesis invalidators:**
- GAAP operating margins remain at or below breakeven for FY2025 despite 40%+ revenue growth. This would mean the cost structure is permanently misaligned.
- Market share drops below 14% for two consecutive quarters, indicating ERP-era share is eroding.
- SBC disclosed at >$150M annualized, meaning adjusted metrics are grossly overstating economics.

**Permanent loss scenarios:**
- Options volume growth stalls (ADV drops below 45M sustained) while MIAX simultaneously loses share. Operating leverage works violently in reverse.
- Founder-CEO deploys $400M cash on value-destructive acquisitions. TISE at $91.5M with minimal revenue contribution is a warning shot.
- Regulatory reform (ORF, maker-taker restrictions) redistributes exchange revenue in ways that disadvantage smaller exchange families.

**Re-evaluation triggers:**
- Any acquisition above $100M
- Volume normalization below 50M ADV for 3+ months
- Citadel or other major ERP warrant holder liquidating position

## 9. Positioning & Portfolio Logic

**No position today.** This is a timing call, not a quality call.

The franchise is real -- a 17% share options exchange with in-house technology, clean balance sheet, and secular volume tailwinds is an attractive asset. But the critical data needed to underwrite normalized earnings power arrives in the next 30-60 days (Q4 earnings Feb 25, 10-K March).

**What moves this out of the too-hard pile:**
1. The 10-K reveals SBC <$100M annualized and FCF >$120M. This would confirm adjusted metrics are directionally reliable and normalized EPS is in the $1.00-1.30 range.
2. GAAP operating margins turn meaningfully positive in Q4 2025 (>10%), proving the breakeven era is ending.
3. Market share holds above 16% through a volume normalization (ADV <55M) -- proving the franchise is durable post-ERP.

**What makes this a short:**
1. SBC >$150M annualized, FCF negative or negligible -- the adjusted metrics are fiction.
2. Market share erodes below 14% as ERP inertia fades.
3. Gallagher announces a large acquisition ($200M+) financed with equity, confirming empire-building over per-share value creation.

**If forced to take a position today:** Small short bias (1% position). The stock has nearly doubled from its IPO price in 6 months, trades at 34x EV/EBITDA vs. 18-20x for mature peers, has never demonstrated positive GAAP operating margins, and the adjusted-to-GAAP gap is extreme. But the secular volume tailwind and clean balance sheet make this a dangerous short, so sizing would be minimal.

**What I would watch post-entry:** Monthly MIAX market share data, OCC industry volume reports, and the 10-K SBC disclosure. These three data points determine whether MIAX is a $60 stock or a $25 stock over the next 12-18 months.
