## Key Upfront Finding

MIAX is a recently-IPO'd (Aug 2025) exchange operator trading at a significant premium to peers on trailing metrics, but the headline GAAP numbers are severely distorted by one-time IPO costs, debt extinguishment charges, and the ramp of SBC post-IPO. The market is pricing in sustained 40%+ volume growth and margin expansion toward peer levels. The central question is whether MIAX can close the operating margin gap with incumbents (MIAX ~13% operating margin vs. NDAQ ~54%) as it scales, or whether structural cost disadvantages persist.

---

## 1. Business Financial Profile

**Classification:** Low capital intensity (technology/platform), high fixed cost structure, significant operating leverage, moderate cyclicality (tied to options market volumes), high revenue quality (transaction-based but recurring in nature given exchange network effects).

**Revenue Model:** MIAX reports gross revenue that includes transaction fees, then deducts rebates/liquidity payments as "cost of revenue." The economically meaningful figure is **net revenue** (revenue less cost of revenue). This is standard for exchange operators but critical: gross revenue of $1.14B in FY2024 reduces to net revenue of ~$276M after ~$864M in rebates. The rebate structure means MIAX competes partly on price to attract order flow.

**Segments (Q3 2025 net revenue):**
- Options: $94.5M (86% of net revenue) -- up 55% YoY
- Futures: $4.8M (4%) -- down from $5.3M YoY
- International: $5.5M (5%) -- up from $0.8M (TISE acquisition, June 2025)
- Other/Corporate: ~$4.7M (5%)

Source: Q3 2025 earnings release (Nov 5, 2025).

### SBC and Adjusted Metrics -- A Critical Issue

MIAX's adjusted earnings definition excludes: share-based compensation, loss on extinguishment of debt, IPO one-time payments, change in fair value of puttable warrants/common stock, litigation costs, impairment charges, and warrant modifications. The exact SBC dollar amount is not broken out in available public search results -- **this is a significant data gap that should be filled from the 10-Q filing**. Given the ~$52M swing from adjusted to GAAP operating income in Q3 alone, and the fact that much of this is SBC (post-IPO grants vest), the SBC burden is likely $30-50M annualized at minimum. This is material on ~$400M+ of annual net revenue.

**Judgment:** The adjusted earnings metric strips out items that are economically real (SBC is real dilution). The degree of adjustment is extreme -- adjusted earnings of $39.9M vs GAAP net loss of $102.1M in Q3. Investors must demand the detailed reconciliation table from the 10-Q/10-K to understand the true normalized earnings power.

### Normalized Earnings Estimate (Assumption-Heavy)

This is the critical exercise. I must distinguish between what I know and what I'm estimating.

**What I know (facts):**
- LTM net revenue through Q3 2025: ~$390-400M (estimate based on quarterly data)
- Q3 2025 adjusted EBITDA: $48M (44% margin on net revenue)
- Q3 2025 adjusted earnings: $39.9M
- FY2024 net revenue: ~$276M
- Analyst estimates (William Blair): FY2025E net revenue ~$395M, FY2026E ~$461M, FY2027E ~$547M

**What I must assume:**
- Normalized SBC as % of net revenue: Unknown from available data. Exchange peers run 5-10% of net revenue in SBC. **Assumption: 8-10% of net revenue, or ~$35-45M annualized on ~$450M net revenue.** This is a key uncertainty.
- Normalized D&A: Not available. Exchange operators typically run 8-12% of net revenue. **Assumption: ~$35-45M.**
- Capex: Not available from public search results. Exchange operators typically spend 3-7% of net revenue on capex. **Assumption: ~$15-30M.**
- Tax rate: Pre-IPO, MIAX had complex tax structure. **Assumption: 25% normalized effective tax rate.**

## 4. Balance Sheet and Financial Risk

**Strengths:**
- Net cash of ~$400M+ post-IPO
- Minimal debt ($6.5-30M depending on what's included)
- No near-term refinancing risk
- Regulatory capital requirements are modest for exchange operators

**Risks:**
- Dilution from SBC and potential future equity issuance. The share count has already grown from the IPO structure. Warrant exercises have added ~2.15M shares per 8-K filings.
- Pre-IPO, MIAX had puttable warrants and puttable common stock that created complex liability structures. These were terminated at IPO, but the legacy dilution is baked in.
- TISE acquisition (June 2025) and MIAXdx sale to Robinhood/SIG JV (Jan 2026, undisclosed terms) create moving parts on the balance sheet.

## 5. Capital Allocation Assessment

- **IPO proceeds:** Used primarily to retire ~$140M in debt and build cash. Prudent.
- **M&A:** Acquired TISE (international exchange) in June 2025. Sold MIAXdx (derivatives exchange/clearinghouse) to Robinhood/SIG JV in January 2026 (retained 10% stake). This shows active portfolio management -- exiting a non-core asset (prediction markets/event contracts) and expanding international presence.
- **Buybacks:** No buyback program announced. With net cash of $400M+, there is capacity, but unlikely in first year post-IPO.
- **Reinvestment:** Heavy ongoing investment in technology platform, new exchanges (Sapphire launched Aug 2024), headcount growth. This explains the breakeven GAAP margins despite strong revenue growth.

## Key Financial Drivers

**1. U.S. Multi-Listed Options Volume Growth**
This is the single most important variable. Options volumes drove 55% net revenue growth in Q3 2025. Industry ADV hit 56M contracts in Q3 2025, up 26% YoY. If industry volumes revert toward historical growth of 10-15%, MIAX's revenue growth rate halves even with market share gains.

**2. Operating Leverage / Margin Expansion**
MIAX has been approximately breakeven on GAAP operating income for 3+ years. The bull case requires the business to "flip" to meaningful profitability as one-time IPO costs fall away and fixed-cost leverage kicks in. Adjusted EBITDA margin of 44% shows the potential, but the gap to GAAP must close.

**3. Market Share Trajectory**
MIAX grew from ~15% to ~17-18% market share in multi-listed options in 2025. The 4th exchange (Sapphire, launched Aug 2024) is a key contributor. Further share gains are possible but face diminishing returns and competitive response from incumbents.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**The net revenue vs. gross revenue distinction masks competitive dynamics.** MIAX reports $1.14B in gross revenue but only $276M in net revenue for FY2024 -- meaning 76% of gross revenue is paid back as rebates/liquidity payments. This rebate ratio (cost of revenue / gross revenue) is a critical but under-discussed metric. If competitive pressure forces higher rebates to maintain market share, the net revenue capture rate deteriorates even if gross volumes grow. The rebate ratio was 76% in 2024 vs 78% in 2023 and 75% in 2022 -- the improvement in 2024 is positive but the level is high relative to peers like CBOE that retain a larger share of gross transaction revenue due to proprietary product advantages (SPX, VIX options).

**Second insight: The SBC overhang is likely larger than the market appreciates.** As a freshly public company with pre-IPO equity compensation that vests/converts post-IPO, the SBC expense trajectory is probably front-loaded and larger than the steady-state level. The Q3 2025 adjusted-to-GAAP gap of ~$142M (adjusted earnings $39.9M vs GAAP net loss $102.1M) is extraordinary even accounting for debt extinguishment. If the detailed reconciliation shows SBC running $40-60M per quarter (annualized $160-240M), normalized earnings are materially lower than the adjusted figures suggest. This will only become clear when the 10-Q/10-K financial statements provide the full SBC disclosure.

---

## Summary Assessment

MIAX is a high-quality exchange franchise operating in a structurally growing market (U.S. options) with network effects and regulatory barriers to entry. The business is real and the volume/market share trajectory is impressive. However, at $40/share the stock prices in substantial future margin expansion that has not yet materialized in GAAP results. The company has been GAAP-breakeven at the operating level for years despite 19% annual net revenue growth, and the gap between adjusted and GAAP metrics is among the largest I have seen. Until the detailed SBC and expense disclosures from the 10-K (likely available in March 2026) clarify the normalized cost structure, the true earnings power remains highly uncertain.

**Verdict: Attractive franchise, but priced for execution on margin expansion that has not yet been demonstrated in reported results. Insufficient data on SBC and FCF to form a high-conviction view. The Q4 2025 earnings release (Feb 25) and first 10-K will be critical catalysts.**
