# U.S. Listed Options Exchange Industry Analysis

## The Bottom Line Up Front

The U.S. options exchange industry is a secular growth business disguised as a mature financial infrastructure sector. Total industry volume has grown from ~4.2 billion contracts in 2016 to ~15.2 billion contracts in 2025 -- a 15% CAGR over nine years. This is not cyclical. The structural drivers -- 0DTE proliferation, retail adoption, ETF options expansion -- are durable and largely irreversible. The industry produced its first 100M+ contract day in April 2025 and a record 110M contract day in October 2025.

But here is the tension the market underappreciates: **volume growth does not translate linearly into revenue growth, and revenue growth does not translate linearly into margin expansion for non-proprietary-product exchanges.** The industry has 18 active exchanges (with IEX targeting a 19th by late 2026), all competing for fungible multi-listed options flow through fee schedules that net to fractions of a cent per contract. The exchanges with structural competitive advantages are those with proprietary products (Cboe's SPX/VIX) or those gaining share through technology differentiation. Everyone else is in a volume-dependent, fee-compressed commodity business.

MIAX trades at ~$40.38 per share (as of Feb 20, 2026). The stock has traded $23-$51 over the past year since its August 2025 IPO at $23/share.

## What Drives Profitability in This Industry

Three revenue pillars, in order of margin quality:

1. **Proprietary/exclusive index products.** Cboe's SPX and VIX options are the crown jewels of this industry. They trade on a single exchange, carry higher per-contract economics, and are not subject to competitive fee pressure. SPX options alone generated 371M+ contracts in 2018 and have grown substantially since. 0DTE SPX options now represent 57-61% of total SPX volume. **This is the highest-margin, most defensible revenue stream in the industry, and only Cboe has it.**

2. **Transaction fees on multi-listed options.** This is where MIAX and most other exchanges compete. Multi-listed options (options on individual stocks, ETFs excluding proprietary indices) can trade on any exchange. Exchanges compete via maker-taker fee structures, rebates, and execution quality. Net revenue per contract after rebates is extremely thin -- measured in fractions of a cent. Volume share matters enormously because the economics are high fixed cost, low marginal cost.

3. **Market data and connectivity fees.** Exchanges charge for real-time market data feeds, co-location, and connectivity. This is a growing, high-margin revenue stream. Cboe's market data and access solutions segment has been growing faster than transaction revenue. For smaller exchanges like MIAX, data revenue is meaningful but subscale relative to the big three families.

**Fact:** Cboe's adjusted EBITDA margin was 65-67% in 2024-2025. This reflects its proprietary product advantage. MIAX's adjusted EBITDA margin was 27% in Q3 2024, expanding to 44% in Q3 2025 -- driven by volume growth and operating leverage, not structural margin superiority.

**Judgment:** The gap between Cboe's margin profile and everyone else's is the single most important structural fact in this industry. It is not closable without proprietary products. MIAX is structurally a lower-margin business than Cboe, regardless of market share gains.

## Industry Pricing Power and Competitive Dynamics

**Pricing power is bifurcated.** Exchanges with exclusive products have significant pricing power. Exchanges competing in multi-listed options have near-zero pricing power.

### Competitive structure: Four exchange families dominate

| Exchange Family | Approx. Multi-Listed Share (2024-2025) | Venues | Key Differentiation |
|----------------|----------------------------------------|--------|---------------------|
| Cboe (incl. BATS, C2, EDGX) | ~30-33% | 4 | SPX/VIX proprietary products |
| Nasdaq (incl. ISE, PHLX, etc.) | ~22-26% | 6 | Scale, technology, PHLX floor |
| NYSE (Arca, Amex) | ~14% | 2 | Parent brand, listed company relationships |
| MIAX (Options, Pearl, Emerald, Sapphire) | ~15-17% | 4 | Technology, speed, fee innovation |
| Others (MEMX, BOX, etc.) | ~10-15% | Various | Niche strategies |

*Sources: Cboe market share data, MIAX press releases, Statista. Shares are approximate and shift monthly.*

**Key competitive dynamics:**

- **Fee schedule arms race.** Exchanges constantly adjust maker-taker rates, rebate tiers, and volume incentives. Net revenue capture (after rebates) has compressed over time as exchanges proliferate. With 18 venues, the buyer (broker-dealer routing flow) has enormous leverage.

- **Technology as differentiator.** MIAX has historically competed on technology -- lower latency, better matching engines. This is a real but depreciating advantage as competitors invest in similar infrastructure. It is necessary to maintain share but insufficient to expand share indefinitely.

- **Venue proliferation as strategy.** Each exchange family operates multiple venues with different fee/priority models (price-time vs. pro-rata, maker-taker vs. taker-maker). This is strategic -- it captures different order flow types. MIAX launched its fourth venue (Sapphire) in August 2024. But each new venue cannibalizes existing venues within the same family and adds operational cost.

- **Options Regulatory Fee (ORF) structure.** Exchanges collect ORF on trades executed at their venue -- estimated at $181-234M industry-wide in 2025. There is industry pressure to reform this. Any changes could redistribute revenue across exchanges in unpredictable ways.

**Fact:** IEX plans to launch an options exchange by late 2026, adding a 19th venue. This is incremental competitive pressure.

**Judgment:** The multi-listed options exchange business is structurally commoditized. Market share can be bought through rebates but cannot be monetized at attractive margins because the next exchange will undercut you. This is a Red Queen problem -- you run faster just to stay in place.

## Supply Elasticity and Capacity Dynamics

"Supply" in this context means exchange venues and their capacity to absorb volume.

**Supply is highly elastic upward.** Launching a new exchange venue costs perhaps $20-50M in technology and regulatory investment, with ongoing operational costs. The barriers to entry are regulatory approval (SEC), technology infrastructure, and achieving minimum viable market share. These barriers are real but surmountable -- as demonstrated by MEMX (launched 2020), MIAX Sapphire (launched 2024), and IEX Options (targeting 2026).

**Supply never contracts.** Exchange venues almost never shut down. Once launched, the marginal cost of keeping a venue running is low relative to the sunk technology investment. Unlike physical commodity industries, there is no "capacity destruction" mechanism. Bad exchanges just languish at low market share rather than closing.

**Implication:** The industry has a permanent one-way ratchet on competitive fragmentation. Every new entrant dilutes the volume pool for incumbents. The equilibrium number of exchanges is determined by the minimum viable market share (~1-2%) needed to cover fixed costs -- and at current volume levels (~60M contracts/day ADV), even 1% share represents meaningful absolute volume.

## Cyclicality Assessment

**Cyclicality: Moderate.** But the cyclical component operates differently than in most industries.

Options exchange revenue is driven by volume, which is driven by:

1. **Market volatility (cyclical).** Higher VIX = more options activity = more volume. This is the primary cyclical driver. The 2020 COVID spike, 2022 rate hiking cycle, and periodic volatility events all produced volume spikes. Conversely, low-volatility regimes compress activity.

2. **Secular volume growth (structural).** The 15% CAGR in volume since 2016 is not volatility-driven. It reflects 0DTE adoption, retail participation (now ~50% of volume), ETF options expansion, and product innovation. This structural floor is rising continuously.

3. **Fee dynamics (structural headwind).** As volumes grow, exchanges compete away per-contract economics. Gross volume growth overstates revenue growth.

**Historical volume (industry-wide, approximate):**

| Year | Total Options Contracts (B) | Notes |
|------|----------------------------|-------|
| 2016 | ~4.2 | Pre-0DTE, pre-retail wave |
| 2019 | ~5.5 | Pre-COVID baseline |
| 2020 | ~7.5 | COVID volatility spike + retail |
| 2021 | ~9.0 | Meme stock era, retail surge |
| 2022 | ~10.3 | Rate hike volatility |
| 2023 | ~11.1 | 0DTE proliferation begins |
| 2024 | ~12.1 | Fifth consecutive record (per OCC) |
| 2025 | ~15.2 | Sixth consecutive record, 26% YoY |

*Source: OCC historical volume statistics, Cboe annual reports, industry press.*

**Key observation:** Even in 2019 (relatively low volatility), volume grew. Even in 2023-2024 (declining VIX from 2022 peaks), volume grew. The secular growth floor has been strong enough to override cyclical weakness. This is unusual and important.

**Judgment:** Cyclicality in this industry manifests as growth rate volatility, not absolute revenue declines. In a severe low-volatility regime, industry volume growth might slow to 5-10% rather than 20%+. But outright volume declines are unlikely absent a regulatory shock. This makes the industry more growth-cyclical than traditional-cyclical.

## Current Cycle Position: Mid-Cycle to Late Cycle (for Volume Growth). Confidence: Moderate.

**Evidence for mid-to-late cycle on volume growth rates:**

- 2025 volume growth of 26% YoY is well above the secular trend of ~15% CAGR. This was driven by elevated volatility (tariff uncertainty, geopolitical events) plus continued 0DTE adoption.
- ADV of 60.4M contracts in 2025 is the sixth consecutive record.
- 0DTE volumes grew 41% YoY in 2025, but the base effect is becoming large. 0DTE already represents >60% of SPX volume. The incremental contribution to total market growth must eventually slow.
- Retail participation is at ~50% of volume -- further penetration gains are limited.

**Evidence against calling this "peak":**

- 0DTE expansion to more underlyings (beyond SPX) is still early.
- International trading hours expansion (Cboe now offers nearly 24/5 SPX options) is additive.
- New product categories (crypto-linked ETF options, more single-stock weekly expirations) provide incremental volume.
- Structural shift toward options-based portfolio strategies (buy-write, defined outcome) creates persistent institutional demand.

**Key uncertainty:** If VIX normalizes to sub-15 for an extended period, does volume growth decelerate to single digits? This has not happened in the post-0DTE era, so there is no clean test case. My base case is that secular growth sustains 8-12% volume growth even in low-volatility regimes, with spikes to 20%+ during volatile periods.

## Mid-Cycle Reference Point

**Definition:** Mid-cycle for the U.S. options exchange industry means:

- Industry ADV of 45-55 million contracts (below the 60.4M 2025 level, above the ~40M 2023 level)
- Volume growth of 8-12% annually
- VIX averaging 16-20 (neither panic nor complacency)
- Per-contract net revenue capture stable (not expanding through pricing, not collapsing from new entrant pressure)
- Adjusted EBITDA margins for technology-differentiated multi-listed exchanges (MIAX's category) in the 30-38% range
- Adjusted EBITDA margins for proprietary-product exchanges (Cboe) in the 60-65% range

**For MIAX specifically (industry-level economics applied to its scale):** Mid-cycle quarterly net revenue of ~$80-90M, adjusted EBITDA of ~$28-34M, implying annual adjusted EBITDA of ~$110-135M. The Q3 2025 result ($109.5M net revenue, $48M EBITDA) was above mid-cycle -- reflecting both share gains and elevated industry volumes.

**Judgment:** Current industry conditions are above mid-cycle. MIAX's Q3 2025 results were significantly above mid-cycle on both revenue and margin. The 44% EBITDA margin benefited from operating leverage on surging volumes. If volumes normalize, margins will compress -- the question is how much.

## The One Industry Factor That Matters

**Total industry options volume -- and specifically, whether the secular growth rate holds above 10% or decelerates toward GDP-like growth.**

Everything else in this industry -- market share shifts between exchanges, fee schedule changes, new venue launches -- is noise relative to the volume trajectory. Options exchanges are high-operating-leverage businesses. A 20% increase in industry volume drops disproportionately to the bottom line. A volume stagnation would expose the fee compression and competitive fragmentation that volume growth currently masks.

**What would cause an inflection:**
- Bearish: A sustained period of sub-15 VIX combined with retail disengagement from options (perhaps driven by a prolonged bear market where retail loses money on options). Or regulatory intervention on 0DTE products (unlikely but not zero probability).
- Bullish: Expansion of 0DTE to more underlyings, international hours driving volume from non-US participants, continued institutional adoption of options-based strategies.

**How to monitor:** OCC monthly volume reports. Watch for ADV falling below 50M contracts on a sustained (3+ month) basis -- that would signal the secular growth story is fading. Also monitor the ratio of 0DTE to total volume: if it plateaus above 30% of total market volume while total volume still grows, the secular thesis is intact.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**Consensus view:** "Options volume is in secular growth, all options exchanges benefit, MIAX is gaining share and just IPO'd at a reasonable valuation."

**What they may be missing (bearish for MIAX's relative positioning):**

The market is treating MIAX's recent share gains and margin expansion as evidence of structural competitive advantage. But the industry structure suggests this is fragile:

1. **MIAX's 15-17% market share is in the most commoditized segment of the industry -- multi-listed options.** It has zero proprietary products. Every contract it trades is a contract that could trade on 17 other venues. Share gains come from fee incentives and technology, both of which competitors can replicate. Cboe's market share, by contrast, includes substantial proprietary product volume that is structurally captive.

2. **The margin expansion from 27% to 44% EBITDA (Q3 2024 to Q3 2025) is primarily operating leverage from a volume surge, not structural improvement.** If industry volumes normalize to mid-cycle levels, MIAX's margins will revert toward 30-35%. The market may be capitalizing peak-cycle margins.

3. **Venue proliferation is a treadmill.** MIAX launched Sapphire in August 2024 and it quickly gained 3.3% share. But where did that share come from? Partly from MIAX's own other venues (Pearl's share dropped 34.7% YoY in 2024). The consolidated MIAX family share has grown, but each new venue carries incremental fixed costs and cannibalizes existing venues.

4. **IEX's options exchange entry (targeting late 2026) adds another competitor specifically targeting market maker flow with adverse selection protections.** IEX has a strong brand with institutional investors. This could pressure MIAX's share in exactly the institutional flow category where MIAX has been strong.

5. **The true competitive moat in this industry is proprietary products, not market share in multi-listed options.** Cboe earns 65%+ EBITDA margins because SPX/VIX can only trade there. MIAX earns 44% in a hot volume quarter because it processes fungible flow slightly faster than alternatives. One of these is durable. The other is not.

**Net assessment:** This is a modestly bearish non-consensus view on MIAX's competitive position within an industry whose overall volume trajectory is genuinely favorable. The industry is good. The question is whether MIAX's position within the industry justifies its valuation premium over what a commodity multi-listed exchange should earn -- or whether the market is confusing cyclically elevated margins with structural margin superiority.

---

## Primary Industry Risk

**Volume growth deceleration.** If the secular growth rate falls from 15%+ to 5% or lower -- due to retail fatigue, regulatory constraints on 0DTE, or a prolonged low-volatility regime -- the industry's high-fixed-cost structure and 18-venue fragmentation would compress margins across all participants. The smaller, non-proprietary exchanges would be hit hardest because they lack the pricing power of exclusive products to defend revenue per contract. MIAX, as a pure multi-listed exchange, would be disproportionately exposed.

Secondary risk: Regulatory intervention on exchange fee structures (ORF reform, maker-taker restrictions, market data fee caps).
