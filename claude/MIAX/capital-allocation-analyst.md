# MIAX (Miami International Holdings) -- Capital Allocation & Management Assessment

**Date:** 2026-02-23 | **Price:** ~$40.38 | **Market Cap:** ~$3.4B | **Shares Outstanding:** ~85.4M

---

## The One Thing That Determines Value Creation

The dominant factor is whether MIAX's founder-CEO Thomas Gallagher continues building exchanges (acquiring assets, launching new venues) or pivots to harvesting the platform he has already built. The company has never operated as a public entity focused on capital return. Every dollar of free cash flow has historically been redeployed into expansion -- new exchanges, new asset classes, new geographies. The question is whether going public changes this behavior or merely gives Gallagher a cheaper currency (equity) to accelerate the acquisition cadence. His 2.83% ownership stake ($85M at current prices) is meaningful but not structurally dominant. At that level, empire-building incentives can outweigh per-share value creation.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**Consensus view:** MIAX is a high-quality exchange operator with attractive secular tailwinds (options volume growth) that just de-levered its balance sheet via IPO proceeds.

**What may be missed:** The legacy Equity Rights Programs (ERPs) created a shareholder base of trading firms (Citadel, Wolverine, Susquehanna) who received equity at ~$5/share against a $23 IPO price. These are not long-term holders -- they are participants who were compensated with equity for directing order flow to MIAX. Citadel converted its 6.2M shares into a pre-funded warrant in June 2025 specifically to obscure its position and avoid beneficial ownership reporting. In February 2026, a warrant holder already exercised 2.15M shares in a cashless exercise. This is not traditional insider selling but it is functionally dilutive and represents an overhang that market participants may underestimate. The fully diluted share count carries more uncertainty than a typical newly public exchange operator.

Additionally, the company reports adjusted EBITDA that excludes share-based compensation, and the specific SBC dollar amounts are not readily transparent in public disclosures. For a company where ERP warrants, pre-funded warrants, and post-IPO equity plans all coexist, true dilution-adjusted economics may differ materially from headline adjusted EBITDA margins.

## 3. Stock-Based Compensation Discipline

**SBC as % of Revenue:** Not precisely quantifiable from available disclosures. The company adjusts SBC out of its non-GAAP adjusted EBITDA, which is the primary metric management emphasizes. [Flag: This is standard for exchanges but creates opacity around the true cost of equity compensation.]

**Known dilution vectors:**
1. Legacy ERP warrants (all earning periods expired as of June 2024, but warrants remain exercisable)
2. Citadel pre-funded warrant: ~6.2M shares, partially exercised (2.15M shares issued via cashless exercise in Feb 2026)
3. Underwriter overallotment: Up to 2.25M additional IPO shares
4. Post-IPO equity incentive plans (details in S-1 but magnitude of future grants unclear)

**Net dilution reality:**
- IPO shares issued: 15M
- Subsequent warrant exercises: 2.15M shares (Feb 2026)
- Current shares outstanding: ~85.4M
- Remaining Citadel pre-funded warrant: ~4.05M shares (estimated, after partial exercise)
- [Judgment: Fully diluted share count is likely ~89-90M, implying the headline 85.4M understates dilution by 5-6%. This is not extreme but it is directionally negative and the overhang from ERP warrant holders -- trading firms with a ~$5 cost basis -- creates persistent selling pressure at prices well above their entry.]

**Does management treat SBC as a real cost?** Management's primary reporting metric is adjusted EBITDA, which excludes SBC. This is common among exchange operators (CBOE, Nasdaq, ICE all do the same), but it means SBC is implicitly treated as a non-cost. No buyback program has been announced to offset dilution.

## 4. Capital Allocation Track Record

### a) Reinvestment

**Organic investment:** MIAX has launched four options exchanges (MIAX Options, MIAX Pearl, MIAX Emerald, MIAX Sapphire), an equities exchange (MIAX Pearl Equities), and rebuilt the MIAX Futures platform (Onyx). Each new exchange requires technology buildout, regulatory approval, and ongoing operational cost.

**Evidence of returns:**
- Combined MIAX options market share grew to ~15.1% in 2024 (across four exchanges) and reached a record 17.4% in May 2025. Each incremental exchange launch (Emerald in 2019, Sapphire in 2024) has gained share.
- Net revenue grew from $114M (2020) to $276M (2024) on a net revenue basis; total revenue from ~$798M (2022) to ~$1.14B (2024). [Note: The difference between net revenue and total revenue reflects transaction rebates and pass-through fees common in exchange economics.]
- Adjusted EBITDA margins expanded from ~27% (Q3 2024) to ~44% (Q3 2025), suggesting operating leverage is emerging.

**Judgment:** Organic reinvestment has been demonstrably value-creative. Building additional options exchanges is a relatively capital-light strategy that leverages existing technology, and the market share gains are real and measurable. This is the strongest element of MIAX's capital allocation track record.

### b) M&A

MIAX has been an active acquirer, completing at least five deals as a private company:

| Deal | Year | Price | Strategic Logic | Outcome (Judgment) |
|------|------|-------|-----------------|---------------------|
| Bermuda Stock Exchange (BSX) | 2020 | Undisclosed | International listings | Modest/unclear contribution |
| Minneapolis Grain Exchange (MGEX, now MIAX Futures) | 2020 | $275K/share in cash + MIH stock at $7.25/share | Futures/commodities expansion | Futures segment remains small ($4.8M net revenue Q3 2025) and declining YoY |
| Dorman Trading (FCM) | 2022 | Undisclosed | Clearing/execution services for futures | Additive but small |
| LedgerX (now MIAXdx) | 2023 | ~$50M (from FTX bankruptcy) | Crypto derivatives (DCM + DCO) | Sold 90% to Robinhood/Susquehanna JV in Jan 2026; terms undisclosed. Acquired from distressed seller, flipped within 3 years -- this is actually good capital allocation if sale price exceeded $50M purchase. |
| The International Stock Exchange (TISE) | 2025 | ~$91.5M (~70.4M GBP) | International listings (Channel Islands) | Too early to assess; significant price for a small exchange |

**Judgment on M&A discipline:**
- The LedgerX acquisition stands out as opportunistic and well-timed. Buying a regulated crypto exchange out of FTX bankruptcy for $50M and selling 90% to Robinhood within three years is sharp capital recycling (assuming the sale price was at or above cost).
- The MGEX/futures pivot has not yet delivered. Futures net revenue was $4.8M in Q3 2025 (down from $5.3M prior year). This is immaterial relative to the options business and raises questions about whether the futures strategy is working.
- TISE at $91.5M is the most aggressive bet -- a non-trivial price for an international exchange with limited volume. This looks more like geographic empire-building than returns-driven allocation. Too early to judge definitively.
- BSX and Dorman were likely small enough that even if they fail, they do not destroy significant value.

**Pattern:** Gallagher is an acquirer by nature. Five deals in five years as a private company. The cadence may accelerate now that MIAX has a public currency and a clean balance sheet. This is the primary capital allocation risk.

### c) Buybacks & Issuance

- **Buybacks:** None announced. No share repurchase program exists.
- **Equity issuance:** IPO raised $345M (15M shares at $23). Proceeds used to repay $178.4M of outstanding debt.
- **Net issuance trend:** The company is a net issuer of equity. Between IPO shares, overallotment options, and ongoing warrant exercises, the share count has only increased since going public.
- **ERP warrant overhang:** Legacy warrant holders with a ~$5 cost basis will continue converting to common stock, creating ongoing dilution. The Feb 2026 exercise of 2.15M shares is likely the first of several.

**Judgment:** MIAX is in net-issuance mode. There is no evidence of buyback discipline or intent. For a company generating ~$135M in trailing FCF, the absence of any return-of-capital framework is notable -- though somewhat understandable for a newly public company still investing in growth.

### d) Debt Usage

- **Pre-IPO:** MIAX carried a 2029 Senior Secured Term Loan. Specific amount not disclosed publicly but at least $178.4M based on repayment from IPO proceeds.
- **Post-IPO (Sep 30, 2025):** Cash of $401.5M, total debt of $6.5M. Debt/equity ratio: 0.8%.
- **IPO use of proceeds:** ~52% of gross IPO proceeds ($178.4M of $345M) went to debt repayment.

**Judgment:** De-leveraging the balance sheet via IPO proceeds was the right decision. MIAX now has a fortress balance sheet ($401.5M cash vs $6.5M debt). The risk is that this cash hoard becomes ammunition for aggressive M&A rather than being returned to shareholders or deployed at high incremental returns. $400M of dry powder in the hands of a proven acquirer is a feature if the deals are good, but a risk if they are not.

## 5. Learning vs Repetition

**Evidence of learning:**
- The LedgerX acquisition-and-flip suggests an ability to recognize when an asset is worth more to another buyer. Selling MIAXdx to Robinhood/Susquehanna rather than continuing to invest in a sub-scale crypto exchange shows capital discipline.
- De-leveraging via IPO proceeds rather than retaining all cash for M&A shows some balance sheet prudence.

**Evidence of potential repetition:**
- The TISE acquisition at $91.5M, completed in the same year as the IPO, suggests the acquisition appetite has not diminished. Gallagher launched an IPO and closed an acquisition in the same quarter.
- MIAX Futures continues to receive investment despite minimal revenue contribution. The threshold for abandoning underperforming initiatives is unclear.

**Judgment:** Mixed. The LedgerX exit is a positive signal. But the cadence of acquisitions and the unwillingness to exit underperforming segments (futures) suggests the default behavior is to build, not to harvest.

## 6. Forward-Looking Capital Allocation Risk

**What management is likely to do next:**
- Use the $400M+ cash balance to pursue additional acquisitions. International exchange deals (following BSX and TISE precedent) or adjacent asset-class expansions are the most likely targets.
- Continue launching exchange venues organically (this is positive and should be encouraged).
- No buyback program is expected in the near term.

**Where capital is most likely to be misallocated:**
- International exchange acquisitions that add geographic breadth but not economic value. BSX and TISE together represent significant capital deployed into non-US listing venues with uncertain revenue contribution.
- Continued investment in MIAX Futures if volumes do not inflect. The MGEX acquisition and Onyx platform rebuild have consumed capital with minimal return so far.

**Whether future FCF is at risk of misuse:**
- Yes. A founder-CEO with a 13-year track record of building and acquiring, now armed with $400M in cash and a public equity currency, is more likely to deploy capital aggressively than conservatively. The lack of any stated return-of-capital framework or buyback program is consistent with this expectation.
