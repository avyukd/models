# Oppenheimer Holdings Inc. (NYSE: OPY) - Rigorous Financial Analysis

**Date:** January 4, 2026
**Analyst Framework:** Buy-Side Financial Diligence Standard

---

## Executive Summary

Oppenheimer Holdings is a middle-market investment bank and broker-dealer with highly cyclical earnings, structurally high compensation costs (62-68% of revenue), and material volatility driven by Capital Markets activity. The business trades at a discount to tangible book value, but normalized earnings power is substantially lower than recent peak results. The $1.6B advisor defection to Ameriprise highlights structural challenges in wealth management retention, though the impact is modest (~3% of AUM). The key analytical question is whether FY2024's $6.91 EPS represents normalized earnings or a cyclical peak.

**Conclusion:** OPY is a financially sound but volatile business trading near book value. Normalized earnings power is approximately $4.00-4.50 EPS, implying fair value absent multiple expansion. The stock offers modest downside protection via book value, but limited upside unless Capital Markets activity sustains.

### The 1-3 Metrics That Drive This Stock

**1. Capital Markets Revenue (Investment Banking + Trading)**
- This is the swing factor. When IB revenues surge (2021, 2024), earnings explode. When they collapse (2022), margins implode.
- Q3 2025 Capital Markets revenue: $162.1M (+30.7% YoY) - this is what's driving the stock
- Watch: Equity underwriting volumes, M&A activity, trading volumes

- The comp ratio barely flexes (62-68% range). This creates a low-operating-leverage business model.
- The liability-based award expense is mechanically tied to stock price - creates bizarre optics where a rising stock price hurts EPS
- **Key question:** Can they ever get comp ratio below 62%? History says no.

**3. Advisor Headcount and AUM Retention**

- **Why it matters:** Wealth Management is the stable ballast; losing advisors compounds over time

## 9. What Consensus May Be Missing

### Non-Consensus Insight: The Liability-Based Compensation Trap

**The Issue:** OPY's reported earnings are mechanically depressed when the stock price rises due to liability-based awards. In Q3 2025, a $8.30 stock price increase triggered $13.5M pre-tax ($0.95/share after-tax) in compensation expense. In FY2024, this was $32.6M pre-tax.

**Why Consensus Misses This:**
1. Most investors look at headline EPS without understanding this mechanical relationship
2. A rising stock price makes EPS look *worse*, creating a perverse dynamic
3. Conversely, if the stock falls, EPS will look *better* due to comp expense reversal

**The Non-Obvious Implication:**
- If you believe the stock is undervalued and will rise, your EPS estimate needs to haircut ~$1.00-1.50/share for the mechanical compensation hit
- The liability-based awards act as a **stealth anti-dilution mechanism** - when stock rises, cash is effectively transferred from shareholders to employees via higher comp expense rather than via share issuance
- This means **book value grows slower than it otherwise would** during periods of stock appreciation

**What This Means for Valuation:**
The "true" normalized earnings power is probably ~$1.00-1.50 higher than reported during stable stock price environments. At $74/share, assuming stock price stability, add-back of liability compensation would push normalized EPS toward $6.00-6.50. But if you're bullish on the stock, you need to model the drag.

### Additional Non-Consensus Points:

1. **Debt elimination is under-appreciated:** Going from $113M in 5.5% notes to debt-free saves ~$6.2M/year pre-tax (~$0.40/share after-tax). This is now fully in run-rate.

2. **Family control creates alignment but also succession risk:** The Lowenthal family transition (Albert to Robert) appears orderly, but 98% voting control by one family creates governance concentration.

3. **Advisor attrition may accelerate:** Two large team defections in 2025 ($1.6B and $2.1B) suggest competitive pressures. Ameriprise is explicitly paying up for advisors.

### Bottom Line

OPY is a **financially solid but highly cyclical** business. The stock trades at ~1x book value with no debt, which provides floor support. However, normalized earnings power is likely $4.50-5.50/share, not the $7-8 recent results suggest. The key risk is Capital Markets reversion to mean - if IB activity normalizes, earnings could compress 30-40%.

**For this valuation to make sense, you must believe:**
1. Investment banking activity remains elevated (not returning to 2022-2023 levels)
2. Wealth Management retains advisors despite competitive poaching
3. The market assigns a ~10x multiple to inherently cyclical earnings

**What breaks financially:**
- Capital Markets revenue decline of 30%+ (happened in 2022)
- Accelerating advisor departures
- Market decline reducing AUM and advisory fees
