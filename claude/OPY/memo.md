## KEY DRIVERS (The 1-3 Things That Matter)

**1. OARs Compensation Mechanics Create Artificial Earnings Suppression**
When OPY's stock rises, compensation expense mechanically increases, depressing reported EPS. 2.6M OARs outstanding, cash-settled, 5-year maturity. Q3 2025: $8.30 stock rise triggered $13.5M pre-tax (~$0.95/share after-tax) hit. FY2024: $32.6M pre-tax. Critically, this works in reverse: the recent pullback ($73 to $68) creates a Q4 earnings tailwind. At stable stock price, normalized EPS is $1.00-1.50 higher than reported during appreciation periods.

**2. Capital Markets Revenue Cyclicality**
This is the swing variable. Investment banking and trading drove EPS from $2.77 (2022) to $6.91 (2024) to ~$8.10 LTM. The Capital Markets segment posted its first profitable quarter since Q3 2022 in Q3 2025 ($12M). Drivers: rising sales/trading volumes, elevated small-cap equity issuance, resurgent biotech sector. The question is durability, but current profits reflect real business momentum, not just market beta.

**3. Owner-Operator Alignment with Historical Total Return Evidence**
Lowenthal family owns 41% economically, 98% voting, with ~$250M personal capital at risk. They've executed 15-18% share reduction over 5 years, eliminated all debt, and returned capital through dividends ($1.00 special dividend declared December 2025). New finding: OPY has outperformed Raymond James (RJF), Stifel (SF), XLF, and SPY on a 2016-2025 total return basis despite trading at steep discount. This suggests persistent market mispricing that patient ownership has historically exploited.

## THESIS IN 3-5 BULLETS

1. **Forward P/E of ~8x prices in significant skepticism.** Even if earnings moderate from LTM $8.10 to normalized $6.00-7.00, you're paying 10-12x for a debt-free business with strong capital returns. The OARs mechanism means reported earnings understate economic reality when stock rises.

2. **Historical total return outperformance is the non-consensus signal.** Despite trading at persistent discount to book and peers, OPY has outperformed RJF, SF, XLF, and SPY over 2016-2025. Market keeps calling this a value trap; long-term returns say otherwise.

3. **Balance sheet is a fortress providing floor protection.** Debt-free after October 2024 redemption, $920M stockholders' equity, TBV ~$70 vs. stock at ~$68. Downside to TBV is minimal; you're essentially getting the operating business for free at current prices.

4. **Capital Markets recovery has legs.** PE dry powder at $2.6T with 8.5-year average hold periods forces exits. Middle-market M&A expected up 26% in 2025. Biotech sector resurgence benefits OPY's IB franchise. Q3 2025 Capital Markets profitability is a leading indicator.

5. **The OARs tailwind sets up Q4 and beyond.** Stock pullback from $73 to $68 creates mechanical comp expense reversal in Q4. If stock stays flat or rises modestly, reported earnings will look better than underlying trends would suggest.

## THE SETUP (Only What Matters)

Oppenheimer is a middle-market investment bank and wealth manager that the market treats as a structurally challenged broker-dealer. The narrative: sub-scale vs. Stifel/Raymond James, advisor relationships are rented not owned, Capital Markets is feast-or-famine.

**What the narrative misses:**

- **The OARs accounting creates a buy-the-dip mechanism.** Stock declines reduce comp expense, boosting reported earnings, which eventually attracts buyers. Stock rises increase comp expense, depressing reported earnings, which creates buying opportunities. This dynamic rewards patient contrarian ownership.

- **The Lowenthal family has run this for 40 years and compounded.** This is not a management team optimizing for the next quarter. They've deleveraged the balance sheet, reduced shares 15-18%, and returned capital. Their $250M at risk means they eat their own cooking.

- **The business has survived multiple cycles.** Wall Street profits swung from $58B (2021) to $26B (2022) to $50B (2024). OPY navigated this, maintained profitability, and emerged debt-free. The "fragile" business has proven durable.

Industry context: Broker-dealer industry consolidated 30% since 2010. Current profits are late mid-cycle to early late-cycle. But OPY's business mix (65% Wealth Management, fee-based) provides more stability than pure investment banks.

## WHY BUY (REVISED FROM NEUTRAL)

**What Changed:**

The citrini research clarified several points that shift the risk/reward:

1. **Historical total return outperformance is documented.** OPY beat RJF, SF, XLF, and SPY from 2016-2025. This is not a value trap - it's a persistently mispriced security that rewards patient ownership. The "too hard" framing was wrong.

2. **Forward P/E of ~8x is genuinely cheap.** Prior memo focused on LTM; forward estimates show earnings sustainable near current levels. At 8x forward with debt-free balance sheet and 32% insider ownership, the margin of safety is real.

3. **The OARs tailwind is mechanical and imminent.** Stock dropped from $73 to $68; Q4 comp expense will benefit. This is a near-term catalyst that creates an optically strong earnings report.

4. **TBV discount provides floor.** At $68 vs. TBV of $70.48, you're buying below liquidation value. The business is free optionality.

**Why Not Still Neutral:**

The original memo correctly identified structural challenges (sub-scale, advisor retention, cyclicality). These remain valid concerns. But the memo over-weighted "things need to go right" framing and under-weighted:

- Historical evidence that patient ownership works
- Mechanical OARs dynamics that reward contrarian entry
- Current price sitting at TBV discount
- Owner-operator alignment that compounds over cycles

**This is not a high-conviction position.** The structural concerns are real. But the risk/reward at current prices favors ownership. The floor is close; the ceiling is meaningfully higher; and historical evidence suggests the market chronically misprices this name.
