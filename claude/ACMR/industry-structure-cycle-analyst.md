# Industry Structure & Cycle Analysis: Semiconductor Wet Processing Equipment (China Focus)

## Industry Definition and Scope

This analysis covers the semiconductor wet processing equipment industry, with specific focus on the China-centric wet cleaning equipment segment relevant to ACMR's operating environment. The unit of analysis is the industry, not any specific firm.

Wet processing equipment includes wafer cleaning, etching, and stripping systems critical to semiconductor fabrication yields. The global wet process equipment market was valued at approximately $6.5 billion in 2023, projected to reach $11.2 billion by 2032 (per Verified Market Reports).

---

## What Fundamentally Drives Profitability in This Industry?

**Primary drivers:**

1. **Semiconductor capex cycles** - Equipment demand is derived demand, entirely dependent on fab capacity additions and technology migrations. When fabs invest, equipment makers thrive; when they pause, orders collapse.

2. **Technology node transitions** - Shrinking geometries and advanced packaging (3D NAND stacking, GAA architectures) increase cleaning complexity and the number of cleaning steps per wafer, driving higher equipment intensity.

3. **Capacity utilization at customer fabs** - The semiconductor industry requires >75% utilization for favorable economics (per Bain). Below this, capex pauses abruptly.

4. **Competitive concentration** - The wet cleaning segment is moderately consolidated: SCREEN, Tokyo Electron, and Lam Research hold >90% of global revenue share (per industry reports). This concentration historically supports pricing discipline.

**For China specifically:**
- **Policy-driven demand** - Government mandates for 50% domestic equipment sourcing create artificial demand floors for local suppliers
- **Export control distortions** - U.S. restrictions have blocked Western competitors (Lam, SCREEN) from advanced sales to China, creating captive markets for domestic alternatives

---

## What Determines Pricing Power at the Industry Level?

**Global wet equipment industry:**
- Oligopolistic structure (SCREEN, TEL, Lam dominate) supports pricing discipline
- High switching costs once tools are qualified into production flows
- Technology differentiation creates customer stickiness

**China domestic segment (where ACMR primarily operates):**
- **Distorted pricing environment** - Domestic suppliers compete on price, often undercutting foreign competitors by 20-40%
- **Subsidies mask true economics** - Government support through Big Fund ($95B+ across three phases) allows below-market pricing
- **Captive customer base** - Export controls and localization mandates reduce competitive pressure from global leaders

This is critical: Pricing power in China is not market-determined. It is policy-determined. This makes "normal" economics harder to define.

---

## How Elastic is Supply, and Over What Time Horizon?

**Equipment manufacturing supply:**
- Equipment supply is moderately elastic over 12-24 months
- Capacity can flex with demand through outsourcing, workforce adjustments
- R&D and technology development cycles are 3-5 years

**Semiconductor fab capacity (what drives equipment demand):**
- Highly inelastic in the short term (fab construction: 2+ years, equipment qualification: 18 months)
- Investment decisions made 2-3 years ahead of production
- Creates classic boom-bust cycles as manufacturers over/undershoot demand

**China-specific dynamics:**
- Supply response is policy-accelerated, not market-driven
- Massive capacity additions continue despite weak pricing signals
- SMIC adding 50,000 12-inch wafers/year ($7B capex in 2025)
- Overcapacity concerns are structural, not cyclical

---

## What Incentives Drive Capacity Expansion or Contraction?

**Normal market incentives (global):**
- Utilization rates above 80% trigger expansion
- Leading-edge node transitions require new equipment regardless of utilization
- Memory prices and inventory levels drive memory capex cycles

**China incentives (distorted):**
- **National security imperative** overrides market signals - "Domestic Substitution" to "Delete America" from supply chain
- Local government subsidies and Big Fund investments drive expansion regardless of profitability
- Equipment purchases pulled forward ahead of tightening export controls
- Market share and self-sufficiency targets take precedence over ROI

The incentive structure in China is fundamentally different from market economies. Capacity is built for strategic reasons, with profitability a secondary concern.

## What Historically Causes Margins to Mean-Revert?

**Global semiconductor equipment cycles:**

| Cycle | Trigger | Depth | Duration |
|-------|---------|-------|----------|
| 2018-2019 | Trade war, memory oversupply | SOX -26% | ~6 quarters |
| 2020 (COVID) | Demand shock | SOX -30% | ~2 quarters |
| 2022-2023 | Inflation, rate hikes, inventory correction | SOX -45% | ~6 quarters |

**Mean reversion mechanisms:**
1. Inventory buildups force destocking and order pauses
2. Memory pricing collapses trigger capex cuts (memory is most volatile segment)
3. Macro shocks reduce end demand, propagating up supply chain
4. Overbuilding leads to utilization drops below 75%, forcing capex pauses

**Industry margin ranges (historical):**
- Peak cycle (2021): Top equipment makers averaged 20%+ profit margins; TSMC 37.6%
- Trough: Economic profits can go negative; 2023-2024 saw aggregate economic losses of $70B and $40B respectively
- Mid-cycle: Industry participants target 15-18% operating margins as sustainable

## How Volatile Are Industry Economics Across Full Cycles?

**High volatility** - This is a deeply cyclical industry.

- Revenue declines of 19-29% occurred in 2022-2023 for U.S. semiconductor machinery manufacturing (per IBISWorld)
- SOX corrections of 25-50% occur regularly every 3-5 years
- Peak-to-trough revenue swings of 30-40% are normal for equipment makers

**Volatility sources:**
1. **Bullwhip effect** - Small demand changes amplify into large order swings
2. **Lumpy capex decisions** - All-or-nothing fab investment creates order cliffs
3. **Memory segment** - NAND/DRAM cycles are extreme (memory capex swung +56% YoY in Q4 2024)

**China-specific volatility:**
- Superimposed on normal cyclicality is export control policy risk
- China equipment spending surged 35% YoY in 2024, now expected to decline 24% in 2025
- This is not normal cyclicality - it is policy-driven demand pull-forward and subsequent normalization

## What Does "Mid-Cycle" Mean for This Industry?

**Quantitative benchmarks (global WFE):**
- Mid-cycle WFE spending: $100-110B annually (we are above this at $125B+ forecast for 2025)
- Mid-cycle capacity utilization: 75-85% at customer fabs
- Mid-cycle operating margins for equipment makers: 15-20%

**Qualitative definition:**
Mid-cycle represents:
- Fab utilization at sustainable levels (no rush orders, no idle capacity)
- Normal replacement and technology upgrade demand
- Inventory levels at target (2-3 months in channel)
- No major pull-forward or destocking dynamics

**Current position vs. mid-cycle:**
- Global WFE appears late-cycle, with 2024 representing record China spending now set to decline
- China specifically is past peak, entering digestion phase after 2024 front-loading
- Memory segment is recovering but volatile
- AI-driven leading-edge demand provides secular tailwind, but this is concentrated in Taiwan/Korea

**Justification:**
1. 4-5 year boom-bust cycles are structural to the industry
2. Revenue swings of 25-40% peak-to-trough

4. China exposure adds policy/geopolitical volatility layer on top of normal cyclicality
5. Memory segment (40%+ of WFE) is extremely volatile

## Structural vs. Cyclical Forces

**Secular/Structural Tailwinds:**
1. AI driving leading-edge capacity expansion (2nm GAA nodes)
2. Advanced packaging complexity increasing equipment intensity
3. China localization mandate creates sustained domestic demand
4. Geopolitical fragmentation driving regional capacity redundancy

**Secular/Structural Headwinds:**
1. China mature node overcapacity threatening pricing globally
2. Export control tightening reducing accessible market for all players
3. WFE market maturity limiting long-term growth to GDP+

**Cyclical Forces (Current):**
1. Memory recovery from 2023 trough - positive but slowing
2. China capex normalization after 2024 peak - negative near-term
3. Inventory cycle largely normalized - neutral
4. Interest rate uncertainty affecting fab investment decisions

## The One Industry Factor That Matters

**China WFE Spending Trajectory**

For equipment suppliers with heavy China exposure, the single most important variable is China's semiconductor equipment purchasing volume and mix.

**Why this dominates:**
- China was 42% of global WFE in 2024
- A 24% decline in China WFE (from $49.5B to ~$38B) is an $11B+ reduction
- No other regional market growth can offset this magnitude
- Export controls create discontinuous, unpredictable demand shifts

**What would cause inflection:**
- Easing of export controls (unlikely under current geopolitical trajectory)
- Accelerated localization reducing import needs (ongoing, gradual)
- China fab overcapacity triggering extended capex pause (emerging risk)

**How to monitor:**
- SEMI quarterly regional WFE data
- Chinese foundry utilization rates (SMIC, Hua Hong reporting)
- Export control policy announcements (BIS, Commerce Department)
- Big Fund disbursement and new fab announcements

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**The Non-Consensus View: China equipment spending has peaked structurally, not just cyclically.**

**Consensus narrative:** China WFE decline in 2025-2026 is temporary normalization after pull-forward; spending will resume growth as localization progresses.

**What consensus may be missing:**

1. **Mature node saturation is real** - China's 28% share of global mature capacity by end-2025, projected to reach 39% by 2027, creates structural overcapacity. There is a ceiling on how many cleaning tools you need for mature fabs that are 70% utilized.

2. **Localization success reduces equipment imports** - As domestic suppliers reach 50% self-sufficiency (targeted for 2025), each dollar of fab capex translates to fewer dollars of import demand. This is a shrinking pie for non-domestic suppliers.

3. **Export controls have forced technology substitution** - Without access to EUV, China cannot profitably expand leading-edge capacity. This caps the addressable market for advanced tools.

4. **Capex-to-demand mismatch is structural** - China is spending at "3x market justifiable levels" (per industry reports). This is unsustainable and will normalize, with unknown timing.

**Implication:** The 2024 peak in China WFE may not be cyclical - it may be a structural high watermark. Equipment suppliers dependent on China face a shrinking addressable market, not a temporarily depressed one.

**Bullish or bearish for industry participants:** BEARISH for firms with concentrated China exposure, particularly if selling into mature node fabs. The combination of overcapacity, localization, and export controls creates a secular headwind.

## Primary Industry Risk

**Mature node overcapacity in China triggering sustained pricing pressure and capex pause**

If Chinese foundries cannot achieve acceptable utilization rates due to oversupply, capex will pause regardless of government mandates. This would simultaneously reduce demand while domestic suppliers gain share, creating a double-negative for equipment revenues.

Secondary risks:
- Further export control tightening reducing addressable market
- Geopolitical escalation creating demand cliff
- Domestic Chinese equipment makers achieving technology parity, eliminating differentiation
