# ACM Research (ACMR): Management & Capital Allocation Analysis

---

- Background: Ph.D. and Master of Engineering in Precision Engineering from Osaka University; B.S. in Precision Instruments from Tsinghua University
- Inventor of stress-free Cu polishing technology; holds 100+ patents in semiconductor equipment

**Ownership Structure:**
- David Wang directly owns approximately 9.1-9.6% of ACMR shares (worth ~$220M at recent prices)

**Board Composition:**
- David Wang (CEO/Founder/Director)

- ACM Research, Inc. (Delaware corporation, NASDAQ: ACMR) - US parent
- ACM Research (Shanghai), Inc. (STAR Market: 688082) - Primary operating subsidiary, ~74.5% owned by ACMR as of late 2025

## 2. Incentive Structure & Alignment

### Compensation Design

**CEO David Wang (FY2024):**
- Base Salary: $266,940
- Bonus: $227,448
- Stock Awards/Options: $0 (in the reported year)
- Other Compensation: $4,219
- **Total: ~$499K**

This is strikingly low for a US-listed semiconductor equipment company. Median CEO pay for comparable US companies is ~$6.9M. Wang's total compensation is **93% below peer median**.

### What Behavior Does This Compensation Encourage?

**Positive signals:**
1. Near-zero reliance on stock-based pay in recent years suggests Wang is not incentivized to manage the stock price or engage in financial engineering
2. Low cash compensation relative to his ownership stake means Wang's primary wealth creation comes from share appreciation, not salary extraction
3. 9%+ ownership creates structural alignment - Wang wins if shareholders win

**Concerns:**
1. Performance metrics tied to recent option grants (545,400 options vesting October 2025) were market-cap based, not ROIC-based. This incentivizes growth/stock price, not necessarily disciplined capital allocation
2. The 2020 performance grant with $7.36 exercise price vested when market cap reached $2.55B - a growth-focused metric rather than return on capital

### Who Benefits If Stock Underperforms?

Given Wang's 9%+ direct ownership and modest cash compensation, he would disproportionately suffer from stock underperformance. This is favorable alignment. However, ongoing 10b5-1 selling suggests he is monetizing his position systematically regardless of stock performance.

## 3. Stock-Based Compensation Discipline

### SBC Magnitude

**FY2024 SBC Estimate:**
- GAAP Operating Expenses: $240.6M
- Non-GAAP Operating Expenses (ex-SBC): $193.4M
- **Implied SBC: ~$47.2M**
- **SBC as % of Revenue: ~6.0%** (on $782M revenue)

For a semiconductor equipment company, 6% SBC/Revenue is moderate - not egregious, but not trivial either. For context, mature tech companies typically run 5-10%, while high-growth names often exceed 15%.

### SBC Trend Concern

Operating expenses increased 33.4% YoY in FY2024, with the company noting "higher personnel costs, stock-based compensation, and allowance for credit losses" as drivers. SBC is scaling with the business, which is expected but warrants monitoring.

### Buybacks vs Dilution Reality

**Critical Finding: ACMR does not have a share repurchase program.**

- Share count has been steadily increasing
- FY2025 shares outstanding: ~64.9M (up 1.34% YoY per recent data)
- No buybacks means SBC dilution is fully borne by shareholders

**This is a flag.** The company generates significant cash flow ($152M operating cash flow in FY2024) but returns none of it to shareholders via buybacks. All SBC dilution flows through to existing owners unmitigated.

### Net Dilution Assessment

Without an active buyback program, the ~6% SBC run rate represents real dilution to shareholders. Over a 5-year period at this rate, existing shareholders could face 25-30% cumulative dilution if SBC continues unabated and no repurchases offset it.

## 4. Capital Allocation Track Record

### a) Reinvestment

**Organic Reinvestment:**
- Heavy R&D investment (operating expenses at 30.8% of revenue, with R&D a significant component)
- New Oregon facility purchase (39,500 sq ft with cleanroom) for US R&D expansion
- New R&D facility in Lingang, China
- Manufacturing capacity expansion in Shanghai

**Assessment:** ACM is a reinvestment-heavy business at this stage. Growth has been strong (40% revenue growth in FY2024, 32% in Q3 2025), suggesting reinvestment is generating returns. However, no explicit ROIC metrics are disclosed, making it difficult to assess capital efficiency precisely.

### b) M&A

**Minimal M&A Activity.** ACM has grown primarily organically since its 1998 founding. No material acquisitions identified in the search. This is a **positive signal** - the company has not pursued empire-building through deals.

The subsidiaries (ACM Shanghai 2005, ACM Wuxi 2011) were organic formations, not acquisitions.

### c) Buybacks & Issuance

**Buybacks:** None. No share repurchase program exists.

**Issuance:**
- The Shanghai subsidiary (688082) has been the primary equity issuance vehicle
- November 2021: STAR Market IPO - 43.4M shares at RMB 85/share (~$577M gross proceeds)
- September 2025: Private placement - 38.6M shares at RMB 116.11/share (~$630M gross proceeds)
- These issuances reduced ACMR's ownership stake from 82.5% to 74.5%

**Critical Observation:** Rather than issuing equity at the US parent level (ACMR), the company raises capital through the Shanghai subsidiary at significantly higher valuations. The Shanghai entity trades at ~CNY 177/share with a P/E of ~48x, compared to ACMR's ~18-19x P/E. This is financially astute - issuing equity where valuation is highest minimizes dilution per dollar raised.

However, each Shanghai issuance reduces ACMR's economic interest in the operating company. Shareholders in ACMR own a shrinking percentage of the actual business.

### d) Debt Usage

**Current Position (Late 2024/2025):**
- Total Debt: ~$170-287M (varying by report date)
- Cash & Equivalents: ~$374-442M
- **Net Cash Position: $203-259M**
- Debt-to-Equity: ~15%
- Interest Coverage: ~145x

**Assessment:** Extremely conservative balance sheet. The company is net cash positive with de minimis leverage. This is prudent for a capital equipment business subject to cyclicality. No evidence of financial engineering or leveraged transactions.

## 5. Learning vs Repetition

**Patterns Observed:**

1. **Consistent strategic focus:** ACM has maintained focus on wet processing/cleaning equipment for semiconductors since founding. No evidence of strategic flailing or pivot chasing.

2. **Geographic expansion discipline:** Entry into China via Shanghai subsidiary (2005) was methodical and has proven successful. The STAR Market listing (2021) was well-timed to access Chinese capital markets.

3. **Dual-listing arbitrage:** The decision to raise capital at the Shanghai subsidiary (higher valuation) rather than the US parent demonstrates financial sophistication and learning. This is a repeated pattern (2021 IPO, 2025 private placement).

4. **No M&A mistakes:** The absence of acquisitions means the absence of acquisition mistakes - a form of discipline, though also potentially luck.

**Concern - Insider Selling Pattern:**

David Wang has established 10b5-1 trading plans and sold consistently:
- March 2025: $3.55M in sales
- September 2025: $4.36M (130K shares)
- November 2024: $1M+ in sales
- December 2025: Multiple sales totaling $4.3M+

While 10b5-1 plans provide legal cover and allow systematic monetization, the pattern of consistent selling by the founder is worth noting. Wang still owns ~9% after these sales, so alignment remains significant, but the selling suggests he is actively reducing concentration.

## 6. Forward-Looking Capital Allocation Risk

### Where Capital Is Likely to Go

1. **Continued R&D reinvestment:** Management has signaled ongoing investment in new product development and US/China facility expansion. The $630M raised via Shanghai private placement is earmarked for "research and development, capital expenditures and working capital."

2. **Working capital absorption:** Semiconductor equipment companies often tie up significant capital in inventory and receivables. ACM had $428M in receivables at one point - this is a cash sink that will grow with the business.

3. **No capital return:** Given no buyback program and no dividend, shareholders should not expect capital returns. All FCF will be reinvested or held.

### Primary Risk: Shanghai Subsidiary Dilution

**The most likely way management destroys value for ACMR shareholders is continued equity issuance at the Shanghai subsidiary level.**

Each share issued by 688082 reduces ACMR's percentage ownership of the operating business. While issuing at premium valuations is smart in one sense, it is effectively converting ACMR shareholders' economic interest into cash held by the subsidiary. If those proceeds are not deployed at returns exceeding the cost of dilution, value leaks.

Current ACMR ownership in Shanghai has declined: 82.5% (2021 IPO) --> 81.1% --> 74.5% (2025)

If this pattern continues, ACMR shareholders could own a controlling but declining stake in the operating company within 5-10 years.

### Secondary Risk: SBC Creep Without Buybacks

With no buyback program and SBC running at 6% of revenue, dilution is a structural headwind. If the business matures and growth slows, SBC as a percentage of value creation could become punitive.

---

## The One Thing That Determines Value Creation

**ACMR's shareholder value creation hinges on whether reinvested capital (in China) generates returns exceeding the effective dilution cost from Shanghai subsidiary equity issuances.**

The business model is straightforward: plow capital into R&D and capacity in China, grow with the domestic semiconductor buildout. The question is whether ACMR shareholders capture those returns or whether they leak out via:
1. Shanghai subsidiary equity issuances reducing ACMR's economic stake
2. SBC dilution at both parent and subsidiary levels
3. Working capital absorption

David Wang's 9% ownership provides significant alignment, but he has access to liquidity through 10b5-1 plans that ordinary shareholders do not. His incentives are structural (ownership) rather than contractual (compensation metrics), which is generally positive.

---

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**The dual-listing structure creates an underappreciated complexity in understanding true shareholder economics.**

Most analysts focus on consolidated financials without parsing the implications of the Shanghai subsidiary's independent capital raising. The fact that 688082 trades at roughly 2.5x the P/E multiple of ACMR creates both an opportunity (cheap capital for growth) and a risk (dilution of parent company's stake).

**Key non-consensus observation:** ACMR is effectively becoming a holding company for a declining percentage stake in a Chinese operating subsidiary. At 74.5% ownership today, each additional Shanghai equity raise pushes this lower. If Shanghai raises capital at this pace (1-2 times per decade), ACMR could own <70% within 5 years.

This makes ACMR shareholders structurally subordinate to Shanghai minority shareholders in some respects - the operating company can issue equity to fund growth, diluting the parent, and there is limited recourse.

The market may be pricing ACMR as a "China semiconductor play" without fully discounting this structural dynamic.
