## Long ACM Research (ACMR) — Condensed Thesis Summary

### Big Picture
- China is aggressively localizing its semiconductor supply chain, especially for AI.
- Domestic champions are being structurally favored due to geopolitics, sanctions, and explicit policy mandates.
- **ACM Research (ACMR)** is positioned to benefit disproportionately as Chinese fabs are effectively *forced buyers* of domestic equipment.

---

### Macro / Policy Backdrop
- China aims to reduce dependence on U.S. semiconductor designs and tools.
- U.S. re-allowed limited Nvidia H200 sales, but China treats this as temporary and non-strategic.
- Result: parallel strategy — *use Western chips where available, but never depend on them*.
- China has largely caught up in **deposition tools** (EPI, CVD, ALD), but **lithography remains the bottleneck** (still struggling at ~28nm).

---

### Why ACMR Specifically
- Added to the U.S. Entity List (Dec 2024), which ironically:
  - Killed global ambitions
  - Cemented an **unassailable domestic moat**
- Core question for 2026: **Who else can Chinese fabs buy from?**

---

### “Forced Buy” Dynamic
**Hard Mandates (Order 79)**
- Applies to critical infrastructure (telecom, banking, cloud).
- Requires **50–100% domestic chip sourcing**.
- Forces SMIC, CXMT, etc. to expand capacity rapidly.
- Foreign suppliers lose access → domestic vendors must fill the gap.

**Soft Mandates (Window Guidance)**
- State-backed fabs guided to **50–70% equipment localization**, especially at mature nodes.
- ACMR increasingly treated as:
  - Default choice for **cleaning tools**
  - Default choice for **electrochemical plating (ECP)**
- Buying from Lam/Tokyo Electron introduces ongoing compliance risk.
- ACMR tools viewed as **lower risk, “safe” option**.

> ACMR doesn’t need to win on performance — only to exist as the compliant option, creating a near-guaranteed revenue floor.

---

### Revenue & Localization Link
- China WFE localization rises from ~15% (2020) → ~62% (2026E).
- ACMR revenue scales in lockstep:
  - ~$157M (2020) → ~$1.1B (2026E).
- Transitioning from “global-local hybrid” → **full national champion**.

---

### Key Growth Drivers
1. **50%+ Localization Mandate**
   - Forces fab expansion
   - Forces equipment purchases from ACMR

2. **CXMT + HBM**
   - CXMT delivered HBM3 samples to Huawei (late 2025).
   - Volume production targeted for 2026.
   - HBM manufacturing is TSV-intensive and **cannot be done without ECP**.
   - ACMR’s Ultra ECP tools are the **domestic standard**.
   - Drives **margin expansion**, not just revenue growth.

3. **Track Tool Optionality**
   - ACMR began shipping Ultra Lith Track systems in late 2025.
   - Enters market long monopolized by Kingsemi.
   - Fabs want second-source suppliers for leverage and security.
   - Even **10–20% share** unlocks meaningful new TAM.
   - Enables platform strategy (cleaning + furnace + ECP + track).

---

### Revenue Mix Evolution
- Shift toward higher-margin segments:
  - ECP (especially HBM/TSV) becomes a major contributor by 2026.
  - Advanced packaging grows alongside AI memory demand.
- CXMT ramp directly shifts ACMR’s mix toward profitability.

---

### Competitive Positioning
- Platform trajectory similar to Naura, but earlier-stage.
- Platform vendors benefit from:
  - Stickier customer relationships
  - Cross-selling
  - Operational integration within fabs
- Stock performance shows strong relative momentum vs Naura, but still catch-up potential.

---

### Valuation / Asymmetry
- Roughly **~9x forward earnings** (assumes ~$4.30 EPS in 2026).
- Upside driven by:
  - Policy-enforced demand
  - Margin expansion via HBM/ECP
  - Optionality from track tools
- Downside cushioned by regulatory revenue floor.

---

### Bottom Line
ACMR is not a bet on technological supremacy — it’s a bet on **policy, coercion, and inevitability**. In a system where customers are mandated to buy domestic, “good enough” wins, and ACMR is positioned as the safest, default supplier as China’s semiconductor buildout accelerates into 2026.
