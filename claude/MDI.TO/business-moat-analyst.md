# Major Drilling Group International (MDI.TO) -- Business Quality & Moat Analysis

## Business Model Summary

Major Drilling is the world's largest specialized contract driller for the mining sector. Revenue (~C$728M fiscal 2025) comes from charging mining and exploration companies on a per-meter or per-shift basis to drill core samples, production holes, and blast holes. The company operates 708 rigs across Canada/U.S., South/Central America, Australasia, and Africa. Cost structure is semi-variable: rigs and infrastructure are fixed, but labor (the largest cost) scales with activity. Approximately 60% of revenue comes from "specialized" work (deep hole, directional, underground), with the remainder from conventional surface drilling. The company does not own mines or mineral rights -- it is purely a services business whose volumes are dictated by customer exploration and development budgets.

## Source(s) of Advantage

**Specialized Equipment and Technical Capability.** MDI's claimed moat centers on owning rigs capable of deep, directional, and underground drilling that smaller competitors cannot replicate without significant capital investment (a new specialized rig costs C$1-3M+). The mechanism: when a mining company needs to drill at 2,000+ meters depth or in complex underground environments, the pool of capable contractors shrinks dramatically. This narrows competitive bidding and supports pricing. However, Boart Longyear (larger by revenue, ~163% of MDI), Foraco, and several regional players also operate specialized fleets. The advantage is real but shared among a handful of scaled players, not unique to MDI.

**Scale and Geographic Reach.** With 708 rigs across five continents, MDI can mobilize equipment to where demand surges, and its balance sheet (net cash position historically) allows it to invest counter-cyclically -- buying rigs and competitors during downturns when smaller players are capital-constrained. The Explomin acquisition (2024) exemplifies this. Scale provides a modest advantage in winning multi-site contracts with senior miners who prefer a single contractor across jurisdictions.

**Fleet Quality.** MDI has invested in fleet modernization, adding new rigs while disposing of older units. Newer rigs have higher productivity, lower downtime, and better safety metrics. This matters because mining companies internalize total project cost (including downtime risk), not just price-per-meter. MDI's record-low safety incident rate (TRIFR 0.74) is a tangible differentiator with safety-conscious senior miners.

**Relationships with Senior Miners.** Over 90% of Explomin's revenue and a substantial share of MDI's legacy business comes from senior mining companies. Long-standing relationships create behavioral stickiness but not structural lock-in.

## Competitive Landscape

The contract drilling market is fragmented at the low end and oligopolistic at the specialized end. Major competitors include Boart Longyear (largest globally), Foraco, Capital Drilling, Geodrill, Orbit Garant, and dozens of regional operators. In conventional surface drilling, competition is intense and commoditized -- barriers to entry are low (used rigs are cheap) and switching costs are minimal. In specialized drilling, the competitive set narrows to perhaps 4-5 global players, but pricing pressure still exists. MDI's Q2 FY2026 results show adjusted gross margins falling from 30.5% to 26.0% year-over-year despite record revenue of C$244M, explicitly attributed to "competitive pricing environment in North America." This is critical evidence: even in an upcycle with record volumes, margins compressed. Competition erodes pricing power even in specialized work.

Entry barriers are moderate. Capital requirements for specialized rigs are meaningful but not prohibitive for well-funded competitors. The real barrier is the combination of equipment, trained crews, safety track record, and geographic presence -- a package that takes years to build but that several competitors already possess.

## Customer Behavior & Revenue Quality

**Switching costs are low to moderate.** Mining companies routinely use multiple drillers simultaneously on the same project and regularly rebid contracts. There is no technical lock-in: drill core is standardized, and rigs can be swapped between contractors. The switching cost is operational disruption (mobilization time, crew familiarization) and safety qualification -- meaningful but not prohibitive. Customers are "lazy" more than "locked in."

**Customer concentration is a risk.** The Indonesian mine incident that shut down MDI's largest customer in that region for a full quarter demonstrates the fragility. MDI does not disclose exact concentration figures publicly, but dependence on senior miners means a handful of large customers drive a disproportionate share of revenue.

**Contract structure is short-term.** Most contracts are per-meter or per-shift with limited long-term commitments. Explomin's longer-term contract profile is an exception. Revenue visibility is poor -- activity can be curtailed with minimal notice as exploration budgets shift.

## Evidence of Moat in the Financials

The financials reveal a cyclical business with moderate returns, not a durable compounder. Fiscal 2025 EBITDA was C$101M on C$728M revenue (13.9% margin), down from C$121M on C$707M the prior year. In the 2013-2016 mining downturn, exploration spending fell from $21.5B to $6.9B globally, and MDI's revenue collapsed proportionally. Current exploration budgets ($12.5B in 2024) remain only 60% of the 2012 peak. MDI has never sustainably earned above mid-teens EBITDA margins through a full cycle. Margins expand in upcycles but are immediately competed away -- Q2 FY2026 proves this in real time: record revenue, lower margins than a year earlier.

## Fragility Analysis

**The weakest point is demand cyclicality.** MDI's revenue is a derivative of mining exploration budgets, which are themselves a derivative of commodity prices and mining company capital allocation decisions. In downturns, revenue can fall 50%+ while fixed costs remain. MDI survived the 2013-2016 downturn due to balance sheet strength, but smaller competitors were destroyed -- only to be replaced by new entrants in the next upcycle.

**Margin fragility in upcycles.** The current cycle demonstrates that volume recovery does not guarantee margin recovery. As activity ramps, MDI must hire and train crews (depressing margins), and competitors re-enter, intensifying price competition. The Explomin acquisition adds volume but at lower margins, diluting the specialized mix.

**Geographic and customer concentration.** The Indonesia shutdown wiped out an entire region's contribution for a quarter. Single-customer or single-geography events create meaningful earnings volatility.

## Moat & Durability Classification

**Moat Strength: Weak.** MDI has operational advantages (scale, fleet quality, safety record) that are real but shared with several competitors. There are no structural barriers that prevent margin erosion during upcycles or revenue collapse during downturns. Specialized drilling narrows the competitive set but does not eliminate price competition.

**Earnings Durability: Low.** Earnings are highly cyclical, tied to mining exploration spending over which MDI has zero control. Through-cycle margins are modest, and the current upcycle is already showing margin compression despite record volumes.

## The One Thing That Matters for Durability

The single most important factor is the global mining exploration spending cycle. MDI's revenue, margins, and returns are almost entirely determined by whether mining companies are spending on exploration. Technology, fleet quality, and geographic reach are second-order factors. If exploration spending doubles, MDI does well regardless of competitive dynamics. If it halves, no amount of specialization prevents severe earnings declines. This makes MDI a cyclical services business first and a "specialized driller" second.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**Bearish non-consensus insight:** The market likely overestimates MDI's ability to capture margin expansion in an upcycle. The bull case rests on operational leverage -- record revenue should mean expanding margins. But Q2 FY2026 demonstrates the opposite: 29% revenue growth with gross margin contraction from 30.5% to 26.0%. The mechanism is that upcycles simultaneously trigger (a) crew training costs as MDI hires aggressively, (b) competitive re-entry from smaller players and equipment reactivation, and (c) acquisition-driven margin dilution (Explomin). MDI captures volume in upcycles but gives back margin. This means normalized earnings may be lower than consensus assumes even in a strong demand environment. The "specialized driller" label masks a business where the competitive moat is narrower than the marketing suggests -- Boart Longyear, Foraco, and others offer comparable specialized capabilities, and in North America (MDI's largest market), pricing is explicitly competitive per management's own commentary.
