# Major Drilling Group International (MDI.TO) -- Management & Capital Allocation Assessment

## 1. Management Overview

Denis Larocque has been CEO since September 2015 and has 30+ years with the company. He is a career MDI employee who rose through the ranks. Total compensation is approximately C$1.73M (29% salary, 71% bonus/equity). Insider ownership is low: Larocque holds ~0.1% of shares (C$741K). The board collectively holds ~C$2.7M. No single insider is a meaningful owner. Institutional ownership is 45%; the general public holds 55%. FMR LLC is the largest shareholder at 8.7%. Shares outstanding: 81.9M.

## 2. Incentive Structure & Alignment

CEO compensation of C$1.73M is moderate for a ~C$840M market cap company. The 71% variable component suggests meaningful performance linkage, but without disclosed metrics tied to ROIC or per-share value creation, the structure likely incentivizes revenue and EBITDA growth -- standard for the drilling industry but not ideal for cyclical capital allocation discipline. Insider ownership under 1% is a clear weakness. Management's downside exposure is primarily through job security, not equity losses. If MDI stock drops 50% in a downturn, Larocque loses roughly C$370K of equity value -- not painful enough to override career incentives to grow the business. The incentive structure encourages growth and scale, not necessarily disciplined capital return.

## 3. Stock-Based Compensation Discipline

SBC is embedded in general and corporate expenses and is not prominently broken out, which itself is a minor yellow flag. For fiscal 2025 (ended April 2025), revenue was C$728M and net income was C$26M. Share count has been relatively stable around 81-82M shares, suggesting SBC dilution is modest but buybacks have only recently begun. MDI initiated an NCIB in October 2025 to buy up to ~4.1M shares (5% of float). In Q2 FY2024, MDI spent C$7.3M buying back 875K shares at ~C$8.31/share. The share count has not declined materially over time, indicating buybacks have been small relative to the float. Net dilution appears low but net share reduction is also minimal. SBC is not a major concern but is also not being actively offset.

## 4. Capital Allocation Track Record

**Reinvestment:** MDI invested C$72.5M in capex in fiscal 2025 on C$728M revenue (10% of revenue), with fleet at 707 rigs. Q2 FY2026 capex was C$11.8M (adding 2 rigs, disposing of 4). The company has historically run a large fleet and cycled older rigs out -- this is operationally sensible. The critical question is timing. MDI invested C$72.5M in capex during FY2025 while net income was only C$26M -- capex exceeded earnings, funded partly by operating cash flow exceeding net income (depreciation add-back). This is aggressive reinvestment at what may be near-cycle-peak activity.

**M&A:** Three notable acquisitions: McKay Drilling (Australia, A$80M, 2021), Explomin (Peru, US$85M, 2024), and Taurus Drilling. McKay was acquired in mid-2021 during a mining cycle trough-to-recovery, which was decent timing. The deal structure (A$40M cash + A$15M shares + A$25M earnout) showed discipline. Explomin was acquired in November 2024 for up to US$85M (US$63M upfront + US$22M earnout). Explomin generated US$95M revenue and US$16M EBITDA -- implying ~5.3x trailing EBITDA on the full earnout, or ~3.9x on the upfront payment. These are reasonable multiples for a specialty driller in a growing region. The earnout structure requiring ~US$21M average EBITDA (vs US$16M trailing) means MDI is paying full price only if the business grows ~30%. This is disciplined deal structuring. However, both major acquisitions occurred during a strong mining cycle. MDI is buying at the top of activity, not at the trough. This is the classic cyclical trap -- growth capex and acquisitions cluster when cash flow is high and confidence is elevated.

**Buybacks & Issuance:** MDI issued shares for McKay (A$15M in stock). The NCIB is new (October 2025). Historically, MDI has not been a significant buyer of its own stock. The company eliminated its dividend in 2015 and has not reinstated it. This is actually disciplined -- suspending dividends during a downturn and not reinstating them shows willingness to prioritize balance sheet health over optics.

**Debt Usage:** MDI moved to a net cash position (C$14.3M net cash) as of Q2 FY2026, with C$149M total liquidity. The balance sheet is conservatively managed. Debt is modest at C$37M against C$46M cash. This is a genuine strength -- the company has avoided leverage addiction that destroys cyclical businesses.

## 5. Learning vs Repetition

Positive signals: MDI survived the 2014-2020 mining downturn without a balance sheet crisis. They cut the dividend in 2015 rather than borrowing to maintain it. They maintained fleet quality through the downturn. The Larocque era (2015-present) spans a full cycle, and the company emerged financially intact. The deal structures (earnouts, mixed consideration) show learning from industry M&A disasters. Negative signals: MDI is now deploying capital aggressively at elevated activity levels. FY2025 capex of C$72.5M with only C$26M net income is a large reinvestment at what could be a cycle peak. Two acquisitions totaling ~C$190M combined in 3 years during a strong cycle is pattern-consistent with pro-cyclical capital deployment.

## 6. Forward-Looking Capital Allocation Risk

MDI has record revenue (C$244M quarterly) and is investing heavily. The risk is straightforward: if mining exploration spending contracts, MDI will be carrying an expanded fleet and integrated acquisitions into a downturn. The conservative balance sheet provides a buffer, but the pattern of heavy investment during cycle peaks is the dominant risk. The NCIB suggests management is starting to think about capital return, but at 5% of float over 12 months, this is modest.

## The One Thing That Determines Value Creation

Whether MDI can resist the urge to make another large acquisition in the next 12-18 months. The company has done two deals totaling ~C$190M in three years during a strong cycle. One more deal at the top would confirm a pro-cyclical empire-building pattern. Conversely, if MDI shifts to aggressive buybacks and debt paydown from here, it would signal genuine cycle-awareness. The next C$100M of capital deployment will be definitive.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

Consensus likely views MDI as a well-run, disciplined driller with a clean balance sheet and a good CEO. The non-consensus insight: insider ownership is negligibly low for a company that requires cycle-timing discipline. Larocque has been CEO for 10 years and owns 0.1% of the company. For a cyclical business where the difference between good and great is entirely about when you invest and when you harvest, having a CEO with minimal equity skin in the game is a structural misalignment. The disciplined deal structures and balance sheet management are real positives, but they may reflect institutional memory from the 2014-2020 downturn rather than structural incentive alignment. When that institutional memory fades -- or when a sufficiently attractive "transformational" deal appears -- there is no ownership-based governor on behavior.

## Final Classification

- **Incentive Alignment:** Mixed -- reasonable compensation but negligible insider ownership for a cyclical business
- **Capital Allocation Discipline:** Moderate -- conservative balance sheet and smart deal structures, but pro-cyclical investment timing
- **Dilution Risk:** Low -- SBC is modest and share count is stable
- **Primary Risk:** Pro-cyclical capital deployment with insufficient insider ownership to enforce discipline at cycle peaks
