# Mining Drilling Services: Industry Structure & Cycle Analysis

**Date:** 2026-02-24

## Industry Economics

Mining drilling services is a capacity-constrained, cyclical business where profitability is driven by utilization rates and pricing power on specialized rigs. The industry's revenue is a derivative of exploration spending, which is itself a derivative of commodity prices and miner profitability. This double-derivative nature amplifies cyclicality in both directions.

Pricing power accrues disproportionately to operators of specialized and underground rigs -- these require trained crews and purpose-built equipment that cannot be replicated quickly. Conventional surface drilling is more commoditized. Industry gross margins historically range from 20% at trough to 35%+ at peak, with the swing driven almost entirely by utilization.

## Where Are We in the Exploration Cycle?

Global nonferrous exploration budgets hit $20.5B at the 2012 peak (per [S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/research/world-exploration-trends-2024)). They collapsed to roughly $7B by 2016, then recovered gradually to $12.9B in 2023 before declining again to [$12.5B in 2024](https://www.mining.com/global-exploration-budgets-fall-as-juniors-tighten-belts-sp/). Current budgets remain 39% below the 2012 peak in nominal terms -- adjusted for inflation, the gap is closer to 50%.

The critical disconnect: gold has risen roughly 65% in the past year to all-time highs above $2,800/oz, yet gold exploration budgets declined 7% in 2024. S&P Global expects spending to [stay roughly flat in 2025](https://www.mining.com/exploration-spending-to-stay-flat-despite-trump-pledges-strong-gold-prices-sp-says/). Grassroots exploration as a share of total budgets is at an [all-time low](https://www.mining.com/budget-allocated-to-grassroots-exploration-at-all-time-low-sp/).

Historical research by [MinEx Consulting](https://minexconsulting.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/NewGenGold-2013-R-Schodde-paper.pdf) shows exploration spending correlates with gold prices at R-squared of 0.89 post-1985, but the relationship operates with a lag. The key variable is not the price level but sustained price strength and its rate of change, which drives sentiment. Historically, the lag between sustained high commodity prices and exploration budget inflection is 12-24 months. In the 2009-2012 cycle, gold broke above $1,000 in late 2009, but exploration budgets did not peak until 2012 -- a roughly 3-year ramp.

**Current assessment: We are in early cycle for the exploration spending upturn.** Gold prices have been sustainably elevated for roughly 12-18 months. The lag to budget response is underway but has not yet materialized in aggregate spending data. Junior miner financing -- the leading indicator -- fell 12% to $10.3B in 2024, though gold-focused juniors are seeing improving access to capital.

## Supply-Side Dynamics: The Capacity Squeeze

This is where the structural argument becomes compelling. The drilling services industry experienced severe capacity destruction from 2013-2020. Rigs were scrapped, crews dispersed, and new equipment investment was minimal. Per the [2025 NOV Rig Census](https://drillingcontractor.org/2025-nov-rig-census-shows-global-drilling-industry-shift-toward-efficiency-consolidation-75701), the global fleet is aging with minimal newbuilds, and the industry has consolidated significantly.

Major Drilling's fleet of 707 rigs sits at just 51% utilization (Q2 FY2026, ended Oct 2025), with [record quarterly revenue of $244M](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/12/10/3203539/0/en/Major-Drilling-Announces-Record-Quarterly-Revenue-for-its-Second-Quarter-2026.html) despite being well below full capacity. Management guides to maximum utilization of 75-80%. The gap between current (51%) and peak (75-80%) represents enormous operating leverage.

The constraint is not rigs -- it is trained drilling crews. Experienced drillers left the industry during the downturn and have not returned. Recruiting and training takes 12-18 months. This labor bottleneck means that even when demand inflects sharply, supply response will be slow, supporting pricing power.

## The Structural Demand Thesis

The bull case rests on three structural forces:

1. **Depleting reserves:** Average copper ore grades have fallen roughly 40% since 1991 (per [industry data](https://rough-polished.expert/en/analytics/140353.html)). Lower grades require more drilling per unit of metal discovered and produced.

2. **Deeper, more complex deposits:** Average mine development timelines have stretched from 12.7 years (2005-2009 cohort) to 17.9 years (2020-2023 cohort), per [EY mining risk analysis](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/insights/mining-metals/risks-opportunities). Deeper deposits require specialized drilling capabilities.

3. **Exploration pipeline deficit:** A decade of underinvestment has depleted discovery pipelines. Major gold discoveries have fallen dramatically even as demand for reserves grows. Mining companies must drill more just to maintain production.

These forces are real and well-documented. The question is timing, not direction.

## Comparison to the 2010-2012 Peak

| Metric | 2012 Peak | Current (2024-25) |
|---|---|---|
| Global exploration budget | $20.5B | $12.5B |
| Gold price (approx) | $1,700/oz | $2,800/oz |
| Junior financing | Robust | Constrained but improving |
| Industry utilization | 70-80% | ~50% |
| Grassroots share of budgets | ~35% | All-time low |

We are nowhere near the 2012 peak in activity terms. Current exploration budgets, adjusted for inflation, are roughly half of peak. Utilization is 50% vs. 70-80% at peak. This is early cycle by any historical measure.

## Counter-Arguments

The thesis could fail or be delayed by: (1) Permitting and regulatory delays extending project timelines further; (2) Capital discipline by major miners who have been burned by prior cycles and may prefer M&A over greenfield exploration; (3) Efficiency gains from AI-assisted targeting and directional drilling reducing required drill meters per discovery; (4) A sharp gold price reversal that kills sentiment before budgets inflect. The M&A-over-exploration trend is the most credible risk -- mining companies have been acquiring reserves through buyouts rather than drilling for them, which could structurally reduce the exploration multiplier on commodity prices.

## The One Industry Factor That Matters

**Junior miner equity financing.** Juniors account for roughly 30% of global exploration spending and are the marginal source of drilling demand. Their ability to raise capital -- driven by commodity prices, risk appetite, and equity market conditions -- is the single best leading indicator of drilling demand inflection. Monthly TSXV financing data is the metric to watch. When junior financing sustainably exceeds $1.5B/month, the drilling upturn is confirmed.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

Consensus acknowledges the exploration underinvestment narrative but underestimates how constrained the supply response will be when demand inflects. The market prices drilling services companies as cyclicals that mean-revert on margins. The variant view: the labor bottleneck and fleet consolidation have created a structurally tighter supply side than in any prior cycle. When the exploration spending inflection arrives -- likely in the next 6-18 months given sustained commodity prices -- utilization will ramp faster and pricing power will be stronger than historical analogs suggest. The 2012 cycle had abundant spare capacity and labor; this cycle does not. This is moderately bullish for industry participants, with the caveat that timing remains the key uncertainty.
