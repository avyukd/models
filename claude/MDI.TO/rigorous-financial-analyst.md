# Major Drilling Group International (MDI.TO) -- Financial Analysis

**Date:** February 24, 2026 | **Price:** ~C$14.70 | **Shares:** ~82M | **Market Cap:** ~C$1.2B

## Business Financial Profile

Contract drilling services for mining companies globally. Capital-intensive, highly cyclical, operationally leveraged to exploration/development budgets which are driven by commodity prices. Revenue is transactional (project-based), not recurring. 709-rig fleet split: 307 specialized, 163 conventional, 239 underground. Geographic mix shifting toward South America (Explomin acquisition, Nov 2024, ~US$85M). Fixed cost base in labor/equipment creates significant operating leverage on utilization swings.

**Cycle position:** FY2023 was likely near-peak margins (19.6% EBITDA). FY2025 was the trough as North American activity softened and Explomin integration/ramp costs compressed margins. H1 FY2026 shows revenue inflecting sharply higher (Q1: C$227M +19% YoY, Q2: C$244M +29% YoY -- record quarter) but margins lagging at 25-26% gross vs. 29-31% prior year due to startup costs and competitive North American pricing.

**Mid-cycle earnings estimate:** Revenue C$750-800M, EBITDA margin 16-17%, EBITDA C$120-135M. After D&A (~C$55M), interest (~C$3M), tax at 25%, normalized net earnings ~C$47-58M or C$0.57-0.70/sh. SBC is modest for MDI (C$5-7M/yr estimated) and already flows through GAAP earnings.

**Peak earnings:** At FY2023-level margins (19.6%) on FY2026E revenue (~C$857M), EBITDA would reach ~C$168M, net earnings ~C$80-85M or ~C$1.00/sh.

## Cash Flow and Conversion

MDI converts earnings to cash reasonably well through cycles. Net cash position grew from near-zero to C$87M over FY2022-FY2024 despite C$70-75M/yr capex. Working capital is a swing factor -- ramp-up periods consume working capital (net debt of C$4M at FY2025 end after Explomin acquisition consumed ~C$90M). Capex runs C$70-75M annually, of which an estimated C$40-45M is maintenance and C$25-30M is growth/fleet expansion. Free cash flow (CFO less capex) likely ran C$50-70M in the good years (FY2023-FY2024) and was roughly breakeven in FY2025 given the Explomin outlay and working capital build.

**Cash conversion check:** FY2023 net earnings C$75M vs. estimated FCF C$60-70M -- reasonable conversion. The business does convert, but growth capex and working capital swings in ramp periods temporarily absorb cash.

## Balance Sheet and Financial Risk

- Net cash of C$14M as of Q2 FY2026 (Oct 2025), with C$149M total available liquidity
- Long-term debt reduced to zero in FY2024, currently minimal (~C$28M, likely Explomin-related)
- Explomin earnouts: up to US$22M over 3 years -- a real but manageable obligation
- The balance sheet is a fortress relative to cycle risk. MDI can survive a prolonged downturn

## Fleet Utilization and Operating Leverage

Q1 FY2026 utilization: 50% overall (specialized 46%, conventional 50%, underground 54%). This is well below prior cycle peaks. In FY2023, utilization was materially higher. The margin leverage story is straightforward: each incremental utilization point drops to the bottom line with high incrementals because fixed costs (labor, equipment depreciation) are already covered. The gap between 50% utilization and 60%+ utilization is the difference between 14% and 19%+ EBITDA margins.

The current situation -- record revenue but depressed margins -- reflects Explomin integration, new project ramp costs, and competitive pricing in Canada. As these normalize, margins should recover toward 16-18% even without full peak utilization.

For a cyclical drilling services company, 9-10x mid-cycle EBITDA is not cheap. Peer multiples for drilling services typically range 4-7x mid-cycle EBITDA. MDI commands a premium for its specialized fleet, balance sheet quality, and global diversification, but 9x+ is pricing in meaningful cycle improvement already.

**What must be true for current price:** Revenue must reach C$850M+ with margins recovering to at least 15-16%. If the precious metals/copper cycle delivers sustained elevated exploration budgets, this is achievable. If gold corrects or budgets disappoint, you are paying 11x trough EBITDA.

## Key Financial Drivers

1. **Gold/copper prices -> exploration budgets -> utilization rates.** This is the dominant driver. MDI's revenue and margins are slaves to commodity-driven exploration spend. A sustained gold price above US$2,000 and copper above US$4.00 drives the thesis.

2. **Utilization recovery -> margin normalization.** The spread between current 50% utilization and 60%+ is where earnings power lives. Each 5pp of utilization improvement likely adds C$15-25M of EBITDA given the fixed cost base.

3. **Explomin margin convergence.** South American revenue jumped 40% in FY2025. If Explomin margins converge toward MDI's historical levels, the blended margin recovery could surprise to the upside.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**The margin snapback is being underestimated.** The Street sees C$0.77/sh for FY2026, which embeds ~14-15% EBITDA margins on ~C$857M revenue. But MDI is currently absorbing one-time integration, training, and ramp costs from Explomin plus new project startups globally. These are inherently temporary. As these projects mature past the startup phase, gross margins should revert toward 28-30% (from 25-26% currently), driving EBITDA margins back toward 17%+. On C$857M+ revenue, that is C$145M+ EBITDA, or C$0.90+/sh in earnings -- 15-20% above consensus. The market is pricing MDI as if current compressed margins are the new normal rather than a temporary integration trough. For an investor who structurally believes in a precious metals exploration upcycle, the combination of revenue growth AND margin normalization creates a double tailwind that consensus is not fully capturing.

The risk is that competitive pricing in North America persists and Explomin margins never converge, making current margins structural rather than temporary.
