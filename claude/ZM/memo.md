**Conviction: Medium**

---

## Key Drivers (The 3 Things That Matter)

1. **Capital return math on a fortress balance sheet.** $7.8B cash, zero debt, $1.9B FCF. At $75/share Zoom can retire 7-8% of its market cap annually through buybacks alone. Share count is already declining. This is the load-bearing variable -- EPS compounds at high-single-digits even on flat revenue.

2. **Enterprise revenue trajectory.** Enterprise (61% of revenue) grew 7.1% in Q4 with large customer count up 9.3%. If Enterprise sustains mid-to-high single digit growth and Online stabilizes, consolidated growth holds 4-5%. If Enterprise decelerates below 5%, the thesis weakens.

3. **SBC normalization.** SBC declined from 29% to 16% of revenue in three years. Net buybacks now exceed dilution. Economic FCF per share is inflecting higher as this gap closes. This is the under-appreciated operational improvement.

## Thesis in 5 Bullets

- ZM trades at 7.9x EV/reported FCF and ~14x EV/economic FCF (after SBC). For a zero-debt, 40% FCF margin business with 4-5% revenue growth, this prices in perpetual decline that is not occurring.
- The stock just dropped 13% on a $0.04 EPS miss caused by a tax true-up, not operational deterioration. Revenue beat. This is a sentiment-driven dislocation on a business generating $1.9B in annual cash flow.
- Capital return is real and accelerating: $1.6B in buybacks in FY2026, net share count declining, with $7.8B in dry powder. At $75, buyback yield alone is ~7%.
- The "Teams kills Zoom" narrative has been consensus for five years. Revenue has stabilized, not collapsed. The coexistence pattern (Zoom for external, Teams for internal) has proven durable.
- FY2027 FCF guidance of $1.7B (down from $1.9B) reflects increased AI/capex investment, not margin deterioration. Non-GAAP operating margins remain ~40%.

## The Setup

Zoom at $75 is a post-COVID orphan stock trading at trough sentiment. Down 87% from 2021 highs, just hit on a $0.04 EPS miss. The market treats this as a declining tech company. The reality is a $4.9B revenue business generating $1.9B in FCF with $7.8B in cash and zero debt, growing revenue 4-5%.

The competitive landscape is real but overstated. Microsoft Teams has 320M DAUs but the overlap with Zoom is massive -- enterprises use both. Zoom survives because (1) it is the default for external meetings, (2) Zoom Phone and Contact Center offer functionality Teams does not match, and (3) IT teams prefer best-of-breed. This coexistence pattern has been stable for five years.

The moat is weak but earnings durability is medium. This is not a compounder -- it is a cash cow at a value price. The right comparison: Zoom is DOCU (stable, cash-generative, limited growth) but with a cleaner balance sheet, better capital allocation, and a lower valuation. It is not SNAP (melting ice cube with governance problems and no cash flow). Zoom generates real free cash flow; SNAP burns it.

## Numbers That Matter

**Normalized earnings anchor:** ~$1.4-1.5B after-tax economic earnings ($4.60-4.90/share). Economic FCF after SBC: ~$1.1B ($3.50-3.60/share).

**Valuation at $75:**
- EV/Revenue: 3.1x
- EV/Reported FCF: 7.9x
- EV/Economic FCF: ~14x
- P/E on normalized earnings: ~15-16x
- Net cash = $25/share (34% of market cap)

**FY2027 guidance:** Revenue $5.07B (+4.1%). Non-GAAP EPS $5.77-5.81. FCF $1.70-1.74B (step-down from $1.92B due to higher investment).

**Buyback math:** At $75/share, $1.5B in annual buybacks retires ~20M shares (6.5% of float). Combined with organic earnings growth and SBC decline, EPS can compound 8-10% annually on 4% revenue growth. Over 3 years, share count could decline 15-20%.

**Scenario framework:**
- **Bull ($100-110, 18 months):** Enterprise accelerates to 8%+, AI Companion drives upsell, NDER crosses 100%, market re-rates to 12-15x economic FCF. 35-45% upside.
- **Base ($85-95, 12 months):** Revenue grows 4%, buybacks continue, market gives credit for capital return discipline. 13-27% upside.
- **Bear ($55-60):** Enterprise decelerates, NDER drops further, large M&A destroys value. 20-27% downside.
- **Expected value is positive.** Base case delivers mid-teens return with buyback support as a floor.

## Risks and Kill Switches

**Kill switch 1: Large M&A.** Yuan's dual-class control means he can pursue a $5-10B acquisition without shareholder approval. The Five9 appetite is documented. If Zoom announces a deal above $3B, re-evaluate immediately. This is the single biggest risk.

**Kill switch 2: Enterprise deceleration.** If Enterprise revenue growth drops below 3% or NDER falls below 95%, the stable cash cow thesis breaks and this becomes a genuine decline story.

**Kill switch 3: Microsoft Teams Phone/Contact Center reaches feature parity.** If Teams closes the functionality gap in the two product areas where Zoom differentiates, the standalone justification erodes.

**Monitoring: Yuan's insider sales.** 58 sells, zero buys over 5 years. Voting/economic divergence is widening. Not a kill switch today, but directionally negative for alignment.

## Positioning and Portfolio Logic

**Position size: 2-3% of portfolio.** This is a medium-conviction value position with a capital return floor. Not a high-conviction compounder -- the moat is too weak and growth too modest for a larger allocation.

**Why now:** 13% post-earnings sell-off on a tax-driven EPS miss creates a better entry. Sentiment is at trough. The buyback machine is running at $1.5B+/year at these depressed prices, which is maximally accretive.

**Pairs/hedges:** Consider pairing long ZM with short a high-multiple, low-FCF SaaS name. ZM is a natural hedge against SaaS multiple compression -- it trades on cash flow, not growth multiples.

**What I watch post-entry:** (1) Enterprise revenue growth rate quarterly, (2) NDER trend, (3) buyback pace and authorization renewals, (4) any M&A announcements above $1B, (5) SBC as % of revenue continuing to decline.

**Comparison to prior decisions:** ZM is a cleaner version of DOCU -- similar "boring cash cow" profile but with a fortress balance sheet ($7.8B cash vs DOCU's more leveraged structure), declining SBC, and active capital return. It is categorically different from SNAP, which had no FCF, governance nightmares, and a genuinely eroding business. ZM generates real cash and returns it.

**The variant edge in one sentence:** The market prices Zoom as a declining business, but buyback-driven EPS compounding of 8-10% on stable 4-5% revenue growth at 8x EV/FCF creates a mid-teens annual return with downside protection from $25/share in net cash -- and the stock just got 13% cheaper on a noise quarter.
