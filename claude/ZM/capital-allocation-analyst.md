# ZM -- Management & Capital Allocation Assessment

**Stock Price:** ~$85 (Feb 27, 2026) | **Market Cap:** ~$27B | **FY2026 Revenue:** $4.87B | **FCF:** ~$1.86B

---

## 1. Management Overview

Eric Yuan, founder-CEO since 2011. Dual-class structure: Class A (1 vote), Class B (10 votes). As of Jan 31, 2025, 262.8M Class A and 42.5M Class B shares outstanding. Yuan holds the bulk of Class B, giving him ~60% voting control despite economic ownership that has declined to the high single digits through systematic 10b5-1 sales (58 sell transactions, zero buys over 5 years). New CFO hired in FY2025 with a $25M equity package. Board is non-staggered.

## 2. Incentive Structure & Alignment

**CEO compensation (FY2025):** Total comp dropped ~87% YoY and ~96% vs FY2023 due to lower equity grants. Yuan's cash comp is modest. His largest disclosed perquisite is ~$2.0M in security/transportation. He holds only ~426K unvested RSUs across 2022-2024 grants -- a trivially small equity pipeline for a CEO of a $27B company.

**Incentive metrics:** Revenue (80% weight) and non-GAAP operating income (20% weight) drive both cash bonuses and performance-vesting RSUs. FY2025 targets were $4.60B revenue and $1.73B non-GAAP operating income. Actual: $4.67B and $1.84B -- resulting in 106.3% cash payout and 100% RSU vesting (capped at target).

**What behavior this encourages:** The 80/20 revenue/profit split rewards topline growth over margin expansion. The performance RSU cap at 100% of target eliminates upside for exceptional performance, reducing the incentive to swing for the fences. This is a conservative structure -- it punishes misses but doesn't reward blowouts.

**Who benefits if the stock underperforms:** Yuan's economic interest is shrinking through continuous selling. His voting control persists regardless of share price via Class B. If ZM stock languishes, Yuan retains control with diminishing skin in the game -- a textbook principal-agent divergence risk.

## 3. Stock-Based Compensation Discipline

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026E |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|---------|
| SBC ($M) | ~$1,285 | $1,057 | $931 | ~$850E |
| Revenue ($M) | $4,393 | $4,527 | $4,665 | $4,869 |
| SBC % Revenue | ~29% | ~23% | ~20% | ~17%E |

SBC has declined meaningfully -- from nearly 30% of revenue in FY2023 to an estimated ~17% in FY2026. This is a real improvement and not just financial engineering.

**Peer comparison:** DocuSign SBC runs ~22% of revenue; Snap ~19%. Zoom at ~17-20% is now competitive or better than peers, having come from a much worse starting point.

**Share count trajectory:** Diluted shares were ~306M in FY2022, ~304M in FY2023, ~309M in FY2024, ~315M in FY2025, and trending toward ~304M by Q1 FY2027 guidance. The buyback is now bending the share count curve downward for the first time.

**Buyback reality check:** $2.7B repurchased under the program as of Q4 FY2026, retiring ~36.3M shares (about 12% of float). An additional $1.0B was authorized in November 2025, bringing total authorization to $3.7B. At current pace ($324M in Q4 FY2026 alone), buybacks now materially exceed SBC dilution. Net share count is declining -- this is not a "buyback that only offsets dilution" situation.

## 4. Capital Allocation Track Record

**a) Reinvestment:** Zoom is spending into AI ("AI-first work platform" narrative). R&D spend is moderate for a software company. Incremental revenue growth remains low (4-5% YoY) -- reinvestment is not yet producing acceleration. The business throws off ~38% FCF margins, limiting the damage from mediocre reinvestment returns.

**b) M&A:** The Five9 bid ($14.7B, 2021) was the defining moment -- an all-stock deal at the absolute peak of Zoom's valuation, which would have been massively value-destructive for ZM shareholders. Five9 shareholders rejected it. Since then, acquisitions have been small and strategic: Solvvy (contact center AI), Workvivo (employee engagement), BrightHire (talent intelligence). None of these were large enough to move the needle positively or negatively. The restraint after the Five9 fiasco is notable.

**c) Buybacks & Issuance:** Buyback timing has been reasonable. ZM repurchased shares in the $65-80 range through much of FY2025-FY2026, which, at ~15x FCF, represents buying back stock at a defensible valuation. The acceleration of buybacks ($2.7B cumulative) while sitting on $7.8B in cash is a positive signal that capital return is becoming the primary allocation channel.

**d) Debt Usage:** Zero debt. The $7.8B cash/investment pile is earning interest income. This is extreme conservatism -- arguably too conservative, as the excess cash earns below the company's cost of equity.

## 5. Learning vs Repetition

The Five9 deal was a potential catastrophe averted by shareholder vote, not by management discipline. However, post-Five9 behavior shows clear learning: smaller tuck-in deals, no large M&A, accelerating buybacks. SBC has been reined in year-over-year. The trajectory is positive.

The open question: if growth continues to decelerate, does Yuan attempt another transformative acquisition? The $7.8B cash pile creates the capacity for a large deal, and the dual-class structure means shareholders cannot block it.

## 6. Forward-Looking Capital Allocation Risk

Zoom generates ~$1.9B/year in FCF with $7.8B on the balance sheet. The most likely capital deployment paths: (1) continued buybacks at $1.0-1.5B/year -- the constructive scenario; (2) a large acquisition in AI, contact center, or collaboration -- the risk scenario; (3) continued cash hoarding -- the inertia scenario.

The dual-class structure is the critical governance risk. Yuan could pursue a $5-10B acquisition without shareholder approval. The Five9 episode suggests appetite for transformative M&A. The only constraint was external (shareholder vote on the target side), not internal discipline.

---

## The One Thing That Determines Value Creation

Yuan's dual-class voting control combined with a declining economic stake is the single most important factor. Today, this structure is benign because Yuan is choosing buybacks. But the structure means shareholders have no veto over a large, potentially value-destructive acquisition. The risk is binary: if Yuan stays disciplined, the ~$1.9B FCF engine compounds nicely through buybacks. If he pursues a large deal, shareholders have no recourse. The Five9 bid proves the appetite exists.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

Consensus treats Zoom as a boring, declining video company with a big cash pile. What they may underestimate is the SBC improvement trajectory -- from ~29% of revenue to ~17% in three years, with net share count now declining. This is genuine operational discipline that directly flows to shareholders. However, consensus may also be over-trusting the buyback narrative: Yuan's personal economic alignment is deteriorating with every 10b5-1 sale (58 sells, zero buys). The voting/economic divergence is widening, and the cash pile creates optionality that could be used constructively or destructively -- with no shareholder check on the latter.

---

## Final Classification

- **Incentive Alignment:** Mixed -- conservative comp structure, but founder economic ownership declining while voting control persists
- **Capital Allocation Discipline:** Moderate -- strong improvement from Five9 era, buybacks are real, but $7.8B cash hoard is suboptimal and large M&A risk remains
- **Dilution Risk:** Low (improving) -- SBC declining as % of revenue, buybacks now exceed dilution, net share count falling
- **Primary Risk:** Yuan uses dual-class control to pursue a large acquisition that shareholders cannot block, funded by the $7.8B cash pile, repeating the Five9 impulse without the external check that saved shareholders last time
