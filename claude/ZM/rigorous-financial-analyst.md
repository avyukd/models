# ZM (Zoom Communications) -- Financial Analysis
**Date:** 2026-02-27 | **Price:** ~$75 | **Market Cap:** ~$23B | **FY ends Jan 31**

---

## Revenue Trends: Stabilized and Slowly Re-Accelerating

| Period | Total Rev ($M) | YoY Growth | Enterprise Rev ($M) | Enterprise % of Total |
|--------|---------------|------------|---------------------|-----------------------|
| FY2024 | 4,527 | ~3.1% | ~2,618 | ~58% |
| FY2025 | 4,665 | 3.1% | 2,754 | 59% |
| FY2026 | 4,869 | 4.4% | ~2,969 (est) | 61% |

**Quarterly FY2026 trajectory:**
- Q1: $1,175M (+2.9%), Q2: $1,217M (+5.0%), Q3: $1,231M (+4.4%), Q4: $1,247M (+5.3%)

Revenue growth has inflected from ~1% in late FY2024 to 5%+ in recent quarters. Enterprise (61% of revenue) is the engine at +7.1% in Q4; Online (SMB/consumer) grew low-single-digits after years of decline. The mix shift toward Enterprise improves revenue quality.

FY2027 guidance: $5.065-5.075B (~4.1% growth). This suggests management sees steady-state low-to-mid single digit growth.

## Profitability: GAAP Profitable, High Margins

| Metric | FY2025 | FY2026 |
|--------|--------|--------|
| GAAP Operating Income ($M) | 813 | 1,124 |
| GAAP Operating Margin | 17.4% | 23.1% |
| Non-GAAP Operating Margin | ~39% | ~39% |
| GAAP Net Income ($M) | 1,010 | 1,900 |
| GAAP EPS (diluted) | $3.21 | $6.18 |
| Non-GAAP EPS (diluted) | $5.54 | $5.92 |

GAAP net income nearly doubled YoY but includes gains on strategic investments that inflate the number. Non-GAAP EPS grew only 7% -- a more honest reflection of operating improvement. The ~39% non-GAAP operating margin is remarkably stable and among the highest in enterprise software.

## SBC and Dilution

| Metric | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026 |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| SBC ($M, est) | ~1,070 | ~931 | ~768 |
| SBC as % of Revenue | ~23.4% | ~20.0% | ~15.8% |
| Shares Repurchased ($M) | ~800 | ~1,094 | ~1,621 |
| Diluted Shares (M) | ~315 | ~315 | ~308 |

This is a genuine positive trend. SBC is declining both in absolute dollars and as a percentage of revenue (from 23% to 16% in two years). Meanwhile, buybacks have accelerated aggressively -- $1.6B in FY2026 alone reduced diluted shares by ~2%. Net share count is declining, which is unusual for a software company of this size. Zoom is now a net returner of capital.

## Cash Flow and Normalized Earnings

| Metric | FY2025 | FY2026 |
|--------|--------|--------|
| Operating Cash Flow ($M) | 1,945 | 1,989 |
| Capex ($M) | ~131 | ~65 |
| Free Cash Flow ($M) | ~1,814 | ~1,924 |
| FCF Margin | 38.9% | 39.5% |

**Normalized Economic Earnings (FY2026):**

| Line Item | $M |
|-----------|----|
| GAAP Net Income | 1,900 |
| Less: Gains on strategic investments (est) | (450) |
| Add back: SBC-related tax benefit reversal (est) | 50 |
| Adjusted GAAP Net Income | ~1,500 |
| Less: SBC (real cost) | (768) |
| Add back: SBC already in GAAP | 768 |
| **Economic Net Income (GAAP already includes SBC)** | **~1,500** |

GAAP already captures SBC. The key adjustment is stripping gains on strategic investments (~$450M contributed to the GAAP-to-non-GAAP gap in FY2026). Normalized after-tax earnings power is approximately **$1.4-1.5B**, or roughly **$4.60-4.90/share** on ~308M diluted shares.

FCF of ~$1.9B exceeds net income due to working capital tailwinds (deferred revenue) and D&A. True economic FCF after acknowledging SBC as a real cost: ~$1.9B OCF minus $65M capex minus $768M SBC = **~$1.1B economic FCF** ($3.50-3.60/share).

## Balance Sheet

- **Debt:** Zero

## Valuation Framework

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Stock Price | ~$75 |
| Market Cap | ~$23B |
| Net Cash | $7.8B |
| Enterprise Value | ~$15.2B |
| EV/Revenue (FY2026) | 3.1x |
| EV/FCF (reported) | 7.9x |
| EV/Economic FCF (after SBC) | ~13.8x |
| EV/Normalized Earnings | ~10-11x |
| P/E on normalized earnings | ~15-16x |

At 13.8x economic FCF for a zero-debt, 40% FCF margin business growing 4-5%, this is not expensive. The market is pricing Zoom as a low/no-growth utility, which is roughly what it is -- but utilities with 40% margins, zero debt, and $7.8B in cash rarely trade at 8x reported FCF.

**What must be true for this price to make sense:** Revenue growth stays at ~4%, margins are at a ceiling, and the cash pile earns minimal returns. If any of these break positively (AI monetization, M&A deployment of cash, or margin expansion), the stock is underpriced.

**What breaks:** If Online segment resumes decline, dragging consolidated growth negative; or if AI competition (Teams, Meet) erodes Enterprise pricing power.

- [x] Persistent negative FCF: **No.** Massive FCF generator.
- [x] Rising SBC as % of revenue: **No.** Declining from 23% to 16%.
- [ ] Margin expansion via accounting: GAAP NI spike is partly investment gains -- not operational.
- [x] Weak cash conversion: **No.** FCF exceeds GAAP earnings.

- [x] Aggressive revenue recognition: **No obvious issues.**
- [x] Related party transactions: **None flagged.**

The FY2026 GAAP NI of $1.9B is misleading -- roughly $400-500M appears attributable to investment gains. Non-GAAP EPS growth of 7% is the real operating story.

## Key Financial Drivers

1. **Enterprise revenue growth rate** -- This is the stock. If Enterprise can sustain 7%+ growth and Online stabilizes, consolidated growth stays mid-single digits and the multiple holds or expands. Contact Center and Zoom Phone are the specific products to watch.

2. **Capital return trajectory** -- With $1.9B in annual FCF and $7.8B in cash, Zoom can retire 7-8% of its market cap annually. If buybacks accelerate (and they have been), EPS growth will materially exceed revenue growth.

3. **SBC normalization** -- SBC declining from 23% to 16% of revenue is a tailwind to economic earnings. If this continues toward 12-13%, economic FCF per share inflects meaningfully higher.

---

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**The cash pile is an underappreciated lever.** $7.8B in cash on a $15B EV is enormous. At current interest rates, this cash generates ~$350-400M annually in interest income (visible in the gap between operating and net income). The market is valuing Zoom as if this cash earns nothing or will be destroyed via a bad acquisition. If Zoom continues its current path -- buying back $1.5-2.0B in stock annually while generating $1.9B in FCF -- the share count could decline 15-20% over three years, compounding EPS growth to high-single/low-double digits even on flat revenue. That math does not require any AI upside to work.
