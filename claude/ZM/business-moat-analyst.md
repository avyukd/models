# ZM (Zoom Communications) -- Business Quality & Moat Analysis

**Stock Price**: ~$85 (Feb 2026). **Market Cap**: ~$27B. **FY2026 Revenue**: $4.87B (+4.4% YoY). **Non-GAAP Operating Margin**: 40.4%. **FCF**: $1.9B. **Cash**: $7.8B.

---

## 1. Business Model Summary

Zoom sells subscriptions for video conferencing, phone (UCaaS), contact center, and collaboration tools. Revenue splits ~61% Enterprise / ~39% Online (SMB/prosumer). Enterprise revenue grew 6.5% YoY; Online is roughly flat. Gross margins are ~80% (non-GAAP), typical of SaaS. The company generates nearly $2B in annual FCF on under $5B revenue -- an unusually capital-light, high-conversion model. Zoom Phone reached 10M seats; Contact Center surpassed 1,100 customers with 100%+ YoY growth.

## 2. Sources of Advantage

**Switching costs -- moderate in Enterprise, weak in SMB.** Enterprise customers integrate Zoom into workflows (Zoom Rooms, Phone, SSO, compliance configurations). The 98% net dollar expansion rate suggests enterprise customers stay but do not meaningfully expand. Online monthly churn of ~2.8% (~29% annualized) reveals the SMB/consumer base is a revolving door. This is the structural weakness: the largest revenue segment by customer count has essentially no lock-in.

**Brand -- real but depreciating.** "Zoom" became a verb during COVID, conferring default-choice status. But brand in video conferencing translates to consideration, not pricing power. Users do not pay a premium because it is Zoom; they use Zoom because it is familiar. This is behavioral inertia, not structural lock-in. The brand advantage erodes as Teams becomes the default for Microsoft 365 shops and as generational habits shift.

**Product quality / reliability -- historically strong, now table stakes.** Zoom's original moat was that it worked better than alternatives. By 2026, Teams, Meet, and Webex have closed the quality gap. Video conferencing UX is largely commoditized.

**Network effects -- weak.** Video conferencing has cross-platform interoperability. A Zoom user can join a Teams call and vice versa. There is no exclusive network to defend. The "everyone has Zoom" dynamic is a coordination default, not a network effect with reinforcing economics.

## 3. Competitive Landscape

The market is a duopoly with an asymmetric threat. Microsoft Teams has 320M daily active users and is embedded in 90% of Fortune 500 companies via M365. Zoom holds ~56% of video conferencing market share by some measures, but this metric obscures that Teams users often have Zoom too -- the overlap is massive.

**The bundling dynamic is the central competitive fact.** Microsoft bundles Teams into M365 at effectively zero marginal cost. The EU forced temporary unbundling, but 84% of Teams decision-makers said they would have bought it standalone anyway -- the bundling complaint was always more about pricing optics than actual displacement. Microsoft re-bundled in late 2025.

Zoom survives alongside Teams because: (1) many enterprises standardize on Zoom for external-facing meetings even when using Teams internally, (2) Zoom Phone and Contact Center offer functionality Teams lacks or delivers poorly, and (3) IT teams sometimes prefer best-of-breed over Microsoft's integrated-but-mediocre approach.

**Who attacks Zoom?** Microsoft does not need to kill Zoom; it just needs to make Teams "good enough" for each use case Zoom expands into. Google Meet is free and improving but has no enterprise traction. Cisco Webex is declining. The real threat is not a competitor -- it is the steady gravitational pull of M365 consolidation.

## 4. Customer Behavior & Revenue Quality

Enterprise: 4,468 customers at >$100K TTM revenue, up 9.3% YoY. Net dollar expansion at 98% -- below 100% means the average enterprise customer is slightly shrinking their spend. This is a red flag for platform expansion narratives: if Zoom Phone and Contact Center were driving meaningful upsell, NDER should be above 100%.

Online: ~2.8% monthly churn means Zoom replaces roughly a third of its SMB base every year. This is a subscription treadmill, not durable revenue. The 74.4% of Online MRR from customers with 16+ months tenure suggests a stable core, but the margin base is fragile.

**Pricing power is limited.** Zoom competes against a product bundled at zero incremental cost. It cannot raise prices aggressively in Enterprise without accelerating Teams migration. In SMB, free alternatives (Meet, Teams free tier) cap pricing. Zoom's price increases have been modest and infrequent.

## 5. Evidence of Moat in the Financials

The 40% non-GAAP operating margin and 80% gross margin are impressive but require context. Zoom slashed growth spending post-COVID and now runs a mature, optimized cost structure. Margins reflect efficiency, not necessarily pricing power. Revenue growth of 4% barely exceeds inflation -- this is a business maintaining, not compounding.

FCF of $1.9B on $4.9B revenue is genuinely strong. But FCF strength in a low-growth SaaS business often reflects harvesting, not reinvestment-driven compounding. The $7.8B cash pile with no transformative M&A executed suggests management itself may lack conviction in organic reinvestment opportunities.

Margin stability across FY2024-2026 is genuine evidence of durability -- the business has not deteriorated despite the Teams threat. But flat-to-low-single-digit growth with sub-100% NDER is not the profile of a business with a widening moat.

## 6. Fragility Analysis

**Primary fragility: Microsoft moves from "good enough" to "actually good" in every Zoom category.** If Teams Phone and Teams Contact Center reach feature parity with Zoom's offerings, the standalone justification for Zoom in M365 shops evaporates. Microsoft is investing heavily here.

**Secondary fragility: AI as the new bundling vector.** Microsoft Copilot is integrated across M365. Zoom's AI Companion is competitive but lacks the data advantage of sitting inside the Office ecosystem. If AI features become the primary value driver in workplace software, Zoom's standalone position weakens further because it has a narrower data surface.

**Tertiary fragility: the Online segment is structurally unprofitable to defend.** SMB customers face zero switching costs and near-zero price sensitivity to free alternatives. This segment will likely continue to erode.

## 7. Moat & Durability Classification

**Moat Strength: Weak.** Zoom has behavioral inertia and a strong brand but no structural barrier that prevents competitive pressure from eroding returns. Switching costs exist at the enterprise level but are moderate and category-specific, not platform-wide. The 98% NDER confirms customers are not expanding, which means the platform strategy is not yet creating compounding lock-in.

**Earnings Durability: Medium.** Current earnings are real and well-protected by the installed base, cost discipline, and cash generation. But the trajectory is one of managed decline in competitive position, offset by efficiency. Earnings can persist at roughly current levels for several years but face gradual compression as Microsoft's platform gravity increases.

## The One Thing That Matters for Durability

Whether Zoom can convert its video conferencing installed base into a genuine multi-product platform before Microsoft closes the functionality gap. The 98% NDER says this conversion is not happening fast enough. Zoom Phone at 10M seats is promising but still small relative to the enterprise communications TAM. If NDER does not move above 100% within two years, the platform thesis fails and Zoom becomes a single-product company competing against a bundled alternative.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**Bearish insight the bulls overlook**: The 98% enterprise NDER is the most important number in the entire earnings release, and it is below 100%. Bulls point to Contact Center growth and AI Companion adoption, but these are not yet showing up in the aggregate expansion metric. If Zoom's best customers are net-shrinking their spend despite all these new products, the platform expansion story has a data problem.

**Bullish insight the bears overlook**: Zoom has survived five years of the "Teams will kill Zoom" narrative and still generates $1.9B in FCF with 40% margins. The bear case has been consensus since 2021, yet revenue has stabilized, not collapsed. The coexistence pattern -- enterprises using both Teams and Zoom for different purposes -- may be more durable than either side expects. Zoom-for-external, Teams-for-internal is a real workflow pattern with staying power.
