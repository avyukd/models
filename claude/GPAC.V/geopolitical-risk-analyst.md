# GPAC.V (Great Pacific Gold Corp) -- Geopolitical & Sovereign Risk Assessment

**Date:** 2026-02-19
**Asset:** Wild Dog Project, East New Britain Province, Papua New Guinea
**Stage:** Exploration (pre-resource, pre-revenue)
**Market Cap:** ~C$69M

---

## Applicability: Geopolitical Risk Is Material

Single-asset, single-jurisdiction explorer in PNG -- frontier jurisdiction with active resource nationalism, weak rule of law, FX convertibility problems, and a pending Mining Bill that could fundamentally alter development economics.

## 2. Sovereign & Regime Risk

PNG is a parliamentary democracy but governance is volatile. PM Marape survived multiple no-confidence motions. January 2024 riots caused A$160M+ damage. State capacity to enforce its own laws is inconsistent.

### History of Intervention Against Foreign Capital

| Precedent | What Happened | Relevance |
|-----------|---------------|-----------|
| **Panguna/Bougainville (1989)** | Landowner uprising, 20,000 deaths, mine closed permanently. Rio Tinto transferred 53.8% stake for nothing in 2016. | Community opposition can destroy a mine permanently. |
| **Porgera (2020)** | PNG refused to renew Barrick's Special Mining Lease. Forced renegotiation, PNG took 51% ownership. | Direct precedent for state-forced dilution. "Take Back PNG" is not rhetoric -- it has been executed. |
| **Ok Tedi** | State nationalized BHP's stake. | Willingness to expropriate when politically expedient. |
| **Sinivit/New Guinea Gold (2014)** | Company abandoned mine in East New Britain citing government failure to renew mining lease. | **Same province, same geology as GPAC's Wild Dog.** Lease renewal is not automatic. |

---

## 3. Mining Bill 2025 -- The Central Risk

Draft released February 2025, replacing the Mining Act 1992:

| Provision | Detail | Impact on GPAC |
|-----------|--------|----------------|
| **State equity up to 30%** | Free-carry, cost deferred from future earnings | Automatic dilution at development stage |
| **Royalty regime** | 5% with state equity; 10% without | Significant for remote high-cost project |
| **No FIFO** | Generally prohibited | Could increase operating costs substantially |
| **Downstream processing** | Must offer 50% to local processors | Future state interference optionality |

**Critical for GPAC:** Worst provisions apply at development, not exploration. Primary near-term risk is **license renewal and tenure security**. EL 2516 granted April 2024, typical 2-year terms.

---

## 4. FX & Capital Controls

The real problem is **convertibility**, not the exchange rate:
- Chronic FX shortage; conversion from kina to USD/CAD can be slow to impossible
- FX outflows exceeded inflows by US$1.6B in 2023
- IMF program ending late 2026 -- FX rationing could re-emerge after

At exploration stage (spending money in), risk manageable. At production stage (getting money out), could materially impair capital repatriation.

---

## 5. Scenario Framework

**Base Case:** Exploration continues, Mining Bill passed but implementation slow, licenses renewed, ENB remains stable. Sovereign risk latent but doesn't crystallize.

**Adverse Case:** Mining Bill passes with 30% state equity intact. MRA increases scrutiny on renewals. Community disputes emerge. FX tightens post-IMF. **30%+ dilution of project economics at development stage.**

**Severe Tail:** License renewal refused (Sinivit precedent in same province). Or at development stage, state demands >30% equity under threat of lease non-renewal (Porgera precedent). **Permanent capital loss.** A micro-cap has no leverage to resist state action. No reliable ICSID protection.

---

## 6. What Consensus May Be Missing

**The market may be underappreciating how the Mining Bill 2025 structurally caps upside for PNG explorers.** The asymmetric value capture is the key:
- Failure case: shareholders lose 100% (same as any jurisdiction)
- Success case: state captures 30% equity via free carry + 5% royalty before shareholders see returns

This creates a structural ceiling on risk-adjusted value of PNG exploration assets vs. equivalent geology in Australia, Canada, or other frontier jurisdictions.

The **Sinivit precedent in the exact same province** is underweighted -- a site-specific, not generic PNG risk.

Counterpoint: market may be *overweighting* security risk for East New Britain specifically. ENB is materially safer than Highlands provinces. Rabaul provides real infrastructure.
