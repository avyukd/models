# ELME Communities (NYSE: ELME) - Financial Analysis
## Formerly Washington REIT | Liquidation Situation Analysis

**Analysis Date:** January 30, 2026
**Analyst Framework:** Buy-side financial due diligence
**Situation:** Active liquidation (not a going-concern analysis)

---

## Executive Summary

ELME Communities is no longer a going-concern residential REIT. On August 4, 2025, the company announced a plan of sale and liquidation following completion of a strategic alternatives review. The Cortland portfolio sale ($1.6B for 19 properties) closed November 12, 2025, and the initial special liquidating distribution of $14.67/share was paid January 7, 2026.

**Current position:** The stock trades at ~$2.82 (post-distribution), with management estimating remaining liquidating distributions of $2.35-$2.80/share, implying total distributions of $17.02-$17.47/share.

**Key conclusion:** This is now a liquidation arbitrage situation, not a traditional REIT investment. The investment case hinges entirely on the spread between current price and remaining distributions, weighted by execution risk on 10 remaining property sales (including a challenged office asset).

## Section 1: Business Financial Profile (Historical Context)

### Company Background
ELME Communities (formerly Washington Real Estate Investment Trust) was a pure-play multifamily REIT focused on the Washington D.C. metro area with a smaller Atlanta presence. The company transformed from a diversified REIT to a residential-focused entity between 2020-2022.

**Pre-liquidation Portfolio (as of Q3 2025):**
- 28 apartment communities (~9,400 units)
- 1 office property (Watergate 600, ~300,000 SF)
- Geographic mix: ~73% DC Metro, ~27% Atlanta

**Business Characteristics:**
| Factor | Assessment |
|--------|------------|
| Capital Intensity | High (typical for REITs) |
| Cost Structure | ~60% fixed (property taxes, insurance, debt service) |
| Operating Leverage | Moderate |
| Cyclicality | Moderate (residential more defensive than office/retail) |
| Revenue Quality | High recurring (12-month leases, 65% retention) |

## Section 10: Key Financial Drivers

### The 1-3 Metrics That Matter Most

**1. Remaining Asset Sale Prices (Especially Watergate 600)**
- This is the ONLY variable that matters now
- Every $10M variance in sale proceeds = ~$0.11/share in distributions
- Watergate 600 alone could swing outcomes by $0.30-0.50/share
- The distribution estimate cut from $18.50 to $17.47 high-end was primarily driven by Watergate uncertainty

**2. Wind-Down Timing**
- Carrying costs on remaining assets (debt service, operating expenses) erode value
- Every month of delay beyond June 2026 = ~$0.02-0.03/share in erosion
- Goldman's $520M term loan has a cost that accrues against remaining equity

**3. Transaction/Wind-Down Costs**
- Legal, advisory, severance, and administrative costs for liquidation
- Management has provided limited visibility on these costs
- Likely $15-25M total, or $0.17-0.28/share

### Why Other Metrics Don't Matter

- **Same-store NOI growth:** Irrelevant for liquidating company
- **FFO/AFFO:** Operating metrics no longer determine value
- **Leverage ratios:** Only matters for debt covenants on remaining term loan
- **Dividend coverage:** Final dividends will be liquidating distributions

## Section 11: What Consensus May Be Missing

### The Non-Obvious Insight: Watergate 600 Is The Hidden Risk

**The market appears to be underestimating the challenge of selling Watergate 600:**

1. **DC office market is severely distressed:** Class B office in DC is trading at 50-70% of pre-COVID values. The "low teens" cap rate estimate from JPMorgan implies significant value destruction vs. carrying value.

2. **Occupancy is declining:** Watergate 600 occupancy is expected to drop from 84.7% to 81-82% by year-end. In a weak office market, this trajectory could accelerate.

3. **The distribution estimate cut tells the story:** The $0.79 reduction in the midpoint estimate (from $18.04 to $17.25) in just 3 months is disproportionate to what 3 multifamily sales would explain. The math suggests Watergate 600 value estimates have been marked down significantly.

4. **No buyer announced:** As of January 2026, Watergate 600 has no announced buyer despite being marketed since August 2025. In a healthy market, a 300,000 SF DC office building would attract interest. The silence is telling.

**Quantifying the risk:** If Watergate 600 sells at $40M instead of $65M (implied by current estimates), that's $0.28/share downside. If it sells at $25M (distressed), that's $0.45/share downside.

### Alternative View: What Could Go Right

The bull case would require:
1. A strategic buyer for Watergate 600 paying for redevelopment optionality
2. Faster-than-expected remaining multifamily sales reducing carrying costs
3. Lower-than-budgeted wind-down expenses

However, the current stock price (~$2.82) appears to already embed a favorable scenario, leaving limited upside and meaningful downside.

## Section 13: Investment Conclusion

### Summary Assessment

| Factor | Rating | Notes |
|--------|--------|-------|
| Earnings Quality (Historical) | Acceptable | Standard REIT metrics, stable FFO |
| Cash Flow Quality | Good | FFO converted to AFFO reliably |
| Balance Sheet Health | Strong | Conservative leverage, no distress |
| Execution Risk | Elevated | Watergate 600, timing uncertainty |
| Valuation | **Unattractive** | Priced above guidance midpoint |

### Final Verdict

**ELME is fairly to slightly overvalued at current prices.**

The stock trades at ~$2.82 against remaining liquidation estimates of $2.35-$2.80, implying the market expects above-midpoint execution. Given:
- The $0.79/share downward revision in just 3 months
- Unresolved Watergate 600 office sale
- DC office market headwinds
- Asymmetric downside risk

...the risk/reward is unfavorable for new positions. The liquidation will complete, but the current price offers insufficient compensation for execution risk.

**A more attractive entry point would be $2.40-$2.50**, which would offer a reasonable spread to the base case with modest cushion for further estimate reductions.

### For Existing Shareholders

The decision to hold vs. sell depends on tax situation and opportunity cost. The expected return is modestly negative but the downside is capped. There is no catalyst for the stock to trade materially higher from here; it will simply converge toward terminal liquidation value over 6 months.
