**FY2025:** $5,931M revenue (+11% YoY). FY2024: $5,361M.

**North America DAU is declining:** 100M (Q4 2024) to 94M (Q4 2025), a -6% drop. This is the highest-ARPU region ($10.88 in Q4). Global DAU growth decelerated to +5% in Q4 from +9% earlier in the year. Management attributed part of this to cutting community growth marketing spend.

**Other Revenue (Snapchat+):** $232M in Q4 (+62% YoY), 24M subscribers (+71% YoY). This is the bright spot but still only 13.5% of Q4 revenue.

## Profitability: GAAP vs. Adjusted

| Metric | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|--------|--------|--------|
| Revenue | $5,361M | $5,931M |
| GAAP Net Income (Loss) | ($698M) | ($460M) |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $509M | $689M |
| Operating Cash Flow | $413M | $656M |
| Free Cash Flow | $219M | $437M |
| SBC + Related Payroll | ~$1,041M | ~$1,031M |

The gap between Adjusted EBITDA ($689M) and GAAP net loss ($460M) is roughly $1.15B, driven overwhelmingly by SBC. Adjusted EBITDA margin improved to 11.6% from 9.5%. GAAP operating margin remains deeply negative.

## SBC: The Central Issue

| Year | SBC ($M) | Revenue ($M) | SBC % Rev | Shares Out (B) |
|------|----------|-------------|-----------|-----------------|
| 2022 | $1,388 | $4,602 | 30.2% | 1.58 |
| 2023 | $1,324 | $4,607 | 28.7% | 1.64 |
| 2024 | $1,041 | $5,361 | 19.4% | 1.69 |
| 2025 | ~$1,031 | $5,931 | 17.4% | 1.76 |

SBC as a percentage of revenue is declining (30% to 17%), which is real progress. But the absolute dollar amount remains ~$1B/year, and share count has grown from 1.58B to 1.76B over three years (+11.4% cumulative dilution).

**Buybacks vs. Dilution:** SNAP repurchased $751M of stock in FY2025. At ~$7 average price, that is roughly 107M shares retired, while SBC likely vested 150-180M+ shares (net count grew ~70M YoY). Buybacks are partially offsetting but NOT fully covering dilution. The $500M authorized repurchase program is insufficient at current SBC run rates.

## Balance Sheet & Debt

- **Cash & Marketable Securities:** $2.9B
- **Long-Term Debt:** ~$3.5B
- **Net Debt Position:** ~$600M net debt

**Debt Structure (per SEC filings):**
- Convertible notes due 2026: $1,265M (conversion price $22.81 -- deeply out of money)
- Convertible notes due 2027: $1,150M (conversion price $89.25 -- deeply out of money)
- Convertible notes due 2028 and 2030: additional tranches
- Senior notes due 2033 ($700M) and 2034 ($550M)

The 2026 convertible ($1.265B) matures within a year. Management has been proactively refinancing -- repurchasing portions of 2026/2027/2028 notes using proceeds from 2033/2034 senior note issuances. The convertibles will settle in cash given how far out of the money they are, which will consume liquidity. This is a manageable but real near-term cash drain.

## Normalized Earnings Reconciliation

| Line Item | FY2025 ($M) | Notes |
|-----------|-------------|-------|
| GAAP Net Loss | (460) | Reported |
| Add back: SBC + payroll | +1,031 | Per earnings release |
| Add back: D&A (est.) | +200 | Estimate based on capex |
| Adjusted EBITDA | 689 | Reported, cross-checks |
| Less: SBC (real cost) | (1,031) | Must be deducted |
| Less: Capex | (219) | FCF = OCF - Capex |
| Less: Interest (est.) | (150) | ~$3.5B debt at ~4-5% |
| **Normalized After-Tax Earnings** | **~($460M)** | Roughly equals GAAP loss |

**The uncomfortable truth:** SNAP's "profitability" disappears entirely once SBC is treated as a real expense. Free cash flow of $437M looks healthy, but that is before the economic cost of dilution. On a per-share basis: FCF/share = $0.25, but SBC/share = $0.59. Shareholders are paying $0.59 in dilution for every $0.25 of FCF.

**Normalized mid-cycle earnings power: approximately zero to slightly negative.** Even assuming continued SBC reduction to 14-15% of revenue and revenue growth to $7B, normalized earnings would be roughly breakeven.

**What the market is implying at $5.25:** At 1.65x revenue, the market is pricing SNAP as a low-growth, structurally challenged business. The consensus target of ~$8-9.50 implies ~$6.5-7B revenue and expanding EBITDA margins, with SBC continuing to decline as a percentage. For the stock to work, you need: (1) revenue growth reaccelerating to 15%+, (2) SBC declining below 12% of revenue, and (3) North America DAU stabilizing.

## Key Financial Drivers

1. **North America ARPU trajectory** -- 60% of revenue comes from NA at $10.88 ARPU. If NA DAU keeps declining while ARPU growth stalls, the revenue engine breaks. This is the single most important metric.

2. **SBC-to-revenue ratio** -- The path from 17% to sub-12% determines whether SNAP can ever generate real (not adjusted) earnings. Every point of SBC reduction on $6B+ revenue = $60M+ of real earnings improvement.

3. **Snapchat+ subscriber growth** -- At 24M subs growing 71% YoY, this is the only revenue diversification lever. If it scales to $1B+ annually, it materially changes the economics.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**The NA DAU decline is structural, not a marketing spend choice.** Management attributed the Q4 DAU decline to cutting growth marketing spend, and the Street largely accepted this framing. But NA DAU has declined for four consecutive quarters (100M to 94M), losing 6% of its highest-value users in one year. Cutting marketing spend is a consequence of poor unit economics on marginal user acquisition, not a strategic choice. If NA DAU continues declining at 4-6% annually, SNAP needs 10%+ NA ARPU growth just to keep NA revenue flat -- a tall order in a competitive digital ad market. The Street models assume DAU stabilizes; the data does not yet support that assumption.

**Second non-consensus observation:** The 2026 convertible note maturity ($1.265B) will consume a significant portion of the $2.9B cash balance within the next year, leaving less room for buybacks precisely when dilution management matters most.
