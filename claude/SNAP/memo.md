# SNAP Inc. -- Investment Decision Memo

## Recommendation

- **Decision:** SELL / SHORT
- **Conviction:** Medium
- **Time Horizon:** 6-18 months
- **Classification:** Core Short (small position)

---

## Key Drivers (The 3 Things That Matter)

1. **North America DAU is in structural decline.** NA lost 6% of users in FY2025 (100M to 94M). This is the highest-ARPU region ($10.88 vs. $1.09 ROW). Management blamed marketing spend cuts; the real signal is that marginal NA user acquisition has negative ROI. NA revenue requires 10%+ ARPU growth annually just to stay flat -- a high bar in a competitive ad market.

2. **SBC is a permanent value transfer from shareholders to employees.** ~$1B/year in SBC against $437M in FCF. Shareholders pay $0.59/share in dilution for every $0.25/share of cash flow. Buybacks are explicitly to "offset a portion of dilution" -- not to return capital. Net share count keeps rising despite $1.5B+ in cumulative repurchases. This is a melting ice cube.

3. **Governance eliminates all error-correction mechanisms.** Spiegel controls 88.5% of votes. No say-on-pay. No activist path. No proxy fight possible. $3B spent on AR over 11 years with zero revenue. Spiegel is a consistent programmatic seller of stock. There is no mechanism to force capital discipline.

---

## Thesis in 5 Bullets

- SNAP has never generated positive GAAP earnings on an annual basis despite $5.9B in revenue. Normalized earnings power is approximately zero after properly accounting for SBC. The "adjusted" profitability narrative is misleading.
- NA DAU decline is structural, not a function of marketing spend optimization. Losing 6% of your highest-value users in one year while growing low-ARPU international users is a deteriorating revenue mix.
- The 32% monthly churn rate exposes that Snapchat has entertainment-grade network effects, not utility-grade. This makes it vulnerable to the next attention platform in a way that iMessage, WhatsApp, and Instagram DMs are not.
- The $1.265B convertible note due 2026 will consume a large portion of the $2.9B cash balance, constraining buyback capacity precisely when dilution management matters most.
- Absolute founder control with no shareholder recourse means the discount should be permanent, not transient.

## The Setup

SNAP trades at $5.25, or ~$9.8B EV (including ~$600M net debt). At 1.65x revenue, the market is pricing this as a structurally challenged business -- but the consensus $9 price target implies the market still expects stabilization and margin expansion. The 4 buy / 19 hold / 2 sell analyst distribution says the Street is neutral-to-hopeful, not pessimistic. There is room for negative re-rating.

The competitive environment is the most brutal in technology. Meta generates more ad revenue in a single quarter (~$50B) than Snap generates in a year. Snap is a 1-2% share player that gets cut first when ad budgets tighten. Gross margins of 55% vs. Meta's ~80% reflect inferior ad pricing power and a structural cost disadvantage (locked into a Google Cloud commitment).

The Specs Inc. spinout of the AR business is either belated capital discipline or financial engineering to obscure ongoing spending. Either way, $3B sunk over 11 years with zero commercial revenue is a damning signal about capital allocation under Spiegel's unchecked control.

**Valuation implied beliefs at $5.25:** The market needs revenue growth re-accelerating to 15%+, SBC declining below 12% of revenue, and NA DAU stabilizing. All three simultaneously is a low-probability outcome.

## Forward Path and Catalysts (Negative)

- **Q1 2026 earnings (April):** If NA DAU prints below 93M, the "temporary marketing cut" narrative breaks permanently.
- **2026 convertible maturity:** $1.265B cash outflow reduces financial flexibility and buyback capacity.
- **Ad market cyclicality:** Any macro softness hits Snap disproportionately as the marginal ad supplier.
- **Specs Inc. funding round:** If outside investors pass or demand punitive terms, it signals the AR thesis is dead and Snap may re-absorb costs.

---

## Risks to the Short

- **TikTok ban:** A U.S. TikTok ban would be a one-time windfall for Snap's user base and ad revenue. This is exogenous and unhedgeable. Size the position accordingly.
- **Snapchat+ acceleration:** If subscription revenue scales faster than expected (40M+ subs), it changes the revenue quality story.
- **Takeout:** At $9B market cap, Snap is theoretically acquirable, but Spiegel's voting control means he would have to agree, and his track record suggests he values control over shareholder returns.
- **Ad market boom:** A strong ad spending cycle lifts all boats, including Snap.

**Kill switches:** Exit the short if NA DAU stabilizes above 95M for two consecutive quarters, or if SBC drops below $700M annually, or if a credible takeout bid emerges.

## Positioning and Portfolio Logic

- **Position size:** 1-1.5% of fund NAV. Small because the TikTok ban risk creates left-tail exposure, and the stock has already declined 90% from highs -- reflexive short squeezes are possible.
- **Entry:** Current levels (~$5.25) or on any bounce toward $6.50-7 as the consensus $9 target pulls in momentum buyers.
- **Hedge:** Pair with a long position in META as a digital ad market hedge. If ad spending booms, META gains offset SNAP short losses. The spread isolates Snap-specific deterioration.
- **Watch post-entry:** NA DAU (quarterly), SBC/FCF ratio (quarterly), Snapchat+ subscriber count, convertible note refinancing actions, insider selling pace.

**Why this deserves capital now:** The Street is still at Hold with a $9 target, implying ~70% upside. The specialist analysis unanimously points to structural deterioration in the core business masked by adjusted metrics. NA DAU decline, SBC treadmill, and governance risk are each independently sufficient to justify a negative view; together they make this a high-quality short. The variant perception is specific: the market treats the NA DAU decline as temporary and SBC improvement as real progress, when the data says otherwise on both counts.
