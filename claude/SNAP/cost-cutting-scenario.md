# SNAP Cost-Cutting Scenario: 40% Headcount Reduction

**Punchline: A 40% RIF gets SNAP to ~$400M GAAP net income, ~$900M FCF, and ~$1.1B adjusted EBITDA. At 15x normalized FCF, the stock is worth $7-8. At 10x, it is worth $5. The current price already reflects a moderately optimistic outcome -- the upside requires Spiegel to act like Zuckerberg in 2023, which his voting control makes unlikely without external pressure.**

## The 40% Cut: Where Do You Swing The Axe?

Target: reduce headcount from ~5,250 to ~3,150 (cut ~2,100 people).

| Function | Current HC (est.) | Cut % | Remaining | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R&D / Engineering | ~2,900 | 45% | ~1,600 | Deepest cut. AR/Spectacles and speculative projects are the obvious targets. Core app maintenance needs fewer people than SNAP employs. |
| Sales & Marketing | ~1,050 | 35% | ~680 | Trim middle management and non-core markets. Protect direct ad sales relationships. |
| G&A | ~800 | 40% | ~480 | Corporate overhead scales down. Legal, HR, finance all shrink with fewer employees. |
| Cost of Rev (people) | ~500 | 25% | ~375 | Trust & safety, content moderation have a floor. Infrastructure is mostly cloud spend, not headcount. |
| **Total** | **~5,250** | **40%** | **~3,150** | |

## Normalized Earnings After SBC

The honest earnings number must subtract SBC. Post-cut:
- GAAP net income: ~$861M
- But SBC of $620M is a real cost to shareholders via dilution
- Cash earnings (net income minus SBC dilution effect): ~$400M
- This is the number that matters for valuation

SNAP with declining NA DAUs (100M to 94M in 2025) deserves a discount. 12-15x FCF is appropriate for a social media company with shrinking core market.

**Fair value range: $5-8/share post-restructuring.** At $5.25 today, the market is pricing in modest improvement but not a radical cost restructuring.

## Risks and Feasibility

**Revenue destruction risk.** Cutting 45% of R&D means killing Spectacles/AR, likely reducing product velocity. If revenue declines 10% as a result, post-cut operating income drops from $1,226M to ~$630M. The margin of safety is real but not enormous.

**Can SNAP function at 3,150 people?** Twitter/X went from ~7,500 to ~1,500 and the product still works (barely). Meta cut 21,000 (25%) in 2023 and the stock tripled. SNAP at 3,150 employees on $5.9B revenue would mean $1.88M revenue/employee -- roughly in line with Meta pre-efficiency. It is plausible.

**The Spiegel problem.** Evan Spiegel holds majority voting control through Class C shares. He has shown no interest in "year of efficiency" discipline. SNAP continues to invest heavily in AR/Spectacles with no clear revenue path. Without activist pressure that can actually force change (impossible given dual-class structure), this scenario is theoretical. This is the single biggest reason the stock is cheap.

**Convertible maturity.** The $1.265B 2026 convertible coming due will consume significant cash. Post-restructuring FCF would cover this comfortably, but current FCF ($437M) does not, meaning SNAP likely refinances at higher rates or uses cash reserves.

**Meta 2023 as template.** Meta's "year of efficiency" cut 25% of staff and operating expenses fell 14% while revenue grew 16%. The result: operating margins went from 25% to 41%. SNAP's situation is comparable in kind but the cuts here are deeper (40% vs 25%) and SNAP lacks Meta's revenue growth engine.

## Bottom Line

SNAP's cost structure supports a viable business buried under speculative R&D and bloated corporate overhead. A 40% RIF transforms SNAP from a GAAP money-loser into a $400M+ cash earner. But the stock at $5.25 already prices in gradual improvement. The restructuring scenario yields $5-8/share -- modest upside at best, and only if Spiegel cooperates, which his governance structure gives him no reason to do. This is a governance discount masquerading as a valuation opportunity.
