## Earnings Quality: Recurring vs. Volatile

FY2025 net earnings of $4.8B include $3.15B in net investment gains -- two-thirds of pre-tax income. The recurring base is materially lower.

**Recurring components (FY2025):** Underwriting profit $1,817M + interest/dividends $2,570M + associate income $816M (Eurobank $474M, Poseidon $287M) + non-insurance $397M = ~$5,600M pre-tax. Subtract run-off drag ($214M) and corporate/interest (~$600M est.) = ~$4,800M pre-tax, ~$4,000M after tax.

**Volatile component:** $3,151M net gains driven by $1,491M common stock gains, $841M Fairfax TRS gains, $385M bond gains, offset by $440M FX losses. This swung from ~$1.1B (2024) to $3.15B (2025).

**Reserve development:** $752M favorable (19th consecutive year) partially offset by $299M adverse development in run-off (latent hazard claims). Net favorable = $453M.

## Red Flags

- **Latent hazard reserves escalating:** $221M (2024) to $299M (2025), +35%. Open-ended liability.
- **Debt/capital rising:** 26.2%, directionally negative.
- **Complexity:** Labyrinth of subsidiaries and associates. $816M associate income requires trusting equity-method accounting.
- No SBC concern. No aggressive revenue recognition. Cash conversion strong (insurance collects premiums before paying claims).

---

## Key Financial Drivers

**1. Interest & dividend income.** The $2.57B figure (up from ~$1.7B in 2023) is THE transformation story. $50B portfolio repositioned into higher rates, locked short-term. Covers ~65% of the market cap's earnings yield alone. Rate sensitivity is the key risk.

**2. Combined ratio sustainability.** Three years sub-94% is historically unusual for Fairfax. Structural improvement vs. hard market tailwind is the open question. Reversion to 96-97% cuts underwriting profit by $500-800M.

**3. Investment gains volatility vs. market perception.** The stock likely trades on reported EPS more than normalized, creating opportunities when gains disappoint.

---

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**The run-off latent hazard exposure is accelerating and under-discussed.** Adverse development went from $221M to $299M (+35% YoY), driven by increasing litigation activity. Sell-side models typically treat run-off as static. If US tort trends continue, this could reach $400-500M annually, offsetting 25-30% of favorable reserve development in the active book. The net development story ($752M favorable minus $299M adverse = $453M net) is less attractive than the headline $752M.

**The 1.35x P/B is a moderate premium.** Pre-2022, FFH traded at 0.8-1.2x book for years. The re-rating reflects the interest income transformation. A 200bps decline in reinvestment rates on the $50B book would reduce interest income by ~$1B annually. The question is whether 1.35x is pricing in earnings normalization or not.
