# FFH.TO -- Fairfax Financial Holdings: Investment Decision

*Note: Fairfax reports in USD. Stock trades in CAD on TSX. USD/CAD = 1.37. All financial figures are USD unless marked C$. P/B and P/E calculated by converting USD to CAD.*

## Recommendation

- **Decision:** BUY
- **Conviction:** Medium
- **Time Horizon:** 12-24 months
- **Position Size:** 2-3% initial

At C$2,326 (1.35x book), FFH is a good-to-great business at a reasonable price with an identifiable margin of safety. Normalized recurring EPS of US$155-180 (C$212-247) puts the stock at 9.4-11.0x mid-cycle earnings — cheap for a franchise with $39B of float at negative cost, 19 years of favorable reserve development, zero SBC dilution, and a 21% BVPS CAGR since 2020. The consensus is Buy with C$2,600-2,950 targets, but the consensus reasoning (extrapolating peak earnings) is wrong — our thesis rests on the stock being reasonably priced even after normalizing for cycle headwinds.

---

## Key Drivers (The 3 Things That Matter)

**1. Interest income trajectory is THE variable.** The $2.57B in interest/dividend income (FY2025) on $50B of short-duration fixed income at ~5.1% yield is the single biggest transformation in FFH's economics. But the US 2-year Treasury sits at 3.44% today — 160bps below the portfolio yield. As bonds mature and roll, interest income declines. A 100bps decline in reinvestment rates on the $50B book = ~$500M annual pre-tax earnings loss. A 200bps decline = ~$1B. This is real — but the stock at 1.35x book already embeds meaningful normalization.

**2. Combined ratio sustainability through a softening cycle.** FFH has run sub-94% for three straight years against a US industry average of ~97%. The industry is entering decisive softening: commercial property rates down 6-9%, reinsurance cat rates down 14.7% at Jan 1 2026 renewals, global reinsurance capital at a record $660B. AM Best forecasts industry CR of 96.9% in 2026. FFH's discipline is real but untested through a prolonged soft market under the emerging leadership team.

**3. Succession.** Prem Watsa is 75. Ben Watsa (46) inherits voting control via dual-class shares but has no insurance operating experience. The entire model — float-funded leveraged investment returns — is a capital allocation strategy, not an operating business model. The $6.1B hedging disaster (2010-2016) proves even Prem's judgment is fallible; the dual-class structure ensures zero accountability for the next mistake. However, the operational management team (Peter Clarke, Brian Young) provides insurance continuity.

## Thesis in 5 Bullets

1. FFH's normalized recurring earnings power is US$155-180/share (C$212-247). At C$2,326, the stock trades at 9.4-11.0x these earnings — undemanding for a franchise with this quality of float, underwriting discipline, and capital allocation track record.

2. The interest income headwind is real but partially priced. At 1.35x book (vs. historical 0.8-1.2x pre-2022), the market is paying a moderate premium that could absorb significant earnings normalization before the stock becomes expensive. Even at US$140 normalized EPS (bear-ish mid-cycle), the stock trades at ~12x in CAD terms.

3. Three of four recent ROE drivers are cyclical: hard market pricing (softening), elevated yields (declining), and equity gains (volatile). The only structural improvement since 2020 is the elimination of the hedging program. But the question is whether 1.35x book requires permanence — and it does not. A 12-14% normalized ROE justifies 1.2-1.5x book.

4. The zero-SBC capital return model is genuinely differentiated. 19% share count reduction since 2020 with no dilution offset. Every buyback dollar is real. This compounding advantage is under-appreciated relative to peers where 30-50% of buybacks offset equity comp.

5. The Kennedy Wilson take-private ($1.65B commitment, Feb 2026) warrants monitoring but is not alarming at <4% of portfolio. It does echo the diversification-over-focus pattern that preceded the lost decade.

## The Setup

FFH's transformation from 2020 to 2025 is real and impressive: BVPS from ~$490 to $1,260 (21% CAGR), share count down 19%, hedging program eliminated, insurance operations delivering sub-94% combined ratios, and a $50B fixed income portfolio repositioned into higher yields. Watsa's 2019-2021 decision to stay short-duration while peers reached for yield was worth billions.

The setup today is different from 2021 (when FFH traded at 0.7x book with rates about to rise), but it is not the opposite extreme. At 1.35x book, the stock sits comfortably within normal valuation range for a well-run P&C insurer earning 15%+ ROE.

The insurance cycle is turning. Jan 1 2026 reinsurance renewals were the most buyer-friendly since 2014. Commercial property rates are falling 6-9%. But even disciplined insurers like Fairfax can earn acceptable returns in moderately soft markets. The key question is whether underwriting margins compress moderately (CR to 95-96%) or severely (97%+).

Fairfax's reserve position deserves credit: 19 consecutive years of favorable development ($752M in 2025). The run-off book's adverse development is accelerating — $221M (2024) to $299M (2025), +35% YoY — driven by latent hazard claims. Net favorable development ($453M) is less impressive than the headline $752M but still meaningfully positive.

## Forward Path & Catalysts

**Near-term (0-6 months):** Q1 2026 will be the first quarter where interest income growth decelerates or flattens — watch for narrative shift. Kennedy Wilson deal closes, consuming ~$1.65B of capital. Book value likely grows 3-5% in H1 2026 on retained earnings alone.

**Medium-term (6-18 months):** Soft market pricing becomes visible in premium growth and combined ratios. Interest income begins declining as bonds roll. If earnings normalize gradually (not abruptly), the stock can grind higher on book value compounding even if the multiple compresses slightly.

**Upside catalysts:** Fed reverses course and hikes rates; mega-cat event re-hardens the market; Watsa makes another contrarian investment call; credible succession plan announced. Any could push stock to C$2,800-3,200.

**Downside triggers:** Interest income declining faster than expected (reinvestment rates below 3.5%); CR above 97% for two consecutive quarters; capital allocation error in soft market; Watsa health event with no succession resolution. Could compress to C$1,800-2,000.

## Risks & Kill Switches

**What would increase conviction (add to position):**
- Price below C$2,000 (1.16x book) — aggressive add to 4-5%
- Evidence that reinvestment rates stabilize above 4.5%
- Credible succession plan with operational insurance leadership
- A hard market re-hardening catalyst (mega-cat, casualty reserve crisis)

**What would trigger an exit:**
- Interest income declining to below $1.8B (structural, not transient)
- Combined ratio above 97% for two consecutive quarters suggesting discipline breakdown
- Capital allocation error (large, expensive acquisition in a soft market)
- Watsa health event combined with Ben Watsa assuming active operational control without insurance management support

**Permanent capital impairment scenarios:**
- Mega-catastrophe ($10B+ Fairfax-specific loss) — low probability, high impact
- Casualty reserve crisis in active book (industry $15.8B adverse development in 2024 is a warning)
- Repeat of conviction-driven macro bet under new leadership with no governance check

## Positioning & Portfolio Logic

**2-3% initial position.** At 1.35x book and ~12x normalized CAD earnings, the risk/reward tilts favorably. The downside in a bear case (C$2,088, -10%) is manageable, the base case offers modest upside plus book value compounding (15%+ ROE on $1,260 BVPS = ~$190/year in book value growth = 11% annual compounding before any multiple expansion), and the bull case offers meaningful upside.

**Why not 5%?** Succession risk is real and unresolvable by outside analysis. The dual-class structure means if the next generation makes a mistake, you have zero recourse. Size accordingly.

**What we watch post-purchase:**
- Quarterly interest/dividend income trajectory (the single most important number)
- Combined ratio trend by subsidiary
- Reinsurance renewal pricing (April 1 and July 1 renewals)
- Succession announcements or Watsa health disclosures
- Run-off adverse development trajectory

---

## Bottom Line

FFH is a genuinely improved business run by a capable (if fallible) founder-operator at a reasonable price. At 1.35x book (not the 1.85x that a naive CAD/USD mismatch suggests), the stock prices in meaningful earnings normalization and offers a margin of safety backed by $39B of float at negative cost, a zero-dilution buyback program, and 15%+ ROE compounding book value. The key risks — interest income rolloff, cycle softening, succession — are real but the valuation provides cushion. Buy at 2-3%, add below C$2,000.
