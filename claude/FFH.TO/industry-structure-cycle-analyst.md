# P&C Insurance & Reinsurance: Industry Structure & Cycle Analysis

*February 2026 | Relevance: FFH.TO (Fairfax Financial Holdings)*

---

## Cycle Position: Late Cycle, Soft Market Accelerating

The P&C industry is exiting its longest hard market since post-9/11 (2018-2024) and entering decisive softening. CIAB Q4 2025 shows commercial P&C at its softest since 2017 -- nine lines seeing premium decreases, large account premiums fell 2.1% (first decrease since Q4 2017). Yet trailing profitability remains excellent: 95.0% combined ratio in 2025, AM Best forecasts only 96.9% in 2026.

### Pricing by Line (Q4 2025)

| Line | Rate Trend | Combined Ratio | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commercial Property | -6% to -9% | Sub-95% | Rapid softening |
| Workers' Comp | Decreasing | 88.8% | Deep profit, invites competition |
| D&O | -3.8% (8th straight quarter) | Profitable | Race to bottom |
| Casualty (ex-WC) | +12% | ~110% | Only line still hardening |
| Commercial Auto | +6.6% | 103.5% | 58 consecutive increase quarters |

The defining feature: **property softens rapidly while casualty must keep hardening due to loss costs.** This bifurcation is critical.

---

## Reinsurance: Capital Flood Signals the Turn

January 1, 2026 renewals were the most buyer-friendly since 2014:
- Global reinsurance capital: $660B (up 8% YoY, per Guy Carpenter)
- Property-cat rates fell 14.7% -- sharpest decline since 2014 (Howden)
- ILS alternative capital exceeded $120B; cat bond issuance surpassed $25B in 2025
- Sidecar capital reached ~$17B, up 70% YoY
- 15 new ILS sponsors entered in 2025

Reinsurer ROE remains 14-16% (well above ~9% cost of capital). KBW notes executives describe 2026 as the "fifth-best year ever." Classic late-cycle paradox: returns are excellent but the conditions creating them are rapidly eroding.

---

## The Casualty Reserve Time Bomb

The most underappreciated industry risk:
- Adverse prior-year development in casualty lines: **$15.8B in 2024, the highest on record**
- Total adverse PYD doubled YoY to $7.8B
- Social inflation added $231-281B in excess liability losses over the past decade (CAS/Triple-I)
- Hard market accident years (2020-2023) are developing adversely -- even elevated pricing was insufficient

The headline 95% combined ratio masks casualty lines running above 110%. Any company with significant casualty exposure faces reserve risk regardless of current pricing.

## Mid-Cycle Benchmarks vs. Current

| Metric | Mid-Cycle | Current (2025) |
|---|---|---|
| Combined ratio | 99-101% | 95.0% |
| Portfolio yield | 3.0-3.5% | 4.0-4.2% |
| Reinsurer ROE | 8-10% | 14-16% |
| Policyholder surplus growth | 3-5% | 6.8% |

**Every metric is substantially above mid-cycle.** This is peak-to-late-cycle profitability driven by post-hard-market pricing plus elevated investment yields.

---

## The One Industry Factor That Matters

**Investment income trajectory.**

The industry can sustain 100-103% combined ratios and still deliver acceptable ROEs when earning 4%+ on float. This means the underwriting cycle's self-correcting mechanism has a much longer fuse than history suggests. If rates stay elevated, the soft market persists longer and goes deeper before forcing discipline.

For Fairfax specifically: $50B in short-duration fixed income earning ~5%, 71% government bonds. This generates ~$2.5B annually in interest/dividend income -- a structural advantage that allows profitability through a soft market that would pressure lower-yielding competitors.

**Monitor:** 2-year Treasury yield (proxy for short-duration reinvestment rates) and the spread between new money yields and maturing portfolio yields. Currently narrowing but still positive.

---

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**Consensus:** "Orderly softening" -- still healthy, normalizing from peak. Most commentary frames 2026 as stable, above-average returns.

**What's actually happening:** The speed and depth of the coming soft market is underestimated because investment income masks the underwriting turn. The $1.2 trillion in U.S. policyholder surplus and $660B in reinsurance capital are the largest capital bases in history. Capital destruction -- the only reliable soft-market ending mechanism -- requires either a mega-catastrophe ($200B+), a severe casualty reserve crisis, or prolonged sub-cost-of-capital returns. None are imminent.

This means:
1. The soft market will likely be longer and deeper than 2014-2017 because the capital base is larger and investment income extends the runway for irrational pricing
2. Casualty reserve development is the most likely re-hardening catalyst, but works slowly (3-5 year development tail)
3. The combined ratio will likely breach 100% by 2027-2028 for the industry; 2025's 95% will look like the peak within 18 months

**This is bearish for the industry broadly but selectively positive for well-capitalized, investment-savvy participants.** Companies that chase premium growth will be casualties. Companies positioned in short-duration, high-yielding fixed income (like Fairfax) have structural insulation.

The non-consensus call: **we are not in "orderly softening" -- we are in the early innings of a 4-5 year soft market.**
