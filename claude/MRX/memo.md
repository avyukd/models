## 2. Key Drivers (The 3 Things That Matter)

1. **Net interest income on $15B+ client balances.** NII was $227M in 2024, up from $122M in 2023. This is rate-sensitive float income at near-zero marginal cost. Every 100bps of rate cuts compresses this by an estimated $60-100M pretax. This is the single largest swing variable in near-term earnings and the most likely source of consensus disappointment.

2. **FCM industry consolidation.** Registered FCMs have declined from 171 to ~65 since 2005. Large banks are structurally exiting clearing. Marex at ~2% market share is a mechanical beneficiary. The StoneX/R.J. O'Brien deal signals mid-tier consolidation is accelerating. This is the durable, structural thesis -- but it is slow-moving and already partially understood by the market.

3. **NINGI Research allegations and securities fraud lawsuit.** Filed August 2025, alleging concealed losses, fictitious intercompany transactions, and off-balance-sheet entities. Lead plaintiff deadline passed January 2026. Marex denies all allegations. For a business with 25x asset/equity leverage, accounting credibility is existential. The 20-F (expected post-March 3 results) is the clearing event -- a clean audit reduces this risk materially; any qualification is catastrophic.

## 3. Thesis Summary

- Marex is a competent operator in a structurally favorable industry (consolidating FCM market, rising barriers to entry).
- 2024 earnings ($3.20 normalized EPS) are inflated by peak-cycle NII and exceptional market-making conditions. Mid-cycle earnings power is $2.00-2.30/share, implying the stock trades at 19-22x normalized -- not cheap.
- The NINGI short report creates binary risk that cannot be underwritten until the next audited filing clears.
- Management alignment is strong (CEO owns 3.6%, made open-market purchases in Oct 2025), and M&A discipline has been genuinely good (buying from distressed sellers at 1-1.7x revenue).
- The market prices this at 12x reported earnings, which looks cheap -- but only if you assume current earnings are sustainable and the accounting is clean. We are not confident in either assumption.

## 5. The Setup

Marex is the second-largest non-bank FCM globally, benefiting from a two-decade structural consolidation of the clearing industry. Post-GFC capital rules made commodity clearing uneconomic for large banks, creating a supply vacuum that mid-tier specialists like Marex fill by default. The business is genuinely capital-light ($12M capex on $1.6B net revenue), generates strong operating leverage, and has been well-managed through a series of disciplined acquisitions.

The problem is timing. Marex went public in April 2024 at the intersection of peak interest rates, elevated commodity volatility, and a transformative acquisition cycle. Every tailwind was blowing simultaneously. The stock has appreciated 72% from its $25 IPO price, and consensus estimates extrapolate conditions that are at or above historical peaks on multiple dimensions.

## 7. Forward Path and Catalysts

**March 3, 2026 -- FY2025 results and 20-F filing.** This is the single most important near-term event. It will simultaneously reveal (a) whether 2025 earnings confirmed or disappointed vs. the elevated 2024 base, (b) whether the auditors issued a clean opinion addressing NINGI's allegations, and (c) updated NII trajectory under any rate changes.

**Fed rate path.** Markets currently price 1-2 cuts in 2026. Each cut directly compresses Marex's highest-margin revenue line.

**NINGI lawsuit resolution.** Active litigation with lead plaintiff appointed. Timeline uncertain but discovery could surface material information in 2026.

**M&A pipeline.** Webb Traders closing in 2026. Future deals will test whether discipline holds at scale.

---

## 8. Risks and Kill Switches

- **Accounting irregularity confirmed:** Any adverse audit finding or SEC investigation is immediately catastrophic given 25x leverage. This is a permanent capital loss scenario. Kill switch: any audit qualification, SEC subpoena, or restatement.
- **Rate cuts exceeding expectations:** 200bps of cuts could compress NII by $60-100M, taking 20-30% off pretax profit with no offset.
- **Market making loss event:** Principal risk-taking in commodities can produce outsized losses in tail events. One bad quarter in metals could wipe a full year of market making profits.
- **Dilution acceleration:** 9% share count increase in 2024. If SBC ramps post-IPO while earnings stagnate, per-share economics deteriorate.

---

## 9. Positioning and Portfolio Logic

**We pass.** The reason is straightforward: we cannot responsibly size a position when the primary risk (accounting integrity on a 25x levered balance sheet) is binary and unresolvable until the 20-F is filed. If the accounting is clean, the stock is moderately overvalued on mid-cycle earnings but has a genuine structural growth story that could justify a premium over time. If the accounting is not clean, the equity is worth substantially less.

This is not a "good company at a fair price" situation. It is a "potentially good company at a full price with an unresolved existential question." The expected value calculation requires assigning a probability to the NINGI allegations being substantive, and we have no informational edge to do that.

**What moves this out of the Too Hard pile:**
1. Clean 20-F audit with no qualifications (March 2026)
2. One or two quarters of results showing NII trajectory post-rate-cuts, allowing us to anchor mid-cycle earnings
3. Lawsuit dismissal or settlement at immaterial amounts

After these conditions are met, Marex becomes an interesting long candidate in the $30-35 range (15-17x mid-cycle earnings), where the structural FCM consolidation story is priced fairly and the growth optionality from M&A comes for free.

**If forced to trade today:** Small short with tight stop above $48. The risk/reward favors the downside into March 3 earnings given elevated expectations, rate headwinds, and unresolved litigation. But the structural quality of the business makes this uncomfortable -- you are shorting into a genuine consolidation story with aligned management. Not a high-conviction position either way.
