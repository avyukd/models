# Commodities Brokerage & Clearing Industry Analysis

**Date:** 2026-02-23 | **MRX Price:** $39.95 | **Market Cap:** $2.86B | **P/E:** 11.5x | **Fwd P/E:** 9.6x

---

## The One Industry Factor That Matters

**Interest rate levels on client segregated funds.** FCMs hold $300B+ in customer margin funds (per CFTC data) that earn interest income at essentially zero marginal cost. Marex's net interest income surged 87% YoY in 2024 to $227M -- this is not a commission business that happened to earn some float; float income now represents roughly 14% of net revenue versus negligible amounts pre-2022. Every 100bps decline in rates directly compresses the highest-margin revenue line across the entire FCM industry. This is the single largest swing factor in near-term industry profitability, and it is not adequately separated from "operating performance" in most analyst coverage.

**How to monitor:** Fed funds rate trajectory. Each 25bps cut reduces Marex-scale interest income by an estimated $15-25M annually. Watch the CFTC's monthly FCM financial data reports for industry-wide customer funds levels.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

Consensus treats this as a "volume-driven business with rate tailwinds." That framing is backwards. **The structural story is the consolidation of the mid-tier FCM layer, and rates are the cyclical overlay masking how much of the recent margin expansion is borrowable versus owned.**

FACT: Registered FCMs declined from 171 in 2005 to approximately 65 in 2024 (CFTC data). Bank-affiliated FCMs now control 92% of customer funds. JUDGMENT: This consolidation is irreversible -- post-crisis capital requirements, compliance costs, and clearing technology investments create barriers that prevent new entrants and squeeze remaining small players. The non-bank independent FCM (Marex, StoneX) is a structural winner of this consolidation because banks periodically exit commodity clearing as non-core (Deutsche Bank, Barclays, BNP have all retrenched from physical commodities at various points).

FACT: StoneX is acquiring R.J. O'Brien (~$766M revenue, ~$170M EBITDA in 2024). JUDGMENT: This signals we are in an acceleration phase of mid-tier consolidation. The remaining independent FCMs are racing to build scale before compliance and technology costs make sub-scale operations uneconomic. This is structurally bullish for survivors but creates acquisition integration risk.

The **bearish non-consensus view**: rate normalization will reveal that 2023-2025 industry ROEs (Marex ~27% adjusted, StoneX 15-19%) are substantially above mid-cycle. Stripping out interest income from float, underlying clearing and execution ROEs are likely 12-16% for well-run operators -- solid but not exceptional.

## Industry Profitability Drivers

1. **Volume and volatility** in commodity derivatives -- these drive commission and clearing fees. Global ETD commodity volumes declined 4.1% in 2024 (per FIA/WFE data), though energy and precious metals rose 14.5% and 23.5% respectively. Volatility matters more than directional volume.

2. **Interest income on segregated client funds** -- as described above, a cyclical windfall at current rate levels.

3. **Market making spread capture** -- requires proprietary risk management capability. Marex's market making (metals-heavy) benefits from episodic dislocations but is inherently volatile; exceptional 2024 copper/aluminum conditions are not repeatable assumptions.

4. **Operating leverage on a fixed-cost compliance and technology base** -- the regulatory infrastructure costs roughly the same whether processing 100K or 500K trades per day, creating meaningful scale advantages.

## Supply and Demand Dynamics

**Demand side:** Secular growth in commodity hedging demand driven by energy transition, supply chain complexity, and financial product proliferation. Commodity derivatives addressable market grows at GDP+ rates over full cycles. ASSUMPTION: This continues at 3-6% annually absent a global recession.

**Supply side:** Highly inelastic in the short term -- building a licensed, capitalized, exchange-connected clearing operation takes 3-5 years minimum. Regulatory barriers (CFTC/FCA registration, exchange membership, capital adequacy) are genuine and rising. New entrants are extremely rare. The supply response to high margins comes via existing player expansion and M&A, not greenfield entry.

## Cyclicality Assessment: MODERATE

The industry has two distinct cyclical overlays:
- **Volume/volatility cycle:** Tightly correlated with commodity price volatility and macro uncertainty. Margins compress during low-vol regimes (2017-2019 was weak; 2020-2022 was exceptional).
- **Interest rate cycle:** A newer but now dominant contributor to profitability swings.

Historical margin range: Industry net margins (for listed comparables) swing between 5-8% at troughs and 12-18% at peaks. Marex's 2024 adjusted PBT margin of ~22% is at or above historical peak for the industry.

## Mid-Cycle Definition

FACT-based benchmarks for a "normal" industry environment:
- Fed funds rate at 2.5-3.5% (versus ~4.25% currently)
- Commodity volatility at long-term average levels (VIX commodity equivalent)
- Industry ROE of 13-17% for well-capitalized operators
- Net interest income contributing 8-10% of net revenue (versus ~14% currently)
- PBT margins of 14-18% for scaled players

JUDGMENT: Current conditions are above mid-cycle on interest income and roughly at mid-cycle on volume/volatility. Blended, I'd characterize the industry as **late mid-cycle / early late-cycle**.

If I knew nothing about any company in this industry, industry economics alone would make me **comfortable but not excited** about capital deployment. The structural consolidation story is genuine and favorable. But current profitability is flattered by interest income that will partially unwind, and the market's forward P/E of 9.6x may already reflect this. The variant bet is on whether consolidation-driven market share gains can outrun the rate headwind -- that is a firm-level execution question I cannot answer from industry economics alone.
