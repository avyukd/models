## Key Drivers (The 1-3 Things That Actually Matter)

1. **AI/HPC Revenue Sustainability** — HPC is now 57% of revenue, up from 30% in 2020. NVIDIA has overtaken Apple as largest customer. The entire thesis hinges on whether AI capex is secular or cyclical. $600B+ hyperscaler capex in 2026 (+36% YoY) is the proof point to watch.

2. **Leading-Edge Yield Gap vs. Competition** — TSMC's 90% yields at 3nm vs. Samsung's 50% and Intel's ~55% is the moat. This gap has persisted for a decade across every node transition. Samsung Foundry lost $725M in Q3 2024 and is shutting production lines. Intel Foundry lost $13B in 2024. Competitors are collapsing, not catching up.

3. **Taiwan Risk Repricing** — ~80% of advanced capacity sits in Taiwan. Arizona ramp (30% of 2nm+ capacity by ~2030) changes the risk profile, but that's 3-5 years away. The question is whether markets are pricing Taiwan as a linear discount or a binary tail risk.

---

## Thesis (Load-Bearing Bullets Only)

- TSMC is the only company in the world capable of manufacturing leading-edge semiconductors at scale. Samsung and Intel have spent tens of billions and failed to close the yield gap. This is structural, not fixable.

- The market is pricing TSMC as if competitors exist. They don't. At 19-20x forward earnings, the implied growth rate is achievable even under conservative AI demand assumptions. If AI capex proves durable, the stock re-rates higher.

- Geographic diversification is succeeding faster than expected. Arizona Fab 1 achieved yield parity with Taiwan in early 2025. The "permanent margin dilution" narrative from overseas fabs may be overstated.

- The stock trades at a 2-4 P/E turn "Taiwan discount" to U.S. tech peers. If Arizona execution continues, that discount narrows.

## The Setup

**What the market sees:** Dominant foundry trading at premium multiples (26-28x trailing, 19-20x forward), exposed to Taiwan tail risk, with peak margins and massive capex cycle.

**What the market underweights:**

1. This is a legal monopoly. No functional competitor exists at leading edge. Samsung and Intel have the capital and engineers but cannot manufacture competitive chips.

2. The industry has bifurcated. Mature nodes (28nm+) are in price war with Chinese oversupply. Leading edge (sub-7nm) is capacity-constrained with 12-18 month equipment lead times. TSMC's revenue mix has shifted to 57% HPC — they are increasingly insulated from traditional foundry cyclicality.

3. Arizona success de-risks the story. If 30% of advanced capacity sits outside Taiwan by end of decade, the "Taiwan discount" should narrow by 2-3 P/E turns.

**Hedging consideration:** If position exceeds 3%, consider tail hedge via puts or pairing with long volatility in semiconductors. The risk is binary — linear hedging won't help if Taiwan scenario materializes.

## Why Not Full Conviction?

This is **not** "too hard," but conviction is capped by:

1. **Geopolitical tail risk is genuinely binary.** If Taiwan scenario materializes, stock is worth close to zero. Arizona insurance pays off only in 3-5 years. Current price does not reflect this as a binary risk — it's priced as a discount factor.

2. **Margins are above mid-cycle.** 57-59% gross margin vs. 50-53% mid-cycle suggests 400-600 bps compression is realistic. Normalized EPS of $10-11 at 22x = $220-242. We're within that range.

3. **AI cyclicality unknown.** Hyperscalers have historically overbuilt then retrenched. $600B 2026 capex is unprecedented. If enterprise AI deployment disappoints, the demand cliff would hit TSMC hard.

**Bottom line:** Excellent business, reasonable price, real tail risk. This is a core holding, not a high-conviction concentrated bet. Buy at 2-3% position size and add on pullbacks that don't change fundamentals.

---

## Final Word

TSMC is the best business in semiconductors and one of the most important companies in the global economy. The market knows this. The question is whether the Taiwan discount is fair compensation for tail risk.

I believe the market is pricing Taiwan risk as a linear discount when it should be viewed as a bimodal distribution. In the base case (90%+ probability), TSMC benefits from decoupling, competitors collapse further, and Arizona success narrows the geographic risk premium. In the tail case (5-10%), it's an existential loss.

The variant perception that matters: **Overseas fab margin dilution is overstated, and competitor collapse is permanent.** If Arizona margins approach Taiwan levels and Samsung/Intel continue to bleed share, the "Taiwan discount" compresses and the stock re-rates to U.S. tech peer multiples.

**Decision: BUY at 2-3% position size. Add on weakness if fundamentals hold. Exit immediately on any Taiwan Strait escalation beyond exercises.**
