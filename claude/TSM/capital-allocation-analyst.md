# TSMC (TSM) - Capital Allocation & Management Analysis

---

## The One Thing That Determines Value Creation

**Capex discipline during demand cycles.** TSMC's value creation hinges on whether management can resist the temptation to over-build capacity during AI euphoria. The 2021-2022 capex spike (49-53% of revenue) coincided with a semiconductor super-cycle that subsequently corrected. Management now guides $40-42B capex for 2025 and $52-56B for 2026 - the highest absolute spend in company history. If AI demand disappoints or cycle turns, TSMC will be left with underutilized capacity and compressed returns. The market assumes management will allocate this capital wisely; the record is good but not perfect.

---

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**The Arizona disaster is a leading indicator of geographic diversification costs.** Morris Chang publicly stated Arizona costs are 50% above Taiwan. The project experienced 1-2 year delays, required flying in 1,000+ Taiwanese workers, and sparked union backlash. Yet TSMC just committed $165B to U.S. expansion. Consensus focuses on geopolitical resilience; the second-order question is whether permanently higher-cost geography will structurally compress ROIC. Management is being forced into value-destructive capital allocation by political necessity, not economic logic.

**Minimal SBC is real, not accounting fiction.** While U.S. tech peers routinely dilute 2-5% annually via SBC, TSMC's share count has been flat for 5+ years (~5.186B shares). RSA issuance of 4.2M shares (2024) represents <0.1% dilution. This is genuine owner-orientation that the market may under-appreciate given conditioning from U.S. tech.

| Component | Amount (NT$) | USD Equiv. | % of Total |
|-----------|--------------|------------|------------|
| Cash (Salary + Bonus) | 453.7M | ~$14.7M | 83% |
| Equity (RSAs) | 88.0M | ~$2.9M | 16% |
| Pension/Other | 6.1M | ~$0.2M | 1% |
| **Total** | **547.8M** | **~$17.8M** | 100% |

### What Behavior Does This Encourage?

**Mixed but acceptable.** Cash-heavy compensation (83%) is atypical for a technology company and theoretically encourages risk aversion rather than long-term value creation. However:

1. Directors who also serve as executives receive no additional director compensation (per Articles of Incorporation)
2. Director compensation capped at 0.3% of annual profits - creates profit alignment
3. Equity component (RSAs) is performance-linked to ESG and business targets
4. Taiwanese compensation norms differ from U.S. - cash-heavy is standard

**The better alignment comes from governance structure:** Founder Morris Chang instilled a culture of reinvestment discipline and customer-centricity. C.C. Wei rose through TSMC over 20+ years; alignment is cultural/reputational rather than purely financial.

**Who benefits if stock underperforms?** Executives still collect ~83% of compensation in cash. This is a structural concern, though mitigated by long tenure and reputational stakes.

## Capital Allocation Track Record

### A. Reinvestment (Capex)

| Year | Capex ($B) | Revenue ($B) | Capex/Revenue | FCF ($B) |
|------|------------|--------------|---------------|----------|
| 2018 | ~10.5 | 34.2 | 31% | - |
| 2019 | 14.9 | 34.6 | 43% | - |
| 2020 | 18.2 | 45.5 | 40% | - |
| 2021 | 30.0 | 56.8 | 53% | 17.2 |
| 2022 | 36.3 | 73.7 | 49% | 17.2 |
| 2023 | ~30 | 70.6 | 43% | 9.6 |
| 2024 | 29.8 | 90.1 | 33% | 26.6 |
| 2025E | 40-42 | ~120+ | ~33% | - |
| 2026E | 52-56 | - | - | - |

**ROIC:** 28-42% (5-year range) vs. WACC ~10%. Consistently earns excess returns on reinvested capital.

**Assessment:** Capital intensity peaked in 2021-2022 (49-53%) during capacity buildout for 3nm/5nm. Moderated in 2023-2024 as revenue grew faster than capex. Now re-accelerating for 2nm and AI demand.

**Concern:** 2025-2026 capex ($40-56B) represents aggressive ramp. If AI demand disappoints, utilization rates compress, and the company will face fixed-cost absorption pressure. The 2023 FCF decline to $9.6B (from $17.2B in 2022) during capex peaks shows sensitivity.

### B. M&A History

**Minimal.** TSMC is not an acquirer. Notable transactions:

| Date | Deal | Amount | Outcome |
|------|------|--------|---------|
| 2012 | ASML 5% stake + R&D commitment | $1.4B | Strategic - secured EUV access |
| 2011 | Powerchip land acquisition | $96M | Fab expansion |

**Assessment:** M&A is not a capital allocation risk for TSMC. The company grows organically through reinvestment, not acquisition. No empire-building behavior.

**Assessment:** Exemplary. Unlike many U.S. tech companies that trumpet buybacks while net diluting shareholders, TSMC's share count discipline is real.

## Learning vs. Repetition

**Evidence of Learning:**

1. **Post-2018 Capex Discipline:** After 2018 capex cuts (lowered to $10.5B from higher guidance), management demonstrated willingness to flex spending with demand
2. **Arizona Adjustment:** Management acknowledged 50% cost premium publicly; adjusting timelines rather than throwing good money after bad
3. **2023 FCF Compression Response:** Moderated 2023-2024 capex after seeing FCF decline from 2022 levels

**Evidence of Repetition:**

1. **Pro-Cyclical Capex (Partial):** 2021-2022 capex peak coincided with cycle peak. However, this was validated by subsequent demand (AI)
2. **Geographic Expansion Despite Economics:** $165B U.S. commitment despite known cost disadvantages suggests political pressure trumping economic rationality

**Overall:** Management demonstrates learning on most dimensions. The geographic diversification issue is structural/political rather than discipline failure.

## Forward-Looking Capital Allocation Risk

### Most Likely Behavior

1. **Massive Capex Acceleration:** $52-56B in 2026, building 8-10 facilities simultaneously. Largest investment cycle in company history.
2. **Continued Dividend Increases:** Modest annual increases maintaining ~30% payout ratio
3. **U.S./Japan/Germany Expansion:** Political necessity driving inefficient geographic allocation
4. **Minimal M&A:** Organic growth strategy continues

### Where Capital Is Most Likely to Be Misallocated

1. **U.S. Operations:** Structurally higher costs, labor challenges, regulatory friction. ROIC on Arizona/future U.S. fabs will be materially lower than Taiwan operations.
2. **Capex Timing Risk:** If AI demand disappoints in 2026-2027, utilization rates compress on new capacity

### What Would Cause Behavior Change

- Significant demand disappointment / AI winter
- Taiwan Strait crisis forcing accelerated diversification
- Government subsidy reductions making U.S. economics even worse
- New management (low probability given recent succession)
