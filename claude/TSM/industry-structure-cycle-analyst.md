# Semiconductor Foundry Industry Analysis

## The One Industry Factor That Matters

**Leading-edge capacity scarcity is the single variable that drives foundry economics.**

TSMC controls 70%+ of advanced node capacity (7nm and below). EUV equipment lead times of 12-18 months and $10-20B fab costs create structural supply inelasticity. As long as AI/HPC demand outstrips leading-edge supply, pricing power is unchallenged.

**What would cause it to inflect:**
- Hyperscaler capex cuts (currently projected $600B+ in 2026, up 36% YoY)
- NVIDIA order book deterioration (currently ~$500B backlog)
- Samsung/Intel yield breakthroughs at 2nm (not imminent)

**How to monitor:** ASML order book (lowest 2026 backlog coverage in 3 years), TSMC quarterly guidance commentary, hyperscaler capex calls.

---

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**Consensus view:** "Late cycle but AI extends the runway"

**Non-consensus insight:** This cycle is structurally different because the industry has bifurcated into two separate markets with opposite dynamics:

| Segment | Cycle Position | Pricing Power | Margin Trajectory |
|---------|---------------|---------------|-------------------|
| Leading edge (sub-7nm) | Mid-cycle / capacity-constrained | Strong, rising | Peak not yet reached |
| Mature nodes (28nm+) | Oversupply / pricing trough | Collapsing | Below mid-cycle |

**The market may be pricing TSMC as a single-cycle exposure.** In reality, TSMC's revenue mix has shifted dramatically: HPC grew from 30% (Q1 2020) to 57% (Q3 2025) of revenue. Smartphone declined from 49% to secondary. This shift means:

1. TSMC is more insulated from traditional foundry cyclicality than its historical margins suggest
2. The "mature node price war" narrative (SMIC 40% discounts) is noise for TSMC's economics
3. TSMC's margins could expand further as 2nm ramps (projected full utilization by Q+4, faster than any prior node)

**Bearish non-consensus:** If AI capex proves cyclical rather than secular (as enterprise IT historically is), the demand cliff would be unprecedented given current capital commitments. TSMC's $56B 2026 capex is a massive fixed-cost bet on sustained AI demand.

## Cyclicality Assessment: **Moderate-to-High** (segment-dependent)

Historical cycle characteristics:
- **Peak-to-trough margin swings:** TSMC gross margin ranged from 46% (2019 trough) to 60% (2022 peak)
- **Cycle duration:** Typically 3-4 years peak-to-peak
- **Downturn triggers:** Inventory corrections (2019), demand shocks (2008), supply overbuilds (2000)

**Current differentiation:** Leading-edge and mature nodes are decoupled. Mature nodes exhibit classic cyclicality (Chinese oversupply, 40% price cuts). Leading edge is supply-constrained with 12-18 month equipment lead times preventing rapid capacity response.

---

## Current Cycle Position: **Late-Cycle (Leading Edge) / Trough-to-Early Recovery (Mature)**

**Leading-edge assessment:**
- TSMC 3nm/5nm fully booked through 2026
- 2nm demand "unprecedented" per TSMC, exceeding 3nm adoption curve
- Utilization >95% at advanced nodes
- Pricing power intact (3-5% ASP increases announced for 2026)

**BUT: Late-cycle characteristics emerging:**
- Record capex ($56B 2026, up 37% YoY)
- ASML 2026 backlog coverage at 3-year lows
- NVIDIA potentially becoming larger than Apple as TSMC's top customer (concentration risk)
- Customer mix shift entirely dependent on AI capex sustainability

**Mature node assessment:**
- Chinese foundries at 25%+ of global mature capacity
- SMIC/HuaHong discounting 40% on 28nm
- Taiwanese mature-node foundries (UMC, VIS) utilization below 70%
- Price stabilization expected H2 2025, but structural oversupply persists

## Mid-Cycle Reference Point

**Definition of "normal" for semiconductor foundry:**

| Metric | Mid-Cycle Benchmark | Current (2025) |
|--------|---------------------|----------------|
| TSMC Gross Margin | 50-53% | 57-59% |
| TSMC Operating Margin | 38-42% | 47-49% |
| Leading-edge utilization | 85-90% | >95% |
| Mature-node utilization | 75-85% | 65-80% (ex-China) |
| Industry capex/revenue | 20-25% | 30%+ |

**Implication:** Current leading-edge margins are 400-600 bps above mid-cycle. The question is whether this is cyclical overshoot or structural repricing due to AI demand inelasticity.

---

## Supply Response Analysis

**Leading-edge supply elasticity: LOW**
- ASML is sole EUV supplier (12-18 month lead times)
- $10-20B per fab, 3-5 year construction cycles
- EUV machines cost $200M+ each
- TSMC's 2nm monthly capacity: 40K wafers end-2025 -> 80-90K end-2026 (slow ramp)

**Mature-node supply elasticity: HIGH**
- China adding 14% capacity annually
- Equipment readily available (no EUV required)
- Government subsidies distorting rational capacity decisions
- SMIC already cut 28nm prices from $2,500 to $1,500/wafer (40% decline)

**Key insight:** The EUV bottleneck is the moat around leading-edge economics. As long as ASML remains supply-constrained, leading-edge foundries cannot overbuild even if they wanted to.

---

## Structural Risks and Mean Reversion Triggers

**What breaks leading-edge economics:**

1. **AI capex proves cyclical** - Hyperscalers have historically overbuilt then retrenched. $600B 2026 capex is 36% above 2025. History suggests such ramps don't sustain indefinitely.

2. **Inference commoditizes** - Training requires cutting-edge chips; inference may shift to mature nodes or custom ASICs, reducing NVIDIA/AMD demand for leading-edge capacity.

3. **Samsung/Intel catch up** - Both are targeting 2nm mass production in 2025-26. Yield issues have plagued both, but breakthroughs would add supply.

4. **Geopolitical disruption** - Taiwan Strait risk, export controls, tariffs (Trump's threatened 30% on EU equipment).

**What breaks mature-node economics:**
- Already broken. Recovery depends on Chinese capacity rationalization (unlikely given government support) or demand recovery (modest).

## Historical Margin Context

**TSMC Margin History (Full Cycle):**

| Year | Gross Margin | Operating Margin | Cycle Phase |
|------|--------------|------------------|-------------|
| 2019 | 46.0% | 34.8% | Trough |
| 2020 | 53.1% | 42.3% | Recovery |
| 2021 | 51.6% | 40.9% | Mid-cycle |
| 2022 | 59.6% | 49.5% | Peak |
| 2023 | 54.4% | 42.6% | Correction |
| 2024 | 55-56% | 44-46% | Recovery |
| 2025E | 57-59% | 47-49% | Expansion |

**Observation:** 2022 was a clear cycle peak driven by pandemic-induced shortages. 2023 corrected. The current expansion is driven by a different demand driver (AI) with different supply dynamics (EUV constraints). Whether this represents a new equilibrium or another cyclical peak is the key debate.

## Second-Order Implications

1. **Current margins are above mid-cycle** - 400-600 bps of margin compression is the base case if/when AI demand normalizes. This doesn't mean TSMC is a "sell," but it means normalizing current margins for valuation purposes would be aggressive.

2. **Customer concentration is rising** - NVIDIA potentially becoming TSMC's largest customer is a double-edged sword. Great for near-term demand, concerning for negotiating leverage and single-point-of-failure risk.

3. **The $56B capex question** - If AI demand inflects, TSMC will have committed to massive fixed costs. Depreciation pressure could compress margins even if revenue holds.

4. **China mature-node oversupply is permanent** - Government support ensures irrational capacity will persist. UMC, VIS, GlobalFoundries will face permanent margin pressure in mature nodes.
