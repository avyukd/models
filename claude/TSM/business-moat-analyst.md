# Taiwan Semiconductor (TSM) - Business & Moat Analysis

## The One Thing That Matters for Durability

**Yield leadership at the bleeding edge is the single factor determining TSMC's competitive durability.**

Everything else flows from this: pricing power, customer lock-in, margin stability, and competitor irrelevance. Samsung has been manufacturing 3nm chips since 2022 but remains stuck at ~50% yields while TSMC approaches 90%. Intel 18A yields are at 55% with no external customers. The yield gap IS the moat.

Why yields matter more than anything:
- A 50% vs 90% yield gap means 2x the effective cost per working chip
- Customers cannot ship products on nodes with inconsistent yields
- Multi-billion dollar chip designs cannot tolerate yield uncertainty
- Samsung and Intel have the capital and engineers but cannot close the gap

This is structural, not cyclical. TSMC's yield advantage has persisted across every node transition for over a decade.

---

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**Bearish insight the bulls overlook**: TSMC's geographic diversification is succeeding faster than expected. Arizona Fab 1 achieved yield parity with Taiwan in early 2025. By 2027, meaningful leading-edge capacity exists outside Taiwan. The market may still be pricing TSM as a pure Taiwan play when it's becoming a global utility.

**Bullish insight the bears overlook**: Samsung's competitive collapse is permanent, not cyclical. Samsung Foundry lost $725M in Q3 2024, shut down 50% of production lines, and has had zero major customer wins at 3nm. Qualcomm, Google, AMD, and Nvidia have all defected to TSMC. Samsung's 2nm yields are below 30% vs TSMC's 60-70%.

## Moat & Durability Classification

**Moat Strength: Strong**

**Earnings Durability: High**

Current margins (56% gross, 40% net) reflect structural advantages, not cyclical tailwinds. Both Samsung and Intel are losing money and market share despite investing tens of billions annually. The primary risk (Taiwan concentration) is diminishing with successful overseas expansion.
