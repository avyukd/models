# TSMC (TSM) Geopolitical Risk Assessment

## The One Geopolitical Risk That Matters

**Taiwan Strait conflict or Chinese blockade would render TSMC's primary assets inaccessible and cause permanent capital loss.** ~80% of TSMC's advanced node capacity sits in Taiwan. No amount of Arizona expansion changes the near-term calculus: a kinetic conflict or sustained blockade equals uninvestable.

The critical question: Is this risk appropriately priced at 27-29x forward P/E, or is the market under/over-weighting it?

---

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**The market may be simultaneously over-weighting *probability* and under-weighting *magnitude*.**

- **Over-weighted**: Headline Taiwan invasion risk dominates narrative. Most analysts price in a permanent "Taiwan discount" of 2-4 P/E turns vs. peers.
- **Under-weighted**: The tail scenario isn't a linear discount problem. If China blockades Taiwan, TSMC isn't worth 20% less—it's worth close to zero for years. Current P/E suggests markets treat this as a moderate continuous risk rather than a binary fat-tailed one.

**Second-order insight**: TSMC *benefits* from US-China semiconductor decoupling in the base case. Export controls on Chinese competitors (SMIC) plus Arizona ramp strengthens TSMC's monopoly position. The same geopolitical tension that creates Taiwan risk also creates competitive moat. Markets may be pricing the downside without fully crediting the upside from decoupling.

**Non-consensus view**: The Arizona "insurance policy" is being discounted as margin dilutive. But if US government views TSMC Arizona as strategic infrastructure, regulatory/political support could exceed expectations. 30% of 2nm+ capacity in Arizona by end of decade fundamentally changes risk profile if achieved.

## Geographic Exposure Map

| Geography | Revenue % (2025) | Assets | Notes |
|-----------|------------------|--------|-------|
| North America | 75% | Arizona: 3 fabs under development, 6 planned | Apple, Nvidia, AMD, Qualcomm |
| China | 9% | Nanjing fab (~2.4% of revenue, ~3% capacity) | 16/28nm mature nodes only |
| Asia Pacific (ex-China) | 9% | - | Diverse fabless customers |
| Japan | 4% | Kumamoto fab (operational 2024) | Sony partnership, 28nm specialty |
| EMEA | 3% | Dresden fab (under construction) | 28nm, automotive focus |

**Asset concentration**: ~80% of advanced capacity (sub-7nm) remains in Taiwan. Kumamoto/Arizona are mature/ramping nodes respectively.

**Customer concentration**: Top 10 customers = 76% of revenue. Nvidia (~22%) has overtaken Apple (~18%) as largest customer. HPC now 58% of revenue vs. 36% in 2020.

## Sovereign & Regime Risk Assessment

### Taiwan
- **Stability**: Mature democracy with peaceful power transitions. No expropriation risk from Taiwan government.
- **Rule of law**: Strong property rights, independent judiciary. TSMC enjoys quasi-state champion status.
- **Primary risk vector**: External—cross-strait relations with China.

### China (Nanjing operations)
- **Revenue exposure**: 9% of sales, 2.4% from Nanjing fab
- **Intervention risk**: Low direct risk to operations given small footprint. Higher risk is being used as leverage in broader US-China disputes.
- **Current posture**: China needs TSMC's mature node chips for auto/industrial applications. Expropriation unlikely given scale of Taiwan operations.

### United States (Arizona)
- **Posture**: Highly supportive. $6.6B CHIPS Act funding secured.
- **Risk**: Regulatory overreach if US-Taiwan relations sour or if TSMC pricing becomes politically contentious.

---

## Regulatory & State Intervention Risk

### US Export Controls (Primary)
- **Current status**: TSMC operates under annual license for Nanjing fab (secured January 2026). License restricts capacity expansion and technology upgrades.
- **VEU revocation**: VEU status ended December 31, 2025; replaced with annual license requiring pre-approved equipment lists.
- **Restrictions**: Cannot expand advanced capacity in China. Nanjing capped at 16/28nm.
- **Impact**: Minimal—Nanjing is 2.4% of revenue. Controls actually *benefit* TSMC by constraining Chinese competitors (SMIC).

### China Export Controls (Retaliatory)
- **Rare earth restrictions**: October 2025 controls on rare earth elements and processing technology. TSMC has 1-2 years of inventory.
- **Current status**: Suspended until November 2026 per US-China October 2025 agreement.
- **Long-term risk**: China controls >90% of rare earth processing. Weaponization remains latent threat.

### Forced Technology Transfer
- **Risk**: Low. TSMC's leading-edge technology is in Taiwan, not China. Arizona fab will bring 2nm to US soil.
- **Japanese/European fabs**: Low political risk jurisdictions with rule of law.

## Conflict & Sanctions Exposure

### Taiwan Strait Scenarios

| Scenario | Probability Assessment | Impact |
|----------|----------------------|--------|
| **Status quo** (gray zone pressure, exercises) | High | Manageable; operations continue |
| **Blockade** (quarantine of ports/airspace) | Low-to-moderate | Severe; supply chain disruption for months, global chip shortage |
| **Kinetic conflict** (invasion attempt) | Low | Catastrophic; Taiwan fabs likely inoperable, global economic depression |

**Key factors**:
- PLA targeting 2027 military readiness for Taiwan contingency (per Pentagon reports)
- Amphibious invasion is "among most complex military operations in modern history"—not high probability near-term
- Gray zone tactics (cyber, economic coercion, military exercises) more likely than kinetic action
- December 2025 "Justice Mission" exercises rehearsed blockade scenarios

### Sanctions/Secondary Risk
- **US sanctions on China**: TSMC compliant; has exited advanced work for sanctioned Chinese entities
- **If Taiwan sanctioned**: Not a realistic scenario
- **Chinese retaliation**: Rare earth controls are the primary lever; currently suspended

---

## Currency & Capital Controls

- **TWD exposure**: Taiwan dollar historically stable vs. USD. Central bank maintains managed float.
- **Capital controls**: Taiwan has no meaningful restrictions on profit repatriation.
- **Hedging**: TSMC reports in USD; natural hedge from North American revenue (75%).
- **China operations**: Yuan exposure minimal given 9% revenue share. Nanjing fab profits repatriable under current rules.

**Assessment**: Currency/capital control risk is low relative to operational risks.

## Scenario & Tail Risk Framing

### Base Case (70% probability)
- Cross-strait tensions persist but remain below conflict threshold
- Gray zone pressure continues (exercises, cyber, economic)
- Arizona ramp proceeds on schedule; 30% of advanced capacity outside Taiwan by ~2030
- TSMC maintains monopoly on leading-edge, pricing power intact
- US-China decoupling benefits TSMC vs. SMIC/Chinese fabs
- **Outcome**: Current valuation appropriate; gradual multiple expansion as geographic diversification progresses

### Adverse Case (20% probability)
- Extended Taiwan Strait crisis (weeks/months of heightened tension)
- Partial blockade or shipping lane disruption
- Customer inventory building accelerates, then demand crashes
- Insurance/shipping costs spike; Taiwan risk premium widens
- Arizona/Japan/Germany ramp accelerated but takes years
- **Outcome**: Stock drops 30-50%; recovers over 2-3 years as diversification completes

### Severe Tail Case (5-10% probability)
- Chinese blockade or kinetic action on Taiwan
- Taiwan fabs inoperable for extended period (months to years)
- Global chip shortage causes economic crisis
- TSMC Taiwan assets potentially destroyed or inaccessible
- **Outcome**: Permanent capital loss of 70-90%+; Arizona/Japan fabs only residual value

## Mitigants & Offsets

| Mitigant | Effectiveness |
|----------|---------------|
| **Arizona expansion** ($165B, 6 fabs, 30% of 2nm+ capacity) | High—but full ramp is 2028-2030 |
| **Japan fab** (Kumamoto, operational) | Moderate—mature nodes only |
| **Germany fab** (Dresden, under construction) | Limited—28nm specialty |
| **Customer concentration in US** | Mixed—reduces direct China exposure but doesn't protect Taiwan assets |
| **CHIPS Act support** ($6.6B direct funding) | Moderate—political capital but doesn't accelerate timeline materially |
| **Strategic importance** (TSMC is irreplaceable for AI/defense supply chain) | High—both US and Taiwan have aligned incentives to protect |
| **China's economic interdependence** | Moderate—conflict would devastate Chinese economy; provides some deterrence |

**Critical gap**: No amount of diversification makes TSMC investable if Taiwan operations are disrupted. The insurance policy (Arizona) only pays off in 3-5 years.

## Key Monitoring Signals

1. **PLA activity**: Watch for exercises exceeding December 2025 "Justice Mission" in scope/duration
2. **US-China diplomatic channels**: Deterioration of high-level communication increases miscalculation risk
3. **Taiwan domestic politics**: Any moves toward formal independence declaration would escalate risk sharply
4. **Arizona timeline**: Delays beyond stated 2027 3nm production signal execution risk
5. **Rare earth controls**: Resumption post-November 2026 suspension would signal relationship deterioration
6. **Customer hedging behavior**: Excessive inventory building by Nvidia/Apple suggests insiders pricing in disruption
7. **Insurance/shipping rates**: Taiwan Strait freight/insurance pricing is real-time risk market

## Bottom Line

TSMC's geopolitical risk is **material and existential in the tail**. The Taiwan Strait question dominates all other considerations. Current valuation (27-29x forward) implicitly assumes low probability of severe scenarios—markets are treating this as a discount factor, not a binary risk.

**The variant perception**: TSMC benefits from decoupling in the base case (moat widening) while facing catastrophe in the tail case (100% loss). This is not a linear risk to be discounted—it's a barbell. The market may be pricing an "average" outcome when the distribution is bimodal.

**Investment implication**: If you believe invasion probability is <5% over 5 years and Arizona ramp succeeds, TSMC is undervalued. If you weight tail risk more heavily or are skeptical of timeline, this may belong in the "too hard" pile regardless of fundamentals.
