## 2. Key Drivers (The 2 Things That Matter)

1. **FDA approval probability and timing for QTORIN in microcystic LMs.** Phase 3 SELVA data is strong (95% improvement rate, p<0.001 on primary and all secondary endpoints, clean safety). NDA submission H2 2026, potential approval H1 2027. This is now largely de-risked -- the remaining risk is regulatory execution, not clinical.

2. **Payer willingness to reimburse at orphan drug pricing against compounded rapamycin.** This is the load-bearing variable the market is underweighting. Rapamycin is generic. Compounding pharmacies already serve this patient population. Unlike a typical first-in-class orphan drug addressing a true therapeutic void, QTORIN addresses an unmet *regulatory* need, not an unmet *therapeutic* need. The commercial trajectory depends on whether the FDA approval stamp changes payer behavior -- a fundamentally harder commercial problem than consensus models assume.

## 3. Thesis Summary

- Phase 3 SELVA data is excellent: 95% responder rate, all endpoints hit, no drug-related SAEs, systemic levels below detection. Approval probability is high (80%+). This is the good news, and it is real.
- At $114/share ($1.35B market cap), the stock is pricing in approval AND successful commercialization with peak sales well above $200M. The gap-up today reflects the clinical de-risking, which was the right move. The question is what is left.
- The compounding pharmacy substitution threat is structural, not transitional. No head-to-head data exists comparing QTORIN to compounded sirolimus. Payers have a rational economic incentive to prefer compounded alternatives at a fraction of the cost.
- A capital raise of $75-125M is near-certain within 12 months. The company has ~$55M cash, burns ~$10M/quarter, and must build commercial infrastructure from zero for a 30,000-patient U.S. population served by a fragmented specialist base. Dilution terms will depend on stock price at time of raise.
- Pipeline optionality (TOIVA in venous malformations, angiokeratomas) is real but years behind and not independently sufficient to justify the current valuation.

## 5. The Setup

Palvella is a clinical-stage rare disease biotech that went public via reverse merger with Pieris Pharmaceuticals in December 2024. Its lead asset, QTORIN 3.9% rapamycin anhydrous gel, is a topical formulation of a well-known generic immunosuppressant targeting rare vascular malformations with no FDA-approved treatments. The company announced positive Phase 3 results this morning, driving a 33% pre-market gap.

The structural tension: orphan drug exclusivity (7 years) provides real protection against branded competitors, but does not block compounding pharmacies that already supply rapamycin to this patient base. The moat is moderate -- real IP through 2038, strong regulatory designations, but narrow because the active ingredient is generic and widely available.

Ligand Pharmaceuticals holds an 8-10% royalty on worldwide commercial sales, compressing unit economics on an already small-market opportunity.

## 7. Forward Path and Catalysts

- **Near-term (0-6 months):** NDA preparation and submission (H2 2026); likely capital raise ($75-125M); TOIVA Phase 2 full data readout
- **Medium-term (6-18 months):** FDA acceptance of NDA; PDUFA date set; potential approval H1 2027; commercial infrastructure buildout; first payer negotiations
- **What the market will notice next:** Capital raise terms and dilution. This is the next material event that could pressure the stock. If the company raises at $100+ (near current levels), dilution is manageable (~6-8%). If the stock pulls back first, dilution could be 15-20%+

## 8. Risks and Kill Switches

- **Thesis invalidation:** FDA Complete Response Letter or NDA refusal. Low probability given Phase 3 data quality and Breakthrough designation, but binary
- **Permanent loss risk:** Peak sales below $75M due to compounding substitution + payer resistance. This would not support the current valuation at any reasonable discount rate
- **Re-evaluation triggers:** (1) Capital raise at a significant discount to market, (2) any payer or PBM signal on formulary positioning, (3) competitor filing for similar indication, (4) management guidance on pricing strategy

---

## 9. Positioning and Portfolio Logic

**No position today.** The stock gapped 33% on strong Phase 3 data. The approval is largely de-risked, and the pre-market price reflects that. What remains unresolved -- commercial execution, payer dynamics, capital raise terms -- will take 12-18 months to clarify. There is no edge in buying a pre-revenue biotech at the top of its risk-adjusted NPV range on the day of a positive catalyst.

**What would change this to a Buy:**
- Pullback to $70-85 range (20-35% below pre-market), which would create margin of safety for commercial uncertainty
- Evidence of favorable payer positioning (formulary inclusion without step therapy)
- Capital raise completed on favorable terms, removing the dilution overhang

**What would change this to a Short:**
- Early payer signals requiring step therapy through compounded sirolimus first
- Capital raise at a significant discount, signaling management concern about runway
- NDA delay or FDA feedback suggesting additional data requirements

**Position size if entry criteria met:** 1-2% starter on pullback; would scale to 3-4% with payer confirmation post-launch.
