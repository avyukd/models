## Why I Am Passing

The market has already closed the gap. The April 2025 Dalton transaction valued CrossingBridge at ~$103-105M. ENDI owns 75%, implying ~$77M of attributable value plus ~$8-10M in other assets (Sitestar, cash, Willow Oak royalty). Total NAV: ~$85-90M. Current market cap: ~$85M.

**There is no discount to close.** The stock is fairly valued on transaction-based math and trades at ~23x normalized earnings -- reasonable for a growing boutique asset manager, but not cheap.

The variant perception opportunity that existed 12-18 months ago (hidden asset manager trading at steep discount) has been arbitraged away by the Dalton transaction crystallizing value.

---

## Key Drivers (The 1-3 Things That Matter)

1. **CrossingBridge AUM** -- Revenue = 0.35% net realized fees x AUM. Every $1B adds ~$3.5M revenue, ~$1M net income. AUM is now $4.1B (Dec 2025), up from $2.3B in 2021.

2. **David Sherman** -- He IS the moat. 35-year track record, sole portfolio manager, 71% economic interest. No succession plan. If Sherman exits, this is a zero.

3. **Fund Performance** -- Low duration credit strategy outperformed massively in 2022 (+1% vs Bloomberg Agg -13%). Continued outperformance drives inflows; underperformance causes redemptions.

---

## Thesis (Bull Case I Am Not Taking)

- CrossingBridge AUM continues compounding 20%+/year toward $6-8B
- Fee realization improves as funds scale past expense cap thresholds
- Sherman remains engaged and performance persists
- Market re-rates from ~23x to 30x+ as growth is recognized

**Why I am not underwriting this:**

The thesis requires betting on (a) continued AUM growth that is already priced in, (b) Sherman's health and engagement over an indefinite period, and (c) no credit market stress causing redemptions. The upside does not compensate for key person risk.

At ~$15.85 per share (~$85M market cap):

These assumptions are aggressive, not conservative. The stock prices in continued execution.

## Red Flags That Reinforce "Pass"

1. **SEC Deregistration (Jan 2024)** -- Unusual for a company whose primary asset is an SEC-regulated mutual fund business. Reduces disclosure. Not inherently bad, but limits ability to verify.

2. **Cohanzick $10M Payment (March 2024)** -- Sherman's entity sold advisory contracts to CrossingBridge for $10M via promissory note. This is a related-party cash extraction disguised as a "consolidation." The market has not meaningfully adjusted for this.

3. **No Succession Plan** -- 71-year-old founder with no disclosed plan. The 2025 equity incentive carve-out (5-10% to employees) is too little, too late to build institutional depth.

4. **Single Fund Concentration** -- CBLDX (~$1.5B) likely generates majority of revenue. Product concentration amplifies performance risk.

---

## What Would Move This Out of the Too Hard Pile

1. **Significant Price Decline** -- At $10-12/share (~$55-65M market cap), the risk/reward becomes interesting. That would imply 15-18x normalized earnings with upside if AUM continues growing.

2. **Succession Plan Announced** -- Clear designation of investment team continuity would reduce key man risk.

3. **Institutional Mandate Wins** -- Large institutional allocations (pension funds, endowments) would signal sticky AUM and provide valuation support.

4. **Insider Buying at Scale** -- Sherman or Kiel adding material personal capital at current prices would signal conviction.

---

## Position Sizing Logic

**Zero.** This is a pass.

If forced to size: 0.5% maximum position given key man risk, OTC liquidity, and reduced disclosure. The risk of permanent capital loss (Sherman exit) cannot be hedged and is binary.

---

## Summary

ENDI was a compelling opportunity 18 months ago when CrossingBridge was hidden inside a shell and the market valued the entire company at a discount to the subsidiary's intrinsic value. That arbitrage is closed.

Today, the stock is fairly valued at ~23x normalized earnings with extreme key man risk, reduced disclosure, and related-party transactions that benefit Sherman. The bull case requires continued AUM growth that is already priced in.

**This is not a bad business. It is a fairly priced business with uncompensatable risks.** The correct decision is to pass and revisit on price decline or structural change.
