# ENDI Corp (CrossingBridge Advisors) - Business Quality & Moat Analysis

## The One Thing That Matters for Durability

**David Sherman is the moat.** CrossingBridge's competitive position rests almost entirely on Sherman's 35+ year track record in credit investing, his Cohanzick pedigree, and the performance reputation that drives institutional allocation decisions. The business is a vehicle for Sherman's investment acumen, not a structural franchise with transferable advantages.

If Sherman exits or performance deteriorates materially, AUM would likely face significant outflows. The April 2025 minority sale to Dalton ($26M for 25%) values CrossingBridge at ~$104M - but this valuation implicitly assumes Sherman remains engaged and performance continues. Key person risk is the dominant factor.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**The bear case underweights distribution leverage; the bull case underweights key person fragility.**

- Bears see a tiny boutique in a fee-compressing industry. They miss that CrossingBridge has built legitimate institutional distribution (sub-advisory deals with RiverPark, AlphaCentric, Destinations; UCITS product for Europe; Nordic partnership with NCI Advisory; 2025 LSEG Lipper Award for Best Fixed Income Small Fund Family). Distribution relationships create stickiness beyond raw performance.

- Bulls see $4.1B+ AUM, growing EBIT, and a ~$100M CrossingBridge valuation against a $85-90M market cap (per recent data). They underweight that boutique credit shops with founder-managers face existential key person risk. Sherman has no succession plan disclosed. The equity incentive plan for employees (5-10% of CrossingBridge interests) was only established in 2025 - suggesting talent retention was not institutionally addressed until recently.

**Non-consensus insight:** The Dalton minority stake sale may signal Sherman's recognition that CrossingBridge needs outside capital and governance to scale beyond a one-person franchise. This is either the first step toward institutionalization (bullish for durability) or a liquidity event that precedes Sherman's reduced involvement (bearish). The answer determines long-term value.

**Additional insight:** ENDI filed Form 15 in January 2024 to deregister from SEC reporting. The stock now trades on OTC Pink. For a company whose primary asset is an SEC-regulated mutual fund business, choosing to reduce parent-company disclosure is an unusual signal that bears watching.

## Business Model Summary

**Revenue source:** Management fees on ~$4.1B AUM (as of Dec 2025). CrossingBridge charges ~0.65-0.85% management fees on advised mutual funds. Sub-advisory arrangements provide additional but smaller revenue streams.

**Who pays:** Mutual fund shareholders and institutional clients. Revenue scales linearly with AUM - no meaningful operating leverage until fixed costs are absorbed.

**Cost structure:** Predominantly fixed (investment team compensation, compliance, fund administration). Variable costs are minimal. High operating leverage once past breakeven.

**Profit center:** Advisory fees from the Low Duration High Income Fund (CBLDX, ~$1.5B AUM) and related credit products. This single fund likely generates the majority of revenue.

**2023-2024 financials:**
- 2023: ~$9.6M revenue, ~$2.5M net income, 26% net margin
- 2024: ~$6.3M free cash flow, ~$6.5M operating cash flow
- EBITDA: ~$8M with 45% EBITDA margin (per public data)

## Source(s) of Advantage

### 1. Investment Track Record (Real, But Personal)

Sherman's performance record is the primary demand driver. CBLDX has a 5-star Morningstar rating with consistently positive returns across market environments:
- 2022: +1.01% vs Bloomberg Agg -13.01% (capital preservation in a brutal year)
- 3-year return: 6.94% vs category 5.23%
- 5-year return: 5.81% vs category 4.63%

**Mechanism:** Institutional allocators and investment consultants select managers based on risk-adjusted returns, team stability, and process consistency. Sherman's track record opens doors that new entrants cannot access.

**Limitation:** This is personal alpha, not structural moat. The track record walks out the door with Sherman.

### 2. Institutional Distribution Relationships (Moderate Switching Costs)

CrossingBridge has built distribution through:
- Sub-advisory mandates (RiverPark, AlphaCentric, Destinations funds)
- UCITS product for European institutional access
- Nordic partnership (NCI Advisory - 2024)
- Direct institutional relationships
- Launched first US-based Nordic High Income Bond Fund (NRDCX) in Sept 2024

**Mechanism:** Once a sub-advisory relationship is established, switching costs exist - due diligence requirements, board approvals, and operational integration create inertia.

**Limitation:** If performance deteriorates, these relationships can be terminated. Distribution does not protect against poor returns.

### 3. Specialized Credit Focus (Narrow Niche, Limited Scalability)

CrossingBridge operates in low-duration, high-yield credit - a specialized niche that is less commoditized than broad equity or investment-grade bond strategies. Strategy includes: ultra-short duration, low duration high yield, responsible credit, and SPAC arbitrage.

**Mechanism:** Boutique credit specialists can differentiate through security selection in less efficient markets. The strategy particularly shines in rising rate environments where duration is punished.

**Limitation:** Niche size caps total addressable market. The strategy cannot absorb unlimited AUM without capacity constraints.

## Competitive Landscape

**Industry dynamics:** Asset management faces structural fee compression. The Big Three (BlackRock, Vanguard, State Street) compete on cost; boutiques must differentiate on performance.

**Direct competitors:** Other boutique credit managers (Golub Capital, Waterfall Asset Management, DoubleLine, Loomis Sayles). CrossingBridge competes on risk-adjusted returns and manager reputation, not scale or fees.

**Fee positioning:** CrossingBridge expense ratios (0.85-0.92%) are described as "below average" compared to peers - reasonable positioning but not a cost advantage.

**Market position:** CrossingBridge is small (~$4.1B AUM) in a massive industry. Size is irrelevant for durability - what matters is whether returns persist.

**Attack vectors:** Larger managers could compete if the niche grows. However, low-duration high-yield is likely too small to attract serious attention from BlackRock or PIMCO.

## Customer Behavior & Revenue Quality

**Concentration risk:** Flagship Low Duration High Income Fund (CBLDX) represents ~$1.5B of ~$4.1B AUM - significant concentration in one product.

**Retention dynamics:** No disclosed churn data. Mutual fund investors are sticky by default (inertia, tax consequences). Institutional investors conduct formal manager reviews and can leave if performance lags.

**Contract structure:** Open-end mutual funds have no contractual lock-up. Investors can redeem daily. This creates permanent vulnerability to outflows if performance disappoints.

**Pricing power:** Limited. Fee compression industrywide constrains pricing upside. CrossingBridge is not a premium-priced product.

**Revenue visibility:** Low. AUM can fluctuate with market conditions and flows. Advisory revenue is marked-to-market daily.

**Critical distinction:** This is "lazy customer" stickiness (inertia, hassle costs) not "locked-in" stickiness (switching costs, integration).

## Evidence of Moat in the Financials

**Margin stability:** 45% EBITDA margin demonstrates the operating leverage inherent in asset management once breakeven is achieved.

**AUM growth:** $2.3B (2021) to $3.4B (Dec 2024) to $4.1B (late 2025) - nearly doubled since 2021. Strong demand signal.

**Resistance to commoditization:** Performance in 2022 (all CrossingBridge funds produced positive returns while Bloomberg Agg was -13%) demonstrates differentiation.

**Valuation anchor:** Dalton's $26M for 25% implies ~$104M CrossingBridge valuation, or ~2.5% of AUM. Typical asset manager transactions range from 1-3% of AUM, suggesting this is fair but not premium pricing. The non-control discount and key person risk likely explain why it's not higher.

## Fragility Analysis

### 1. Key Person Risk (Extreme)

David Sherman has 35+ years of investment experience. No disclosed succession plan. Sherman owns ~87% voting control of ENDI through affiliates. The business has no structural moat independent of Sherman's involvement.

The 2025 equity incentive plan (5-10% of interests to employees) is a first step toward retention and succession, but it was established very recently.

**Probability of erosion:** Certain eventually; timing unknown. This is the dominant risk.

### 2. Performance Mean Reversion

Active managers' outperformance tends to mean-revert. A 2-3 year period of underperformance would trigger institutional redemptions.

**Probability:** Moderate. Sherman's track record is long, but credit cycles can produce extended periods of relative underperformance.

### 3. Fee Compression

Industry-wide fee compression is structural. CrossingBridge's fees are sustainable today but face long-term pressure.

**Probability:** High over 10+ year horizon; manageable near-term.

### 4. Credit Blow-Up

Low-duration credit strategy depends on credit selection. A few bad credits can crater returns and reputation.

**Probability:** Low but non-zero. Sherman's focus on "return of capital before return on capital" suggests conservative positioning.

---

## Moat & Durability Classification

**Moat Strength:** Weak

The moat exists, but it is personal (Sherman's reputation and track record) rather than structural. The business has no brand, network effects, or switching costs that would survive a change in management. Distribution relationships provide modest stickiness, but these are table stakes for any established manager.

**Earnings Durability:** Medium

Current earnings are durable as long as Sherman remains engaged and performance holds. The ~$6M annual free cash flow is supported by growing AUM and stable margins. However, durability beyond Sherman's tenure is unknowable and likely low.
