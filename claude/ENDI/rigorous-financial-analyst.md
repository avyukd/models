## Key Finding: Hidden Asset Manager Trades at Deep Discount to Subsidiary Sale Price

The April 2025 sale of a 25% stake in CrossingBridge for $25.9M implies a $103M+ valuation for the subsidiary alone. ENDI's entire market cap is ~$85M. The market is either (a) assigning zero or negative value to everything outside CrossingBridge, (b) applying a steep holding company discount, or (c) mispricing the stock.

## Key Financial Drivers

**The 1-3 metrics that matter:**

1. **CrossingBridge AUM** - Revenue is a direct function of AUM. Every $1B of AUM adds ~$3.5M revenue and ~$1M net income at current margins.

2. **Investment Performance** - AUM growth depends on fund performance attracting inflows. CrossingBridge's low duration credit strategies have performed well in rising rate environments.

3. **Fee Realization Rate** - Expense waivers compress realized fees. As funds scale, waivers should decline and margins expand.

**What would cause inflection:**
- Positive: Continued AUM growth toward $6-8B; institutional mandates
- Negative: Credit market drawdown; manager departure; competitive fee pressure

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**Non-Consensus Insight:**

The market appears to price ENDI as if the CrossingBridge transaction validated current valuations. It did not. The buyer (Dalton) acquired a non-control minority stake at a full price -- this is typical in growth capital deals designed to keep founders incentivized.

**What the footnotes reveal:**
1. Cohanzick (Sherman's entity) sold advisory contracts to CrossingBridge for $10M via promissory note in March 2024. This is a related-party transaction that effectively transferred fee income from one Sherman-controlled entity to another, funded by company cash.
2. Willow Oak was sold to a former employee (Jessica Greer) for a 10-year royalty stream (10% of gross revenue). This is an unusual structure that suggests ENDI wanted to exit a non-core business without recognizing an immediate loss.

**The market is likely missing:**
- The $10M Cohanzick payment was effectively a cash extraction from ENDI, not a value-creating acquisition
- Net shareholder value creation from CrossingBridge must be measured after subtracting related-party transfers

## Conclusion

ENDI is a straightforward bet on CrossingBridge's continued AUM growth. At ~$85M market cap vs. ~$103M implied CrossingBridge value (based on the 25% sale), the stock appears reasonably valued -- not cheap. The premium valuation is justified only if AUM growth continues at 20%+ annually.

**What must be true for current valuation:**
- AUM continues growing to $5-6B within 2 years
- Fee realization improves as funds scale
- Sherman remains engaged and performance continues

**What breaks:**
- Credit market stress causing outflows
- Key person risk materializes
- Fee compression in fixed income space

**Verdict:** Fairly valued on normalized earnings at ~23x P/E. Upside requires continued strong AUM growth. The recent minority sale transaction does not prove the stock is cheap -- it proves a strategic buyer was willing to pay up for growth optionality.
