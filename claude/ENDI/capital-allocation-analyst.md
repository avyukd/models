# ENDI Corp: Management & Capital Allocation Analysis

## Lead Finding

ENDI Corp represents a rare alignment case: founder-CEO David Sherman controls 61%+ voting rights AND holds 71% economic interest in Cohanzick, his investment vehicle. This is structural alignment, not contractual. The non-consensus insight is that the dual-class structure, typically a governance red flag, actually benefits minority shareholders here because Sherman's economic exposure through CrossingBridge's success vastly exceeds any extraction opportunity. The 25% CrossingBridge stake sale at 8.5x EBITDA ($105M implied valuation vs. $65M market cap) crystallized hidden value, demonstrating Sherman prioritizes AUM growth capital over personal liquidity.

---

## 1. Management Overview

**CEO:** David Sherman (since August 2022 merger close)
- Founded Cohanzick Management in 1996
- Founded CrossingBridge Advisors in 2016
- 35+ years fixed income investment experience
- Owns 71% of Cohanzick Management, which holds ~61% voting control of ENDI

**Board Chair / Director:** Steven Kiel
- Founder of Arquitos Capital Management (2009)
- Former CEO/Chairman of Enterprise Diversified (pre-merger)
- 10%+ owner designation per SEC filings
- Owns ~715,507 shares (per 2019 filings, likely higher post-merger)

**Governance Structure:**
- Dual-class: Class A (economic + voting) and Class B (voting only, no economic rights)
- Cohanzick received at merger: 2.4M Class A shares, 1.8M Class B shares, warrants for 2.05M Class A at $8 strike (expire August 2027)
- Class B shares redeem 1:1 as Class W-1 warrants are exercised
- Result: Sherman cannot vote both Class B and underlying warrant shares simultaneously

**Share Count (March 2025):** 5.3M Class A + 1.8M Class B = 7.1M total voting shares
Post-warrant exercise: ~6.65M shares outstanding

## 2. Incentive Structure & Alignment

### Ownership Economics

Sherman's alignment is structural:
- 71% of Cohanzick x 61% voting control = ~43% effective economic/voting interest in ENDI
- All CrossingBridge management fees flow through ENDI
- Sherman's wealth creation is tied to AUM growth, not salary extraction

**What behavior does this encourage?**
Growing AUM and fund performance. Sherman earns 0.65% management fees on mutual fund AUM (less after expense caps). At $4.1B AUM, gross fee revenue run rate is ~$26M annually. His effective ~43% economic stake means ~$11M+ in value accrues to Sherman through ENDI ownership, dwarfing any reasonable salary.

**Who benefits if the stock underperforms?**
No one meaningfully. Sherman's voting control prevents hostile action, but his economic incentive is entirely aligned with minority shareholders. The Class B shares have zero economic rights - they cannot receive dividends or liquidation proceeds.

### Compensation Design

Limited public disclosure post-SEC deregistration. Based on available proxy data:
- Say-on-pay vote conducted in 2023
- Three-year frequency for future advisory votes approved
- 2022 Omnibus Equity Incentive Plan approved

The structure suggests minimal cash compensation relative to ownership value. Asset managers of this size typically pay founders primarily through distributions, not salary.

### Red Flag Check

**Absent:** Excessive perks, golden parachutes, related-party transactions
**Present:** High insider ownership, founder operational control, deregistration reduces disclosure (minor concern)

## 3. Stock-Based Compensation Discipline

### SBC Assessment

ENDI operates primarily as an asset manager, not a tech company. SBC is not a material issue:
- 2022 Omnibus Equity Incentive Plan exists but no evidence of aggressive grants
- Share count relatively stable: 5.27M Class A (Dec 2024) vs 5.31M Class A (Mar 2025)
- Net increase of ~40K shares over the period - minimal dilution

### Dilution Reality

The primary dilution event was structural at merger close (2022):
- Pre-merger Enterprise Diversified shareholders received 2.65M Class A shares (~39% of post-merger Class A)
- Cohanzick received 2.4M Class A + warrants for 2.05M more shares
- Total potential dilution from warrants: 2.05M shares at $8 strike

At current prices (~$17), warrant exercise brings in $16.4M cash while adding shares. This is accretive dilution - shareholders get cash at below-market prices.

**Key observation:** No evidence of SBC used to enrich management at shareholder expense. Dilution sources are transparent and tied to value-creating transactions.

## 4. Capital Allocation Track Record

### a) Reinvestment

**Discipline: High**

CrossingBridge's organic growth is exceptional:
- AUM: $2.6B (Jan 2024) -> $3.4B (Dec 2024) -> $4.1B+ (Dec 2025)
- Growth rate: 30%+ in 2024, 19%+ in 2025
- Minimal capital required for growth - asset management is capital-light

Reinvestment has focused on:
- Fund launches (Nordic High Income Bond Fund, Sep 2024)
- Geographic expansion (NCI Advisory partnership in Denmark)
- Cohanzick acquisition (March 2024) - consolidated advisory business

**Returns on capital:** Adjusted operating margins of 46-50%+ demonstrate reinvestment efficiency.

### b) M&A

**Discipline: High**

| Transaction | Date | Assessment |
|-------------|------|------------|
| CrossingBridge merger with Enterprise Diversified | Aug 2022 | Reverse merger structure minimized cash outlay. Sherman gained public currency; Enterprise Diversified shareholders gained high-quality asset manager. Win-win. |
| RiverPark Short Term High Yield Fund acquisition | Early 2024 | Acquired existing fund, adding ~$1B AUM. Bolt-on, not empire building. |
| Cohanzick advisory business purchase | Mar 2024 | Consolidated related business under ENDI. Clean-up transaction. |
| NCI Advisory (16% -> 25% stake) | Nov 2024 | $898K for 16% additional. Nordic market expansion. Small, strategic. |
| 25% CrossingBridge stake sale to Dalton/CBE | Apr-May 2025 | Raised $25.9M growth capital without diluting ENDI equity. Valued CrossingBridge at ~$105M vs. $65M ENDI market cap. Proved hidden value. |

**M&A verdict:** Every transaction has been small, strategic, and value-accretive. No evidence of overpaying or empire building. The CrossingBridge partial sale was particularly shrewd - raised capital at 8.5x EBITDA (6.5x net of cash retained in CrossingBridge) without diluting public shareholders.

### c) Buybacks & Issuance

**No material buyback program.** This is appropriate given:
- Stock was undervalued (proven by CrossingBridge sale valuation)
- Growth capital needs exist for fund expansion
- Cash better deployed in operations

**No equity issuance** beyond merger-related and minimal SBC.

Capital return philosophy appears to be: grow the asset base, let compounding work. Reasonable for a company at this stage with 30%+ AUM growth.

### d) Debt Usage

**Conservative**
- Only debt: $10M note
- Cash and investments: $55M (Sep 2025)
- Net cash position: ~$45M
- Debt/EBITDA: ~0.8x on $12.5M annualized adjusted EBITDA

No financial engineering. Leverage is minimal and appropriate.

## 5. Learning vs. Repetition

**Limited history** to assess mistakes and corrections. However:

- SEC deregistration (Jan 2024) suggests learning - cost savings prioritized over prestige of OTCQB listing. Rational decision given limited trading volume and shareholder base.
- Willow Oak sale (May 2024) - exited non-core business for 10-year revenue share rather than holding subscale asset. Shows willingness to prune.
- CrossingBridge partial sale structure - raised growth capital without diluting equity, suggesting sophisticated capital allocation thinking.

**Behavior pattern:** Pragmatic, value-focused, willing to take unconventional paths.

## 6. Forward-Looking Capital Allocation Risk

### What Management is Likely to Do Next

1. **Continue organic fund launches** - Nordic fund, potential new strategies mentioned
2. **Small bolt-on acquisitions** - existing fund complexes, advisory contracts
3. **Eventually return capital** - once AUM growth moderates, expect dividends or buybacks

### Where Capital Misallocation Risk Exists

1. **M&A overpayment risk is low** - Sherman's track record and value orientation suggest discipline
2. **Key man risk** - Sherman is the business. Succession planning is unclear
3. **Market dislocation risk** - fixed income funds can experience redemptions in credit stress; AUM could decline rapidly in a credit crisis

### FCF Misuse Probability

**Low.** Sherman's incentives are aligned, track record is clean, and the business generates high-margin cash flows with limited reinvestment needs.

## The One Thing That Determines Value Creation

**Sherman's continued engagement and AUM growth trajectory.**

CrossingBridge is David Sherman. His 35-year track record, fund performance, and institutional relationships drive inflows. If he remains engaged and AUM compounds at 15-20%+ annually, ENDI's value will compound accordingly. If he steps back or performance deteriorates, the business is at risk.

This dominates all other considerations because:
- Operating margins are already 46-50%+
- Capital allocation is already disciplined
- The variable is AUM growth, which is almost entirely Sherman-dependent

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**The dual-class structure is a feature, not a bug.**

Typical analysis: Dual-class = governance risk = discount warranted.

Reality at ENDI: Sherman's 71% economic interest in Cohanzick, which owns 61%+ voting control, means his incentives are massively aligned with minority shareholders. The Class B shares have NO economic rights. Sherman cannot extract value through dividends, liquidation preferences, or related-party transactions without enriching Class A shareholders proportionally.

The market likely applies a generic "controlled company discount" without recognizing that:
1. Sherman's wealth is entirely tied to CrossingBridge performance
2. The partial sale proved management would crystallize value rather than hoard control
3. Deregistration saves costs, not hides malfeasance

**This makes me more trusting of management, not less.** The ownership structure is among the cleanest founder-alignment cases in small-cap land.
