# Crude Oil Tanker Industry Analysis

## The One Industry Factor That Matters

**Effective fleet supply is the dominant variable.** Not gross orderbook, not headline demand growth — but the *net tradeable* fleet available for compliant cargoes.

Three forces are compressing effective supply simultaneously:
1. Shadow fleet absorption (11%+ of fleet now dedicated to sanctioned trades)
2. Fleet age degradation (40% of crude tankers over 15 years old)
3. Sanctions hitting older vessels hardest (46% of shadow-dedicated vessels vs. 40% in 2024)

The market is pricing the orderbook like it's 2009, when it was 46% of fleet. Today it's 14% — and 70%+ of deliveries won't arrive until 2026-2027. The supply constraint is real but time-limited.

**What flips this:** A sanctions unwinding (Russia deal, Venezuela normalization) returns 200+ vessels to compliant trades within 90 days. This is the key binary risk.

---

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**Consensus view:** "Mid-cycle rates with orderbook risk in 2026-2027."

**Variant view:** This is not mid-cycle — this is a structurally-constrained upcycle that consensus is underweighting because:

1. **The shadow fleet is permanent fleet destruction.** 46% of vessels loading Russian/Iranian/Venezuelan crude *only* load sanctioned cargoes — they cannot return to compliant trades due to secondary sanctions risk, insurance exclusions, and charterer policies. These vessels are functionally dead to the mainstream market.

2. **Scrapping is structurally broken.** Zero VLCCs scrapped since October 2022. The shadow fleet is absorbing vessels that should be recycled. This seems bullish (tight supply) but is actually a coiled spring — any rate weakness triggers a scrapping wave that could remove 15%+ of tonnage.

3. **The orderbook math is misunderstood.** Yes, the ratio hit 14% (9-year high). But this must be netted against:
   - 18-20% of fleet over 20 years old (recycling candidates)
   - 11%+ permanently in shadow fleet
   - Limited shipyard capacity (40-50 VLCCs/year max globally; Korean yards absent)

   Effective orderbook-to-tradeable-fleet is closer to 8-10%.

4. **Red Sea rerouting is priced as temporary.** Despite ceasefire announcements, Suez transits have not recovered. Carriers need insurance guarantees that don't exist. If this persists through 2026, it's 6%+ permanent ton-mile demand boost.

**Implication:** Bullish for industry participants through 2026, but with a sharp left-tail risk from geopolitical normalization.

## Industry Fundamentals

### Supply Side

| Metric | Current | Historical Context |
|--------|---------|-------------------|
| Orderbook-to-fleet | 14.1% | 9-year high, but vs. 46% in 2008 |
| Fleet age (average) | 14+ years | Highest in 20+ years |
| Vessels 20+ years | 18-19% | Scrapping candidates |
| Shadow fleet share | 11%+ | Permanent removal from compliant trades |
| Scrapping YTD 2025 | 36 vessels (all types) | Near-zero for VLCCs |
| VLCC deliveries 2025 | ~7 | Rising to 40 in 2026, 58 in 2027 |

**Fleet growth projections (per Arctic Securities):**
- 2024: 0.4%
- 2025: 0.8%
- 2026: 1.6%

**Critical context:** Net fleet growth understates effective supply tightness. When you remove shadow fleet (11%+) and aging vessels with restricted charterer access (vessels 17+ affected by sanctions), tradeable supply is materially smaller.

### Demand Side (Ton-Mile)

**Structural tailwinds:**
- Atlantic-to-Asia crude flows lengthening routes (US, Brazil, Guyana production growth)
- Red Sea avoidance adding 3,500nm per voyage (persisting despite ceasefire)
- OPEC+ supply restoration increasing Middle East loadings (September surge drove rates to 5-year highs)

**2025 reality:**
- Global oil supply grew ~2.8 million b/d
- OPEC restored first tranche of voluntary cuts
- India shifted from Russian crude to Persian Gulf (sanctions effect) — longer ton-miles

**2026 risks:**
- OPEC+ paused further production increases through Q1 2026
- IEA forecasts potential 5 million b/d inventory build Q1 2026
- Non-OPEC production growth expected to slow in H2 2026

## Cycle Position Assessment

### Where Are We?

**Late cycle, not mid-cycle.** Here's why:

| Cycle Indicator | Current Reading | Interpretation |
|-----------------|-----------------|----------------|
| Spot TCE rates | $100,000-130,000/day | 3-5x above mid-cycle (~$25-35K) |
| 1-year timecharter rates | Elevated | Strong forward curve |
| Orderbook acceleration | 490% YoY contracting increase | Classic late-cycle ordering response |
| Asset prices | VLCC newbuild at $119M | Near cycle highs |
| Scrapping | Collapsed | Earnings too strong to scrap |

**The classic late-cycle tell:** Orderbook surging *because* rates are high. This is reactive, not anticipatory. The 2026-2027 delivery wave was contracted in 2023-2024 when rates were already elevated.

### Historical Cycle Comparison

| Period | Orderbook/Fleet | What Happened |
|--------|-----------------|---------------|
| 2008-2009 | 46% | Massive oversupply through 2015 |
| 2015-2016 | Peak rates | Triggered ordering wave |
| 2017-2019 | Trough | Excess capacity absorbed |
| 2020 | COVID spike | Storage demand, then crash |
| 2021-2022 | Negative TCEs | Worst VLCC earnings on record |
| 2023-2025 | 14% and climbing | Current upcycle |

**Pattern:** This cycle is tighter than 2008 (much lower orderbook), but spot rates are extreme by historical standards. The industry has been here before — 2015 rates of $55,000/day timecharter triggered the ordering that caused 2017-2019 pain.

### Mid-Cycle Definition

**What "normal" looks like for crude tankers:**
- VLCC spot TCE: $25,000-40,000/day
- Fleet utilization: 85-90%
- Orderbook-to-fleet: 8-12%
- Scrapping: 15-25 VLCCs annually
- 1-year timecharter: $30,000-45,000/day

**Current deviation:** Spot rates 3-4x above mid-cycle. This is not sustainable absent structural change.

## Risk Analysis

### What Breaks Industry Economics

**Primary risk: Geopolitical normalization**

1. **Russia sanctions relief** — 183 tankers sanctioned by Biden administration alone. A Trump-era deal could return 100+ vessels to compliant trades within months.

2. **Shadow fleet unwind** — If Russian/Iranian crude can be transported on mainstream tonnage, ~11% of fleet re-enters compliant trades.

3. **Red Sea resolution** — Full Suez normalization removes 6%+ of ton-mile demand.

**Combined probability:** Non-trivial. A comprehensive Russia deal + Red Sea normalization could add 15-20% effective supply while removing 6%+ demand. Rates would collapse.

### Secondary Risks

- OPEC+ production pause extending (bearish for demand)
- Global recession reducing crude demand
- Accelerated ordering response to current rates (already happening)

### Upside Scenarios

- Extended sanctions regime / geopolitical escalation
- Accelerated scrapping of 20+ year vessels (could offset 2026-2027 deliveries)
- IMO 2030 regulations forcing fleet renewal
