# DBI.AX -- Dalrymple Bay Infrastructure -- Rigorous Financial Analysis

**Price: A$5.31 | Shares: 496M | Market Cap: ~A$2,633M | Date: 2026-02-23**

---

## The Core Tension

DBI is a regulated toll-road on met coal exports with 100% take-or-pay contracts. The market prices it as a stable infrastructure yield play. The critical question is whether A$1.95B of net debt on a single-asset coal terminal is sustainable through a secular demand transition, and whether the interest cost step-up destroys the equity return story.

## Balance Sheet & Financial Risk

| Metric | FY24 |
|---|---|
| Total Debt | A$2,036M |
| Cash | A$90M |
| Net Debt | A$1,946M |
| Net Debt / EBITDA | 7.0x |
| Total Equity | A$1,088M |
| Net Debt / Equity | 1.8x |

**This is the single most important number in the analysis: 7x net debt/EBITDA.** For context, typical infrastructure companies operate at 4-6x. DBI is at the aggressive end.

**Interest rate step-up is the key near-term risk.** Weighted average cost is ~4.63% until mid-2026, stepping to ~6.5% thereafter as 2021-vintage swaps roll off. On A$2B of debt, that is roughly A$37M of additional annual interest expense -- a 28% increase from FY24's A$132M. The December 2025 refinancing (A$1.07B) improved this from a previously guided 8%, saving an estimated A$75M through 2030.

At 6.5% all-in cost on A$2B, annual interest is ~A$130M. EBITDA of ~A$294M leaves ~A$164M before tax and maintenance capex. After A$45M tax and A$15M maintenance capex, distributable cash is ~A$104M or A$0.21/share -- barely covering the current distribution rate.

## Capital Allocation

NECAP program: A$430M of committed non-expansionary capex expected to add A$0.62/tonne to TIC by 2027. This is funded through the regulated asset base (i.e., debt-funded, recovered through higher charges). It is accretive to FFO but adds to leverage.

8X expansion (capacity increase beyond 84.2Mtpa) is delayed, with customer engagement pushed to H1 2026. Given met coal demand uncertainty, this may never proceed -- which paradoxically is fine, since the current contracts are take-or-pay regardless.

Buybacks: negligible. Net issuance: flat at ~496M shares. No dilution concern.

## Valuation Framework

At A$5.31, market cap is ~A$2,633M. EV = ~A$4,579M.

| Metric | Value | Implied |
|---|---|---|
| EV/EBITDA (FY25) | 15.6x | Rich for coal infrastructure |
| P/FFO | 15.2x | Reasonable for regulated infra |
| FCF Yield | 6.0% | Adequate |
| Distribution Yield | 4.6% | Below infra peers |
| P/E (normalized) | 30.6x | Expensive |

**What must be true at A$5.31:** Met coal demand must sustain 84Mtpa+ throughput beyond 2028 contract renewal. TIC must continue escalating with CPI. Interest costs must stay manageable post-step-up. No regulatory resets to the lighter-handed framework.

**What breaks:** If contracts are not renewed at current volumes post-2028, the take-or-pay floor drops and debt serviceability deteriorates rapidly. A 20% volume decline would cut EBITDA by ~A$60M, making the debt stack unserviceable at current levels.

Comparable EV/EBITDA for regulated Australian infrastructure (Aurizon, Atlas Arteria) is 10-13x. DBI trades at a premium despite higher leverage and coal exposure.

## Key Financial Drivers

1. **Interest costs post-mid-2026**: The step-up from 4.63% to 6.5% compresses equity returns. Every 100bps on A$2B = A$20M pre-tax. This is the swing variable for distributions.

2. **TIC escalation / NECAP recovery**: CPI-linked TIC growth plus the A$0.62/tonne NECAP uplift by 2027 are the only organic earnings drivers. No volume growth.

3. **Contract renewal post-2028**: 100% take-or-pay at 84.2Mtpa expires June 2028. Re-contracting risk is existential for the equity at 7x leverage.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**The interest step-up math is worse than it appears.** Consensus focuses on the A$75M savings from the December refinancing, framing it as positive. But the baseline comparison is wrong -- the old guidance of 8% was a worst case. The real story is that DBI goes from ~A$95M of annual interest (at 4.63% on ~A$2B) to ~A$130M (at 6.5%) starting mid-2026. That A$35M increase flows directly through to distributable cash. At a 70% payout ratio, it implies distributions could be flat to down in FY27 even as EBITDA grows, unless NECAP TIC uplift fully offsets. The market is pricing 3-7% distribution growth; the math suggests the next 18 months will be a squeeze.

Additionally, at 15.6x EV/EBITDA, the market is pricing DBI as perpetual infrastructure, ignoring the terminal risk of a coal-dependent asset in a decarbonizing world. The 50-year lease runs to 2051, but economic life may be shorter.

**Judgment: Fully priced.** The yield is adequate but not compelling given leverage, single-asset concentration, coal transition risk, and an imminent interest cost step-up. A re-rating to 12x EV/EBITDA (still generous) implies ~A$3.53B EV or ~A$3.20/share equity value.
