# Coal Export Terminal Infrastructure -- Queensland, Australia

**Date:** 2026-02-23

---

## Industry Economics: What Drives Profitability

This is a regulated toll-road business, not a commodity business. Terminal operators do not take coal price risk directly. Profitability is driven by: (1) regulated tariff levels set by the QCA, (2) contracted capacity utilization under take-or-pay agreements, and (3) the volume of coal flowing through the Bowen Basin supply chain. The TIC (terminal infrastructure charge) is inflation-linked and set via a regulatory access undertaking reviewed every five years (current AU expires July 2031). At A$3.72/t for TY-25/26, the tariff is a trivial share of coal value (met coal at ~US$200/t), meaning terminal pricing is not a meaningful cost for miners -- a structural advantage for the terminal operator.

**Fact:** Dalrymple Bay Terminal is fully contracted at 84.2 Mtpa on 100% take-or-pay through June 2028 with evergreen renewal options. Even under force majeure, users must pay.

**Fact:** BHP's Hay Point Coal Terminal (55 Mtpa) sits adjacent but is a captive BMA facility -- it does not compete for third-party throughput. This makes DBT effectively a natural monopoly for multi-user met coal export from the central Bowen Basin.

## Industry Structure

Queensland exported ~200 Mt of coal in FY2024. The terminal infrastructure is a tight oligopoly:

- **Dalrymple Bay Terminal (DBT):** 85 Mtpa nameplate, multi-user, QCA-regulated
- **Hay Point Coal Terminal (BHP):** 55 Mtpa, captive single-user
- **North Queensland Export Terminal (NQXT/Abbot Point):** 50 Mtpa, approved expansion to 60 Mtpa
- **RG Tanna/WICET (Gladstone):** Serves southern Bowen Basin

Rail infrastructure is the binding constraint. The Goonyella rail system feeding Hay Point requires substantial capex to expand capacity, creating a hard physical bottleneck that protects incumbent terminal operators from competitive bypass. New terminal construction faces extreme permitting barriers -- no greenfield coal export terminal has been built in Queensland in over a decade.

**Judgment:** The competitive structure is about as favorable as any infrastructure industry gets. Physical bottlenecks, regulatory barriers, and take-or-pay contracting create near-zero competitive threat over any realistic planning horizon.

## Cyclicality Assessment: LOW (with a critical caveat)

The terminal business is structurally low-cyclicality due to take-or-pay contracts and regulated pricing. Revenue volatility is minimal across coal price cycles -- miners pay the TIC regardless of whether they ship coal. The inflation linkage provides real revenue growth independent of coal market conditions.

However, the **secular risk is real and non-trivial.** The relevant cycle is not coal pricing but coal production volume in the Bowen Basin over a 10-30 year horizon.

## Current Cycle Position: MID-CYCLE (Stable)

Terminal utilization is effectively at capacity. Australian met coal exports are forecast at 147-152 Mt for FY2025-26, with volumes expected to rise modestly before potential decline toward 2030 (per S&P Global). India is offsetting weakening Chinese demand. The terminal is not experiencing cyclical stress.

**Confidence: High** for the next 3-5 years. Contracts run through 2028 with evergreen renewals. Mid-term visibility is unusually good for any infrastructure asset.

**Confidence: Low** beyond 2030. Structural met coal demand trajectory is genuinely uncertain.

## Mid-Cycle Reference Point

"Normal" for this industry means: full take-or-pay contracting, ~80-85% physical throughput vs. nameplate capacity, TIC escalating at or slightly above CPI, and EBITDA margins in the 35-40% range (DBI reported A$280M EBITDA on A$784M revenue in FY2024, ~36% margin). Mid-cycle returns are set by the QCA's regulated return framework, not by market forces.

## The One Industry Factor That Matters

**Bowen Basin mine production sustainability.** Not coal prices. Not terminal capacity. Not tariff rates. The single question is: will the Bowen Basin continue producing 150+ Mt of met coal annually over the terminal's remaining useful life?

Signals to monitor:
- BHP has paused Goonyella Riverside and Peak Downs expansion plans and cut 750 Queensland coal jobs in late 2025
- Bowen Coking Coal entered voluntary administration; Bluff and Broadmeadow East mines on care-and-maintenance
- No major new Bowen Basin met coal mine has commenced development recently
- Australia's met coal production is forecast to rise through ~2027-28 before a potential structural decline

**Judgment:** The upstream mine investment cycle is turning negative. This is not yet reflected in terminal economics due to take-or-pay protection, but it is the leading indicator that matters over a 5-10 year horizon.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**Consensus view:** DBI is a safe, inflation-linked yield play insulated from coal price volatility. The bull case emphasizes take-or-pay contracts, monopoly position, and potential 8X expansion to 99.1 Mtpa.

**What consensus may underestimate -- BEARISH:** The QCA-mandated remediation provision has been set at A$850M (a 96% increase from the 2017 determination of A$433M, per IEEFA). Port Waratah Coal Terminal, a comparable facility, carries only A$101M in remediation provisions. This is an enormous embedded liability that grows with inflation and could consume significant terminal value at end-of-life. The expansion to 99.1 Mtpa is being pursued at exactly the moment upstream mine investment is contracting -- there is a real scenario where expanded capacity goes underutilized.

**What consensus may underestimate -- BULLISH:** Met coal is structurally different from thermal coal. There is no scalable substitute for coking coal in blast furnace steelmaking. India's steel production growth provides a demand floor that offsets Chinese decline. The terminal's take-or-pay structure means it gets paid even in scenarios where physical throughput declines 20-30%. The asset may be more durable than the "stranded asset" narrative suggests.
