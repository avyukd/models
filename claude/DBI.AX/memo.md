---

## Key Drivers (The 3 Things That Matter)

1. **Interest cost step-up mid-2026.** Weighted average cost moves from ~4.63% to ~6.5% as swaps roll off. On A$2B of debt, that is ~A$35M of incremental annual interest -- a direct hit to distributable cash that the market has not fully priced.

2. **Contract renewal post-June 2028.** 100% take-or-pay at 84.2Mtpa expires. Re-contracting risk is the existential variable for equity holders sitting under 7x net debt/EBITDA.

3. **TIC escalation + NECAP recovery.** CPI-linked tariff growth plus A$0.62/t NECAP uplift by 2027 are the only organic earnings drivers. No volume growth exists, and the NECAP mechanism is maintenance capex relabeled as growth through regulatory accounting.

---

## Thesis: Why We Pass

- DBI is a regulated monopoly toll road on met coal exports with strong near-term contractual protection -- but the stock is priced as perpetual infrastructure at 15.6x EV/EBITDA while comparable regulated Australian infra (Aurizon, Atlas Arteria) trades at 10-13x.
- The interest step-up compresses distributable cash in FY27. Consensus expects 3-7% distribution growth; the math suggests flat to down unless NECAP tariff uplift fully offsets, which is uncertain.
- At 7x net debt/EBITDA on a single coal-exposed asset, the equity is a leveraged bet on met coal demand sustaining 84Mtpa+ throughput beyond 2028. The margin of safety is thin.
- The A$850M remediation provision (96% increase from 2017 determination) is an embedded tail liability that grows with inflation and receives almost no market attention.
- No clear variant perception exists that would give us an edge. The bull case (met coal demand persists, toll road structure insulates) is consensus. The bear case (stranded asset) is also consensus. Neither side is obviously mispriced enough to trade.

## The Setup

DBI owns a 99-year lease on the world's largest met coal export terminal (85Mtpa nameplate). It charges a regulated per-tonne toll (TIC) set by the QCA on a RAB-return model. 100% take-or-pay through June 2028. No commodity price exposure. No competitive bypass risk -- rail infrastructure physically locks Bowen Basin producers into Hay Point port.

The business quality is exceptional. Geographic monopoly, structural customer lock-in, inflation-linked pricing, regulatory certainty through 2031. As a business, this is as good as infrastructure gets.

The problem is the price and the capital structure, not the business.

## Numbers That Matter

- **Normalized FFO (FY25):** A$173M, or ~A$0.35/share
- **Post-step-up distributable cash (FY27E):** ~A$104M or ~A$0.21/share (after A$130M interest, A$45M tax, A$15M maintenance)
- **Current distribution:** A$0.264/share (guided TY-25/26), implying ~A$131M total -- this exceeds our FY27 distributable cash estimate
- **EV/EBITDA:** 15.6x (peers at 10-13x)
- **P/FFO:** 15.2x (reasonable in isolation)
- **FCF yield:** 6.0% (adequate but not compelling given risk profile)
- **Net Debt/EBITDA:** 7.0x (aggressive for single-asset coal infra)

**What must be true at A$5.31:** Met coal demand sustains 84Mtpa+ beyond 2028, TIC escalates with CPI, interest costs stabilize post-step-up, no regulatory reset. At a more defensible 12x EV/EBITDA, equity value is ~A$3.20/share -- 40% below current price.

## Forward Path and Catalysts

**Near-term (0-12 months):** Interest step-up begins mid-2026. If H2 2026 interest expense prints higher than consensus models, distribution growth narrative breaks. FY25 results (released recently) showed resilience; the test comes in FY26-27.

**Medium-term (12-24 months):** Contract renewal discussions for post-June 2028 capacity become visible. Any signal of non-renewal or reduced contracted volumes would reprice the equity materially given 7x leverage.

**Longer-term:** 8X expansion decision (capacity to 99.1Mtpa) is the upside optionality, but it requires customer commitment at exactly the moment upstream mine investment is contracting. BHP pausing expansions and cutting jobs is a leading indicator.

---

## Risks and Kill Switches

**What invalidates the pass (would make us reconsider as a Buy):**
- Stock falls to A$3.50-4.00 range (10-12x EV/EBITDA), creating genuine yield margin of safety
- Interest costs refinanced lower than 6.5% assumption
- Contract renewals announced early at or above current volumes

**What would make this a Short:**
- Mine closures accelerate beyond current pace
- QCA regulatory reset reduces allowed returns
- Contract non-renewals post-2028

**Permanent capital loss scenario:** Accelerated steel decarbonization + Bowen Basin mine closures reduce throughput below debt service capacity. At 7x leverage, a 20% EBITDA decline (~A$60M) makes the debt stack unserviceable.

---

## Positioning and Portfolio Logic

**No position.** The business is excellent but the price assumes perpetuity on a coal-dependent asset with aggressive leverage. The near-term interest step-up creates a catalyst for disappointment, but the magnitude (~A$35M) is not large enough to drive a material de-rating on its own. The real risk is post-2028 contract renewal, which is too far out to size a position around today.

**What moves this out of the too-hard pile:**
- Price correction to A$3.50-4.00 (yield above 6.5% on post-step-up distributable cash)
- Clarity on contract renewals beyond June 2028
- Evidence that Bowen Basin mine investment is stabilizing rather than contracting

This is a good business at a full price with structural headwinds on the horizon. The right trade is patience.
