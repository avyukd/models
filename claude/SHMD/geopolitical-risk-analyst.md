# SCHMID Group (SHMD) - Geopolitical Risk Assessment

## Applicability: MATERIAL

SCHMID operates in PCB/semiconductor capital equipment with:
- Significant Asia/China revenue exposure (majority of sales historically)
- Manufacturing facilities in China (Zhongshan, Guangdong)
- Customer base concentrated in Taiwan, China, Korea
- Products serving advanced packaging/AI server PCB applications
- Direct exposure to US-China semiconductor trade tensions

---

## 1. Geographic Exposure Map

**Revenue Geography (Estimated):**
- Asia (incl. China, Taiwan, Korea): 60-70% historically
- Europe: 20-25%
- Americas: 5-10%

**Manufacturing Footprint:**
- Germany (Freudenstadt): HQ, primary R&D, production
- China (Zhongshan, Guangdong): Local production for Asian customers
- Smaller facilities: Turkey, USA

**Key Customer Geographies:**
- China: PCB manufacturers, electronics interconnect companies
- Taiwan: Major PCB/IC substrate producers
- Korea/Japan: Through partners and direct sales
- Europe: Standard PCB producers

**Supply Chain:**
- German engineering and critical components
- Local assembly/customization in China
- Dependent on global semiconductor supply chain for components

---

## 2. Sovereign & Regime Risk Assessment

**China:**
- Authoritarian regime with increasing state direction of economy
- History of technology transfer pressure on foreign companies
- Growing hostility to Western technology restrictions
- Local manufacturing presence creates operational leverage for Beijing
- Risk: Moderate-High. China facility could face pressure or be weaponized in trade disputes

**Taiwan:**
- Stable democracy but existential conflict risk with China
- Key customer geography for IC substrate/advanced PCB
- Risk: Low operationally, High geopolitically (Taiwan Strait scenario)

**Germany:**
- Stable rule of law, predictable regulatory environment
- Increasing pressure from US on semiconductor export controls
- Risk: Low for direct operations

---

## 3. Regulatory & State Intervention Risk

**US Export Controls (Primary Risk):**
- BIS has progressively expanded semiconductor manufacturing equipment (SME) controls
- December 2024 rules added 24 new SME categories, expanded FDPR
- Advanced packaging equipment now controlled for China-destined sales
- SCHMID's InfinityLine products for mSAP/PLP serve advanced packaging applications
- **Key question**: Does SCHMID equipment fall under controlled ECCNs?

**Current Assessment:**
- PCB wet processing equipment (SCHMID's core) is NOT currently on controlled lists
- However, as equipment moves toward advanced packaging (chiplet integration, HBM), control scope may expand
- BIS explicitly added "ECAD software for advanced semiconductor packaging involving multiple chips or chiplets"
- Back-end packaging that "does not alter IC technology level" currently excluded

**German Export Controls:**
- Germany has delayed/scrutinized semiconductor equipment exports to China (e.g., SUSS MicroTec delays in 2023)
- SCHMID not publicly identified as a "chokepoint" firm, but regulatory climate is tightening
- Risk: Moderate and increasing

**China Regulatory Risk:**
- Foreign equipment suppliers could face licensing/certification hurdles
- Potential preference for domestic suppliers under "dual circulation" policy
- Risk: Moderate

---

## 4. Conflict & Sanctions Exposure

**US-China Trade Conflict:**
- SCHMID explicitly cited "trade conflict between USA and China" as 2024 revenue headwind
- Customer investment hesitancy directly tied to policy uncertainty
- Not a sanctions target itself, but customer base includes Entity List-adjacent firms

**Taiwan Strait:**
- Severe tail risk for any company with significant Taiwan/China exposure
- Military conflict would devastate SCHMID's customer base and likely strand China assets
- SCHMID has customers in both Taiwan and China - exposed to both sides of any escalation

**Sanctions Risk Assessment:**
- Primary sanctions: Low (German company, no known restricted activities)
- Secondary sanctions: Moderate (sales to Chinese defense/surveillance-adjacent customers could trigger scrutiny)
- Customer Entity List risk: Elevated (Chinese PCB manufacturers are in proximity to controlled sectors)

---

## 5. Currency & Capital Controls

**Euro/RMB Exposure:**
- China revenues denominated in RMB or USD
- Euro reporting creates translation volatility
- China has capital controls but they're not typically binding for trade receivables

**Repatriation Risk:**
- China profits could be trapped if relations deteriorate severely
- Current risk: Low, but tail risk exists

**Recent Impact:**
- RMB weakness has affected Chinese customer purchasing power
- Contributes to demand softness in 2024-2025

---

## 6. Scenario & Tail Risk Framing

**Base Case: Status Quo**
- Trade tensions remain elevated but manageable
- SCHMID continues to sell to Chinese customers with delays and friction
- Gradual recovery in orders as customers adjust to new normal
- Revenue volatility but no structural impairment

**Adverse Case: Export Control Expansion**
- BIS expands SME controls to include wet processing equipment for advanced packaging
- German authorities align with US restrictions, require export licenses
- SCHMID loses ability to ship advanced equipment to China without licenses
- Revenue impact: 20-40% decline in accessible market
- Partial mitigation through Southeast Asia and Europe pivot

**Severe Tail Case: Taiwan Conflict or Comprehensive Decoupling**
- Military conflict in Taiwan Strait or comprehensive US-China decoupling
- SCHMID's China facility potentially seized or stranded
- Taiwan customer base paralyzed
- Write-off of China assets, loss of 50%+ of addressable market
- Potential permanent impairment of business model

---

## 7. Mitigants & Offsets

**Strengths:**
- Headquarters and primary R&D in Germany (protected jurisdiction)
- Not currently a controlled technology supplier (unlike lithography)
- Customer diversification across Asia, Europe, Americas
- Recent orders from Southeast Asia suggest geographic optionality
- AI/advanced packaging growth benefits from friend-shoring trends

**Weaknesses:**
- Small company with limited political leverage
- China facility creates operational exposure
- Customer base heavily concentrated in geopolitically sensitive regions
- No disclosed BIT protections or arbitration clauses for China operations

**Strategic Options:**
- Could accelerate non-China capacity if controls tighten
- Southeast Asia (Malaysia, Taiwan allies) offers partial refuge
- US/Europe expansion possible but market is smaller

---

## The One Geopolitical Risk That Matters

**Export control expansion to include PCB/advanced packaging wet processing equipment.**

If BIS determines that SCHMID's InfinityLine equipment for mSAP and panel-level packaging is "semiconductor manufacturing equipment" under the expanded definitions, and extends controls accordingly:
- SCHMID would require licenses for China sales
- Licenses would likely be denied for advanced applications (AI server PCBs)
- This would structurally impair the company's largest market and highest-growth segment
- German alignment with US controls (already demonstrated with SUSS MicroTec) makes this plausible

**Trigger:** BIS rulemaking expanding SME definitions to include advanced PCB manufacturing equipment serving semiconductor packaging applications.

**Impact:** Loss of 30-50% of addressable market. Potential permanent de-rating.

---

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**The market may be overpricing China risk in the near term, but underpricing escalation optionality.**

Current observations:
1. SCHMID trades at distressed multiples, partly reflecting China demand weakness
2. The trade war impact is visible and discussed - this is likely priced
3. However, SCHMID's equipment is NOT currently controlled - it can still sell to China
4. Recent November 2025 orders from China for AI server PCB equipment confirm access

**The non-consensus insight:**
- If the AI/advanced packaging cycle materializes as expected, SCHMID benefits disproportionately
- The company is positioned in the PCB value chain that sits just below the control threshold
- This creates a "best of both worlds" optionality: serving China's AI infrastructure buildout while not being captured by US restrictions
- However, this optionality is fragile - any BIS expansion that captures wet processing would eliminate it

**Net assessment:** Market may be pricing in demand weakness but not fully appreciating either the upside (continued access) or the tail risk (control expansion). The risk is non-linear, not gradual.

---

## Final Classification

**Geopolitical Risk Level:** High

**Primary Risk Vector:** Export control expansion; secondary: customer demand volatility from trade tensions

**Tail Risk Severity:** Material to Existential (depending on control scope expansion and conflict scenarios)

**Key Monitoring Signals:**
1. BIS rulemaking on semiconductor manufacturing equipment definitions
2. German export control decisions on peer companies (SUSS MicroTec, others)
3. Entity List additions of Chinese PCB/IC substrate manufacturers
4. SCHMID's own geographic revenue mix and order commentary
5. Taiwan Strait military activity indicators
