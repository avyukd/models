# Flughafen Zurich AG (FHZN) - Management & Capital Allocation Analysis

## Management Overview (Factual)

**CEO**: Lukas Brosi (since May 2023)
- Former CFO from 2017-2023
- Total compensation: CHF 1.1 million (2024), with CHF 420,000 fixed salary
- Personal shareholding: CHF 637,000 worth of stock
- Promoted internally after Stephan Widrig departed to become CEO of Allreal Holding AG

**Board Chairman**: Josef Felber

**Key Ownership Structure**:
- Canton of Zurich: 33.33% + 1 share (legally required to hold minimum one-third at all times)
- City of Zurich: 5%
- Lazard Asset Management: ~5%
- Retail/Individual investors: ~34%
- Insider ownership (management + board): <1%

**Governance Notes**:
- Swiss semi-public joint-stock company
- 5% voting cap for most shareholders (Canton exempt up to 49%, City up to 10%)
- No shareholder agreements disclosed
- No bonus or participation programs for Board members

## Incentive Structure & Alignment

### Compensation Mix

**CEO (Lukas Brosi)**:
- Fixed salary: CHF 420,000 (~38% of total)
- Variable (cash + equity): 100% of fixed salary at target = CHF 420,000
- Total: CHF 1.1 million (including benefits/social contributions)
- Equity portion: One-third of variable paid in shares, blocked 4 years

**Management Board**:
- Target bonus: 100% of fixed salary for CEO, 50% for other executives
- Performance floor: Below 70% achievement = zero bonus
- Performance cap: 150% of target bonus at maximum
- 2024 achievement: 100% (via Board discretion, citing "very good performance")

### Performance Metrics

**Through 2024**:
- Primary metric: EBIT vs. financial plan (excluding aircraft noise)
- Single financial metric = growth-oriented, not return-focused

**Starting 2025 (New Scheme)**:
- One financial target + three qualitative targets
- Five dimensions: Business Development, Economics, Innovation/Efficiency, Quality/Customer Experience, ESG
- Specific KPIs: ASQ score >4, MSCI ESG rating target of AA

### What Behavior Is This Compensation Encouraging?

**Positive signals**:
- 4-year share blocking period creates medium-term alignment
- Moderate total pay (CEO pay ratio 9.8x median employee)
- No severance or joining bonuses paid in 2024
- Board receives no performance-linked pay (reduces empire-building incentive at governance level)

**Concerns**:
- EBIT target (not ROIC) encourages growth over returns
- International expansion capex not penalized in bonus structure
- Board used discretionary override in 2024 to set 100% achievement despite impairment losses in international segment
- New qualitative targets for 2025 add subjectivity

**Who benefits if stock underperforms?**
- Management still receives 38%+ in fixed salary and benefits
- Canton of Zurich's stake is statutory (cannot sell below 33%), creating passive shareholder dynamic
- Low insider ownership (<1%) means limited wealth destruction for executives in downside scenarios

## Stock-Based Compensation Discipline

### SBC Analysis

- Share count: 30.70 million shares (stable, -0.01% YoY)
- No formal buyback program identified
- No material dilution from options (no options held by management or board)
- Diluted EPS (TTM): CHF 10.94

**Assessment**: SBC is modest and well-controlled. One-third of variable compensation paid in blocked shares creates alignment without excessive dilution. This is not a Silicon Valley-style SBC problem. Share count has been essentially flat for years.

**Dilution Risk Rating**: LOW

## Capital Allocation Track Record

### a) Reinvestment

**Zurich Airport (Core)**:
- Since 2000: Average CHF 1 million per working day invested
- ROIC target: >7.5% for the Group
- Current ROIC: 7.4% (2024 TTM), above WACC of ~4.4%
- Core airport generates high-single-digit returns, acceptable for regulated infrastructure

**Assessment**: Core reinvestment appears disciplined with returns meeting hurdles. The regulated Swiss asset generates predictable cash flows.

### b) International Expansion (The Critical Question)

**Total Committed Capital (Estimated)**:
- Brazil (Florianopolis, Vitoria, Macae, Natal, 25% Confins): ~CHF 200+ million
- Chile (Iquique, Antofagasta): ~CHF 100+ million
- India (Noida): ~CHF 750 million
- **Total International: ~CHF 1.0-1.1 billion**

**What Has International Expansion Actually Delivered?**

| Metric | 2024 |
|--------|------|
| International Segment Revenue | CHF 130.9 million |
| Revenue ex-construction accounting | CHF 100.9 million |
| YoY Growth | +31% (ex-construction) |
| Impairment Loss (Iquique) | CHF 7.8 million |
| International Passengers (LatAm) | 14 million |

**Returns Analysis**:
- CHF ~100 million revenue on CHF 1+ billion invested capital
- Implied revenue yield: <10% on invested capital
- EBITDA contribution: "High single-digit CHF millions" expected when Brazil was acquired (2019)
- Noida (the largest investment): Expected EBITDA-neutral in first year of operations

**The Honest Assessment**:

The international segment is generating revenue growth but returns are opaque. Management does not disclose segment-level EBIT or ROIC for international operations. What we know:
1. Chile (Iquique) already took a CHF 7.8 million impairment due to cost overruns and delayed commissioning
2. Noida has been delayed multiple times, with opening pushed from late 2024 to Q2 2025
3. Noida will be "EBITDA-neutral" in year one despite CHF 750 million invested
4. Management guides for "high single-digit EBITDA" contributions from Brazil years after acquisition

**Red Flags**:
- No published ROIC for international segment specifically
- Impairments already occurring (Chile)
- Construction delays (India, Chile)
- Penalty clauses at Noida (CHF 300k/month) passed to contractor, suggesting tight margins
- 40-year concession in India means returns spread over very long horizon

### c) Buybacks & Issuance

- No share buyback program
- Share count essentially flat
- No dilutive equity raises
- Dividend suspended 2020-2022 (COVID), resumed 2023

**Assessment**: Neutral. No buybacks, but no dilution either. Capital returned via dividends (current yield ~2.4%).

### d) Debt Usage

- Invested capital: ~CHF 4.3 billion (2020 post-expansion)
- Conservative leverage for regulated infrastructure
- Maintained investment-grade profile through COVID
- Suspended dividends rather than issuing equity during crisis

**Assessment**: Prudent. Behaved like a conservative infrastructure company during stress.

## Learning vs. Repetition

**Evidence of Learning**:
- COVID response: Suspended dividends, cut costs, didn't issue dilutive equity
- Noida delays: Penalties contractually passed to Tata Projects (smart structuring)
- Maintained capex within CHF 750 million budget despite delays

**Evidence of Repetition**:
- Chile impairment after construction delays/cost overruns
- Continued international expansion (Natal acquired 2024) despite mixed results from earlier deals
- Still not disclosing international segment ROIC after years of investment
- Management guides for "neutral" or "high single-digit" EBITDA from multi-hundred-million investments

**Verdict**: Mixed. Management shows operational competence but has not demonstrated ability to generate attractive returns on international capital. The pattern is: acquire concession, invest significant capital, face delays/cost overruns, eventually generate modest EBITDA, never disclose actual returns.

## Forward-Looking Capital Allocation Risk

### Where Capital Is Going (2025)
- Zurich: CHF 300-350 million
- International (primarily Noida completion): CHF 300 million
- **Total: CHF 600-650 million**

### Most Likely Sources of Value Destruction

1. **Noida ramp-up slower than expected**: Management already tempered expectations from "CHF 10 million EBITDA" to "neutral." If Delhi traffic growth disappoints or competition intensifies, this CHF 750 million asset may struggle to earn its cost of capital for years.

2. **Additional Latin American deals**: With operational experience in Brazil, management may be tempted to bid on more concessions. Historical pattern suggests they will pay up for growth without ROIC discipline.

3. **Opacity on international returns**: As long as management doesn't report segment-level ROIC, shareholders cannot hold them accountable for international capital allocation decisions.

4. **Canton of Zurich passivity**: With 33% locked in a statutory holder that cannot sell, management faces reduced market discipline.

---

## The One Thing That Determines Value Creation

**Single Dominant Factor**: Whether Noida generates acceptable returns on CHF 750 million invested.

Noida represents ~70% of cumulative international capex and ~15% of the company's market capitalization. If Noida becomes a profitable, growing asset earning 7%+ ROIC, international expansion will be vindicated. If Noida struggles with competition from Delhi's existing airports, slower-than-expected traffic, or operational issues, this will be a value-destructive investment that absorbed capital that could have been returned to shareholders.

Everything else is secondary. Brazil is generating modest positive contributions. Chile has already impaired. Zurich core is stable and profitable. Noida is the bet.

---

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**The Non-Consensus Insight**: The market likely believes "Swiss airport operator = conservative, disciplined capital allocation."

**The Reality**: Flughafen Zurich has quietly deployed CHF 1+ billion into emerging market airport concessions without ever disclosing segment-level returns. The company has:
- Already written down CHF 7.8 million on Chile
- Delayed Noida opening multiple times
- Guided for "neutral" EBITDA in year one from a CHF 750 million investment
- Been acquiring new assets (Natal, 2024) while existing investments haven't proven out

**What a lazy analyst misses**:
1. **International segment ROIC is never disclosed** - you cannot verify whether international expansion creates value
2. **Impairments are buried** - CHF 7.8 million Iquique write-down noted but not emphasized
3. **Noida expectations have quietly deteriorated** - from "CHF 10 million EBITDA contribution" per analyst estimates to "neutral first year" per management
4. **Canton ownership creates governance gap** - 33% holder cannot sell regardless of capital allocation quality

**Does this make me more or less trusting of management?**
Less trusting. The lack of segment ROIC disclosure combined with already-occurring impairments and expectation downgrades suggests international expansion may be value-neutral at best. Management is operationally competent but has not demonstrated ability to generate attractive returns from international capital deployment.
