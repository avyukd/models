# Flughafen Zurich AG (FHZN) - Geopolitical & Sovereign Risk Assessment

## Applicability Check: MATERIAL

Flughafen Zurich operates airport concessions across three emerging markets (Brazil, India, Chile) with significant capital deployed and multi-decade operational commitments. International airport revenue reached CHF 130.9 million in 2024 (approximately 10% of group revenue), with the Noida greenfield project representing CHF 750 million in committed capital. Geopolitical exposure is unambiguously material and warrants full analysis.

## 2. Sovereign & Regime Risk Assessment

### Brazil
**Political Stability**: MODERATE
Brazil operates as a functioning democracy with periodic power transitions. The Lula administration (2023-present) represents a center-left government with stated support for PPP models over full privatization. No fundamental threats to democratic governance.

**Rule of Law**: MODERATE
Constitutional protections for foreign property exist (Article 5), but judicial enforcement is slow. The Natal airport case demonstrated that concession termination processes, while legally available, can take 3-4 years to resolve. Property rights are protected in principle but enforcement delays create operational risk.

**History of Intervention Against Foreign Capital**: LOW
Brazil has not expropriated foreign airport investments. The Natal termination was a negotiated "friendly exit" with compensation based on unamortized capex (R$610mm gross payment). No pattern of hostile action against airport concessionaires.

**Current Attitude Toward Foreign Investment**: FAVORABLE
Brazil attracted $66bn FDI in 2024 (5th globally). The government actively promotes airport concessions, with 50+ additional airports planned for privatization. 100% foreign ownership now permitted in airlines. Regulatory environment improving.

**Likelihood of Adverse Action**: LOW
No indicators of policy reversal. Risk is operational/economic (traffic shortfalls, currency) rather than political seizure.

### India
**Political Stability**: HIGH
BJP-led government under Modi with strong parliamentary majority. Uttar Pradesh under Yogi Adityanath is politically aligned with federal government. No regime change risk through 2029 elections.

**Rule of Law**: MODERATE
India operates under common law tradition with functioning courts. However, infrastructure disputes can be prolonged. The Celebi security clearance revocation (2024) demonstrates that government can use regulatory tools to favor domestic operators when desired.

**History of Intervention Against Foreign Capital**: MIXED
India has not nationalized airports but has shown preference for domestic operators (Adani Group). The alleged 50-year concession grants to Adani vs. statutory 30-year limits suggests regulatory flexibility for favored parties. Foreign operators operate successfully (GMR, Flughafen Zurich) but are not politically connected.

**Current Attitude Toward Foreign Investment**: FAVORABLE
100% FDI permitted in airports. Modi government actively promotes infrastructure PPPs. Recent corporate tax cuts for foreign companies (40% to 35% in 2024). However, declining interest from foreign airport investors noted due to regulatory complexity.

**Likelihood of Adverse Action**: LOW-MODERATE
Direct expropriation unlikely. Risk is regulatory asymmetry favoring domestic players and cost overrun exposure on greenfield projects.

### Chile
**Political Stability**: MODERATE-HIGH
Despite concerns about Boric-era policy shifts, Chile has maintained institutional stability. The 2023 constitutional referendum rejection demonstrated checks on radical reform. Mining sector concerns have not translated to infrastructure seizures.

**Rule of Law**: HIGH
Strongest legal framework in Latin America. Airport concession contracts enforced through established PPP framework under Ministry of Public Works.

**History of Intervention Against Foreign Capital**: VERY LOW
Chile has the best record in Latin America for honoring concession agreements. No airport expropriation history. Lithium nationalization (2023-2024) targeted natural resources, not infrastructure assets.

**Current Attitude Toward Foreign Investment**: FAVORABLE
Well-established PPP framework continues. Santiago airport expansion proceeding under private management. Airport concessions explicitly exempt from nationalization discourse.

**Likelihood of Adverse Action**: VERY LOW
Chile represents the lowest sovereign risk jurisdiction in FHZN's emerging market portfolio.

## 3. Regulatory & State Intervention Risk

### Brazil - Regulatory Risk Assessment

**Forced Divestment**: LOW
No mechanism or political will for forced divestment. Concession contracts include termination clauses with unamortized capex compensation.

**License Revocation**: LOW
ANAC operates as independent regulator. No pattern of arbitrary license revocations. Sustainability awards to FHZN airports (Florianopolis, Vitoria) suggest positive regulatory relationship.

**Regulatory Targeting**: LOW
Foreign operators treated equally. Recent liberalization allows 100% foreign airline ownership. No evidence of discriminatory treatment.

**Price Controls**: MODERATE
Airport fees regulated by ANAC (DAR 50 equivalent). Fee caps have historically constrained revenue upside during traffic shortfalls. However, FHZN secured fee increases in 2024.

**Primary Regulatory Risk**: Fee cap constraints during demand weakness, not political intervention.

### India - Regulatory Risk Assessment

**Forced Divestment**: VERY LOW
No precedent or legal mechanism for airport concession revocation without compensation.

**License Revocation**: LOW
Concession secured through competitive bid with Uttar Pradesh government. Political alignment between state (BJP) and center provides stability.

**Regulatory Targeting**: MODERATE
The Celebi case demonstrates willingness to revoke security clearances on national security grounds, potentially disadvantaging foreign operators. FHZN operates through 100% subsidiary (YIAPL) with local registration, mitigating some risk.

**Construction/Cost Risk**: HIGH
This is the primary regulatory risk vector. Noida has triggered CHF 21mm ($2.5mm) penalty for September 2024 deadline miss. Cost overruns absorbed by concessionaire. Supply chain constraints outside FHZN control but financial consequences rest with the company.

**Primary Regulatory Risk**: Greenfield execution risk and regulatory penalties, not concession security.

### Chile - Regulatory Risk Assessment

**Forced Divestment**: VERY LOW
No precedent in airport sector. Boric's nationalization agenda targets lithium/mining, not transport infrastructure.

**License Revocation**: VERY LOW
Well-established concession framework under Ministry of Public Works.

**Regulatory Targeting**: VERY LOW
Airport regulation (DGAC) operates independently of political interference.

**Environmental Permitting**: LOW-MODERATE
Boric administration tightening environmental requirements. Antofagasta/Iquique in water-scarce region (Atacama) but existing operations grandfathered.

**Primary Regulatory Risk**: Minimal. Chile represents clean regulatory environment.

## 5. Currency & Capital Controls

### Currency Exposure Summary

| Currency | Exposure | 10-Year Depreciation vs CHF | Volatility |
|----------|----------|---------------------------|------------|
| BRL | Revenue + Assets | -50% (approx.) | HIGH |
| INR | Future Revenue + Assets | -20% (approx.) | MODERATE |
| CLP | Revenue + Assets | -39% | MODERATE |

### Brazilian Real (BRL) Risk
**2024 Performance**: BRL depreciated 15.4% vs CHF
**Key Risk**: Macroeconomic instability is the PRIMARY driver of airport concession renegotiations in Brazil, per academic study covering 59 airports (2013-2023). Exchange rate volatility directly impairs CHF-denominated returns.

**Mechanism**: Revenue earned in BRL, costs partially in BRL, but parent reporting in CHF. Sustained depreciation erodes equity value of Brazilian assets. No natural hedge available - this is structural currency mismatch.

### Indian Rupee (INR) Risk
**2024-2025 Performance**: INR depreciated ~16% vs CHF
**Key Risk**: CHF 750mm investment made in hard currency; returns will be INR-denominated. Rupee weakness during initial operating years could materially impair project economics.

**Mitigant**: INR is more stable than BRL with lower volatility. RBI maintains managed float with large reserves. Capital controls are minimal.

### Chilean Peso (CLP) Risk
**2024-2025 Performance**: CLP depreciated ~5% vs CHF YTD
**Key Risk**: Copper price dependency creates commodity-linked volatility. Smaller absolute exposure limits impact.

**Mitigant**: Chile has most stable macro framework in Latin America. Central bank credibility high.

### Capital Repatriation Risk

**Brazil**: NO RESTRICTIONS
Law 14,286 (2021) modernized FX framework. All foreign investments registered with Central Bank. Repatriation tax (IOF-FX) reduced to 0% as of June 2025. No barriers to dividend upstreaming.

**India**: NO MATERIAL RESTRICTIONS
Automatic route for FDI in airports. Repatriation requires standard documentation but no approval barriers.

**Chile**: NO RESTRICTIONS
Fully convertible currency. No capital controls.

**Assessment**: Capital control risk is NOT a meaningful concern for FHZN's current portfolio.

## 6. Scenario & Tail Risk Framing

### Brazil Scenarios

**Base Case (70% probability band)**
Concessions operate as designed. Traffic grows with Brazilian GDP. BRL volatility creates earnings noise but not value destruction. International revenue contribution remains 8-12% of group.

**Adverse Case (25% probability band)**
Severe Brazilian recession (similar to 2015-2016). Traffic collapses 20-30%. Fee caps prevent revenue recovery. BRL depreciates another 30-40%. Concession economics impaired but compensated exits available under Brazilian law.

**Impact**: CHF 50-100mm impairment. Material but not existential given group scale.

**Severe Tail Case (5% probability band)**
Populist government comes to power with anti-foreign investment stance. Concession terms unilaterally modified. Arbitration required.

**Impact**: CHF 150-200mm at risk (full invested capital). Precedent suggests compensation would be paid, but recovery could take years.

### India (Noida) Scenarios

**Base Case (60% probability band)**
Airport opens late 2025. Ramp-up takes 3-5 years. Eventually reaches 12mm pax/year capacity. Contributes positively to EBITDA by 2028-2029. CHF 750mm investment generates acceptable returns.

**Adverse Case (30% probability band)**
Further construction delays. Cost overruns exceed CHF 100mm. Traffic ramp-up slower than projected as Delhi incumbents (IGI) compete aggressively. Project NPV significantly below base case. 10-year payback extends to 15+ years.

**Impact**: CHF 100-200mm NPV destruction. Not a write-off but disappointing returns.

**Severe Tail Case (10% probability band)**
Project failure. Delhi IGI dominance persists. Low-cost carriers bypass Noida. Political relationship deteriorates. Multi-hundred-million CHF impairment.

**Impact**: CHF 300-500mm value destruction. Would represent material hit to group value but not solvency threat.

### Chile Scenarios

**Base Case (85% probability band)**
Concessions operate to term. Modest traffic growth. Stable returns. Portfolio exits on schedule.

**Adverse Case (14% probability band)**
Copper price crash impairs Antofagasta/Iquique traffic (mining-dependent regions). Revenue shortfalls but concession structure intact.

**Impact**: CHF 20-40mm below expectations. Immaterial at group level.

**Severe Tail Case (1% probability band)**
Radical constitutional change enables infrastructure nationalization.

**Impact**: Extremely unlikely given institutional strength. Not a credible planning scenario.

## 7. Mitigants & Offsets

### Geographic Diversification
Zurich Airport (Switzerland) represents ~90% of revenue. Group can absorb total loss of any single international market without solvency risk. Diversification is the primary mitigant.

### Contractual Protections
- **Brazil**: 30-year concessions with termination compensation based on unamortized capex (demonstrated in Natal case: R$610mm gross payment)
- **Chile**: Established PPP framework with arbitration mechanisms
- **India**: 40-year concession with standard termination provisions

### Bilateral Investment Treaties (BITs)
- Switzerland-Brazil BIT: Provides investment protection and arbitration rights
- Switzerland-India BIT: Terminated in 2017 by India; FHZN investment made after termination - NO BIT PROTECTION
- Switzerland-Chile BIT: Provides investment protection

**Critical Note**: The absence of Swiss-India BIT coverage for the CHF 750mm Noida investment is a meaningful gap. Disputes would be resolved in Indian courts, not international arbitration. This is underappreciated by market participants.

### Political Leverage
- **Brazil**: FHZN airports recognized as sustainability leaders. Positive regulatory relationships. Not politically contentious.
- **India**: Noida project politically important to UP government and Modi administration as showcase infrastructure. Government unlikely to act adversely during construction/early operations.
- **Chile**: Low-profile operations in mining regions. No political salience.

### Exit Optionality
Airport concessions are marketable assets. FHZN could exit positions through sale to strategic buyers (other airport operators) if risk profile changes. Liquidity is imperfect but exits are feasible.

## The One Geopolitical Risk That Matters

**Indian Rupee Depreciation + Noida Execution Risk = The Thesis Killer**

The single geopolitical/sovereign factor that could permanently impair this investment is not expropriation, nationalization, or regulatory seizure. It is the combination of:

1. **Continued CHF 750mm deployment into a project with execution problems** (delays, penalties, cost overruns)
2. **INR depreciation eroding returns** when project finally generates cash
3. **No BIT protection** if disputes arise
4. **Greenfield risk** in a market where foreign airport operators have limited track record

The Brazil and Chile exposures are manageable - mature assets with established cash flows and tested exit mechanisms. India is the bet that could go wrong in ways that matter.

**What Would Trigger It**:
- Further multi-year delays pushing operations to 2026-2027
- Cost overruns exceeding CHF 100mm
- INR depreciation to 130+ per CHF (from current ~114)
- Traffic materially below projections due to IGI competition or economic slowdown

**Impact**: CHF 300-500mm NPV destruction, representing approximately 10-15% of current enterprise value.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

### The Non-Consensus Insight: India Risk is Underpriced, Brazil Risk is Overpriced

**What Lazy Analysis Assumes**:
"Brazil is risky, India is a growth opportunity"

**What the Evidence Shows**:

**Brazil Risk is OVERPRICED**:
- No expropriation history in airport sector
- Functional termination/compensation mechanisms proven (Natal case)
- BIT protection in place
- 30-year operating history with foreign concessionaires
- Currency is the real risk, not politics - and currency risk is visible and priceable

**India Risk is UNDERPRICED**:
- No Swiss-India BIT since 2017 - disputes go to local courts
- CHF 750mm greenfield commitment (largest single international bet) in a market with limited foreign operator track record
- Domestic operator preference demonstrated (Adani's rapid expansion, Celebi security clearance revocation)
- Project already delayed with penalties triggered
- Currency depreciation will compound execution problems

**The Market Error**:
Investors apply a "EM risk premium" to Brazil based on headlines while treating India as high-quality growth exposure. The actual risk profile is inverted:
- Brazil: Known risks, proven protections, manageable scale
- India: Larger commitment, weaker protections, execution in progress

**Implication**:
The market may be correctly pricing Brazil exposure but underweighting India execution and protection gaps. If Noida disappoints, it will matter more than any Brazilian outcome.

### Secondary Non-Consensus Point: Chile is Mispriced as "EM Risk"

Chile's geopolitical risk is closer to developed market than emerging market. Lumping FHZN's Chilean airports with "Latin American exposure" overstates sovereign risk. Chile has:
- Investment-grade sovereign credit
- Strongest rule of law in region
- No nationalization history in transport infrastructure
- Stable, well-designed PPP framework

Chile should be treated as "safe EM" rather than generic Latin American risk.

**Geopolitical Risk Level**: MODERATE

**Tail Risk Severity**: MATERIAL (India exposure could impair 10-15% of enterprise value in adverse scenario)

**Key Monitoring Signals**:
1. Noida construction timeline - further delays beyond Q4 2025 would signal deeper problems
2. INR/CHF exchange rate trajectory - sustained depreciation below 120 CHF/INR compounds project stress
3. India-Switzerland diplomatic relationship - any BIT renegotiation would be positive
4. Brazilian concession renegotiations - any adverse precedent affecting other operators
5. Traffic data from Florianopolis, Noida post-opening - operational performance indicators
