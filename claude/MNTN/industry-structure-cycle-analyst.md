# Connected TV (CTV) and Digital Advertising Industry Analysis

**Analysis Date:** January 2026
**Industry Scope:** Connected TV advertising, programmatic video, performance TV, ad-supported streaming
**Analytical Framework:** Industry economics, cycle position, structural dynamics

---

## Executive Summary

The CTV advertising industry sits at the intersection of two massive forces: the secular decline of linear TV and the maturation of digital advertising infrastructure. This creates an unusual situation where **genuine structural growth coexists with intensifying competition and deteriorating unit economics for many participants.**

**The core tension:** CTV advertising is undeniably growing, but the market has likely overestimated both the durability of premium pricing and the ability of independent ad tech players to capture value as supply expands and walled gardens strengthen.

---

## Industry Definition and Boundaries

### What CTV Advertising Actually Is

CTV (Connected TV) advertising refers to video ads delivered through internet-connected television devices—smart TVs, streaming sticks (Roku, Fire TV), gaming consoles—within streaming content. This includes:

- **AVOD (Ad-Supported Video on Demand):** Free ad-supported streaming (Tubi, Pluto TV, Freevee)
- **Hybrid SVOD:** Subscription services with ad tiers (Netflix, Disney+, Max, Peacock, Paramount+)
- **vMVPD:** Virtual cable bundles (YouTube TV, Hulu + Live TV, Sling)
- **FAST (Free Ad-Supported Streaming TV):** Linear-style channels on streaming platforms

### Adjacent But Distinct Markets

- **Linear TV advertising:** Traditional broadcast/cable; declining but still ~$60B+ annually in the US
- **Digital video (non-CTV):** YouTube, social video, in-feed video; different economics and measurement
- **Programmatic display:** Related infrastructure but different inventory characteristics

**Key distinction:** CTV is not simply "TV ads on streaming." It represents a fundamental shift in how TV advertising is bought, measured, and valued—from reach/frequency models to increasingly performance-based metrics.

## Industry Economics: The Value Chain

### Understanding Where Value Accrues

The CTV advertising value chain has distinct layers, each with different economics:

```
ADVERTISERS (Demand)
       |
       v
AGENCIES / TRADING DESKS (Demand Aggregation)
       |
       v
DSPs - Demand Side Platforms (Bid Execution)
       |
       v
AD EXCHANGES / SSPs (Inventory Aggregation)
       |
       v
PUBLISHERS / STREAMERS (Supply - Content + Audience)
       |
       v
CTV PLATFORMS (Distribution - Roku, Fire TV, etc.)
```

### Economics by Position

| Position | Gross Margins | Defensibility | Structural Trend |
|----------|---------------|---------------|------------------|
| Streamers/Publishers | 60-80% on ad revenue | HIGH (content/audience) | Improving as scale grows |
| CTV Platforms (Roku, etc.) | 45-55% on platform revenue | MODERATE (switching costs) | Pressure from OS consolidation |
| SSPs | 15-25% take rate | LOW (commoditizing) | Severe compression |
| DSPs | 10-20% take rate | LOW-MODERATE | Compression + consolidation |
| Independent Ad Tech | 10-30% | LOW | Existential pressure |

**Critical insight:** The "ad tech tax" (total fees between advertiser spend and publisher receipt) in programmatic CTV can reach 40-50%. This is unsustainable. The structural trend is toward **disintermediation**—publishers building direct sales capabilities and platforms capturing more of the stack.

### What Drives Industry Profitability

**At the industry level, CTV advertising profitability is determined by:**

1. **Supply/demand balance of premium video inventory** - The single most important factor
2. **Measurement/attribution capabilities** - Justifies premium CPMs vs. other digital
3. **Degree of fragmentation** - More fragmented = more value to aggregators; consolidating = value shifts to publishers
4. **Advertiser mix** - Brand vs. performance; DR advertisers demand measurable ROI
5. **Linear TV decline rate** - Faster decline = more budget shift = demand tailwind

## The Supply/Demand Framework: This Is What Actually Matters

### The Core Dynamic

**CTV advertising is a market where supply is growing faster than demand, but both are growing.** This is the single most important fact for understanding industry economics.

#### Supply Side (Inventory)
- **Accelerating:** Every major streamer has added or expanded ad tiers (Netflix: Nov 2022, Disney+: Dec 2022, etc.)
- **Ad-supported streaming hours** are growing 20-30%+ annually as of late 2024/early 2025
- FAST channels proliferating (lower quality but abundant inventory)
- **Implication:** Premium CPM pricing is under structural pressure as scarcity diminishes

#### Demand Side (Ad Budgets)
- Total US TV ad spending (linear + CTV) is roughly flat to slightly declining in aggregate
- CTV is capturing share from linear, but **not creating net new TV ad dollars at the rate supply suggests**
- Digital native advertisers (DTC brands, apps, etc.) adding incremental demand but not at premium CPMs
- Economic sensitivity: advertising is 0.5-1.0x GDP beta; recession = demand destruction

### The Math Problem Nobody Discusses

**Fact:** Linear TV advertising was ~$65-70B in the US at peak (2016-2018). It's now closer to $55-60B and declining.

**Fact:** CTV advertising was ~$25-30B in 2024 and projected to reach $40B+ by 2027 (per eMarketer/IAB estimates).

**Implication:** Even aggressive CTV growth doesn't fully replace linear TV ad revenue decline. The total TV advertising pie may be **shrinking** while CTV's share grows. This is not bullish for industry pricing power.

### CPM Trajectory: The Leading Indicator

| Period | CTV CPMs (Approximate Range) | Driver |
|--------|------------------------------|--------|
| 2018-2020 | $35-50+ | Scarcity premium, early adoption |
| 2021-2022 | $30-45 | Supply expansion begins |
| 2023-2024 | $20-35 | Netflix/Disney+ flood, FAST growth |
| 2025-2026 | $15-30 (estimate) | Maturation, performance pressure |

**Note:** These are illustrative ranges based on industry reports (IAB, eMarketer, trade publications). Actual CPMs vary significantly by inventory quality, targeting, and measurement.

**Key observation:** CTV CPMs are converging toward digital video norms, not staying at "TV premium" levels. This is a structural, not cyclical, phenomenon.

## Competitive Structure Analysis

### The Playing Field

**Walled Gardens (Consolidated Power):**
- **Amazon** - Fire TV + Freevee + Prime Video ads + DSP (full stack control)
- **Google/YouTube** - YouTube TV + YouTube CTV + DV360 (dominant in measurement)
- **Netflix** - Microsoft partnership but building in-house
- **Disney** - Hulu + Disney+ + ESPN+ (moving toward unified ad platform)

**Platform Layer (Moderate Concentration):**
- **Roku** - ~30% US CTV device share, owns platform and Roku Channel
- **Amazon Fire TV** - ~25-30% share, aggressive on advertising
- **Apple TV** - Premium but ad-limited; strategic wildcard
- **Smart TV OEMs** - Samsung, LG, Vizio building ad businesses

**Independent Ad Tech (Fragmented, Under Pressure):**
- DSPs: The Trade Desk (dominant independent), others struggling
- SSPs: Magnite, PubMatic, Freewheel (Comcast)
- Measurement: Nielsen, Comscore, iSpot, VideoAmp (in flux)
- Specialists: MNTN, Innovid, TVSquared (acquired), Tatari (acquired)

### Competitive Dynamics: Structural Forces

**1. Vertical Integration is Accelerating**

Major streamers are pulling ad sales in-house. Netflix built its own ad tech. Disney unified its ad platform. This is not cyclical—it's structural. As streaming matures, publishers want:
- Higher take rates (cut out intermediaries)
- First-party data control
- Direct advertiser relationships

**Implication for independent ad tech:** The addressable market is shrinking as premium inventory moves to walled gardens and direct deals.

**2. The Trade Desk Has Separated from the Pack**

Among independent DSPs, The Trade Desk has achieved escape velocity through:
- Scale advantages in data and inventory access
- CTV-first strategy executed early
- UID2 (identity solution) creating ecosystem lock-in

**This is relevant because:** The independent ad tech space is not "fragmented and consolidating"—it's **fragmenting into TTD and everyone else.** This raises the bar dramatically for smaller players.

**3. Measurement is the Unresolved Battleground**

CTV's promise is "TV reach with digital measurement." But measurement remains:
- Fragmented (multiple currencies, no standard)
- Contested (Nielsen vs. alternatives)
- Incomplete (attribution across devices/platforms is hard)

Whoever wins measurement wins the right to justify premium pricing. This battle is unresolved.

### Secular/Structural Trends (Durable)

| Trend | Direction | Confidence | Evidence |
|-------|-----------|------------|----------|
| Cord-cutting / streaming shift | Linear to CTV | HIGH | 10+ years of consistent data |
| Programmatic share of TV buying | Increasing | HIGH | Automation economics |
| CTV inventory supply | Increasing | HIGH | Every streamer adding ads |
| Ad tech disintermediation | Increasing | MODERATE-HIGH | Publisher behavior |
| Performance measurement demands | Increasing | HIGH | Advertiser sophistication |

### Cyclical Factors (Temporary)

| Factor | Current State | Reversion Expectation |
|--------|--------------|----------------------|
| Ad spending growth | Moderate (post-2022 reset) | Follows GDP + election cycles |
| CTV CPMs | Under pressure | May stabilize but not recover to 2020 levels |
| Streaming subscriber growth | Slowing | SVOD maturation is structural |
| Ad tech consolidation | Active (2023-2025) | Will slow after weak players exit |

### The Critical Distinction

**Consensus view:** "CTV is secularly growing, therefore participants will benefit."

**Reality:** CTV advertising volume is structurally growing, but value capture is shifting toward publishers and platforms, away from independent ad tech. **Growing industry =/= good economics for all participants.**

## Cyclicality Assessment

### Advertising Industry Cyclicality (Baseline)

Advertising is inherently cyclical:
- Typically 0.5-1.0x GDP sensitivity
- Recessions cause 10-20% ad spending contractions historically
- Recovery is usually sharp (V-shaped)
- Political/election years create demand spikes

### CTV-Specific Cyclical Patterns

CTV has experienced only one full economic cycle (COVID-2020 through 2024), making pattern identification preliminary:

**2020 (COVID Shock):** CTV was relatively resilient vs. linear TV (streaming hours surged)
**2021 (Recovery):** Explosive growth, possibly overstated by stimulus-driven DTC spending
**2022 (Tightening):** Growth moderated; ad tech valuations collapsed
**2023-2024 (Normalization):** Steady growth but at more sustainable rates

**Assessment:** CTV appears **MODERATE cyclicality**—less cyclical than linear TV (structural tailwind provides floor) but still economically sensitive. The 2022-2023 reset suggests CTV is not immune to macro conditions.

### Cyclicality Rating: MODERATE

**Rationale:**
- Structural shift from linear provides demand floor
- But not immune to advertising budget cyclicality
- Performance/DR segment more stable than brand
- Limited historical data creates uncertainty

## Mid-Cycle Economics: What "Normal" Looks Like

### Defining Mid-Cycle for CTV Advertising

**"Normal" for CTV advertising means:**

1. **CTV captures 35-45% of total TV ad spending** (vs. ~30% today, ~15% in 2020)

2. **CPMs stabilize in the $20-30 range** for premium inventory, with significant quality tiering

3. **Take rates compress to digital norms:** 15-25% total ad tech tax (vs. 40%+ historically)

4. **Consolidation complete:** 3-4 scaled independent ad tech players survive; rest absorbed or fail

5. **Measurement standardized:** One or two dominant currencies emerge

6. **Publisher direct sales dominant:** 60%+ of premium CTV bought direct or via publisher platforms

### Quantitative Mid-Cycle Benchmarks

| Metric | Peak (2020-2021) | Current (2025) | Mid-Cycle Normal |
|--------|------------------|----------------|------------------|
| CTV ad spend growth | 35-40%+ | 15-20% (est.) | 8-12% |
| Premium CTV CPMs | $40-50 | $25-35 | $20-30 |
| Ad tech take rates | 35-45% | 30-40% | 20-30% |
| Programmatic % of CTV | 40-50% | 55-65% | 70-80% |

**Note:** These are analytical estimates based on industry reports and directional trends. Actual figures vary by source and definition.

## The One Industry Factor That Matters

### Identifying the Dominant Variable

**The single most important variable for CTV advertising industry economics is: SUPPLY/DEMAND BALANCE OF PREMIUM VIDEO INVENTORY**

Everything else is secondary. Here's why:

1. **When supply is scarce:** CPMs are high, publishers have pricing power, ad tech can charge premium take rates, everyone makes money.

2. **When supply is abundant:** CPMs compress, publishers compete on price, ad tech becomes commoditized, only scaled players survive.

**We are transitioning from (1) to (2).** The Netflix and Disney+ ad tier launches in late 2022 marked the inflection point. The industry will never return to 2020-era scarcity.

### What Would Cause It to Inflect

**Bullish scenario (supply tightens):**
- Major streamer exits ad-supported model (unlikely)
- Significantly faster linear TV decline accelerates budget shift
- New privacy regulations restrict digital video more than CTV

**Bearish scenario (supply floods):**
- Apple launches aggressive ad-supported tier
- YouTube CTV inventory expands significantly
- FAST channel proliferation continues unchecked
- International CTV matures faster than expected

### How to Monitor

**Leading indicators:**
- Netflix/Disney+ ad-tier subscriber growth and engagement metrics
- FAST channel viewership trends (Tubi, Pluto ratings)
- CTV CPM indices from ad tech earnings calls
- Linear TV rating declines (faster = more CTV demand)
- Upfront/NewFront ad commitment levels

## What Consensus May Be Missing

### The Variant View

**Consensus belief:** "CTV is the future of TV advertising. Linear decline + streaming growth = massive TAM expansion for CTV advertising participants."

**What consensus gets wrong:**

### 1. The Total TV Ad Pie Is Likely Shrinking, Not Growing

Most CTV analysis assumes linear TV ad budgets will largely transfer to CTV. This ignores:

- Linear TV advertisers skew older, less digital-native; some budgets will simply disappear
- Performance measurement in CTV reveals waste that was hidden in linear; budgets adjust down
- Attention fragmentation means TV (all formats) competes with TikTok, gaming, etc.

**Implication:** CTV's share of TV advertising is growing, but the absolute addressable market may be smaller than the "linear + CTV = huge TAM" narrative suggests.

### 2. Ad Tech Disintermediation Is Underpriced

The market treats CTV ad tech as a growth sector. But:

- Every major publisher is building or has built direct ad sales capabilities
- Walled gardens (Amazon, Google) are expanding aggressively
- The Trade Desk's dominance leaves little oxygen for other independents

**Implication:** Independent ad tech (excluding TTD) faces existential pressure, not just growth moderation. The investable universe in "CTV ad tech" is shrinking.

### 3. The "Performance TV" Promise Has Structural Limits

CTV's pitch is "TV with digital measurement." But:

- Cross-device attribution remains imperfect
- Privacy changes (iOS, cookie deprecation) affect CTV measurement too
- "Performance TV" ROI is often worse than social/search for direct response
- Brand advertisers want reach, not attribution

**Implication:** CTV will capture TV brand budgets but may struggle to win incremental performance dollars from Google/Meta. The market likely overestimates performance TV's addressable market.

### Bullish or Bearish?

**This variant view is BEARISH for independent CTV ad tech and NEUTRAL for the industry overall.**

The CTV advertising market will grow. But value capture is concentrating, not dispersing. Investing in "CTV exposure" through ad tech intermediaries is increasingly a losing proposition versus investing in publishers/platforms directly.

## Primary Industry Risks

### What Breaks Industry Economics

**1. Advertising Recession**
- GDP-driven ad spending contraction of 10-15% would stress all participants
- CTV would outperform linear but still decline
- Probability: Macro dependent; always a latent risk

**2. Walled Garden Dominance**
- Amazon, Google, Netflix, Disney capture 80%+ of CTV ad spend
- Independent ecosystem withers
- Probability: MODERATE-HIGH; already in progress

**3. Measurement Fragmentation Persists**
- No standard currency emerges; advertiser frustration limits CTV budgets
- Linear TV maintains "reach" advantage
- Probability: MODERATE; industry working toward standards

**4. Privacy Regulation Escalation**
- CCPA/GDPR-style restrictions limit targeting/measurement
- CTV's measurement advantage erodes
- Probability: MODERATE; regulatory momentum exists

**5. Total TV Advertising Secular Decline**
- Linear + CTV combined shrinks as attention fragments
- CTV growth slows dramatically
- Probability: LOW-MODERATE; underappreciated long-term risk

## Historical Context: Learning from Adjacent Markets

### Digital Advertising Parallels

CTV is following patterns seen in earlier digital advertising markets:

**Display Advertising (2005-2015):**
- Fragmented ad tech ecosystem emerged
- Massive consolidation followed (Google acquired DoubleClick, etc.)
- Take rates compressed from 40%+ to 15-20%
- Value concentrated in platforms, not intermediaries

**Mobile Advertising (2010-2020):**
- Initial fragmentation and premium pricing
- Consolidated to Google + Meta duopoly
- Independent ad tech marginalized
- Privacy changes (iOS 14.5) disrupted economics

**CTV Pattern (2018-2028?):**
- Fragmentation and premium pricing (2018-2022)
- Consolidation underway (2023-2026)
- Value likely concentrates in streamers + platforms
- Independent ad tech space shrinks

**Historical lesson:** Betting on "independent ad tech" has been a losing trade in digital advertising. Why would CTV be different?

## Final Assessment: The Three Things That Matter

### 1. Supply Has Caught Up to Demand

The CTV scarcity premium is over. Netflix, Disney+, and aggressive FAST expansion have flooded the market. CPMs are structurally lower, and independent ad tech pricing power is diminished. This is **the defining change** of the 2022-2025 period.

### 2. Value Is Concentrating, Not Dispersing

Despite industry growth, the opportunity set for independent participants is shrinking. Publishers and platforms are capturing more of the stack. The Trade Desk has separated from the independent DSP pack. Everyone else faces structural headwinds.

### 3. The Market Conflates Industry Growth with Participant Opportunity

CTV advertising will grow. This does not mean all CTV-exposed companies will prosper. The market appears to underweight disintermediation risk and overweight the "rising tide lifts all boats" narrative.
