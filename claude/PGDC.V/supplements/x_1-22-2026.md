Since the cat is out of the bag, I wanted to publicly disclose why I decided to invest in Patagonia Gold. 

I usually don't put money into stocks that are already up a lot, but this is special.

$PGDC.V

Bottom line - fully funded, fully permitted mine coming online literally this month that seems likely to produce 1M or more AuEQ ounces (heavily gold weighted, but a lot of silver) at an AISC well under $2000.
 
Basically the story is this. Two junior mining companies merged in 2019-2020. One of these mining companies, Hunt Mining, had been exploring and acquiring properties (including a processing plant / refinery), while the other, Patagonia, had been doing exploration and some mining. It was the 2010s in Argentina, though, and both faced issues, on both the inflation front and the permitting front.

Both of these companies were backed by extremely wealthy guys that put hundreds of millions of dollars of their own money into exploration and development. 

Now it's sort of the perfect storm - a better government, inflation hitting multi decade lows in Argentina (outlook for 2026 is just 10%), everything permitted, everything funded, and now, everything built. It's common for mining stocks to derisk around the time they go to production and that's what's happening here. 

Just to drive home how big of a deal this is, it's the first permitted mining project in Rio Negro in a very, very long time, and the only new gold mine coming online in Argentina. It's a massive achievement to develop very high grade properties. This mine is an easy surface play, but some of the other properties show bonanza type grades over decent intercepts.

Has the stock run too much already? I don't know, but I'm not selling, because I've done NPV calcs on the first mine, and I've looked at the resource base and portfolio on their > 2.5M acre land package (comparable to mid tier and even smaller senior miners).

The market cap is $275M. This is a lot higher than the < $20M they traded for a year ago. Why was the market cap so low? Because you had a small and wealthy group of insiders that were funding this, basically without talking about it at all, without any dilution. They got bank financing and did ring fenced preferred share financing. One of the two main guys sold a company for $1.9B. The other built the largest cabinet maker on the west coast. These guys didn't *need* public money. Together, insiders own ~75% of the capital structure.

What's baked into the valuation at $275M? Frankly, not much. That's approximately what has been invested over the past 30 years by both primary insiders, including during all the years when you basically couldn't do gold mining in Argentina so assets were going for a song.

If you just take the mine coming online now and ignore the other assets, the gigantic land package, etc., it starts making a lot more sense why this stock is up 2000% in a year, much of that action coming over the past few months as it became apparent that the completion of the mine was in fact imminent. We're talking first pour within 90 days, and there's already a purchase agreement lined up with Asahi.

Napkin math, 50k ounces AuEq at $4500/oz comes out to $225M, at a $1500 AISC you're talking $150M/year. And the Argentine government website says it will produce 97k ounces/year. If you get 97k ounces per year and a 5 year mine life, at current gold prices (and this mine has a grade of 2g/t of gold and 20g/t of silver), you're looking at something like $275-300M per year in gross profits. 

This opportunity was "hidden in plain site" because management was silently executing instead of talking about it. They hadn't updated their investor presentation in like 4 years. They actually bought back a little bit of shares. All the financing was done internally. 

But basically, because it was the first real mine built in Argentina in a long time, you basically just couldn't hide all of the activity - the permitting, the hundreds of people working on it, the media buzz. Literally 4-6 months ago you could still buy this stock for pennies. Some very sharp people did, and they have not been selling, which is why shares have rallied so sharply as the market has begun to grasp the value here.

The capital structure is fairly simple. There is ~$50M in debt, and the project was partially financed using ring fenced preferred shares that split profits 80-20 with common until that $40M financing is paid off, then the current mine goes to a 60/40 split in favor of common shareholders. Current shareholders benefit from the decades of investment by the two primary insiders, which in my estimation amount to over $300M including current financing. 

So again, to the napkin math, you're getting 60% of 700k ounces of gold and ~10M ounces of silver, that will be mined over the next 5-10 years, at a cost of somewhere in the neighborhood of $1500 AISC.

That's > $3B worth of gold in the ground and > $800M worth of silver in the ground, which will be extracted at somewhere around a 65% gross margin. For $247M, you're getting more than 60% of $1.5B in NPV, and one of the largest land packages in the Americas and an ensemble of high grade follow up projects to boot. 

Considering all this, the 2000% increase in market value over the past year ($0.04 to $0.80) doesn't seem crazy at all. What's crazy is that a year ago, you could've gotten a 60% share in one of the most important mining projects in the history of Argentina for $20M.

I expect there to be volatility, and the prudent thing would be to wait to see the Q2 numbers (I expect first pour in Q1), but I think there is a really good chance this company becomes a mid tier miner, and I think if you really look at what they have, there is a pathway to a 9 figure acquisition by a senior miner (which by the way, is who they acquired most of this portfolio from, in the days where it was practically impossible to do mining in Argentina). 

Regarding the criticisms around the lack of communication, which would you rather have? A company that puts out presentations, constantly dilutes, and deletes the old presentations when they don't hit their goals? Or a company that silently and successfully executes on bringing a fairly good sized mine to production with insider money? Since the stock has started moving, the company has been disclosing more of the reality on the ground, which you can also independently confirm (as I did) with local media reports, phone interviews with local officals, and drone footage.

Again, I expect volatility, but I also expect that eventually the market will realize that this hidden in plain sight gem is worth a LOT more than the current $275m. The path from $0.06 to $0.60 was simply price discovery as market participants began to discover this story, learn the background, and see what was unfolding. The hardest part in junior mining stocks is finding something that's:

1) Heavily insider owned
2) Fully funded
3) Fully permitted
4) Economic
5) Construction done, entering production

This checks all the boxes. I believe it was a unicorn a year ago, an impossibly, unbelievably rare opportunity. Today, there has been price discovery, but I challenge you to find anything else that checks all the above boxes and still trades at such a huge discount to NPV. 

Your criticisms welcome. I remain long.
