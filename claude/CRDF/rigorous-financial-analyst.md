# Cardiff Oncology (CRDF) -- Financial Assessment

**Date:** 2026-02-24 | **Price:** $1.70 | **Market Cap:** ~$115M | **Shares Outstanding:** 67.4M

## Business Financial Profile

Pre-revenue clinical-stage biotech. Single-asset company: onvansertib (oral PLK1 inhibitor) targeting RAS-mutated metastatic colorectal cancer. Zero recurring revenue ($593K in royalties for FY2025). 100% of value is option value on clinical/regulatory success. Capital-intensive drug development with no near-term path to cash generation.

## Balance Sheet and Cash Runway

**Cash position (12/31/2025):** $58.3M (per FY2025 earnings release). Company guides runway into Q1 2027, roughly 12 months from today.

**Quarterly cash trajectory:**
- Q1 2025: ~$14.5M opex, ~$79M starting cash (estimated)
- Q2 2025: $71.0M cash; ~$8.3M operating cash burn
- Q3 2025: $60.6M cash; ~$10.8M operating cash burn
- Q4 2025: $58.3M cash; implied ~$2.3M net burn (likely aided by timing/investment maturities)

**Annual burn:** FY2025 total opex was $49.6M. Net loss was $45.9M. Cash operating burn averaged roughly $10M/quarter through Q2-Q3, with some variability. A reasonable normalized quarterly burn assumption is $10-12M.

At $10-12M/quarter burn against $58.3M cash, runway extends 5-6 quarters from year-end 2025, consistent with the Q1 2027 guidance. This is tight but adequate to reach FDA discussion milestones in H1 2026.

**Critical point:** A Phase 3 registrational trial will cost substantially more than the current Phase 2. Phase 3 CRC trials typically cost $80-150M+. The company cannot fund a Phase 3 from current resources. A capital raise is not a possibility -- it is a certainty.

## Burn Rate Composition

FY2025 operating expenses (per earnings release):
- R&D: $35.3M (71% of opex), down from $36.9M in FY2024
- SG&A: $14.2M (29% of opex), up from $12.5M in FY2024
- Total: $49.6M vs. $49.3M prior year

R&D declined modestly as the Phase 2 trial completed enrollment. SG&A rose $1.7M, likely reflecting costs tied to CEO/CFO transition and pre-commercialization build. Expect R&D to inflect sharply upward if/when a Phase 3 initiates.

## Dilution History and Capital Structure

**Shares outstanding:** 67.4M basic.

**December 2024 offering:** 15.4M shares at $2.60/share, raising ~$40M gross. This was a 30%+ dilution event at a significant discount to the then-prevailing price range. The offering was described as "oversubscribed," suggesting institutional demand, but the pricing tells you the real story -- they took the capital at whatever price cleared.

**Historical dilution pattern:** The share count has grown substantially over the company's life (the company was formerly Trovagene). Clinical-stage biotechs with no revenue have one funding source: equity. Every 12-18 months, expect a raise.

**Accumulated deficit:** Not precisely confirmed from available data, but net losses of ~$46M/year on a company that has been burning cash for years implies an accumulated deficit likely exceeding $250M.

**Warrants/options:** Specific outstanding figures are not available from the press releases reviewed. The 10-K would contain full detail. Given SBC is embedded in the $49.6M opex and the company regularly grants options, fully diluted share count is likely 75-85M shares (assumption based on typical clinical-stage biotech option pools of 10-15% of basic shares).

## Forward Financing Scenario

The company will need to raise capital within the next 6-9 months, almost certainly before initiating a Phase 3. Here is the math:

**Scenario: $80-100M raise (minimum for Phase 3 initiation)**
- At $1.70/share, a $90M raise requires issuing ~53M new shares (before discount)
- With a typical 10-15% offering discount, call it $1.45-1.55/share: ~58-62M new shares
- This would nearly double the share count to ~125-130M shares
- Post-money market cap would be ~$200-220M on $150M+ in cash

**Scenario: Partnership or licensing deal**
- A pharma partner could fund Phase 3 in exchange for ex-US rights or profit share
- This would preserve equity but reduce the ultimate value capture
- Given the strong Phase 2 data (72.2% ORR, HR 0.37), partnering leverage has improved materially

**Dilution risk is the single most important financial variable for this stock.** The timing, pricing, and structure of the next raise will determine whether current shareholders participate in upside or get diluted into irrelevance.

## Comparable Valuations

Clinical-stage oncology biotechs with positive Phase 2 data targeting RAS-pathway cancers:

| Company | Stage | Market Cap | Notes |
|---------|-------|-----------|-------|
| Revolution Medicines (RVMD) | Phase 1/2, multi-asset RAS(ON) | ~$22.7B | Multiple RAS programs, $2B Royalty Pharma deal |
| Relay Therapeutics (RLAY) | Phase 1, RAS family | ~$1.5B | Earlier stage, broader platform |
| Mirati Therapeutics | Acquired by BMS | $4.8B + $1B CVR | Had approved KRAS G12C drug |
| Cardiff Oncology (CRDF) | Phase 2 complete | ~$115M | Single asset, first-line mCRC |

Cardiff's $115M valuation is extraordinarily low relative to the clinical data quality. A 72.2% ORR with HR 0.37 in first-line RAS-mutated mCRC is a differentiated dataset. However, the discount reflects real risks: single asset, no FDA agreement on registrational path yet, cash constraints, and the PLK1 mechanism (not a direct RAS inhibitor) may face skepticism on durability.

The valuation gap between CRDF and RVMD/RLAY is partly explained by platform breadth and cash position, but even adjusting for those factors, CRDF trades at a steep discount to clinical-stage oncology peers with comparable or weaker efficacy signals.

- [x] **Persistent negative free cash flow** -- Inherent to pre-revenue biotech; not a red flag per se but a structural constraint
- [ ] Rising SBC as % of revenue -- Revenue is immaterial; SBC is embedded in opex but not outsized
- [ ] Margin expansion via accounting -- N/A, pre-revenue
- [ ] Weak cash conversion -- N/A
- [x] **Balance sheet fragility** -- 12 months of runway with a Phase 3 to fund
- [ ] Aggressive revenue recognition -- N/A
- [x] **Leadership vacuum** -- Interim CEO, searching for permanent CEO and CFO simultaneously. This is a material governance risk heading into the most important regulatory and financing period in the company's history.

## Key Financial Drivers

1. **Dilution timing and pricing** -- The next capital raise determines shareholder outcomes. A raise at $3-5 (post-data catalyst) vs. $1.50 (current levels) is the difference between manageable and destructive dilution.

2. **FDA registrational pathway** -- If FDA requires a full Phase 3 (large, randomized, long PFS/OS endpoint), the capital need is $100M+. If an accelerated pathway is possible (smaller trial, ORR endpoint), the economics improve dramatically.

3. **Partnership optionality** -- A pharma licensing deal could solve the funding gap without equity dilution. The Phase 2 data quality creates real negotiating leverage.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

The market is pricing CRDF as if the Phase 2 data is incremental. A 72.2% ORR vs. 43.2% SOC with HR 0.37 in first-line RAS-mutated mCRC is not incremental -- it is potentially registrational-quality data. RAS-mutated mCRC is a large, underserved market with ~$3-5B in annual treatment spending.

What the market may be underweighting is that this data, if confirmed, creates meaningful partnering leverage that could solve the balance sheet problem without catastrophic dilution. The leadership vacuum (no permanent CEO or CFO) during this critical window is likely suppressing partnership discussions and contributing to the valuation discount. Filling those roles is arguably as important as the FDA meeting.

The variant perception here is not about the science -- it is about whether the company can execute the financing and regulatory strategy competently with an incomplete management team. The data is strong. The corporate execution risk is what keeps this cheap.
