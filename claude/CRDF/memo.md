## The Setup

RAS mutations occur in ~50% of mCRC patients (~50K new US cases/year). First-line treatment has been FOLFOX/FOLFIRI + bevacizumab for 20+ years with no meaningful innovation. Existing KRAS inhibitors (sotorasib, adagrasib) only address G12C mutations (3-5% of patients) in later-line settings. Onvansertib targets all RAS mutations via PLK1 synthetic lethality -- a fundamentally larger addressable population in the more valuable first-line setting.

No competitor is running a registrational trial for a PLK1 inhibitor in first-line mCRC. Revolution Medicines (RMC-6236) is a longer-term competitive threat but is years behind in mCRC development.

The leadership vacuum (no permanent CEO or CFO) is a legitimate concern and likely contributes to the discount. However, it is also the kind of problem that gets solved quickly when a company suddenly has valuable data.

## Risks and Kill Switches

**Thesis invalidators:**
- FDA demands a large, long Phase 3 with OS primary endpoint AND no partner materializes -- this creates a dilution spiral that destroys equity value
- Phase 2 data does not replicate in larger trial (sample size risk is real)
- Tolerability issues emerge at scale that narrow the differentiation vs. historical PLK1 failures

**Permanent loss scenarios:**
- Company fails to raise capital or find a partner by Q1 2027 and enters dilution death spiral
- Competitive leapfrog -- Revolution Medicines moves pan-RAS inhibitor into first-line mCRC faster than expected

**Kill switches:**
- Exit if FDA Type B meeting results in requirement for full Phase 3 with OS endpoint AND no partnership is announced within 60 days
- Exit if management team remains incomplete (no permanent CEO) after 3 months
- Reduce if stock reaches $4-5 range (near prior highs) on squeeze alone without fundamental catalyst confirmation
