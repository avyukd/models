# Nylon 6 / Caprolactam / Ammonium Sulfate Industry Analysis

## The Bottom Line Up Front

This industry is in a prolonged trough driven by massive Chinese capacity additions that have fundamentally altered global supply-demand dynamics. Global caprolactam capacity stands at ~9,500 KT against ~7,000 KT of demand -- roughly 74% utilization globally, with nylon 6 utilization at just 65%. Chinese capacity alone grew from 4.3M tons in 2020 to ~8.9M tons in 2025, a 13% CAGR. That is not a cycle -- it is a structural oversupply event that has broken the old industry equilibrium. European rationalization (BASF, Fibrant, DOMO, Spolana) is removing capacity, but it is a rounding error against Chinese additions.

The non-consensus view: **this trough is shallower than it should be for North American integrated producers, and consensus may be underestimating the asymmetry of the recovery path.**

---

## What Drives Profitability in This Industry

Three interlocking product markets determine economics:

1. **Caprolactam spread over benzene/cyclohexane feedstock** -- the core conversion margin. Global pricing is set by the marginal Asian producer. North American pricing typically carries a $200-400/MT premium over Asian spot due to logistics, quality, and customer relationships.

2. **Nylon 6 resin conversion margin over caprolactam** -- driven by downstream demand (auto, textiles, packaging). This margin compresses when oversupply allows downstream buyers to pit suppliers against each other.

3. **Ammonium sulfate co-product credit** -- 2.0-2.2 tons of ammonium sulfate are produced per ton of caprolactam. This is not optional; it is a stoichiometric byproduct. For an integrated producer, ammonium sulfate revenue offsets caprolactam production costs. When fertilizer prices are strong, it effectively subsidizes the nylon chain. When weak, it becomes a disposal problem.

**Fact:** The caprolactam byproduct route accounts for 62% of global ammonium sulfate supply.

**Judgment:** For a North American integrated producer making its own ammonia and sulfuric acid, the margin structure is fundamentally different from a standalone caprolactam converter. The ammonium sulfate leg can carry the business through nylon troughs -- and this is exactly what happened in 2024-2025.

## Industry Pricing Power and Competitive Dynamics

**Pricing power is near zero for caprolactam and nylon 6 on a global basis.** These are commodity chemicals with published benchmark pricing. The marginal producer is a Chinese coal-to-caprolactam plant with variable costs well below Western producers' total costs.

Key pricing mechanisms:
- Caprolactam: Formula-based (benzene + conversion spread), with quarterly or monthly resets in North America. Asian spot pricing heavily influences global sentiment.
- Nylon 6 resin: Caprolactam + conversion margin, negotiated with OEM/Tier 1 customers.
- Ammonium sulfate: Seasonal fertilizer pricing, correlated with nitrogen fertilizer complex (urea, UAN) and sulfur markets.

**Structural factor:** China's caprolactam capacity is increasingly coal-based rather than benzene-based. This decouples Chinese marginal costs from the oil/benzene price complex that drives Western producers' costs. When oil is high, Chinese coal-based producers have a cost advantage. When oil is low, the gap narrows. This is a permanent structural change from the pre-2015 era.

## Supply Elasticity and Capacity Dynamics

**Supply is highly inelastic downward in the short run, and highly elastic upward in China over 2-3 years.** This is the core problem.

### Capacity Timeline

| Year | China Caprolactam Capacity | Global Capacity (est.) |
|------|---------------------------|----------------------|
| 2020 | 4.3M tons | ~7.5M tons |
| 2022 | ~5.5M tons | ~8.5M tons |
| 2024 | 6.95M tons | ~9.5M tons |
| 2025 | ~8.9M tons | ~11M+ tons |

**Fact:** China added more caprolactam capacity in the last 5 years (~4.6M tons) than the entire non-China world operates.

**Fact:** Global capacity utilization has fallen from mid-80s% range to ~74% globally and ~65% for nylon 6, despite steady demand growth of 2.5-3% annually.

### Supply Response Mechanisms

**Upward:** Chinese provincial governments subsidize new chemical complexes as economic development. Capacity decisions are partially decoupled from price signals. This is a structural driver of oversupply that does not self-correct through normal market mechanisms.

**Downward (finally happening):**
- **BASF:** Cut European caprolactam by 100 KT/yr, shut Ludwigshafen plant (2023-2024)
- **Spolana (Czech Republic):** Exiting caprolactam and PVC by mid-2025 (~70% workforce cut)
- **Fibrant (Netherlands):** Ceasing caprolactam production at Chemelot (announced Oct 2025). Utilization had fallen to 50%.
- **DOMO (Germany):** Filed insolvency Dec 2025. Three subsidiaries including DOMO Caproleuna (caprolactam, PA6 producer). Plants in emergency operation. Investor search ongoing.
- **China:** Producers implemented 20% phased production cuts in Q4 2025 as losses exceeded 600 RMB/ton.

**Judgment:** European rationalization is structurally permanent -- those plants will not restart given energy cost disadvantage (50%+ higher than even neighboring countries in the Netherlands case). This removes perhaps 300-500 KT from the Western market permanently. But Chinese capacity additions dwarf this. The net global position is still oversupplied.

## Cyclicality Assessment: Where We Are

### Historical Cycle (Using AdvanSix as Industry Proxy)

| Period | Industry Phase | Adj. EBITDA (est.) | Revenue | EBITDA Margin |
|--------|---------------|-------------------|---------|---------------|
| 2017-2018 | Mid-cycle | ~$160-180M | ~$1.4-1.5B | ~11-12% |
| 2019 | Downturn | ~$120-140M | ~$1.3B (est.) | ~9-10% |
| 2020 | Trough (COVID) | ~$100-120M | ~$1.17B | ~9% |
| 2021 | Recovery/Boom | ~$267M | ~$1.68B | ~16% |
| 2022 | **Peak** | **$308.5M** | **$1.95B** | **~16%** |
| 2023 | Sharp decline | $153.6M | $1.53B | ~10% |
| 2024 | Trough | $142.1M | $1.51B | ~9.4% |
| 2025 | Trough (stabilizing) | $157M | $1.52B | 10.3% |

*Note: 2017-2020 EBITDA figures are estimates based on fragmentary data. Revenue data is sourced from public filings.*

**Key observations:**

1. The 2021-2022 peak was driven by post-COVID restocking, supply chain disruptions, and tight capacity before the full wave of Chinese expansion came online. Peak EBITDA margins of ~16% were approximately 2x mid-cycle levels. **This will not recur** absent another exogenous shock.

2. The current trough ($142-157M EBITDA range) is notably higher than would be expected given the degree of global oversupply. This is because the ammonium sulfate/plant nutrients segment is performing well -- buffering the nylon trough.

3. Full-cycle EBITDA range: roughly $120M (trough) to $310M (peak), with mid-cycle around $160-180M.

## Current Cycle Position: Late Trough / Early Recovery. Confidence: Moderate.

Evidence for trough:
- Company describes "extended trough in the nylon cycle"
- European producers literally going bankrupt (DOMO) or shutting down (Fibrant, Spolana)
- Chinese producers cutting production 20% in Q4 2025 after losses exceeding 600 RMB/ton
- Caprolactam prices in Northeast Asia at ~$1,190/MT (near multi-year lows)
- Global nylon 6 utilization at 65% -- well below profitable levels for marginal producers

Evidence this is late-trough, not early-trough:
- European capacity destruction is accelerating and permanent
- Chinese producers have begun self-correcting (production cuts, operating rate down to 71.8% in Dec 2025)
- AdvanSix EBITDA stabilized and slightly improved in 2025 vs. 2024
- Ammonium sulfate pricing supported by rising sulfur costs ($475-520/long ton sulfur contract vs. $310 prior)

**Key uncertainty:** Chinese capacity additions continue. Another 400+ KT/year is expected through 2027. If demand doesn't absorb this, the trough extends. The question is whether Chinese production discipline holds or whether provincial incentives override rational economic behavior.

## Mid-Cycle Reference Point

**Definition:** Mid-cycle for this industry means:
- Global caprolactam utilization in the 78-82% range
- Nylon 6 pricing that allows Western integrated producers to earn 10-12% EBITDA margins
- Ammonium sulfate at pricing consistent with the broader nitrogen fertilizer complex (not stressed up or down)
- No acute supply disruptions or post-crisis restocking dynamics
- Benzene/feedstock costs at levels that do not create extreme margin compression or windfall

**Quantitative benchmark (using AdvanSix as proxy):** Mid-cycle adjusted EBITDA of ~$160-180M on revenue of ~$1.4-1.5B, implying EBITDA margins of 10.5-12%.

**Judgment:** The 2025 result of $157M EBITDA / 10.3% margin is slightly below mid-cycle, but closer to it than the raw nylon cycle position would suggest. The plant nutrients business is subsidizing the nylon trough. True mid-cycle would require some nylon recovery from current levels.

## The One Industry Factor That Matters

**Chinese caprolactam capacity growth vs. Chinese domestic demand absorption.**

Everything else -- European closures, ammonium sulfate pricing, benzene costs, automotive demand -- is secondary to this. China has added 4.6M tons of capacity in 5 years. If Chinese domestic nylon 6 demand continues growing at 5-7% per year (it grew 24% in 2024, likely a restocking anomaly), this capacity gradually gets absorbed and global pricing recovers. If Chinese demand disappoints -- particularly if the property/construction sector remains weak -- overcapacity persists and Chinese producers export at marginal cost, destroying pricing for everyone else.

**What would cause an inflection:** Either (a) Chinese demand absorbs enough capacity to push Chinese operating rates back above 80% sustainably, or (b) meaningful Chinese capacity closures/mothballing (which would require a prolonged period of losses, and may be delayed by government support).

**How to monitor:** Chinese caprolactam operating rates (published by SunSirs, CCFEI). Below 75% = continued pain. Above 80% = recovery signal. Currently 71.8% (Dec 2025) -- the trough indicator.

## What Consensus May Be Missing

**Consensus view:** "This is a normal commodity chemical trough and the cycle will turn as it always does."

**What they may be missing (bullish for North American integrated producers):**

The structural competitive position of a fully integrated North American producer is dramatically better than it was in any prior cycle. Here is why:

1. **European supply destruction is permanent, not cyclical.** BASF, Fibrant, DOMO, Spolana -- these are not temporary shutdowns. European energy costs make restart uneconomic. This permanently removes 300-500 KT of Western capacity that historically competed for the same customers.

2. **Tariff protection is higher than ever.** General US tariffs on Chinese chemical imports, plus the current US-China trade environment, create a meaningful barrier to Chinese caprolactam entering the North American market. The sole domestic producer has structural protection that didn't exist in prior troughs.

3. **The ammonium sulfate co-product has become the margin anchor, not the nylon chain.** With sulfur costs rising ($475-520/long ton, up from $310) and global ammonium sulfate demand exceeding 26M tons, the fertilizer leg of the business is no longer just a byproduct credit -- it is a strategic asset. In 2025, plant nutrients revenue ($564M) exceeded nylon solutions ($310M) + caprolactam ($271M).

4. **The trough floor is higher than prior cycles.** AdvanSix earned $142-157M EBITDA through 2024-2025 despite the worst nylon cycle conditions since the Chinese capacity wave began. In 2019-2020, before full integration benefits and before the plant nutrients business was optimized, trough EBITDA was likely $100-120M. The structural floor has been raised by $30-50M annually.

**Net assessment:** Consensus is correct that the nylon cycle is in a trough. But consensus may be wrong about the magnitude of upside when the cycle turns, because they are applying old-cycle mental models to a market where the North American competitive position has structurally improved. A return to mid-cycle nylon conditions, layered on top of the already-strong plant nutrients business, could produce $200M+ EBITDA -- well above what consensus models as "mid-cycle" based on historical averages that include periods when the business was less integrated and less optimized.

**This is a modestly bullish non-consensus view for North American integrated producers specifically -- not for the global industry.**

---

## Primary Industry Risk

**China never rationalizes.** Provincial governments continue subsidizing capacity additions. Chinese demand disappoints due to property/construction weakness. Chinese producers export at marginal cost, compressing global pricing indefinitely. European closures have already happened, so there is no further Western rationalization buffer. The industry enters a "Japan-style" extended trough where capacity never clears because the marginal producer is not driven by profit maximization.

Secondary risk: Natural gas and sulfur cost inflation erodes the ammonium sulfate margin that has been supporting through-trough earnings.
