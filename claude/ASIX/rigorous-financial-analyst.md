# ASIX (AdvanSix Inc) -- Financial Analysis at $18.20

**Date**: February 23, 2026
**Market Cap**: ~$490M (26.9M shares x $18.20)
**Enterprise Value**: ~$685M (net debt ~$195M)

---

## The Headline: A Cyclical Trough Business Trading at 4.4x Trough EBITDA -- But the "Normalized" Question Is Everything

At $18.20, the market is pricing ASIX at roughly 4.4x its 2025 adjusted EBITDA of $156.8M. That looks cheap -- until you realize 2025 was itself a trough year, and the company generated only $6M of free cash flow on $1.52B of revenue. The real question is whether ASIX's normalized mid-cycle earnings power justifies the current price or whether the capital intensity of this business permanently impairs free cash generation.

## 2. Cash Flow and Normalized Earnings (The Critical Section)

### Free Cash Flow History

| Year | Operating CF ($M) | CapEx ($M) | FCF ($M) | D&A ($M) |
|------|-------------------|-----------|----------|----------|
| 2022 | ~225 | ~107 | 184.2 (est. per 2023 release) | ~68 |
| 2023 | ~118 | ~107 | 10.2 | ~73 |
| 2024 | 135.4 | 133.7 | 1.7 | 76.2 |
| 2025 | 122.9 | 116.4 | 6.4 | 79.7 |

*Sources: FY2022-FY2025 earnings releases.*

**This is the most important table in the analysis.** Despite generating $49.3M in GAAP net income in 2025, AdvanSix produced only $6.4M of free cash flow. In 2024, net income of $44.1M yielded just $1.7M of FCF.

The culprit: **capital expenditures massively exceed depreciation**. In 2025, capex of $116.4M was 46% above D&A of $79.7M. In 2024, capex of $133.7M was 75% above D&A of $76.2M. This is not a business where D&A approximates true maintenance capex -- this is a capital-hungry chemical plant that requires sustained reinvestment.

### GAAP to Normalized Earnings Bridge (FY2025)

| Item | Amount ($M) | Per Share |
|------|-------------|-----------|
| GAAP Net Income | 49.3 | $1.80 |
| SBC (already deducted in GAAP; est. ~$15M) | -- | -- |
| **Adjustment: Excess capex over D&A** | **(36.7)** | **($1.34)** |
| **Normalized "owner earnings"** | **~12.6** | **~$0.46** |

**Note on SBC**: Exact SBC figure not extractable from press releases. Adjusted diluted EPS of $2.28 vs. GAAP of $1.80 implies a $0.48 spread that includes non-recurring items and likely SBC addbacks. SBC is already deducted in GAAP net income, so the key adjustment is the excess capex over D&A -- the true cash cost of maintaining this asset base that GAAP does not fully capture.

**The normalized "owner earnings" in trough conditions are approximately $0.46/share -- or roughly a 40x P/E at $18.20.**

Even in the peak year of 2022, with $308.5M in EBITDA, FCF was only ~$184M. The asset base is aging and growing -- gross PP&E rose from $917.9M (YE 2024) to $963.7M (YE 2025).

### Mid-Cycle Normalization Attempt

Mid-cycle EBITDA margins based on 2019-2025 cycle: average ~11.5% on average revenue of ~$1.5B = ~$173M EBITDA.

If management hits its 2026 capex target of $75-$95M (midpoint $85M) and D&A runs ~$80M, the capex/D&A gap narrows to ~$5M:

| Mid-Cycle Scenario | Amount ($M) |
|---------------------|-------------|
| Mid-cycle EBITDA | 173 |
| Less: D&A | (80) |
| Less: Interest (~$12M) | (12) |
| Pre-tax income | 81 |
| Less: Tax at 24% | (19) |
| Plus: 45Q credits (~$12M avg) | +12 |
| Net Income | 74 |
| Plus: D&A | 80 |
| Less: Capex ($85M target) | (85) |
| **Normalized FCF** | **~$69M** |
| **Per share (26.9M shares)** | **~$2.55** |

**The $85M capex guidance for 2026 is the single most important assumption.** If management delivers, FCF transforms from ~$6M to potentially $50-70M+. If capex creeps back toward $110-130M (as in 2024-2025), the business generates effectively zero FCF.

## 4. Capital Allocation Assessment

- **Dividend**: $0.64/share annually ($0.16/quarter). Yield of 3.5% at $18.20. Cost ~$17M/year.
- **Buybacks**: $75M additional authorization announced. Actual repurchases minimal in 2024-2025 -- share count flat at ~26.9M. Management conserving cash given capex demands.
- **CapEx**: Dominant use of cash. 2024: $133.7M. 2025: $116.4M. Guided $75-$95M for 2026.
- **Net capital return**: Dividend of ~$17M vs. FCF of $6.4M = dividend not covered by FCF in 2025. Shortfall effectively financed by the revolver.

**The dividend is not well-covered on a true FCF basis at current capex levels.**

## 5. Red Flags Checklist

- [X] **Persistent weak free cash flow**: Three consecutive years (2023-2025) of near-zero FCF despite positive GAAP earnings.
- [ ] Rising SBC as % of revenue: SBC appears modest (~1% of revenue). Not a concern.
- [ ] Margin expansion driven by accounting choices: No evidence.
- [X] **Weak cash conversion**: 2025: $49.3M net income vs. $6.4M FCF = 13% conversion. 2024: $44.1M vs. $1.7M = 4%.
- [ ] Balance sheet fragility: Manageable but trending revolver increase worth monitoring.
- [ ] Aggressive revenue recognition: No evidence.
- [ ] Related party transactions: No evidence.
- [X] **CapEx significantly above D&A for multiple years**: Given revenue has been flat, this supports the interpretation that D&A understates true maintenance requirements.

## 7. Key Financial Drivers

**1. The Capex/D&A Gap (This Is the Entire Thesis)**

AdvanSix has spent $116-$134M/year on capex against $76-$80M in D&A. Management guides $75-$95M for 2026 and $80-$100M for 2027. If they deliver, FCF goes from near-zero to $50-70M+. If they revert, FCF stays near zero. Everything else is noise relative to this variable.

**2. Nylon/Caprolactam Cycle Positioning**

EBITDA margins of 10% compare to peak of 16% (2022). Capacity rationalization occurring globally. Every 100bps of margin improvement on $1.5B revenue = ~$15M EBITDA.

**3. Plant Nutrients as the Earnings Floor**

Plant Nutrients ($564M, 37% of revenue) has been the consistent performer, driven by strong North American ammonium sulfate demand. Provides earnings ballast at cycle trough.

## 8. What Consensus May Be Missing

**The 45Q Tax Credit Optionality Is Material and Under-Discussed**

Management expects ~$18M in 45Q carbon capture tax credits in 2026 (pending DOE approval) and $100M+ cumulative through 2029. These could keep the cash tax rate below 10% through 2027. On current earnings, $18M/year represents ~37% of 2025 GAAP net income.

**The risk**: DOE approval is not guaranteed. If 45Q credits fail to materialize, the tax rate normalizes to ~24%.

**The second non-consensus observation**: At $85M capex with 45Q credits, ASIX generates $2.50-$3.00/share in FCF at mid-cycle (sub-7x FCF). At $120M capex without 45Q credits, it generates $0.50-$1.00/share (20x+ FCF). These are radically different businesses. Both variables (actual capex spend and DOE approval) will be known within 12 months.
